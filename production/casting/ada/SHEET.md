# Ada Vane

The old woman of the street. A principal of LEDGER
(production/casting/CASTING.md); she keeps her name, and her surname (Jafar, 25
September). Text only: face, clothes and voice samples are the builder's.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 78 (born 1912) |
| **Origin** | A widow of the Hook. (The August prototype card made her a retired schoolteacher; Jafar left that out on 28 September: her years of teaching could not be spoken of without children.) |
| **Occupation** | Keeps a window on Quay Street (CASTING.md; canon names only "Ada"). In the routines of 28 September her step is in the terraces across from Mickey's. |
| **Build** | Small, about 5 ft 0 in and shorter than she was; hunched. The old of 1990 were visibly shorter still than the average. |
| **Face** | Deeply lined, bright-eyed; full dentures (a fifth of adults had no natural teeth in 1988, a third of the unskilled). |
| **Hair** | Grey, set; outdoors under a headscarf tied under the chin, and in the wet a clear plastic rain hood over it. |
| **Clothes, 1990** | Indoors a nylon overall over a skirt and cardigan, and slippers. Outdoors a wool or tweed coat, fur-lined ankle boots, a shopping bag. |
| **How she moves** | Slowly, in short steps, a hand to the railing or the wall. On her step or at her window, watching; she turns her whole body to look, not just her head. |
| **Voice** | Thin, old and sharp; broad local vowels; her dentures soften the s. Approved in July: p276 (a woman, 24, English, Oxford). **Needs an older voice:** 24 against 78, and an Oxford accent against a widow of the Hook. The most urgent of the older voices. |

## Three lines

1. "I've sat at that window a long time, love. Long enough to know when to say nothing."
2. "Don't stand on my step with that face on. Come in or go on."
3. "I don't gossip. I remember. It's not the same thing."
