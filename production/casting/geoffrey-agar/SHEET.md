# Geoffrey Agar

Head of the old-money machine. A principal of LEDGER
(production/casting/CASTING.md); until 24 September he was Aldous Vane, and the
old name survives only as an internal id (aldous). Text only: face, clothes and
voice samples are the builder's.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 62 (born 1928) |
| **Origin** | A timber-merchant family of the town (casting research). |
| **Occupation** | Solicitor; a former long-serving councillor, now honorary alderman: aldermen as such were abolished in 1974, and a council can make a former member an honorary alderman for past service (Local Government Act 1972, section 249), which suits old money exactly. Heads the old-money machine of corruption and lawyers (canon). |
| **Build** | Tall and spare, about 5 ft 11 in, a slight stoop he does not admit to. |
| **Face** | Long, clean-shaven, rimless glasses. |
| **Hair** | Silver, swept back. |
| **Clothes, 1990** | A dark three-piece suit: the waistcoat had been going since the war, so it marks him as old-fashioned and old money. White shirt, a sober tie, a watch chain. Outdoors a knee-length dark wool overcoat with a velvet collar, the smart man's coat of the years he learned to dress; black leather gloves, black lace-ups, a black umbrella. Bareheaded: the bowler had gone by the 1980s. |
| **How he moves** | Slowly, and never seen to hurry. Waits for doors to be opened. Speaks quietly enough that people lean in. Sits back; lets the other man fill the silence. |
| **Voice** | Quiet and precise, educated vowels, never hurried. Approved on 24 September: p226 (a man, 22, English, Surrey). The accent suits his class; **the age does not: he needs an older voice** (22 against 62). |

## Three lines

1. "Sit down. I find most things go better sitting down."
2. "I don't make threats. I make sure people have read the paperwork."
3. "I prefer things done properly. It saves everybody so much trouble later."
