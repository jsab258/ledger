# The street's regulars: thirty casting sheets, 1990

The second tier of production/casting/CASTING.md: about thirty people the
player passes day after day. Text only: faces, clothes and voices are the
builder's, and no voice is cast without Jafar's yes. Judged as one contact
sheet on the town's approval page; the approval, when given, is
REGULARS.md.approval.json beside this file (tools/approvals.py), and it lapses if
this file changes. On his yes these sheets replace the older cards for the same
ids in tier2-batch-1.json (game-design/ and the game's StreamingAssets), which
give some of them other ages and trades.

## How they were chosen (decided within canon, DECISIONS 30 September)

- **Who.** Everybody who already walks the street's routines
  (production/specs/hook-cast.json), so every sheet is somebody the player
  actually meets, except six whose roles repeat another's:
  - the laundry's presser and a laundress (Iva, Selma);
  - the pawn's clerk and appraiser (Marta, Ines);
  - the pawn counter's morning locksmith (Jelena);
  - the customs assistant (Goran).

  They stay in the routines as crowd faces, the third tier. Tanja, a laundress
  in the routines, is laid off now (row 8), so the laundry's day loses her. The ids are the
  routines' own, kept from the old studio. Added, as CASTING.md asks: Grace
  Rinaldi's caff, her Italian father's before her, and the Tangs' takeaway on
  the parade. Their routines, and a bay for the takeaway (the parade's empty
  unit is free), are handovers.
- **Ages and sexes: CASTING.md's census shares, exactly.**
  - 8 in their twenties;
  - 8 aged thirty to forty-four;
  - 6 aged forty-five to fifty-nine;
  - 5 aged sixty to seventy-four (3 women);
  - 3 over seventy-five (2 women);
  - 14 men and 16 women in all.
- **Trades: the census where canon allows it.**
  - As the census asks, nearly: four retired (and Doreen, who won't) and two
    out of work, on a street whose work went with the docks.
  - Canon changes the rest. Canon and the street's facts have two drivers and a
    dispatcher, not four drivers. The routines put the quay, the docks, the pawn
    and the laundry on the street, so dock and shipping has six where the census
    has two. The fish market's own workers are in the crowd.
  - Two keep house, a housekeeper and a landlady, both for others; the census
    has three.
  - The outfit's man at the ferry landing stays unnamed and out of these
    sheets, as canon has him.
- **Names.**
  - First names are drawn from the ONS top 100 for each person's birth decade.
  - Surnames come from Hull's 1990 directory and Guppy's East Riding and
    Lincolnshire lists (production/research/casting/notes/names.md).
  - None is a principal's surname.
  - Every name was checked against well-known people. Dropped: Kevin Ward (a
    rugby league player), Wayne Dobson (a television magician), Bilocca
    (Lillian Bilocca of Hull, 1968), Doyle for a priest's housekeeper (Father
    Ted's Mrs Doyle), Peter Chan (a film director) and Tweedy (Chicken Run).
  - No first name one letter from a principal's (Roy and Ron, Carl and Carol),
    and no surname a pun in a fishing port (Codling).
  - A Hong Kong wife keeps her own surname.
- **Look: CASTING.md's period traits, counted.**
  - a moustache on 2 of the 6 men aged 35 to 60;
  - a perm or set on 4 of the 8 women over 45;
  - glasses on 10 of 30;
  - dentures on 6 of the 12 over 55;
  - 4 stout;
  - smokers: 5 working men and 6 women;
  - faded tattoos only on the old trawlerman;
  - nothing groomed.
  - Nobody is dressed like a principal: no mullet and gold (Danny Cammack), no
    moustache and car coat (Keith Garbutt), no cardigan or glasses on a chain
    (Sheila), no set and heavy gold (Maureen Jensen), no feathered bob or
    blazer (Carol Ellis), no donkey jacket (Ron's own), no short dark hair and
    clean shave (Tom).
- **Voice.** A slot, not a voice, by age: younger (20s), middle (30 to 44),
  older (45 to 74), oldest (75 and over). The accent is the Humber's own unless
  the sheet says otherwise. The pool needs eleven: eight local, one Irish (Cork)
  and two Hong Kong English, one past CASTING.md's eight to ten.
- **The content rule.**
  - No drink, no betting and no children, in any line or trait, and nobody's
    trade touches either.
  - Nobody is a teacher, since teaching cannot be spoken of without children
    (Jafar, 28 September, of Ada).
  - Nothing explains why the docks went: the outline keeps that "underneath,
    never explained".

## Notes for the builder, when he says yes

- **Faces.**
  - One of CASTING.md's sixteen crowd faces each, individualised by hair,
    glasses and wear as the "look" column says.
  - The Tangs need two East Asian faces of their own. The crowd set has one
    East Asian face, and a white face re-skinned is wrong. They are placed as a
    couple, never as a lone token.
- **Voices.** Slots, not voices, cast only with Jafar's yes. Nora Cronin's Cork
  and the Tangs' Hong Kong English each need a real speaker's reference of that
  accent, checked before he hears it (the gate).
- **Routines** (the town writes them; the builder places them). Albert mends
  nets mornings only. The six folded into the crowd keep theirs. Grace and the
  Tangs need theirs, and the takeaway a bay: the parade's empty unit.
