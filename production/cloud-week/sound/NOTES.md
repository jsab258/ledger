# Quay Street sound: notes (12 October 2026, unit 10, proof P13)

## What was made

97 files, all 48 kHz 16-bit WAV, rebuilt exactly from 46 CC0 recordings by `make_sound.py` (BRIEF.md says what each must sound like and holds every number):

- **21 ambience loops, 60 to 75 s, seamless**: traffic, gulls, harbour, wind, voices through a window and a radio through a window, each in day, night and rain (18), and the rain on pavement, on an awning and in a gutter (3, rain only). Beds are stereo; gulls, voices, radio and the pavement rain are mono. Eighteen are 75 s; the rain traffic, the day wind and the rain harbour are 60 s (their recordings, the road 67 s, the wind 66.5 s and the rigging whistle 72 s, are too short for 75 s without playing part of them twice). Mixed at their own levels (the 60 s loops repeated, the mix measured over the 300 s in which all come round together) the day bed measures -36.6 LUFS, night -40.3, rain -33.5 (targets -36.5, -39.5, -33.5).
- **40 footsteps**: paving, setts, asphalt, wet, wood, 8 variants each (mono, 133 to 260 ms).
- **36 other one-shots**: shop door (open 2, close 3, latch 4), house door (open 3, close 5, latch 3), shop bell 5, phone box (door creak 5, door shut 3, a telephone ringing 3).
- Previews: 16 spectrogram JPEGs in `production/previews/cloud-week/sound/` (in git; drawn by ffmpeg with the picture's top set by resampling, because ffmpeg's `stop` option draws a false bright line one tenth of the way up every panel, 960 Hz on a 10 kHz panel, 1.2 kHz on a 12 kHz one: it is a drawing artefact, pink noise shows it too, and the 12 October first build's notes misread it as sound) and 9 listening clips (Ogg Vorbis, at most 20 s: the three bed mixes, a walk on each surface, the doors). **The clips are not in git** (the size guard refuses audio; the repository's root `.gitignore` lists `production/previews/cloud-week/sound/*.ogg`, so a clone on the PC does not see them as untracked): `make_sound.py` writes them beside the JPEGs and into `/home/user/ledger-scratch/sound-out/clips/`. The WAVs and the clips live in `/home/user/ledger-scratch/sound-out/` and are rebuilt by one command, `make_sound.py`; on the PC they go to `F:\LedgerTools\sound-out`. The three mix clips carry +10 dB over their real levels, with a peak limiter, so they are audible (the rain clip is encoded at a lower Vorbis quality to stay under 300 KB); they are for judging what is in each bed.

## Rebuild

```
python production/cloud-week/sound/fetch_sources.py   # the sources, 102 MB, into SOUND_SOURCES
python production/cloud-week/sound/make_sound.py      # 97 WAVs (226 MB) and the 9 clips into SOUND_OUT, manifest.json; about 12 minutes
python production/cloud-week/sound/check_sound.py     # checks/check_v1.json: "N of M rows pass" (1235 of 1235 on the 12 October rebuild after the review); about 12 minutes
python production/cloud-week/sound/make_previews.py   # the 16 spectrogram JPEGs, several minutes
python production/cloud-week/sound/check_sound.py --negative-controls   # breaks eleven outputs on purpose and shows the two grain tests on made-up records; each must fail by name (13 of 13 caught); about 12 minutes
```

Needs ffmpeg, ffprobe, curl, numpy and scipy; no other package. The defaults are the cloud's folders; on the PC set SOUND_SOURCES and SOUND_OUT (for instance `F:/LedgerTools/sound-sources` and `F:/LedgerTools/sound-out`). Every step is deterministic (fixed cut points, seeded random numbers, seeded dither): a second run gives byte-identical WAVs, and `manifest.json` holds each file's sha256. `fetch_sources.py` refuses any source whose page does not say CC0, and downloads one file at a time, because Freesound's server answers 403 to parallel downloads.

## What is known to be short

- **The Freesound sources are lossy.** Each is the page's public high-quality preview (MP3, 160 to 190 kbps); the lossless original needs a free Freesound login, which is his to use or not. The 8 BigSoundBank sources are lossless. To swap in an original: put the file in the sources folder under the same name, run `fetch_sources.py --verify` after updating its md5, and rebuild.
- **Setts have no recording.** Two searches (Freesound: cobblestone, cobbled street, cobbles, setts, granite setts, paving stones, pavers, uneven stone, old town stones, pavés, adoquin; BigSoundBank: cobblestone, paving, stone) found one cobblestone footsteps take, at about 6 dB signal-to-noise: one step in 45 passed the clean-step tests. `step_setts_*` are steps on a stone patio and on old rough slabs: the nearest hard-stone steps found, not setts.
- **The phone box's ring is a house telephone.** A GPO 746 (1970s bell, double ring), not a kiosk payphone: searched phone box, phone booth, telephone box, kiosk, payphone, public telephone, British and UK telephone ring; no British kiosk ring is CC0.
- **Day traffic is a busy road made distant** by a low-pass and a leveller; night traffic is a quiet street in Hilversum; the rain traffic is a wet road in France with 8 dB more tyre hiss above 1.5 kHz (my processing, not a recording). The wind and some rain sources are French or Canadian. Nothing was heard.
- **Rain has one intensity.** The 5 October ruling has dry overcast, drizzle and rain; drizzle is not built separately (turn the three rain layers down for it, or the game fades them).
- **Gulls are few**: the day loop has nine calls from four takes, four of them re-pitched by 4 to 7 percent, in 75 s; night has one bird passing (a fifth take) and one far call; rain has two far calls.
- **The radio is a measured guess at a radio.** It is speech in bursts (0.6 to 2 s) with gaps (0.7 to 3 s) of different lengths, the grains hopping 0.8 to 1.6 of their length so some touch and some leave a gap, then 300 Hz to 3 kHz, 5 dB of body at 1 kHz, a little cone distortion, a faint hiss and a low-pass for the window (2.6 kHz day, 2.2 night, 1.5 rain). Measured, it differs from the voices: under 0.3% of its power below 250 Hz (the day voices 5%), 6.6% between 1 and 2.5 kHz (the voices 2%), its 95% roll-off 1233 Hz (voices 844), 65% of its 100 ms frames 15 dB or more under its loud ones (voices 9%). But its recording ("Muffled Talking", processed and already muffled: nine tenths of its power between 445 and 727 Hz) has almost nothing above 1 kHz to work on, so the band is widened by distortion and not by content; and at most 0.1% of its power may lie above 2.5 kHz (the "no word" guard), so it is a 300 Hz to 2.5 kHz sound in practice. Whether it reads as a radio or as more people talking, and whether a word can be caught, only an ear can say; if not, use a longer CC0 adult-speech recording with a full band. It is made from the scrambled voice of someone talking far away; no music.
- **The speech recordings are short** (19.7 s for the night voices, 18.8 s for the radio, 33 s for the rain voices, 33 s and 19.7 s for the day voices), so a moment of them has to come back. The build lays every grain in a place chosen so that none returns sooner than 20 s (the radio and the night voices, which use little of theirs: bursts and phrases with real gaps), 12 s (day voices) or 7 s (rain voices, a dense murmur that needs more than the 33 s offer); the figures are in BRIEF.md and `manifest.json` records every grain.
- **Cut points for doors were read from envelopes and spectrograms, not by ear.** "Open", "close" and "latch" are the best reading; the shop door's open and close come partly from a heavy glass-and-metal theatre door and an old glass door. Twelve one-shots are peak-limited and sit under their target (up to 6.7 LU under, the sharpest wet splash), never over.
- **Loops are 75 s or 60 s** (the rain traffic, the day wind and the rain harbour are 60 s). Layers started together repeat in lockstep less often for it, but they still do: start each at a random point in its loop.
- **The wet steps are the weakest set.** The two puddle takes are noisy and hold few single steps: judged against each step's own peak, 1 footfall dies away by 20 dB, 4 by 14 dB and 8 by 12 dB. The 8 wet steps are the 8 that die away by 12 dB (the other surfaces by 20), with a 40 ms fade-out and an 8 ms fade-in; whether they still sound chopped or abrupt is for the ear. The footsteps' second wooden-floor take (fs_575898) has no footfall that dies away by 20 dB and is retired (46 sources, not 47).
- **The footsteps are levelled to one loudness.** Natural differences between steps are gone; the game varies level and pitch. Paving is boots and trekking boots; Tom's trainers (`tools/ue/make_steps.py`, production/assets/steps) are separate and untouched.

## Only an ear can judge (for Jafar, and the fresh listener)

1. Does the day bed read as a small British harbour town, or as somewhere else? (Distance of the traffic; the Hilversum and French sources.)
2. Gulls: right birds, right number, right nearness?
3. Voices and radio: behind glass, no word, no child (the sources are adult speech, but nobody has heard the loops). Every number in the check about them is a measurement of how the sound was built, not of how it sounds. Still only for the ear: (a) whether a single short word survives inside one grain (up to 150 or 160 ms: a one-syllable word could); (b) whether the radio reads as a radio or as more people talking, and whether its distortion, bursts and gaps suit a small speaker; (c) whether a bit of speech keeps coming back (a moment of the 19 s recordings returns after 20 s at the soonest: the night voices and the radio, 12 s the day voices, 7 s the rain voices); (d) whether the night voices, now in phrases with gaps and flat-topped grains that hop 0.85 of their length, still read as two people murmuring, or as a pulse; (e) any child's voice or singing.
4. Harbour: are there voices or people in the water recordings, or in the far boat engine (its page mentions a man and his dog aboard and wading birds; only the engine band below 700 Hz is used)? (The Dorset pages mention none; the Dorset takes open with what a comment on the page calls an "audio intro", so their first 12 s are not used.)
5. Rain mix: loud enough, the three rains in their places, the gutter not nagging.
6. Footsteps: does each surface sound like its name; do eight variants sound like one step repeated? Do the wet steps (cut more loosely, 8 ms fade-in, 40 ms fade-out) still sound chopped at the end or abrupt at the start? Is the setts stand-in good enough or does it want a recording on the PC?
   - Also by ear: the rain harbour (its drone is gone; is anything engine-like left?), the rain traffic's right ear (the whine is gone), the night traffic and the rain awning (steady tones notched out), the night wind (darker and quieter than the day wind: is that what 'lower, darker' should sound like?), and the three loops that are now 60 s (rain traffic, day wind, rain harbour): listen across their ends for a bump.
7. Doors, bell, phone box: which movement is open and which close, which click is the latch; does the bell read as a sprung coil bell above a door; is the ring right for 1990?
8. Anything modern, anything musical, anything alcoholic, anything a child said, anywhere.

## Changed after the review (12 October 2026; REVIEW-v1.md, 0 faults, 12 narrow points)

The fresh reviewer could not hear; neither could the builder. Every fix below is measured, and what only an ear can judge is in the section above. Points as numbered in the review.

1. **Beds that played part of their own recording twice: fixed.** Rain wind is one 79.5 s cut of its 90.75 s recording (75 s loop). Rain traffic and day wind are 60 s loops of one 64 s cut (their recordings are 67 s and 66.5 s). The rain harbour is also a 60 s loop of one 64 s cut of its water and its whistle: its whistle was repeated at a 59 s lag (the review said leave it; the new test measures it at 0.81 against a limit of 0.5, so it could not stay). BRIEF.md case 2 and the check now read "60 to 75 s" (`loop_seconds_max` joined `loop_seconds_min` in the targets block). New case 22 reads every loop (each channel at 8 kHz) in 4 s windows against the whole loop as a circle, at every lag from 1 s to half the loop, and fails above 0.5: the first build's loops measure 1.000 (wind rain, 9 s lag; rain traffic, 19 s; day wind, 36 s) and 0.81 (rain harbour); the rebuilt ones 0.06, 0.08, 0.07 and 0.10, and the highest of all 21 is 0.43 (the radio and the night voices, whose short windows hold one or two grains). A repeat put in on purpose (9 s of the night harbour copied 30 s later) fails by name.
2. **Two stray tones: fixed, and the new test found two more.** The rain harbour's water is high-passed at 200 Hz (order 4) instead of 100 Hz, and its drone's lines (20 in the left channel, 22 in the right, 46 to 320 Hz) are notched out as well, because a high-pass lowers a tone but not how far it stands over its neighbours (the review's own measure). Highest narrow peak left between 60 and 220 Hz: 10.0 dB over its neighbours (175 Hz, right channel, about 34 dB under that channel's power), against 19.8 before. The rain traffic's 1281 Hz whine (right channel) is notched out of the cut before the hiss boost, with the recorder's other lines (398, 6406 and 9000 Hz and the hum at 59.5 and 78 Hz). The method, `detone` in make_sound.py, finds steady narrow lines in every bed's source cuts (8 s frames, the median over time, 11 dB over the floor; 9 dB for the rain harbour) and notches each; what it found is in `manifest.json` (`notched_tones_hz`). New case 23 (1 s frames, 1 Hz bins, the median over time; no peak over 15 dB above its neighbours between 60 Hz and 12 kHz, each channel) fails the first build's rain harbour (+19.8), rain traffic (+17.3), and also its rain awning (a 117 Hz hum, +21) and night traffic (590 Hz, +16, in the quiet floor), which the review had not found; all four are notched now. The highest peak left in any loop is 12.6 dB (3.4 kHz in the gutter loop, a ring of the gutter or a whine: for the ear). A faint 1234.5 Hz tone put into a loop, 36 dB under its channel's power, fails by name.
3. **Wet steps cut off while sounding, steps starting inside a sound: fixed.** `stepcut.cut` now judges "died away" against the step's own peak (the last 20 ms at least 20 dB under it; the first build let a noisy take's floor excuse 7 to 10 dB) and rejects a cut whose 20 ms before the cut lie within 20 dB of its peak or that has no 20 ms before it. All five surfaces were re-cut, 8 variants each, none fewer: paving 4 of 8 steps replaced, setts 3, asphalt 5, wood 6, wet 7 (steps are now picked from the cleaner footfalls; the paving, setts, asphalt and wood sets end at least 21.8 dB under their peak and start from at least 25.0 dB under). The wet set could not meet 20 dB: its two puddle takes give 1 such footfall, 4 at 14 dB, 8 at 12 dB, so the wet steps are the 8 that die away by 12 dB, with a 40 ms fade-out (the review's alternative) and an 8 ms fade-in (the review's alternative for starts); they end at least 22.3 dB under their peak as written and start from at least 13.2 dB under. Retired: the second wooden-floor take (fs_575898) has no footfall that passes, so the sources are 46, not 47 (BRIEF.md, sources.json "rejected", THIRD-PARTY.md). New case 24 measures every step's end on the WAV and its start again on the source (independently of the cutter: it agrees within 0.6 dB). A step cut off while sounding fails by name.
4. **Night wind brighter and louder than the day wind: fixed.** Its low-pass is 1000 Hz (was 1800) and its target -47 LUFS (was -45; day -46). Spectral centroid 450 Hz against the day's 478 Hz, 95% roll-off 908 against 1374 Hz, 1.8% of its power above 1 kHz against 20.6%, 3.8 dB spread of level against 13.6 dB. The night bed mix now measures -40.3 LUFS (target -39.5, within 1.5). New case 11 rows: night wind darker, quieter and steadier than the day wind. The centroid is only a little lower; by ear.
5. **Speech grains running on for about 225 ms: fixed.** `grain_babble` now plans every grain (`grain_plan`): grains starting within 320 ms of each other take places in a recording that are never the same or next to each other (50 ms apart at least), so a run of speech is never longer than one grain (150 ms voices, 160 ms radio). Case 12 reads the build's record of every grain (manifest.json, `plan`) and, written afresh in the check, finds the longest grain and the longest run; a record made up with one grain taking up where another stops (a 230 ms run) fails. It is the build's own record, not a measure of the audio; that the record matches the audio is shown by the byte-exact rebuild and for repeats by case 22.

## For the PC

The cloud has no Unreal, so the import format and the game's hooks are unknown here and nothing was guessed. Plain facts for whoever imports: PCM WAV, 48 kHz, 16-bit; no loop markers (set the loop flag in Unreal); mono for the sounds that should sit in space (gulls, voices, radio), stereo for beds; loop targets are integrated LUFS at nominal fader, one-shot targets are highest 400 ms momentary LUFS. How weather chooses day, night or rain and how the layers fade is the game's code, not decided here.
