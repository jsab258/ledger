# Unit 4.1, fascia signs, try 2: the small things (hanging signs, glass lettering, small panels), fresh review

11 October 2026, cloud week 42. One view: the four hanging signs (the set has no locksmith's: Rita's three balls, the launderette's box, the ironmonger's KEYS CUT board, the chandler's CHANDLERY board), the 20 glass lettering rows plus the empty unit's whitewashed window and the grocer's green glass, and the small panels (the letting board, the five hours signs). Judged close, at 1 to 2 m, and at street distance for the hanging signs. The ten fascia boards are not in this view.

How I looked: every PNG of the set opened 1:1 and enlarged 2 to 4 times; the glass tiles laid over a dark shop interior I made (a dim gradient with blurred shapes and a faint sky sheen), at 1:1 and 2x; the hanging faces and the whitewashed window brought down to 4.4 mm and 8.2 mm a pixel (8 m and 15 m away on a 1920-pixel, 60-degree screen); counters counted on the pixels; the font measured from its file; the maker's own `ink_width` run for the fix below. Judged against the target (TARGET.md 6.1 to 6.3, target.json), the maker's NOTES.md and amendments A8 to A12, the Hook sheet, the street as it stands (shop-fronts-whole, 8 Oct), and six dated photographs (last section).

Two faults, each on one item, each with an exact fix. Everything else passes, with the narrow points listed.

## Faults, worst first

### 1. The empty unit's whitewashed window: a torn-paper outline and a comb of drips (the build's)

- **What and where.** `glass/empty_unit/window_whitewash` (3250 x 1800 mm). The fill is right. The OUTLINE is wrong all the way round: a saw-tooth margin of spikes every 4 to 14 mm, 10 to 80 mm deep, along the top, both sides and the foot. At the foot, 22 to 32 dribbles hang in a row. All are vertical, 2 to 6.5 mm wide and 35 to 190 mm long. Every one starts on the coat's foot line and ends in a round bead. Close up, the window reads as a sheet of parchment torn round its edge. At 8 m it is still a torn sheet with a comb hanging under it, and you cannot miss it: it is the biggest piece of glass on the parade, at eye height.
- **What the photograph shows instead.** Sidmouth, "Closed down" (geograph 1499069, 2009): the coat on a shop window ends where the cloth or brush strokes end. The edge is soft, swept and curved, thinner toward the glazing, with no spikes. There are no drips: whiting laid on with a cloth does not run. Garston 1986 (geograph 4595946) shows the dead shopfront of the period: boarded and fly-posted, never edged with a jagged frame.
- **Whose.** The build's. The target asks only for "whitewash wash, brush arcs, nothing legible". The cause is in `fascia_small.render_window_wash`: the margin is `abs()` of noise at 70, 14 and 4 mm feature sizes. The abs folds the noise into cusps, and the 14 and 4 mm octaves make the spikes. The dribble loop draws `r.integers(22, 32)` identical runs from the foot line.
- **Exact fix.** (a) Margin: use signed smooth noise with no `abs()`, at feature sizes of 150 mm and up only (drop the 14 and 4 mm octaves). Margins: top 10 to 50 mm, foot 15 to 60 mm, sides 10 to 45 mm. Widen the feather from 12 mm to 30 mm, and multiply the feather band by the stroke-ring texture the function already builds, so the edge breaks up along the cloth's arcs. Keep the missed corners as they are. (b) Dribbles: 0 to 3 a window (`r.integers(0, 4)`), at least 400 mm apart. Each starts 40 to 200 mm ABOVE the coat's foot, where the coat is thickest (alpha above 0.9). Each is 20 to 120 mm long, tapers from 4 mm to 1 mm wide, and fades in alpha from 0.7 to 0.2, with no bead at the end. Acceptance: no stretch of the outline moves more than 10 mm in 30 mm of edge; at 4.4 mm a pixel no comb shows; nothing legible.

### 2. CHANDLERY: the counters are filled in (the build's)

- **What and where.** `signs/chandler_hanging_board`, faces a and b. The D and the R are solid, with no bowl. The A has a pinhole. The H and the N are solid blocks with a hairline slit, and the C's counter is closed to a line. On face a the pixels hold NO enclosed counter of 40 mm2 or more. The word should give three: A, D and R. People pass right under this board (its foot is at 2.54 m), and from there it looks misdrawn. At 8 and 15 m it reads as a block stencil, "C?ANDLE?Y" made out by the word's shape.
- **Cause.** A8's condensed face. Alfa Slab One is squeezed to 0.534 of its width, and `fascia_paint.squeeze_delta` (restore 0.55) then dilates every glyph sideways by about 4.5 mm each side at this weight. Measured on the font file, the squeezed internal gaps are only 9 to 13 mm (D 9.9, R 9.3, E 9.5, C 10.3, H 11.1, A 12.3, N 13.3 mm), so the dilation shuts them all. An ultra-heavy face squeezed to half its width cannot keep its counters.
- **What the photographs show instead.** The signwritten heavy capitals on the 1983 ship chandler's fascia in Hull (geograph 3378991), on the 1987 Whitby board (geograph 351747) and on the 1979 Grasmere board (geograph 5101534) all keep open counters. A long word on a short board is lettered in a narrower or smaller letter, never in a filled one.
- **Whose.** The build's. The target's cap of 105 mm did not fit the 800 mm board, which the maker rightly reported. The filled counters come from the squeeze and the stem growth.
- **Exact fix.** In `fascia_small.render_sign_face`, for the chandler's board: set the least squeeze `sq_lo = 0.70` and turn off the stem growth for this face (`squeeze_delta` returns 0). The fit loop then stops at cap 95 mm, squeeze 0.70, ink 682 mm in the 684 mm free width (run with the maker's own `ink_width`: 95 mm at 0.70 gives 682.0). Gaps at that size, from the font: D 10.7, R 10.1, E 10.3, C 11.2, H 12.0, A 13.3, N 14.4 mm. Scale the shade to 10 x 95/105 = 9 mm. Acceptance on the pixels of both faces: three enclosed counters (A, D, R), each at least 75 per cent of the same counter in a plain squeezed re-render with no growth; internal gaps in H, N, C and E of at least 9 mm; G10 mask at least 0.90 against that re-render.

## Narrow points (each a PASS on its own; exact fixes)

1. **LAUNDERETTE's A** (`signs/steam_laundry_box`, both faces). The same stem growth leaves its counter at 67 mm2, a pinhole. Fix: `restore = 0.30` for this face. Acceptance: the A's counter is at least 75 per cent of a no-growth render. Everything else on the box is right.
2. **The fishmonger's brushed hours, "7.30"** (`glass/fish_market/23_hours-fish-market`). Patrick Hand's 7 is crossed by design (checked on the font), and a crossed 7 looks continental on a British shop window. Fix: in `fascia_brush.brush_text`, drop the 7's short horizontal centre-line stroke at mid-height.
3. **The brushed whitewash rows** (FRESH DAILY, SHELLFISH, SMOKED FISH, the hours). Nearly every downstroke ends in the same two-pronged dry tail, and at 1 to 2 m the repeat shows. Fix: draw each stroke's end from {forked 30, blunt 40, tapered 30 per cent} by the row's seed. The tails are rightly made of transparency, not grey paint (the colour stays at 216 while the alpha falls).
4. **The letting board** (`panels/letting_board`). The red vinyl TO LET is pristine while the paint peels all round it. Fix: let about a fifth of the loss patches that touch a letter run under it (vinyl lifts with the paint), and fade the red about 10 per cent toward orange. The target's own point: a 1990 board names its agent and number. The 1983 Hull photograph shows an agent's board with name, address and telephone. When the town mints an agent, add one line under TO LET (variant V5); until then it stands.
5. **The caff's card** (`panels/hours_signs/hours_tea_rooms`). The two tape strips are flat tan rectangles square to the corners. Fix: one 25 mm strip of yellowed clear tape across each top corner at 30 to 45 degrees, alpha 0.35 to 0.45, running over the card's edge onto the glass.
6. **The launderette's engraved plate** (`panels/hours_signs/hours_steam_laundry`). An engraving cutter cuts one width with round ends, but Libre Franklin's thick and thin strokes show. Fix: stroke the letters' centre lines at a constant 2 mm with round ends (the brush module already strokes centre lines).
7. **The grocer's green glass** (this view's glass reading of a board in the other review). Horizontal tone bands of about 10 grey levels (4 to 5 L*) run across the slabs. That is the timber boards' damp-band ageing, and back-painted glass does not take damp. Fix: bring the bands under 1.5 L*, or turn them into the faint brush marks of the back paint. Slabs, joints, chrome strips and the cream letters are right.
8. **Hanging boards on long chains** (the target's, geometry, not these pictures; Judgement, no photograph reached for it). The ironmonger's and chandler's boards hang on chains 0.70 and 0.65 m longer than first designed, set by the target review's mount fix. A signwriter's board hangs on short eyes or S-hooks under the arm. On a long chain it swings and reads as a stage prop. Fix: 0.10 to 0.15 m hangers. The lowest points rise to about 3.15 m, which still clears the 2.5 m minimum.

## What is right

- **KEYS CUT** (ironmonger's board, both faces). A real signwriter's board: Old Standard Bold capitals in two lines filling the board, a solid vermilion block shade extruded down and to the right (it shows through the counters as a true shade does), a black rule border, and paint lost along the grain and at the edges. Face b has its own wear, and each face reads left to right from its own side. Its lettering matches the period boards (Grasmere 1979, Whitby 1987).
- **The launderette's box sign.** White acrylic, blue cut-vinyl condensed geometric capitals between two rules, the bronze extrusion ring (metal 1, roughness 0.33), dead flies lying at the foot inside, and an even lit face. Its kind and size match the small projecting lit box on the 1986 Garston photograph.
- **Rita's three balls.** Two over one, 0.26 m each. Gilt flakes along the pressed halves' seam, there is rust at the crown, and the rain runs follow the meridians.
- **The gilt glass rows** (WATCHES, JEWELLERY, LOANS, EST. 1884, EST. 1879, Rita's hours, and the figures 1, 5, 11, 13 and 16). Gold leaf with a black shade down and to the right, with metal 1 and roughness 0.15 on the leaf. They fit their panes, and the EST. lines are the period's habit (Whitby 1987: "ESTABLISHED 1872").
- **The fishmonger's whitewash rows.** Brushed, uneven, every letter its own; a few runs, and dry ends that are transparency. A shop's own window hand.
- **The vinyl rows** (SERVICE WASHES, the newsagent's two lines on the door, now a 545 mm tile that fits the 0.68 m door light, his hours, and the 9, 14 and 18). Clean 1980s cut vinyl at the right sizes.
- **The fanlight numbers.** Every shop has its own face and technique. The empty unit's 7 flakes to clear glass: the holes are alpha 0 (checked), not dark paint.
- **The whitewash fill itself.** Overlapping cloth swirls with tide marks, thin wiped spots and missed corners: right. Only its outline and the drips fail.
- **The hours signs.** Each trade has its own kind: brushed whitewash, gold leaf and white vinyl on the glass, an engraved laminate plate with four screws, a marker-pen card. Every line matches the cast's hours (checked against hook-cast.json).
- **Content.** No drink, gambling or children; no real name or brand; every word is a trade word. Each face reads correctly from its own side.
