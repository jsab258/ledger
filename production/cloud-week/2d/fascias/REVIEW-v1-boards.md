# Quay Street fascias, unit 4.1, v1: fresh review of the ten boards

Cloud week 42, 9 October 2026. A fresh reviewer who did not write the target and did not make the boards, and has not seen any other verdict. Nothing but this file was written. Nothing was committed.

**The view judged.**
- The sheet of all ten boards: fascias-all-ten-v1-2026-10-09.jpg.
- Each board alone, and Mickey's with its gilt letters laid over.
- The full-size PNGs in the scratchpad (base colour, height, ORM, wear).
- I reduced the boards to about 7 mm and 10 mm a pixel. That is what the board is across the 6 m road, from the far pavement at 1.6 m eye height, at 2560 x 1440 and at the game's lower internal resolution.
- I also squeezed them about 28 to 1 along their length, as the hook camera sees them down the street.

**Judged against.**
- canon.md and RULINGS.md.
- TARGET.md, target.json and TARGET-REVIEW.md, including the maker's six amendments in the manifest.
- The Hook sheet and its measured board (H1).
- shop-fronts-whole-2026-10-08.jpg.
- P1 (gilt craft), P2 and P3 (paint loss and scuffing on real painted timber).
- The period notes the target cites: 4-SIGNAGE-AND-WEAR.md, PERIOD-PRINT-AND-FONTS.md and PAINTED-FRONTS-2026-10-07.md.

**Where this is limited.** No photograph of a 1990 British fascia could be opened from here: Wikimedia, Geograph and Flickr are refused. So the period judgement rests on four things:
- the project's own notes;
- P1, which is a 2019 restoration and so useful for craft only;
- P2 and P3, which are real weathered painted timber;
- my own knowledge of provincial parades of the late 1980s.

The letters, words and layouts I can judge firmly. How a 1990 board aged I can judge only against P2 and P3 and the notes, and against what real paint does.

## Faults, worst first

### 1. The wear is one confetti filter laid on every painted board, not paint failing. (The maker's.)

**Where.** Mickey's, the fish market, Rita's, the chandler and the ironmonger. It shows most on the chandler (pale flecks on navy, a "starry" look), on the left third of Mickey's, and on the fish board (dark specks on pale grey).

**What the boards show.**
- Each board carries thousands of separate small flakes, measured on the height maps:

| Board | Flakes |
|---|---|
| Rita's | 1,366 |
| Mickey's | 2,099 |
| Fish market | 3,189 |
| Ironmonger | 3,865 |
| Chandler | 4,723 |

- The flakes are a median 5 to 6 mm across, and none is larger than about 50 mm.
- Their shape statistics are the same on every board: bounding-box aspect median 3.0, 90th percentile 5.0.
- They are scattered in even horizontal bands along the whole board. They never merge.
- They are kept off every letter and rule, which the wear module's header states ("nothing touches a letter"). Rita's name band has 0.0 per cent loss against 2 to 7 per cent either side. Mickey's letter zone is 1 per cent against 9 per cent at its left end. So every name sits in a clean halo inside a speckled field.
- Every board's flakes show the same two substrates: one tan wood (116,102,86) and one grey primer. That holds on slate, pale grey, oxblood, navy and buff alike.
- Apart from the flakes, the boards are evenly toned. There is no dirt gathered at the bottom edge, at the ends or under the cornice.

At the road's distance the flakes are 1 to 5 pixels: a fine sand-like speckle across the board. Put five boards side by side and the same speckle reads as one filter over ten owners.

**What a real one shows instead.**
- **P2, the target's own photograph of real flaking paint.** Loss runs in long strips along the grain. It joins up into bare runs hundreds of millimetres long, and it is heavy on some planks and nearly absent on others.
- **P3.** Scuffs and abrasion run along the grain.
- **The project's note** (4-SIGNAGE-AND-WEAR.md, Ageing by material) gives paint on timber as: "chalking, cracks along the grain, chips at edges"; "top-down grime, dirt under ledges".

On a real 1990 board, paint fails where water sits and runs:
- the bottom edge and its drip;
- the board's ends and joints;
- round nail heads and fixings;
- along checks in the grain.

The lettering fails with the ground, because it is paint on the same board. Chips show whatever colour lay underneath, which differs from shop to shop. Dirt gathers in a band at the bottom and in streaks down from the cornice.

Matching P2's median flake size and aspect, one ellipse at a time, does not give P2's pattern. The pattern is in how the loss joins up and where it sits.

**Amendment (the maker's).**
- Draw loss as grain-following strips that are allowed to join up.
- Weight it towards the bottom edge, the ends, any board joint and the fixings, and let it cross letters.
- Give each board its own failure mode: Mickey's sound and chalky; the fish board crazed and chipped at the bottom edge; the chandler flaking at its ends in larger sheets.
- Draw the substrate per board from an older colour plus primer and wood.
- Add a grime gradient: darker at the bottom edge, and streaks down from the cornice.
- Judge it at 7 mm a pixel beside P2, not at 1:1.

### 2. The empty unit's "bare timber" reads as fish-scale or crazy paving, not wood. (The maker's.)

**Where.** The whole empty unit board outside the buff painted-out patch: both ends, and the bands above and below it.

**What it shows.**
- About 18,000 separate scale-shaped flecks (15.7 per cent of the board, median 4 mm), packed edge to edge in even rows.
- No grain line, check or split can be seen at 1:1 or at distance, although the target asks for "grain strong (amplitude 3 L*)".
- At the road's distance it is a dark pebbled texture, like bark or rough asphalt.

**What a real one shows instead.** Timber whose paint has long gone weathers in two ways:
- **In the open:** silver-grey, with open grain lines and splits along the length.
- **Under shelter:** darker brown, with the grain still reading.

Any old paint that survives lies in a few larger islands, mostly in the sheltered upper part and around nail heads. P2's darker planks show this. The target says "a few patches of old paint", not 18,000.

**Amendment (the maker's).**
- Make the grain the main feature: long dark lines and checks at the target's amplitude, with a grey weathered tone in the lower half.
- Replace the scale field with a handful of large paint islands, 10 to 30 of them, each 50 to 400 mm long along the grain.

### 3. The gull marks are one cream "puffball on a stick" stamp, placed in the middle of the boards. (The maker's.)

**Where.** All 22 marks: six on the empty unit (up to about 70 mm), four on the chandler (two stacked one over the other at board x 4820 to 4839), and one or two on Mickey's, the fish market, Rita's, the grocer, the newsagent, the ironmonger and the tea rooms. Most sit mid-height on the face (y 120 to 390 mm).

**What they show.**
- Every one is the same shape: a clean, flat, off-white rounded silhouette with one thin straight drip under it.
- They read as little cotton-wool trees or stickers. The maker's own docstring promises a grey-brown ring and spatter, but no ring or spatter can be seen.
- They are among the brightest marks on any board, so they are the first wear a player sees, and they repeat.

**What a real one shows instead.**
- **The project's notes.** Droppings land "on top edges", on "sills, fascia tops and ledges" and "under the ledges gulls use" (4-SIGNAGE-AND-WEAR.md). Rule-placed wear follows physics: streaks fall downward from where the bird sat.
- **Why mid-face marks are wrong.** A fascia sits under a projecting cornice, so a bird on the cornice drops past the board. A splat in the middle of a vertical, sheltered face is the unlikely case.
- **What a real mark looks like.** It is chalky white with a darker grey-green centre. It has a ragged splash edge with scattered satellite drops, and it runs downward as a long streak, with thin, partly see-through edges.

**Amendment (the maker's, within the target's counts).**
- Put the marks on the top 60 mm of the board, as runs coming down from the cornice nose, and at the board's ends.
- Give each its own outline, a darker centre and spatter.
- Keep any mark more than about 40 mm wide to the empty unit, where neglect explains it.

### 4. Mickey's shows its name twice: the old ghost "MICKE…" beside the new gilt letters, with the ghost's 'S showing between the new M and I. (The target's spec; the maker built it exactly.)

**Where.** Mickey's board.
- The ghost is a whole MICKEY'S in Marcellus SC at cap 245, centred at board x 2705, ink 1921 to 3489.
- It is drawn unbroken (broken fraction 0.0), at +3.5 dE towards blue.
- The new raised letters stand at 2996 to 5114.

**What it shows.** With the letters laid over:
- "MICKE" stands clear to the left, in the same face and the same word.
- The ghost's apostrophe and S show in the gap between the new M and I, at about board x 3285 to 3490.
- A row of ten black pin holes runs across the ghost's cap line.

At 7 mm a pixel the ghost reads plainly. The player's own office, the nearest board to the hook camera, then reads "MICKE MICKEY'S", like a doubled texture or an echo.

**What a real one, and the sheet, show instead.**
- The Hook sheet (H1) shows gilt capitals standing out from a clean slate-blue board, with nothing else on it.
- When a 1990 signwriter moved a name, the old letters were painted out in the ground colour before the new ones were fixed. What can survive is a faint relief of the old brush edges, seen in raking light, or the screw holes of old applied letters. A colour-shifted, unbroken copy of the same word, running under the new letters, does not survive.
- There is a second problem. The ghost is described as brush-painted, but the pin holes belong to applied letters. One story or the other, not both.
- The project's note makes the ghost "of the last trade's letters". The brand bible's "repainted a shade off each time" points to a tonal difference across the whole board, not a legible second name.

**Amendment (the target's, then the maker's).** Choose one of these:
- (a) No ghost on Mickey's. Keep the pin holes or screw holes of old centred applied letters, about 3 mm each, with small rust halos.
- (b) Keep the ghost as relief only, in the height map at +0.1 to +0.2 mm, with the base-colour difference 1 dE or less, and broken at least 60 per cent.

Either way, nothing of the ghost may show inside the new letters' bounding box.

## Notes (narrow points; on their own they would not fail a board)

1. **Rita's gold leaf is too dark and brown for metal.** The target's gold_leaf 1990 colour (195,161,77) is used as metallic 1, roughness about 0.30. That is a reflectance of about (0.55, 0.36, 0.07) linear, half that of real gold (about 1.0, 0.77, 0.34; sRGB about 255,227,157), and more orange. In the engine it will read as dull bronze or brass rather than gilding. Gold does not tarnish, so 1990 leaf on a dirty board still reads bright yellow-gold under grime. P1's leaf is clearly paler and brighter than this. The target's choice (Judgement). Either:
   - keep the metal near gold's own values and put the dirt in roughness and a thin grime layer; or
   - make the letters yellow-ochre signwriter's paint (non-metal), which many 1990 boards actually were.

   The glass gold-leaf rows have the same colour. Mickey's brass_gilt (167,149,109) comes from the sheet's lit picture, not from a reflectance, so it has the same caution in the engine.
2. **Rita's repaint patch** ([2300, 140, 3300, 340], 3 dE) is a hard, square-cornered rectangle behind PAWNBROKER, faint at 1:1. A repaint is cut in by brush with an uneven, slightly feathered outline. The maker's.
3. **The ironmonger's ghost** is the same word, about 15 mm lower and 5 per cent larger, at 4 dE darker. Round the new letters it shows as grey fragments, like misregistered print. It is not visible at the road's distance. A different, older trade word, or relief only, would read as a real repaint. The target's.
4. **The fish board's ground** (196,202,206) is a cool blue-grey with horizontal streaks. With the dark specks it can read as brushed or galvanised metal. Aged 1990 white alkyd gloss goes warm and yellowish, with grey-brown dirt. The ground colour is the target's; the streaks are the maker's.
5. **Rain runs** are thin pale lines about 3 mm wide, like scratches. Real runs under a cornice are softer and wider, and mostly darker: dirt carried down, with paler washed tracks beside it.
6. **Mickey's contact shadow** in the texture alone shows a full dark MICKEY'S. It is right under the raised letters. If the letter mesh is ever culled at a distance level of detail, the board will show a dark name. Keep the letters at every level of detail.
7. **The words are trade nouns only.** Six of ten boards carry a trade and no name: grocer, newsagent, ironmonger, tea rooms, chandler and the empty unit. Each could be real. Together they are the familiar "SHOP" signal of a game street. Most 1990 parade fascias carried a proprietor's or business name, often with a telephone number. This is canon's limit and the town's to mint (TARGET.md section 11 item 5; variant V5), not the maker's. I would mint names before any of these boards reach his page.
8. **Placement left to geometry.** The two box signs and the tea room panel show their frames as drawn rectangles. Their depth and returns are geometry, so judge them again once in the engine.
9. **Outside this view (not judged).** In the signs-and-glass sheet, Rita's three balls show grey horizontal bands across the gilt, and the empty unit's "7" looks notched. A reviewer of that view should look at both.

## What is right

- **Words, canon and content.** Every word is spelled right and matches the target's approved list:
  - Mickey's: MICKEY'S.
  - Fish market: FISH MARKET, WET FISH, SHELLFISH, SMOKED.
  - Rita's: RITA'S, PAWNBROKER, 5.
  - Steam laundry: LAUNDERETTE, SERVICE WASHES, DRY CLEANING, STEAM LAUNDRY.
  - Grocer: FAMILY GROCER, HIGH CLASS PROVISIONS.
  - Chandler: SHIP CHANDLER, ROPE · PAINT · CHARTS · TWINE, 13.
  - Tea rooms: BREAKFASTS, LUNCHES & TEAS, Tea Rooms.
  - Ironmonger: TOOLS & HARDWARE, PAINTS & PARAFFIN, IRONMONGER.
  - Newsagent: NEWSAGENT.

  The minted names are canon's and the cast's. No board mentions alcohol, gambling or children. There are no real brands and nothing after 1992. No proprietor name is invented.
- **No visible build fault in the letters.** Nothing is clipped, mirrored, misplaced or seamed. Every board reads left to right as the game shows it. Mickey's name stands over its door at the right of the board, as on the sheet and in the street today.
- **Variety: ten owners, as far as layout, colour and letter go.** Ten grounds, ten letter families and nine layouts:
  - painted timber in four generations;
  - two lit 1980s box signs;
  - a flat brown caff panel;
  - a 1930s back-painted glass fascia;
  - a bare, painted-out empty unit.

  Seen down the street from the hook camera, each board is distinct by colour and mass.
- **Sign-writing craft.**
  - **Block shades.** They are solid 45-degree extrusions, as P1 shows: black on the fish board's vermilion, black under Rita's gilt and the chandler's white Egyptian, and vermilion under the ironmonger's black roman.
  - **Rita's keyline.** The concave cut corners match P1.
  - **Fonts.** Each is a fair stand-in for its period. Condensed sans for the fishmonger; Futura-like for the laundry box; Franklin for the newsagent box; Cooper-like Fraunces for the caff; Deco geometric capitals for the 1930s grocer; Egyptian slab for the chandler.
- **Legibility.** Every name reads plainly at 7 and 10 mm a pixel. So do the 70 mm trade lines, including the vermilion on pale grey thanks to its black shade.
- **Other details.**
  - FISHMONGER under FISH MARKET is broken and faint, and is the right kind of ghost.
  - The grocer's three slabs, mastic joints, chrome edge and speed lines read as a period glass front.
