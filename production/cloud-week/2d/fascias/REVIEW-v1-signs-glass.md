# Unit 4.1, try 1: hanging signs, glass lettering and small panels. Fresh review.

9 October 2026, cloud week 42. View: `production/previews/cloud-week/fascias/fascias-signs-glass-v1-2026-10-09.jpg`, with the full-size PNGs and `manifest.json` in the scratch folder (each item looked at 1:1 and enlarged, glass tiles laid on a dark interior). Judged against TARGET.md, target.json, TARGET-REVIEW.md, the Hook sheet, the street as it stands (shop-fronts-whole, 8 Oct), the project's period notes, the maker's NOTES.md, canon.md and RULINGS.md. I did not write the target or make the signs, and I have seen no other verdict.

**Limits.** No photographs of 1990 shop glass or hanging signs were reachable here, and the target reached none either. Where I say "a real one shows", that comes from my own knowledge of late-1980s British shop fronts and the project's cited notes (Picture Sheffield t13138, Marshall's Hull 1989, the KCD2 hanging-board frame). It is not a measurement. Nobody has seen these in the game's camera and exposure.

The bar: would each item pass as real 1990 provincial British signage in a photoreal 2026 game, seen across a 6 m road from 1.6 m eye height, and for glass also from 1 to 2 m?

## Faults, worst first

### 1. The empty unit's whitewashed window does not look like whitewash. (The maker's.)
- **What.** The whole 3.25 x 1.8 m pane (`glass/empty_unit/window_whitewash_rgba.png`) is a mid-grey haze, mean alpha 0.53. Uniform, perfectly horizontal streaks are combed across all of it, like a motion-blur or brushed-metal filter. Over the haze run thick beaded loops, about 150 mm wide, with blobs strung along them like beads. They make metre-sized curly shapes: a "(" at the left, an "X" and a "K" in the middle and right. On a dark interior the window reads as grey lace or a giant scribble, and parts of it nearly read as letters. At 1:1 it is all soft blur with no edge anywhere.
- **Where.** The tile itself. It is the largest single picture on the sheet and the one seen from everywhere on the street.
- **What a real one shows.** Whiting (or Windolene) put on with a wet cloth or brush. It reads white to cream from across the road, mostly opaque. The hand leaves overlapping circular or fan-shaped swirls, 0.3 to 0.6 m across, whose strokes follow the hand rather than one horizontal direction. There are thin and clear spots, crisper edges where a stroke stops, a clear margin at the glazing bars and corners the cloth missed, and dribbles at the foot. A finger-wiped peephole or a scrawl is optional.
- **Whose.** The target asks for "brush arcs, nothing legible". The arcs are there, but this is not what a brush or cloth leaves.

### 2. The three hanging faces are under-filled: one thin line of small letters across an empty board. (The target's contradiction, and the maker's choice of fix.)
- **What.**
  - LAUNDERETTE: 51 mm of ink on a 450 mm face, which is 11 per cent of the height.
  - KEYS CUT: 65 mm on 380 mm, 17 per cent, shade included.
  - CHANDLERY: 99 mm on 500 mm, 20 per cent, shade included.
  - The lettering covers only 5 to 8 per cent of each face. The rest is plain ground.
  - The maker asked whether this reads. The words are legible near to. At 10 m along the pavement, KEYS CUT's 55 mm cap is about 19 arcminutes, roughly 9 pixels tall at 2560 wide with a 90 degree view. It is gone well before 20 m, and down-street reading is a projecting sign's whole job. From across the 6 m road the faces are seen obliquely, which makes this worse.
  - The bigger fault is that the boards do not look laid out by a signwriter. They look like a word fitted to a box by software.
- **Where.** All six faces: the laundry box a and b, the ironmonger's a and b, the chandler's a and b.
- **What a real one shows.** A signwriter fills a hanging board.
  - Two-word legends go on two lines. KEYS over CUT reaches about 110 mm cap inside the 550 x 380 board's rule.
  - Egyptians and romans are drawn condensed to fill the width. CHANDLERY condensed reaches about 110 to 120 mm on the 800 x 500 board.
  - A lit projecting box is lettered to its edges. LAUNDERETTE in a condensed sans, or on a wider box, gets 80 to 100 mm.
  - If any field is left over, it carries a device such as a key outline or an anchor, never blank ground.
- **Whose.** The target set one line at a cap the face cannot hold (it says so in the maker's report). The maker kept the boards and shrank the letters, which keeps every number but loses the sign. The target needs to allow two lines and condensed drawing, or bigger boards (lowest points re-run).

### 3. The fishmonger's whitewash lettering reads as a font with a stone texture, not brushwork. (The target's font; the maker's fill.)
- **What.**
  - FRESH DAILY, SHELLFISH and SMOKED FISH are Patrick Hand: a monoline, round-ended felt-tip hand.
  - Each glyph is filled with isotropic grey blotches, like terrazzo, inside clean font outlines.
  - Repeated letters are the same shape apart from a little jitter: the S, H, E, L and F of SHELLFISH and FISH.
  - FRESH DAILY's baseline is at 0.95 m, waist height beside the pavement, exactly where a player stands 1 to 2 m away.
- **What a real one shows.** Whiting or white poster paint put on with a flat brush or a finger.
  - Strokes vary thick to thin with the brush's angle.
  - There are hair streaks along each stroke, a loaded blob where the stroke starts and a dry-brush tail where it ends.
  - Edges are uneven, with an occasional run below a heavy stroke.
  - The capitals are often slanted, with an underline or "!" flourish, and every letter is different.
  - From 6 m these rows pass as white hand lettering. From 1 to 2 m they do not.

### 4. TOBACCONIST & CONFECTIONER cannot sit where it is specified. (The target's; the maker reported it but did not settle it.)
- **What.** The vinyl line is 1.69 m wide (cap 70 mm), for the newsagent's shop-door glass, which is 0.68 to 0.9 m wide. Placed as given, it runs across the door stiles, frame and pilaster, which is a fault any player sees. Placed anywhere else, it is not the target's item.
- **What a real one shows.** On a door: the trade in small vinyl, about 25 to 40 mm cap, in two lines (TOBACCONIST / & CONFECTIONER), or no trade line at all. On the window: a single band across the head of the glass. The letter craft itself is right.

### 5. Five identical enamel hours plates on five different trades' doors. (The target's choice; the maker's 12-hour clock.)
- **What.** The pawnbroker, fishmonger, launderette, newsagent and caff all carry the same 300 x 190 mm white enamel plate: same blue border, same bold sans, same corner dots, same layout. A player walking the doors meets one sign five times.
- **What a real one shows.** In 1990 each shop's hours were its own: gold leaf or vinyl on the door glass, a stationer's printed "OPENING HOURS" card with the times written or stuck in, a handwritten card, or a plastic sign. Custom enamel for shop hours is improbable, and five matching ones are not credible.
- **The tea rooms' plate.** It reads "MON-SAT 6.30-10", which on a plate reads as 6.30 to 10 in the morning. The cast has 06.30 to 22.00. A real caff would write "6.30am - 10pm" or "6.30 - 10.00pm". The plates' rule forbids am and pm, so the rule needs a way to say it.
- **Right as it is.** The hours match `hook-cast.json`, and every plate's words fit its rule.

### 6. The ageing on the signs and the letting board is a filter, and the edges are untouched. (The maker's; the target gives the hanging signs no wear numbers.)
- **What.**
  - The grounds of the hanging boards, the box faces and the TO LET board carry a low-frequency cloud mottle. On the chandler's navy it reads as suede or denim rather than painted timber.
  - Short grey dashes about 3 mm wide and 40 to 60 mm long float in mid-field: under KEYS CUT, under TO LET's "T", and near the top corner of the LAUNDERETTE face. They start and stop in the open, unattached to any edge or fixing.
  - Edges, corners, rules, the rope and every letter are pristine.
- **What a real one shows.** A hanging board fails first at its bottom rail and corners: chipped, bare and grey. Its paint chalks evenly, letters and shade included. The red shade fades first. Dirt runs come from the top edge, the chain eyes or the screws, and cross the letters. The KCD2 frame the asset plan cites shows a board "as weathered as the plaster round it". The dashes are sub-pixel at 6 m. The clean edges and letters are visible from under the sign.

### 7. The ironmonger's fanlight reads "1 6". (The maker's.)
- **What.** Old Standard TT's tabular "1" leaves a gap of about 37 mm before the "6" at 110 mm cap. At the fanlight's 1 to 2 m distance this reads as two figures.
- **What a real one shows.** A gilder spaces figures by eye and closes the 1 up to its partner. The other numerals (13, 14, 18, 5, 9) are spaced well.

## What is right

- **Words.** Every word is spelled right and is a trade word, a figure or a minted name.
  - There is nothing of drink or gambling and no children. LOANS is pawnbroking, not gambling. TOBACCONIST is allowed by the content rule.
  - No proprietor, brand or agent is invented. Every brand is fictional.
  - The street numbers are odd on the east parade (1 to 13) and even on the west block (14 to 18), rising with street x on both sides.
  - The hours match the cast. Only the newsagent and the caff open on Sunday, which is consistent with Sunday trading law before 1994. The fishmonger's and pawnbroker's Wednesday half-day is period-right.
- **No mirrored or clipped text.** Every hanging face reads left to right from its own side; the two faces of each sign are drawn separately (34 to 55 per cent of pixels differ). Every glass tile reads correctly from the street. Every tile has 15 mm or more of clear margin, with zero alpha at its edges.
- **The gilt glass is the best work on the sheet.** WATCHES, JEWELLERY, LOANS, EST. 1884, EST. 1879, 5 and 13 are the right craft: gold in a period roman or Egyptian with a solid black block shade, spaced as a gilder would. With the ORM's metal 1 and roughness 0.15 behind glass, this is what a 1990 pawnbroker's or old ironmonger's toplight looks like.
- **The colour ways and letter kinds are right for each trade.**
  - KEYS CUT: black roman with a vermilion block shade on buff, inside a black rule.
  - CHANDLERY: a white Egyptian with a black shade on navy, inside a painted rope.
  - LAUNDERETTE: a Futura-like sans in blue cut vinyl on white acrylic, in a bronze frame.
  - SERVICE WASHES and the 9, 14 and 18: plain cut vinyl, a sound late-1980s touch.
  - The fishmonger's 3: white with a red shade, matching its board.
  - TO LET: red on white, with four screws and rust runs under the lower two.
  - The flaking 7 on the empty unit.
- **The checks.** 433 of 433 pass, with 47 negative controls. The maker reported the target's contradictions instead of hiding them (hanging caps, the newsagent's door, G3, G4 and the mirror margin).

## Notes (narrow, not seen at the street's distances)

1. The laundry box's emissive is flat: every row reads 183 outside the letters, with no tube banding. A 140 mm deep box with two tubes shows soft bright bands at night, and usually a shadow of dead flies along the bottom inside. The face's cream mottle reads as parchment rather than yellowed opal acrylic.
2. Mickey's "1" in Marcellus SC is a hairline figure at 110 mm and reads as "I" or "|" from a few metres. The grocer's "11" in Josefin Sans reads close to "II". A heavier or flagged figure would read as a number.
3. FRESH DAILY at 0.95 m sits at the height of the fish slab behind the glass. Whitewash is usually set high, above the display, so the fish can be seen. That is the target's.
4. The TO LET board carries no agent and no number, because none is minted. A commercial agent's board would carry both. A bare red-on-white "TO LET" bought from an ironmonger also existed, so it passes. Its ground is cream where the target says white.
5. The 7's flaking is three clean horizontal wedge bites. Paint on glass lifts in irregular, sharp-edged flakes with some loss at the edges.
6. Outside this brief, but on the sheet: Rita's ball textures carry blue-grey streaks running round the lower half of each ball, as if scuffed "where hands reach". Hands do not reach 2.65 m. Gilt balls dull, darken and rust at the hanger instead. This is for whoever judges the balls.
7. The hours plates' corner "chips" are identical round dots at the exact corners of every plate, so they read as screw holes. That is acceptable as screw holes.
