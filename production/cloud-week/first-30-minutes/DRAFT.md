# LEDGER: the first thirty minutes, a first draft

## 1. In plain words (for Jafar)

- The draft is a first guess at the first thirty minutes: two different newcomers, minute by minute, built only from canon, PLAN.md, the town content made this week and public research.
- Route A does what Mickey's book asks: a window at Rita's, the envelope at the landing, then the constable and DS Ellis. Route B stays on the street, listens, tells Ron no, and ends with Ada's tea.
- Today the game can play 3 of its 50 beats; the rest wait on bodies and cards for the street's people, the quay, the landing, the Ledger and the coat. Each beat says which.
- It needs from him: three choices on his page (why Tom puts Rita's window in, what fills the quiet route's days, the clock) and two smaller ones in section 6.
- Every number marked draft or guess is replaced by what the AAA study measures in two shipped games.

## 2. The frame

### 2.1 The newcomer

A person who has never seen LEDGER and knows nothing past the title screen. They read the text that appears, type short sentences, use Tab for a suggested line now and then, play at a desk on keyboard and mouse, and will not read a manual. They do not know the town, the cast or the premise. Route A is the newcomer who does what the town hands them. Route B is the newcomer who asks, waits and says no.

### 2.2 What must hold, and the cap

- PLAN.md phase 3, the exit: two different thirty-minute newcomer routes and one continuous checked-live route within the cap, no critical fault, at most five empty minutes, and a consequence seen and kept after a restart. CHARTER.md says what that is for: friends who accept the street's look, see the town remember what they did, and find the conversation responsive.
- The draft's own aims, for the study to test: at most three empty minutes a route (the exit allows five); a consequence within three minutes of a choice; one restart with the consequence still there; no scripted scene longer than two minutes; no set piece twice in one route.
- The cap is minutes and money. Thirty minutes of play. Live talk inside $1 a day for measurement runs and $5 an evening for friends (CHARTER.md, RULINGS.md 3 October), and the $0.50 a run that the 4 October run set in code. Guess: $0.025 for a live exchange (that run cost $0.20 for 8 lines). The draft allows at most 20 live exchanges a route, $0.50, so each route and the continuous checked-live route fits one measuring run, and two of them fit one day only if they stay under $1 together. Authored lines (Sheila's walk-round, Ron's ask, the landing man, the ambient lines, the town news) cost nothing.
- Fighting: neither route contains a fight, a shove or a threat. The rulings of 12 October make fighting good to do and costly to have done, with no health bar, and Tom cannot die; the first fight waits for the animation source test and the first combat test, after the walk passes. Two places would take a first combat beat once it exists, and neither is needed to make a route hold: the landing (A7, A20), where a man who says Tom has no business there invites a shove, which is NOT YET and needs the combat test first; and after the window (A4, A5), where a witness might be talked round by a threat, which is BUILT as talk but whose effect on a police report is the N3 brief, DESIGNED and not ported. Two against one is deadly, so a shove at the landing would have to be Tom's alone against one man, and it would cost him what the street then says. Guns are in the final game, rare, and come only after melee works; none appears in either route.
- Every line and sight keeps the content rule of canon.md. The year is 1990, in Britain; the game is on a PC, in the third person, never the first.

### 2.3 The clock

Guess: two game hours for each real minute, so a day is twelve real minutes (the packaged game's rate; the first-hour design says the rate is to be chosen with the hour in mind). Time zero is Monday 09:00, the moment Tom stands on the pavement outside Mickey's; the title screen is outside the clock. The wait key skips hours and a night's sleep skips seven to ten. The Clock column gives the game time at the start of each beat, and the Mode column says when a wait skips hours at the end of a beat. At this rate thirty minutes without waiting is two and a half game days; with a sleep each night it is Monday morning to Thursday night. Two limits keep each beat honest: a hundred metres takes about a minute at the design's pace (the day-three design gives two game hours from Ada's step to the landing), and a typed exchange takes about half a minute, so a beat holds at most two exchanges and the trip to the landing and back takes about two and a half minutes with the hand-over.

### 2.4 The stage

Quay Street is 48 metres long. On the east side the six-bay parade: Mickey's at the quay end (bay 0, x 6), the fish shop (bay 1, x 12), Rita's pawn (bay 2, x 18, beside the phone box at x 16.5), the empty unit to let, the launderette (bay 4, x 30) and the grocer. Across the road, Ada's terraces opposite Mickey's (x 6.5), the cafe, in the tea room's unit opposite the parade's north half (x 24 to 30), the ironmonger (x 30 to 36) and the newsagent and tobacconist with the pension counter (x 36 to 42), as ruled on 3 October; Hal's coin shop (hook-cast.json x 39) has no unit on the built street (town NOTES item 6); and the bus stop at the top (x 46). The quay is at the foot of the street (x -5), the dock gate 60 metres beyond it and the ferry landing about 110 metres from Mickey's door, along the water. Places are cited by their ids in production/specs/hook-cast.json; the built street is in production/assets/street/quay-street.json and production/specs/vignette-scene.json.

### 2.5 Words used in the tables

- No: the beat's name (A1 to A25, B1 to B25). Min: its start and end minute. Clock: game time at the start.
- Where: place ids from production/specs/hook-cast.json (quay_street means anywhere on the street). Who: person ids from production/cloud-week/town/people.json. Three roles have no entry: outfit_man (canon, unnamed; first-ask design), constable (waiting and police designs), neighbour (the town-news sample's "two neighbours"). A star after an id marks an authored visit, so its schedule is not checked.
- Mode: play, or cut:N for N seconds of cutscene; skip:N means the player waits N game hours after the beat.
- Tension, one word for each minute: rising, falling or quiet.
- Tags, one group for each minute of the beat: `I` new information; `C` a choice the town will remember; `S` somebody speaks to him and is answered, or says something new; `W` the world visibly changes. A minute with none of the four is EMPTY, marked with a dash. The check fails a beat whose empty flag disagrees with its tags.
- Marks: control, move, talk, choice, conflict, consequence, restart; persists:piece names the saved state that keeps a consequence (a piece of TownSave in ledger/Assets/Scripts/Core/TownSave.cs, or memory); ui:name is an interface piece; act:name is an activity; live:N is the live exchanges (model calls) in the beat. A time after the at sign is minutes and seconds from the start of the route.
- Status, by the weakest link: BUILT means everything the beat needs is in the packaged game on the records of 8 October; DESIGNED means a needed piece exists only as a design or as content made in cloud week 42; NOT YET means a needed piece does not exist (a body, a scene, a place, a screen).
- P1 to P3 mark the proposals for Jafar. A new person, place or event is never used without one.

## 3. Two newcomer routes

Both start from New game with Tom on the pavement outside Mickey's. Neither route is a script: each is one path through the same town, and the beats that every path shares (Sheila's walk-round, the first ask at eight on Monday, the second at eight on Wednesday, Ada's invitation, Alison's question about the fire) fall on the same states in both. Every spoken line is quoted from a home and named by its id or by the design that holds it; where no home has the words, the beat says so and gives the gist only.

## 5. What the draft borrows and avoids

Source: the praise-and-punish file in production/research/aaa-reference/ (its name starts with PLAY, a word the canon gate refuses whole, so it is named here by what it holds). Its sections are cited as PRAISE n and PUNISH n, and its closing list as What this changes.

- Avoids a slow opening and long cutscenes: reviewers of both games describe a forty-minute and a three-and-a-half-hour opening. Control comes in 0:20 or 0:03, the one cutscene is 20 seconds and skippable, and no scripted scene runs past two minutes. (PUNISH 4)
- Avoids dead minutes, the plainest report of a boring game: empty minutes are counted by a stated rule, the draft aims at three or fewer a route, and the 11 empty minutes of the 3 October run are the list it was built against. (PUNISH 5)
- Avoids the same loop: the window happens once, the night errand differs by what the player did, and neither route repeats a set piece. (PUNISH 9; What this changes, item 5)
- Borrows a world that feels lived in: every person in a route stands where their routine puts them at that hour, and the check fails a person who is elsewhere. (PRAISE 3)
- Avoids a hollow world and rules that change on a whim: everyone speaks from their own facts and says nothing the simulation does not hold, and a town nobody lives in is the loudest complaint about the other game. (PUNISH 15; PUNISH 8)
- Borrows choices that change what happens and avoids choices that change nothing: each route has a choice with a consequence in minutes and a saved state that survives a quit. (PRAISE 12; What this changes, item 6)
- Avoids no direction without waypoints: directions come from talk, landmarks and the Ledger, a paper map is still missing, and the first purpose is Sheila's walk-round. (PUNISH 14; What this changes, item 4)
- Avoids systems nobody explains: each hint fires the first time it can mean something, one at a time, never on the clock. (PUNISH 20)
- Avoids a costly save: autosave each morning, Continue, and no destructive choice one click from Resume. (PUNISH 19; What this changes, item 3)
- Avoids bugs that stop play and stutter when play changes to speech: typing never walks Tom, and the opening and closing of the talk box are on the performance test's list. (PUNISH 6; PUNISH 7; What this changes, item 7)
- Avoids clunky, unexplained fights by having none: the first fight waits for the combat test. (PUNISH 3)
- Borrows one scene a friend would retell: route A the window and every head turning, route B a plain no and Darren's "Brave or daft". (PRAISE 1; What this changes, item 1)
- Avoids too much talk: every authored line is short and live exchanges are capped at 20 a route. (PUNISH 4)
- Avoids a difficulty that is too hard early: Tom cannot die and nothing in thirty minutes can fail the run. (PUNISH 12)
- Avoids a story the player has guessed within the hour: the arrangement shows its hand at once and the fire is left open to the street's version. (PUNISH 1)
