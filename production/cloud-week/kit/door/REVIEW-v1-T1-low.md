None that a player would see as wrong at this distance.

## Notes (narrow points, or not the piece)

1. **Fanlight: what shows through the glass (context, not the piece).** Through the clear pane you see, left to right, a sliver of empty space, the context wall's bare brick inner reveal (brown courses across the left half of the visible pane), then the frame's inside face. These are visible at 1:1. In P1 (glass rows 95-142) the pane is an even blue-grey, sRGB about 110-130: sky reflected over a dark hall. Neither the target nor the build of the door is at fault. The context has no hall. The assembled house needs a dark interior or plastered inner reveal behind the fanlight, or at walking distance the pane reads as a window onto brickwork.
2. **The black leaf's contrast (light and material, not form).** At 1:1 the leaf runs from luminance 25 to 59 out of 255. P2's black leaf, seen square on, runs 35-50, with creases at 10-22 and lit moulding tops up to 77: the same kind of black. The panels read through the lit left and bottom members of each bolection, the lock-rail band and the weatherboard. The upper panel's outline is faint. Check black and dark green at this distance in the engine with the gloss on.
3. **Stop face against the reveal return (light, not form).** The stop's side face (luminance about 116) and the buff reveal return (about 105) are close in value. Hue and the jamb face's bright 6 px strip (about 146) keep them apart. The engine's white gloss should open the gap further.
4. **Context only.** Seen at 8x, a 12 mm red-brick ledge shows at the foot of the right reveal, on the threshold: the plinth's top run level through the reveal, as target section 4.7 asks. It cannot be seen at 1:1. The arch ring reads paler and smoother than P1's buff ring. Neither is part of the piece.

## What is right

- **Recess.** The far reveal's buff return foreshortens correctly. The near arris cuts the leaf, transom and fanlight cleanly, and the near reveal face is rightly out of sight. The arch soffit and the head stop's underside show from below, as an eye at 1.6 m would see them.
- **Frame.** The stop's side face and the narrow jamb face read as two separate strips. The head is cambered under the soffit, its curve smooth. The keep on the jamb sits just below the cylinder lock, as in P1 (lock at x 258, keep at x 272, rows 290 and 300-314).
- **Transom and fanlight.** The weathered slope catches the light, with the flat face and lip below it. The transom's splayed end runs past the stop edge and stands proud over the stop face. The pane sits in its bead under the head.
- **Leaf.** The visible part is what geometry allows:
  - a sliver of the muntin, with the brass edge of the letter plate;
  - the right upper panel, its bolection raised (lit left and bottom members, dark crease);
  - the lock-rail band, its moulded profile silhouetted against the stop face. It overhangs about 20 mm of the stop face, as the band's 22 mm projection requires;
  - the right lower panel and the weatherboard's belly and nose;
  - the cylinder lock on the right stile.
- **Threshold and step.** The threshold has a rounded nose over a recessed riser. The step has a half-round nose with a thin undercut shadow beneath it (luminance 79-87), a base face, rounded plan corners, and it steps back beside the plinth. This is P1's step (rows 562-610) seen from the side.
- **Build.** No gaps or light leaks at the arris, the head or the jamb foot. No floating parts. No faceting on the step nose, the threshold nose or the head's camber.
