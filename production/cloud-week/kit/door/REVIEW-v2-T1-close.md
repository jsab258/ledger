# Front door 3.1, T1, try 2: fresh review of the "close" view (lock rail and ironmongery from about 1.2 m)

Reviewer: a fresh subagent, 9 October 2026. Judged: `close.png` and `close_plate.png` (1600 x 1200, scratchpad `door-build/v2/T1/`), against P1 `door-photo-01-teignmouth-four-panel-red.jpg` (480 x 640, about 5.17 mm a pixel on the door) and P2 `door-photo-02-tottenham-six-panel-black.jpg` (675 x 1200, 2.317 mm a pixel), read on the preview copies only (the source pages cannot be reached from this cloud). Crops, pixel profiles and a side-by-side are in the scratchpad's `review-door-T1-close-v2/`. Judged: form, proportion, profiles, fixings and construction; not the light, the black paint, the pale ground or the absence of wear. No other review was opened before this one was written.

## Faults, worst first

1. **The bell push reads as a modern video doorbell (build's).** Where: `close.png`, on the right jamb's face, x 1060-1097, rows 416-540: a matt near-black slab with r 6 rounded corners and, at its centre (1078, 478), a bright brass ring 17 mm across round a black domed button. At this distance that shape (a black rounded pill with a lit-looking ring round a central button) is the silhouette of a 2010s smart doorbell, and a player will read it as one. It is the one fitting on the jamb, at eye level, in the middle of the view. What the photograph shows instead: P1, x 271-276, rows 300-314 (pixel values read directly): a plain dark-grey oblong (95-120 against the white jamb's 245), with no bright element anywhere in it (a 17 mm brass ring would be 3 to 4 px of 150+, as P1's brass letter plate reads 165-229), and only a darker horizontal band across its full width at rows 308-309, a little below its middle (a slot, or the shadow under a small flush button). At P1's resolution its ends read straight. The size (22 x 72.6), place (centred on the jamb face) and height (top 52 mm below the lock's centre, P1 10 px) are right. The brass bezel, the domed button and the rounded corners have no number in the target. They are the builder's own (NOTES, "Mine, with no number in the target: ... the bell push's build"), and the target describes a dark steel plate with a slot. The fix is to keep the dark oblong and its size and lose the ring. Either use a plain plate with a small flush button or slot as P1 shows, or use a recognisable pre-1990 British push of that size, for example a square-cornered Bakelite or brass oblong with a plain small button. Either way it must not read as a modern doorbell.

## Notes (narrow points, not failing on their own at this distance)

- **The cylinder's plug is large for its collar, and its keyway is a plain rectangle** (target's numbers). In `close.png` (911-976, 303-369) the plug face is 28 mm inside a 43.5 mm collar (0.64), with a 3 x 9 rectangular keyway that reads as a small dash. On P2's cylinder (centre about 488, 495) the plug is about half the collar (about 20 in 38), and its keyway is a profiled slot with a darker round at its foot. A Yale-type face also shows the plug's shear-line circle at about 15 mm, which this face lacks. This is visible at arm's length, not at 1.2 m. Separately, P1's cylinder face (rows 288-292) is a dull grey, about the door's own value, while its letter plate is bright brass. The render gives both the same bright brass. That is left to the engine's materials, as NOTES says.
- **The lock-rail band reads more deeply reeded than P1's.** In `close.png` (rows 940-1060, x 600-800) the two creases fall below the flat leaf's value (31 against 37) and the rolls barely rise above it (45-49). On P1 (x 140-240, rows 363-378) the whole band stays brighter than the leaf (creases 136-139, rolls 150-165, against the leaf's 100). In the side-by-side, P1's band reads as a broad, nearly plain lit face with a lit top edge and a crisp shadowed underside. Black gloss under this sky exaggerates every change of normal, so this is not a measured fault. If the engine's first lit view shows three distinct reeds, flatten the creases further (build's).
- **The bolection mouldings may be plainer than P1's.** On P1 the members show three lit lines with two creases across their roughly 50 mm: the bottom members of both upper panels at rows 347/350/353 (creases 348 and 352), and the vertical members at x 174/177/181. The render's members show one broad lit slope and the crest (`close.png` rows 813-880, `close_plate.png` all four mouldings). P1 at 5 mm a pixel cannot settle a 7 mm-deep profile, and P2's bolections (the broad lit slope and the outer step, its lock-rail crop) agree with the render. This is a note for the target, not a fault.
- The letter plate's flap sits back inside the rim with a dark gap round all four sides, and its lifting lip is a separate small bar with notched ends (`close_plate.png` 755-843, 425-778). P1 cannot show whether its flap is recessed or flush. P1 does show the flap's top gap (row 197, dark under a bright top rim, rows 193-196), which the render has. Acceptable.

## What is right

- **The lock: place, size and stand-off.** The centre is between the panel moulding's outer edge and the stop, as on P1 (P1 10.25 px and 9.75 px either side; render 73 px and 82 px). It sits 377 mm above the band's top (P1 73 px = 378 mm), and the collar is 44 mm (P1 8.4 px). The collar now stands proud with a rounded edge, lit above and shadowed below: the crescents P1 shows at rows 286-287 and 293. It is no longer a flat coin. The keyway is vertical.
- **The letter plate.** It is vertical on the muntin: 76 x 242 against P1's 15 px x 46.9 px (78 x 243). Its top is 89 mm below the panels' top moulding edge (P1 16.5 px = 85 mm). The aperture is 48 x 190 with its top 22 below the plate's top. The plate covers about two thirds of the muntin's visible width, has a 45 degree chamfered rim and casts a shadow line under it (P1 row 239). The flap is hinged at the top with its gap. No maker's mark.
- **The lock-rail band: height, place and ends.** It is 72.5 high (P1 14 px), with about 40 mm of plain rail between its top and the upper panels' bottom mouldings (P1 8 px). It has a square underside casting a soft shadow down the bottom rail, as P1 does at rows 378-390. It runs between the stops and ends square 2 mm short of each.
- **Construction.** The stiles run through and the rails sit between them: fine joint lines at the stiles' inner edges along the lock rail above and below the band (`close.png` x 868) and along the top rail (`close_plate.png` x 268 and 1355), and across the muntin's ends (rows 868 and 145-150), stopping short of the mouldings (P2 shows such lines). The panels are flat, and the moulding mitres are clean and closed.
- **The frame.** The jamb face is flat and square-edged. The stop's 74 mm return shows as a narrow shadowed strip between leaf and jamb, and the jamb meets the brick reveal cleanly. There are no gaps, holes, floating parts, overlapping faces or visible faceting anywhere in either view. The collar, ring and rolls are smooth.
