## The three places the builder did not follow a review as worded

1. **The keyhole 70 below the pull's foot, not its centre. The builder is right.** The pull is 270 long, so a keyhole 70 below its centre would lie behind the bar, which reaches 135 each way. Placed below the foot, T2's keyhole sits at v 1031.6 and F3's at v 1050. That is an ordinary mortice-lock height. I measured 67 mm on the pictures from the pull's foot to the keyhole's centre (`T2-close_left`: foot at row 845, keyhole centre at about 955, 1.63 px a mm; `F3-close_left` the same).
2. **The scraper's scroll direction. The builder is right, and my try 1 point 12(e) was wrong.** In D10 (`d10-poole-boot-scraper-2014.jpg`) the left end has a straight post (x 465 to 510) that carries the blade. A separate curved leg starts on the ground outboard of it (x 355 to 405). The leg leans in to the post at mid-height, then rises, turns over outward and spirals in. The spiral's centre is at about (427, 280), which is 40 to 80 px outboard of the post. In `T1-close_scraper` the scroll sits outside each standard and curls outward, as D10 does.
3. **Which levers keep the round rose. The builder is right.** My "keep the round rose" rested on D5, and D5 is F6's door, which takes HD2a and keeps its rose. HD2c is T3's, from D4. In D4 (x 990 to 1000, rows 352 to 388) the dark mark at the lock edge is about 35 px tall at about 6 mm a pixel, so about 200 mm. That fits a 170 mm backplate with its lever, and not a 40 mm rose. The 57 mm between lever and keyhole is the standard British sashlock spacing.

## PL-T3-street-dip: a narrow point, not a fault; the builder's reading is half right

I measured it on `T3-street-v2-2026-10-13.jpg`. **The camera is to the left of the door.** The wall recedes to the right: the plinth is at row 960 at the left edge and row 820 at the right. The right-hand reveal is the one in view (x 875 to 910). A left-hand step's wall therefore faces away from the camera, and the 6 mm lip hides 6 × tan 30° = 3.5 mm of the floor behind it. The builder's geometry is right.

What I measured, as a fraction of the face's 180:

| | left-hand outlines | right-hand outlines |

**The builder's remedy is wrong.** It says D4's light is not flat and that the engine's sun will show the lines. But D4 is a wet, overcast day: the pavement is wet and there are no cast shadows at the sill or the reveals. On its own door (rows 420 to 450 and 270 to 300) the outer outlines dip 20 to 22 per cent on both sides: the left one at x 885 to 886 (146 to 147 against 185 to 186), the right one at x 973 to 976 (162 to 165 against 202 to 207). The build's equal 6 x 6 coves do not. This game is overcast, so the sun is no remedy.

**Why only a narrow point.** At 4 m the two panels still read as two panels: 11 of the 16 outlines pass, and the foot and top lines dip 21 to 39 per cent. At close range all 16 read (`T3-close`, `T3-close_light`). The row also asks for more than the photograph shows on the inner outlines. The street view belongs to the street reviewer, who may weigh it more heavily. The fix is narrow point 3.

## Narrow points (each to be fixed by the builder; none fails the piece alone)

1. **F7's thumb lever stands upright above the plate instead of projecting out (builder's, from my ambiguous try 1 wording "rises ... at 15 degrees").** Where: `F7-close`, x 898 to 920, rows 343 to 395: a black tab that stands 20 mm above the plate's top point. In `F7-close_oblique` (x 760 to 810, rows 275 to 400) it is an upright blade. In `HD4.glb`, `iron_latch_thumb` runs from z 70.3 to 114.1 but only 13.7 out from the plate, so it leans 17 degrees off the door's face. A thumb lever leaves the plate pointing at the street and is pressed down. One that stands upright like this cannot work, and it reads as a fin. **Fix:** rotate `iron_latch_thumb` about its slot at v +72 so it leaves the plate at 75 degrees to the face, which is 15 degrees above the horizontal, pointing out and up. Keep it 45 long, so its end is about 43 out and 12 above the slot, over the bow's top end and about 5 clear of its dome. Round its last 20 mm into a 20 mm pad. Add to FU-F7-handle-HD4 a test that the thumb reaches at least 40 out from the plate and rises at most 15 above its slot.
2. **The pull and the bow stand too close to the door (target's, from my try 1 wording; the builder flagged it).** In hardware lists "stand-off" means the clear gap behind the grip. The builder read it as face to axis, so HD3 has 20.5 clear and HD4's bow about 21. That is too tight for a hand: a pull of this kind gives 30 to 40. It cannot be seen straight on, but it shows in `F7-close_oblique` and will show from any side. **Fix:** set the clear gap to 30. That puts the grip's axis 39.5 off the face (off the plate for HD4). HD3's `proud` changes from 39.5 to 49, and the posts are lengthened to match. Add a row that measures the clear gap behind each grip, at least 28. Narrow point 1 still clears: the thumb lever passes 6 above the moved bow's dome.
3. **T3's outer outlines do not read from the camera's far side in flat light (target's profile and row; the one failing row).** See the section above. **Fix:** (a) Calibrate PL-T3-street-dip to D4 on the same lines: at least 10 per cent on the eight outer outlines, at least 3 per cent on the eight inner ones. (b) On the outer outline only, cut a square quirk 3 wide and 3 deep into the margin at the foot of the cove, on all four sides of both panels. The builder's 1.4 x 1.4 V lifted the far-side lines to only 3 to 6 per cent; a slot of twice the width and depth, seen into from either side, should clear 10. Leave the inner outlines as plain coves. Re-run the row on a new street picture. If the quirk still fails, list the far-side outer lines as a known shortfall for the game's light; do not tune the row.
4. **The chrome-look parts read at about half the brightness of P2's plate (target's colour).** `F2-close_plate`: LP2's flap reads 83 and its rim 85 against the black door's 36, a ratio of 2.3. In P2 the flap reads 172 and the rim 155 against the door's 42 to 48, a ratio of 3.7 to 4.1. LK2's collar reads 77 against P2's latch at 143. Part of the gap is the review scene, whose flat plates reflect the dark ground. But the target's `chrome_nickel`, sRGB (170, 170, 168), is linear 0.40, while measured chromium is 0.55. **Fix:** set `chrome_nickel` to sRGB (196, 197, 196) and keep roughness 0.35. Do this in the target for LP2, LP4, HD2a, HD2b, HD2c, LK1, LK2 and the spyhole ring, which is one material. Then judge it beside P2 in the game's light.
5. **A small nick in the 9 (builder's).** `F3-close_head`, x 880 to 890, rows 545 to 575, and `F3-close` at the top: a small step where the 9's stem meets its bowl. The stem's arc (centre 0.52, radius 0.43) and the bowl (centre 0.50, radius 0.42) are not tangent. **Fix:** in `doors_lib._G["6"]`, which the 9 is drawn from, make the first stroke `_arc(0.50, 0.52, 0.42, 0.46, 50, 180, 14) + [(0.08, 0.30)]`. The stem then runs straight down and meets the bowl at its leftmost point (0.08, 0.30) on a tangent.
6. **The scraper's scroll is faceted (target's point list, as built).** `T1-close_scraper`, both scrolls (x 275 to 410 and 1100 to 1270, rows 365 to 530): 8 to 10 straight facets of 16 to 18 mm each. In `standard_profile_xz` the spiral about (35, 169) has a point every 30 degrees. **Fix:** resample the outer spiral (radius 35 to 23) and the inner one (23 to 11) every 7.5 degrees, and smooth the normals of the curved band; the flat sides stay flat. Acceptance: no facet on the scroll longer than 5 mm.
7. **The target's makers still hold the old numbers (target's; records).** `target_drawing.py`, `td_*.py`, `make_target.py` and `make_doc.py` would undo section 16 if they were run. That is why `A3:T1/F1/F2:mouldings_literal` fail. It also breaks "one home per fact": a derived file disagrees with its home. **Fix:** apply `target_changes_try2.py`'s corrections to those files (the ring 4.8 outside the opening; reeded 2.8 + 1.2; T3's profile, border and second cove; F7 at 47; BS1's mirrored list; LP2 chrome; the HD2b and HD2c backplates; KH1 sunk; the numbers).
