## For the next nine shops

What Rita's four reviews taught, in words that hold for any shop. Start every shop with these; each has a check in this kit (`check_try2.py`) that fails the build.

1. **No two parts of a front share a visible face plane.** A box added beside a kit part, a jamb on a jamb, a filler flush with a frame: coincident faces render black in Cycles and z-fight in the game. Clip the kit's part where yours begins, or stand yours 1 mm off, and never add a second part that does the first one's job. An elevation test and a backdrop test (FRN-07, FRN-08) cannot see an overlap: run FRN-10 (visible coplanar overlaps, and no dark patch of 35 mm or wider, under luma 8, in the day frames; a line of shade under a cornice is natural and a strip is not, so measure the patch in millimetres, not in pixels).
2. **The night glass shows reflection only.** A Glossy BSDF (GGX 0.02) weighted by 0.2 x Fresnel at IOR 1.5 over a Transparent shader. Never mix Transparent with a Glass BSDF on a closed slab: a ray bends at one face and not the other and everything behind the pane is drawn two or three times. GLS-03 measures it (p95 against the reflection-off render at most 15), and only against a render of the same size, samples and seed: a half-size 16-sample render's own noise read 18.8.
3. **Everything in the near metre must be seen from the far camera.** The sight line over the window's bottom rail hides whatever stands lower than 8 to 28 mm above the bed. Lay the front row on a velvet riser raked 20 degrees (w 0.05 rising to the step), and measure the front row from the straight-on street camera alone (DSP-03), not from the best of two cameras. A piece under 20 px at that camera (a 4 mm chain) is judged from the close cameras: a glazing bar can hide it from one.
4. **One head light leaves stacked tiers dark.** At night each tier shades the one below. Put a strip light 8 mm under the front edge of every tier board (3.20 x 0.02 m, tipped 30 degrees toward the back, 3000 K, on the display's own light channel, invisible to camera, glossy and transmission rays) and lower the head light to match (LIT-08). Pale goods reach the room's brightness long before black goods do; the red velvet saturates in its red channel and the burnt-pixel limit (PIC-05g counts any channel at 250) decides the strength.
5. **Do not write a condition a pale pavement cannot meet.** The gate's footway goes on the spill's channel and its pool is a stand-in (the game's own is owed); keep the pavement out of the glass-versus-pool comparison.
6. **A dark-void check measures the largest connected patch, not the share.** A slab is one big patch; room shade under shelves is a scatter of small ones (LIT-02b).
7. **Every placed thing goes through one intersection test** (SO-07, 2 mm tolerance), the room's fixtures included: a shelf stops at the upright, a jamb under the head, a hinge stile inside the opening, a glass slab beside its neighbour, a till clear of the board above it, a box clear of the ceiling.
8. **Real-size goods on boards 0.45 m apart read plain.** Add a riser shelf 0.20 above the board and 0.20 deep, fill it to about 85 per cent with the shallow goods, stand the slim ones in front of it, and fill the rest from the pool by count until nothing is left over. Goods with a ticket need 8 mm in front of them for the turn of the piece.
9. **No regular grid of the same thing.** Wall clocks in two staggered rows, never three in a line, each a different time (turn the hand meshes about the dial's axis through the hub). Counts drive the layout: write the counts the build placed back into the target.
10. **Slim tools are measured by rows, not by area.** Hang them in rows by family across the whole board, gaps 0.04 to 0.06, and test each row's width (80 per cent); allow the board and the tool 0.10 off the wall so thick tools keep 0.9 of their size.
11. **Scripted small goods must read at 100 per cent.** A watch needs a painted dial (hour marks, two hands, a ground); a chain needs links (60 of 4 x 1.2 mm turned 90 degrees to each other), not beads.
12. **A kit model with a foreign fitting is mended, not kept.** The projector's lead carried a continental plug: cut it and script a British 13 A plug (51 x 45 x 25 mm, three flat pins). Look at every cable, plug and socket of a model before it is placed.
13. **Hardware stands where the target puts it.** The bell goes on the top rail 90 mm in from the hanging stile, not on the 115 mm stile; test the position against the rail's own u range (FRN-03). A paper notice in the shade of a shelf needs light paper and dark ink (235, 232, 222 and 25, 25, 50) and a drop of 0.05 m.
14. **Gilt lettering mirrors a black night world.** The fascia's name reads darker than its board in the gate at night; the row is owed on the PC (PC-06: a luma ratio of 1.3 or more either way), not fixable in the gate.
15. **Write each check against what the eye sees as well as against the cause.** Try 2's elevation and backdrop rows passed while two black strips stood beside the side door: a pixel count under luma 8 sees what a geometry test misses, and a geometry test (coplanar faces) finds it before a render.

## For the next shops (what batch A taught, in words that hold for any shop)

16. **Test the layout before the render.** `build_shop.py --shop X scenecheck` builds the scene and runs SO-01 to SO-07 in four seconds. The ironmonger's floor run had three bags, three paraffin cans and a dustbin in 1.3 m (23 intersecting pairs) and the first hour of its render was thrown away; the check would have said so before it started.
17. **A kit model is often a layout, not an object.** The tea set is a row of cups, a row of saucers, a teapot and a sugar bowl; office_notepads is ten loose pads; stationery_supplies is a front elevation of pencils and a cup (lay the pens flat, stand the cup up). `sub_template` takes pieces by name, `compose` puts loose pieces together (a cup moved onto its saucer). Read the mesh names and extents before placing a model whose bounding box is a metre wide.
18. **The target's counts and zones disagree with each other and with the room.** 24 magazines at 0.19 across in a 1.0 m zone, 100 packs on a 2.6 m gantry, a dustbin and three bags and three cans in a 1.3 m run, a 0.55 m table with chairs in a window 0.55 deep: decide, build what fits at a believable size, and name each change (each is in `Changes after the build`).
19. **Glass over goods in a room picture** (a cake dome, a pension screen) renders with the shadow-ray trick of `GRoom.mat(glass=True)` and reads as glass; it never goes black.
20. **A front's variant parts need their own numbers checked, not Rita's.** The M1 window's transom is 50 high under a 30 mm cover plate, the M1 door's bars over the door are the 50 mullion carried up (not the 28 toplight bar): each was a gap in FRN-07 until the target's own sections were read.
21. **Text on a Blender curve is not the size the 4.1 script measured.** Set the size from a measurement of the built mesh (capitals 329 mm and a word 2113 mm at size 0.573, spacing 1.08), then the row FAS-04 holds it.
22. **Nothing lying flat on the bed can be seen from the far pavement.** The sill hides the lowest 40 mm, so a pad, a pen set or a trowel on the bed reads 0.00 to 0.30 in DSP-03 however it is placed. Raise it (a 60 mm tray or plinth), stand it up (pens in a pot, a card on a stand) or lean it; and a paper or a card shingled over its neighbour must overlap by under a third and be 12 mm off its plane (the 4 mm sheets at 6 mm cut through each other once a degree of tilt was added).
23. **A model's own material can glow.** The Poly Haven lightbulb has a filament emission of strength 10: harmless by day, a lit lamp burning in the dark shop of night_dark. `GRoom.template` now sets the emission of every material it imports to zero; a room's own lights are made by the room, so they are untouched. Look at night_dark at 100 per cent, brightened, before the hour of the gate.
24. **Measure noise where nothing is textured.** The picture-noise row read 2.29 on the ironmonger's plaster at 64, 128 and 256 samples alike: the plaster's grain, not noise. The ceiling above the back wall reads 0.40. The row now takes the ceiling, and writes the wall's number beside it.
25. **Read every number of a layout against the whole window or run before placing the third thing.** The ironmonger's window at full size needed 3.6 m of bed in 2.3 m: two toolboxes, two cans and three brooms were put to 0.80 to 0.85 and said so; a good layout is a table of u and v written down first (the right end took three tries).

## For the next shops (what batch B taught, in words that hold for any shop)

28. **Write the target's counts against real sizes before laying anything out.** The chandler's room asked for 4 life jackets, 2 lifebuoys and 3 sou'westers on a 2.1 m board, the window for 3 pairs of boots in 0.9 m and 5 coils in a metre: at 0.9 to 1.1 of their real size they do not fit. A table of (what, how many, real width, run available) takes ten minutes and says what is left out before an hour of layout and a render are spent on it. What does not fit is named, never shrunk.
29. **A price card stands before its own goods** (tea rooms close review narrow 2). Cards on stakes before bed goods; a hung good's card tied to the good with a short cord (find the surface by a ray up from the card and hold it 1.5 mm under it, so DSP-03 reads no intersection and DSP-05 reads no float).
30. **A hung thing needs a hook that is its own part.** A rope coil hung straight on a rail intersects it (the rail runs along u, the coil's axis along y); an 8 mm peg through the coil's ring, standing off the rail, is a part that holds it and passes DSP-03. A coil must also be thin enough that a point of its highest quarter touches the peg (DSP-05).
31. **The mullions of the window are in the layout.** From the far pavement a mullion hides 50 mm of what is behind it and the glass line of the sill 40 mm: a tray under a mullion showed 0.47 of its ice, a lemon behind one 0.49, and two lemons had been placed on one spot. Write the lights' widths down (here 0.83, 0.78, 0.78, 0.83 m) and place every front-row piece inside a light; DSP-03 now names the object it flags.
32. **A table, a bed and a back panel are supports.** The slab, the ice bed under the trays and the white back panel are what the goods stand on, so the sill and the goods hide most of them from the street (0.21 to 0.23 seen): they are `support` pieces, as the tea rooms' cover and the crates are, and DSP-03's half-seen rule is for goods.
33. **Find a dark shop's or a lit shop's spill with two 8-sample frames, not a full gate.** `LEDGER_GATE_RES=1280x720 LEDGER_GATE_SAMPLES=8 LEDGER_GATE_VIEWS=CLOSE_35:night_lit,CLOSE_35_B:night_lit python3 build_shop.py --shop X gate` renders the two close night frames in about two minutes and `checks` then reads PIC-05g and LIT-08 from them. The fish market read 0.0001 at 70 W, 0.0018 at 80 W, 0.0064 at 90 W and 0.02 at 110 W (the default 175 W: 0.05): the burn climbs steeply, so take the value with margin (80 W). The launderette needed 70 W and the chandler 150 W with one 6 W strip (it is lit and its goods are dark); the grocer's is in its section.
34. **A room with a dark dado and a dark floor burns before it reaches the approved mean.** The pawnbroker's means (day 119.5, night_lit 79.8) put the grocer's and the chandler's upper walls and orange goods over 250 (PIC-05 0.039): calibrate to day 100 and night_lit 70 (`MEAN_OVERRIDE`; PIC-02's floor is 0.7 of the approved). A burnt share of 0.0102 is a fail: take the day light 5 per cent down and render the day state alone (`LEDGER_ROOM_STATES=day LEDGER_REUSE_CAL=1`).
35. **A blackboard is not black.** The fish market's slate (36, 44, 40) read under luma 12 at STREET_30 and was the whole of LIT-02b's black patch (0.0100 against 0.01): make a slate (66, 76, 70) or lighter, a real chalkboard is dark grey-green.
36. **A good that burns in the night frames is toned at build time, not lit down.** `d.tone(names, k)` multiplies a model's base colour and `d.tone_metal(names, f)` its ARM blue (metallic) channel; the chandler's lanterns needed both (a brass lantern at metal 1.0 reflected the strip lamp into a white patch).
37. **Do not trust a test frame for a row that reads a pair.** GLS-03 and GLS-04 compare a gate frame with a render of the same size, samples and seed with the reflection off: a frame re-rendered alone at 8 samples after a spill test fails them at once. Re-run the whole gate (or leave the check alone) after a spill test.
38. **A dark paint is a black void behind the glass.** The gate's day card is lit at 0.15, so a picture pixel under luma 28 reads under 12 (LIT-02b's black) and under 14 at night_lit: a bottle-green dado (luma 40 to 45) in the shadow of a board reads 17. Read the room picture's darkest 2 per cent before the gate: `qroom.py` shows the share under 28 in two minutes. A paint lifted 1.35 times in sRGB and a low soft fill (`GB.add_fill`: 20 W by day, 30 at night_lit, a bounce and not a lamp) took the grocer's share from 0.024 to 0.004; the room's tubes and sun then come down to hold the mean and keep the whites from burning (the day burnt share 0.0102 at 47 W of sun, 0.0043 with the fill and 31 W).
39. **Test a glass at shallow angles with its normals pointing out.** The coordinator's house-windows finding (a closed slab with a Fresnel mix is a mirror on its back face) was tested on the shop gate's own glass (`sw_gate.make_glass`, GLS-05): a slab built with its normals pointing inward read 0.00 at 35 degrees and passed once its normals were recalculated outward (night 0.91 to 0.96, day 0.80 to 0.94 of a bright card at 15, 20, 35 and 50 degrees); every glass face of the four fronts points out. A test that fails for its own mesh is worse than none: check the normals first.
40. **Fruit in a crate needs a sloping bed.** A greengrocer's crate is heaped and tilted; at real size (0.2 m high) the front wall hides a flat bed's goods from a far pavement and the rear rows hide behind the front ones. A bed of green display paper that slopes up toward the back (the front fruit's centre at the rim, 0.09 higher behind) took 31 apples and 6 lemons from under half seen to every piece half seen or more, and the row DSP-03 stays strict.
41. **Move a failing piece, not the row.** An apple hidden by a jamb from one close camera and by its neighbours from the other went to the top of the heap; a tray under a mullion went into a light. Each is a line in the target's changes, and no goods threshold was changed to pass. What was changed is named: the tables the goods stand on (the fish slab, its ice bed and back panel, the grocer's step A and plinths) are `support` pieces, which they are, and the grocer's ROOM-01 compares counts to the target's with a stated tolerance and names the shortfall (147 tins and 100 packets for 160 and 90).
