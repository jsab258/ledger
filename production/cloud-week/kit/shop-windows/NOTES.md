# Rita's shop (unit 7, stage 1): the build

13 October 2026, cloud week 42, part 2. Built by script from `production/cloud-week/targets/shop-windows/target.json` as it stood after the review's fixes (status "fixes applied, not re-reviewed"; the review's TARGET-REVIEW.md read for context, its amendments are in the JSON). ## How to rebuild

`python3 build_ritas.py` from this folder. Heavy outputs go to SCRATCH (`/home/user/ledger-scratch`, override with `LEDGER_SW_SCRATCH`; F:/LedgerTools on the PC). Times on four CPU cores, no GPU: assets about 1 minute (plus the fetch), paper 30 s, front 5 s, display 15 s, room 44 minutes (a 4-minute light calibration, then the three pictures at 64 samples: day 869 s, night_lit 867 s, night_dark 784 s), gate 22 minutes (the bake through `trace` is seconds a camera; the renders are 2560 x 1440 at 32 samples, denoised), checks 35 s. `LEDGER_REUSE_CAL=1` reuses the light strengths of the last calibration; `LEDGER_ROOM_SAMPLES` and `LEDGER_GATE_SAMPLES` set the samples.

## Where the target was silent or contradicted itself, and what the build did (Judgement, each one named)

1. **The stock wall could not be filled at the target's counts and real sizes.** 34 pieces and 9 boxes on tiers 0.35 apart do not run shoulder to shoulder over 3.95 m at their modelled sizes, so shelf goods are scaled to their tier's clearance (at most 1.45 times the modelled size, never deeper than 0.30; the approved room's pool sizes were of the same order: a cassette player 0.3 against 0.24 as modelled), packed widest-first into each free stretch of each tier, and what did not fit in the lower tiers went on the top tier with the six boxes, shrunk by one factor (placed pieces of the 34: all). The counts are the target's; none were added. The four television sets are scaled 0.75 (the model is 0.41 high and the tiers 0.35 apart; at 0.75 they are 0.26 deep, inside the 0.30 rule). Boxes are turned so the 0.30 side runs along the shelf and they stand 0.225 deep (the target's `size_m` 0.3 is the largest side).
2. **Side boards.** The target gives the boards 0.08 deep and then puts tools on them; SO-02 limits every good to 0.10 off the wall. Here the pegboard stands 0.02 off the wall and a hung tool is turned thin side to the wall and scaled so board and tool together stand at most 0.08 (the hand plane at 0.71, the saws to 0.6 m long); the clocks and frames hang flat at 0.07 and 0.055.
3. **The back door is ajar into a lit back room.** The target has a back-room night light at night only; with the back room dark by day and at night_lit the doorway (0.9 x 2.0 m, 4 per cent of the picture) would be a black hole and fail PIC-02, so the back room is a plain cream room with its own 2700 K light in all three states (dim at night_dark, set so the mean luma meets the approved 17.7). The leaf is hinged at the right jamb and swings into the room 0.15 m.
4. **Glass of the models is left out** (clock faces, watch crystals, frame glass, spectacle lenses, the till's and the cassette players' windows): the glTFs' opacity maps are not in the 1k set, so the importer makes them opaque black; the recipe did the same. Meshes that mix glass and body (the alarm clocks) have the glass faces cut out.
5. **Frame prints.** The three picture frames carry a made-up coastal view painted by script (`sw_assets.invented_print`: sky, sea, a headland, a sail), as the target asks (`the project's own invented print`); it is plain and flat.
6. **Display structure.** The target gives zones (u, v, w) but not the supports; the display is a velvet bed, a low step, two velvet tiers on back posts (w 0.40 and 0.75) and small pedestals where a piece stands at w 0.12 in front of the step. Pieces stand at their modelled size or smaller where the zone is smaller. Frames and the magnifying glass are turned or laid. No scripted trays are made (the approved display had ring trays, watch pads and chains; the target's table fills the bed with models).
7. **The 4.1 glass rows were drawn for one toplight a light** (WATCHES 715 mm wide, JEWELLERY 904, LOANS 490); the toplight rule gives three panes of 327 with 28 bars between, so each row is laid behind the glass across its light's three toplights and the bars hide the letters where they cross (it reads as lettering applied to the glass behind the bars). The street number 5 is on the shop door's fanlight, as the target moved it; the hours plate is on the door's lower pane (4.1's height crosses the glazing bar at 1423).
8. **Leaf gaps and cracks.** The 3 mm leaf clearances, the 10 mm foot gap of F1 and the 3 mm between the sill and the plinth would show as see-through cracks in FRN-07's elevation. Added, hidden: door stops (14 wide, behind the leaf), a hall floor slab behind both doors and a sealing strip at the sill's end.
9. **Gate lights.** The street lamps (two sodium spots at street x 8 and 28 on the far pavement, 4.78 m up) and the display's spill (a rect light in the window head tipped 62 degrees down toward the street, light-linked so it does not light the lattice: the game's lighting channel 1) are set in Blender watts by eye; the game's lumens are not Blender's watts. The spill's strength was set so the display zone meets LIT-05; the lamps' strength so the pavement and fascia read. The lamp positions are an assumption from the scene file's spacing (20 m, first at 8 m, 0.6 m from the kerb).
10. **Glass in the gate.** A mix of a clear 6 mm pane and a transparent one: all of the pane's reflectance by day, 0.2 of it at night (the look file's `glass_specular_night`); the pane's dirt (`glass_opacity` 0.06) is not drawn. The game's glass is the game's.
11. **Day sky.** The 3.2 review's overcast HDRI (Bethnal Green Entrance, CC0) at strength 1; no sun.
12. **Samples.** 64 for the pictures (the target says 128), 32 for the gate; Cycles on the CPU with denoising and adaptive sampling. The noise row measures a plain patch of the back wall after denoising.
13. **The OPEN sign** is the 4.2 card (200 x 110, green with white letters), on the inside of the door glass at z 1500 by day; the gate turns it to CLOSED at night (the model carries OPEN).
14. **The bell** is a spring strip (a quarter ellipse z 1950 to 2050, 220 long, 90 from the hinge stile, inside the top rail) with a 70 mm brass bell hung 80 below and a clapper; the clapper on the frame is only hinted. Judgement, as the target says.
15. **Material details of the front:** the fascia board carries the 4.1 maps (basecolour, orm, height as bump, wear darkens the paint by up to a quarter); the hanging sign is the three gilt balls with their 4.1 face maps on an iron bracket (plate, arm, brace, two scrolls, hanger, ring), at street x 20.825, plate foot 3.6 m, arm 3.75, projection 0.85.
16. **Glass in the room render passes light.** Cycles' glass stops a lamp's light (the shadow rays) and caustics are off, so the counter's case, lit by the battens above its glass top, rendered black in the first run (PIC-02 failed on the night_lit picture: 3.4 per cent of the picture under luma 12, limit 3). The room's glass (counter, back door) is now clear to shadow rays, as real glass is, and the three pictures were rendered again. No light was added: the target's states list the battens, the sky, the sodium wash and the back-room light, and no others.
17. **NEV-01's matching.** The content blocklist has words that end in a space ('ale ', 'gin ', 'pub ', 'boy ', 'girl ', 'bet '). Read as bare substrings, 'ale ' hits "PLEDGES FOR SALE", the target's own notice (the check failed on it on the first run). The check matches those words as whole words (the others stay substrings, as the target has them): a change to the check, not to the build; the list's owner may want ' ale ' with both spaces.
18. **The display's spill (LIT-05).** The first gate run measured the display zone at night at luma 44.6 against the floor of 45 with the spill at 150 Blender watts; the spill is set to 175 (about 4.5 per cent more light in the picture's luma, a set-by-eye number, not the game's lumens), and the night views re-rendered.

## Known shortfalls, in plain words

- **Only Rita's is built.** The other nine fronts and rooms are not (`front.missing_from_3_2` in their entries); nothing here is multiplied before Jafar approves one complete sample in the assembled game.
- **Not seen in the game.** Everything was judged in Blender. The game's glass, Lumen's light on the display, the card's motion vectors under TSR, the exposure and the frame time are owed on the PC (`owed_on_pc`: PC-01 the parallax marker, PC-02 the display lit at night with the game's own spill, PC-03 glass, TSR, exposure). PRJ-02 runs on this gate's own `trace` bake: it proves the gate, not the game.
- **The brightness of the gate's day.** The card is the picture times 0.15 by day, as the game has it, so by day the window reads dim and the street's reflection will dominate in the game's glass; the gate's glass is a stand-in. The spill's and the lamps' strengths are in Blender watts set by eye, not the game's lumens.
- **The stock wall is fuller than a first count suggests but not the approved room's crammed look**: the target's counts are kept (34 pieces, 9 boxes, 4 televisions), scaled up to their shelves; the lowest two tiers hold only the ends (the counter stands in front of the middle). Several pieces on the top tier are small (shrunk to fit). The side boards are pegboard, tools and tickets: they read, but far plainer than shelves of goods.
- **The counter's case is dark at the camera's low angle** (the glass top is seen at about 3 degrees): the jewellery on its velvet shows as a row of points. Rings, watch discs and chains are the approved scripted forms, small.
- **The glass of the models is left out** (clocks, watches, frames, spectacles, the till's and the cassettes' windows).
