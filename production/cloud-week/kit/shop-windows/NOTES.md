## For the next nine shops

What Rita's four reviews taught, in words that hold for any shop. Start every shop with these; each has a check in this kit (`check_try2.py`) that fails the build.

1. **No two parts of a front share a visible face plane.** A box added beside a kit part, a jamb on a jamb, a filler flush with a frame: coincident faces render black in Cycles and z-fight in the game. Clip the kit's part where yours begins, or stand yours 1 mm off, and never add a second part that does the first one's job. An elevation test and a backdrop test (FRN-07, FRN-08) cannot see an overlap: run FRN-10 (visible coplanar overlaps, and no dark patch of 35 mm or wider, under luma 8, in the day frames; a line of shade under a cornice is natural and a strip is not, so measure the patch in millimetres, not in pixels).
2. **The night glass shows reflection only.** A Glossy BSDF (GGX 0.02) weighted by 0.2 x Fresnel at IOR 1.5 over a Transparent shader. Never mix Transparent with a Glass BSDF on a closed slab: a ray bends at one face and not the other and everything behind the pane is drawn two or three times. GLS-03 measures it (p95 against the reflection-off render at most 15), and only against a render of the same size, samples and seed: a half-size 16-sample render's own noise read 18.8.
3. **Everything in the near metre must be seen from the far camera.** The sight line over the window's bottom rail hides whatever stands lower than 8 to 28 mm above the bed. Lay the front row on a velvet riser raked 20 degrees (w 0.05 rising to the step), and measure the front row from the straight-on street camera alone (DSP-03), not from the best of two cameras. A piece under 20 px at that camera (a 4 mm chain) is judged from the close cameras: a glazing bar can hide it from one.
4. **One head light leaves stacked tiers dark.** At night each tier shades the one below. Put a strip light 8 mm under the front edge of every tier board (3.20 x 0.02 m, tipped 30 degrees toward the back, 3000 K, on the display's own light channel, invisible to camera, glossy and transmission rays) and lower the head light to match (LIT-08). Pale goods reach the room's brightness long before black goods do; the red velvet saturates in its red channel and the burnt-pixel limit (PIC-05g counts any channel at 250) decides the strength.
5. **Do not write a condition a pale pavement cannot meet.** The gate's footway goes on the spill's channel and its pool is a stand-in (the game's own is owed); keep the pavement out of the glass-versus-pool comparison.
6. **A dark-void check measures the largest connected patch, not the share.** A slab is one big patch; room shade under shelves is a scatter of small ones (LIT-02b).
7. **Every placed thing goes through one intersection test** (SO-07, 2 mm tolerance), the room's fixtures included: a shelf stops at the upright, a jamb under the head, a hinge stile inside the opening, a glass slab beside its neighbour, a till clear of the board above it, a box clear of the ceiling.
8. **Real-size goods on boards 0.45 m apart read plain.** Add a riser shelf 0.20 above the board and 0.20 deep, fill it to about 85 per cent with the shallow goods, stand the slim ones in front of it, and fill the rest from the pool by count until nothing is left over. Goods with a ticket need 8 mm in front of them for the turn of the piece.
9. **No regular grid of the same thing.** Wall clocks in two staggered rows, never three in a line, each a different time (turn the hand meshes about the dial's axis through the hub). Counts drive the layout: write the counts the build placed back into the target.
10. **Slim tools are measured by rows, not by area.** Hang them in rows by family across the whole board, gaps 0.04 to 0.06, and test each row's width (80 per cent); allow the board and the tool 0.10 off the wall so thick tools keep 0.9 of their size.
11. **Scripted small goods must read at 100 per cent.** A watch needs a painted dial (hour marks, two hands, a ground); a chain needs links (60 of 4 x 1.2 mm turned 90 degrees to each other), not beads.
12. **A kit model with a foreign fitting is mended, not kept.** The projector's lead carried a continental plug: cut it and script a British 13 A plug (51 x 45 x 25 mm, three flat pins). Look at every cable, plug and socket of a model before it is placed.
13. **Hardware stands where the target puts it.** The bell goes on the top rail 90 mm in from the hanging stile, not on the 115 mm stile; test the position against the rail's own u range (FRN-03). A paper notice in the shade of a shelf needs light paper and dark ink (235, 232, 222 and 25, 25, 50) and a drop of 0.05 m.
14. **Gilt lettering mirrors a black night world.** The fascia's name reads darker than its board in the gate at night; the row is owed on the PC (PC-06: a luma ratio of 1.3 or more either way), not fixable in the gate.
15. **Write each check against what the eye sees as well as against the cause.** Try 2's elevation and backdrop rows passed while two black strips stood beside the side door: a pixel count under luma 8 sees what a geometry test misses, and a geometry test (coplanar faces) finds it before a render.
