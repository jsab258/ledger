Review of the street view of Quay Street's kerbs and covers kit (unit 3.7, try 2), render `street.png` (camera (-0.8, 2.9, 1.6) to (0.6, 0, 0.05), lens 30). Fresh reviewer, 12 October 2026. ## Narrow points for the builder, worst first

1. **The concrete channel stands proud of the road and of the setts.** Where: the far left of the frame (about px 0 to 200, y 440 to 510). The pale slab in front of the bullnosed kerb shows a lit front edge and a step at its end against the setts. Its blocks (`channel` to `channel.003`, x 2.806 to 6.490) run from z -0.105 to +0.020. The top is therefore 20 mm above the sett tops (z 0) and 14 mm above the asphalt beside it (z +6), so water on the road could not reach it. Every photograph shows the channel at or below the road edge: Birbeck Street concrete-kerb view, the urban_street_03 kerb run, and St Albans 2022. Whose fault: the build. In try 2 the channel was raised 20 along with the concrete kerb. The target's missing rule for where concrete meets granite let this happen. Exact fix: lay the four channel blocks at z -0.125 to 0.000, flush with the setts and 6 below the asphalt, as along the setts. Leave the concrete kerb top at 115 so it stays level with the granite and the flags. Its upstand then reads 115 on this stretch, not the target's 95. The 95 was measured on a re-surfaced street (PM03), and Quay Street in 1990 is not re-surfaced, so 115 is believable. If the target wants 95, the target must lower the kerb and the flags behind it by 20 along the stretch.

2. **There is no joint between the two sett courses.** Where: along the whole channel, clearest lower right (px 1250 to 1600, y 700 to 840). Course A runs y 0.0005 to 0.1035 and course B y 0.1045 to 0.2035, so they meet at 1 mm. The cross joints are 12 mm, dark and recessed. The channel therefore reads as one strip of tiles with a hairline down it. In the photographs, the joint between the courses is as wide and dark as the cross joints: urban_street_03 kerb-run view; St Albans 2022 (Geograph 7380232); Belfast 2016 (Geograph 5163333). Whose fault: the target and the build. The target gives the courses as y 0 to 104 and 104 to 204 with no joint between them, and the build followed that literally although the target says "joints 11". Exact fix: course A setts at y 0.000 to 0.0985, mortar 0.0985 to 0.1095 (recessed 8, like the cross joints), course B setts at 0.1095 to 0.204.

3. **The setts are crisp rectangles.** Where: the whole channel, plainest in the near setts at the lower right. Each sett has straight sides, square corners, a 1.5 mm chamfer and a 4 mm dome, so they read as modern sawn setts or pavers. Every photograph of old channel setts shows hand-dressed stones. The top arrises are rounded by roughly 10 to 20 mm, the sides are irregular, and the joints vary in width. See urban_street_03 kerb-run view, Belfast 2016, St Albans 2022 and Plymouth 2023 (Geograph 7581550). Materials will soften the faces but not the outlines. Whose fault: the target, which asks only for "domed 4". Exact fix: round all four top arrises to R 10, round the corners in plan to R 12, vary each sett's width by up to plus or minus 6, skew the sides by up to 2 degrees, and make the joints 8 to 16 at random (mean 11).

4. **A sliver of stone stands next to the grate.** Where: the right-hand end of the grate in the view (about px 775 to 795, y 630 to 655), where a thin pale line shows between the grate and the next sett. A course-B sett only 8.4 mm long sits at x 0.5711 to 0.5795, against the grate's end at 0.580. A mason fills a gap that small with mortar, never stone. Whose fault: the build. Exact fix: no cut sett shorter than 60 mm. Remove the sliver and fill x 0.5711 to 0.5795 with the dark mortar or bitumen joint. The neighbouring sett keeps its length.

5. **Two course-A setts run under the grate.** Where: hidden under the grate's kerb-side edge, so not visible in this view. The setts at x 0.4507 to 0.6484 and 0.8675 to 1.0377 reach y 0.1035, while the grate starts at y 0.090, so the solids overlap by 13.5 mm across. The middle sett (x 0.6604 to 0.8555) is correctly cut to y 0.0895. Whose fault: the build. Exact fix: cut both setts to y 0.0895.

6. **The kerb line is ruler-straight.** Where: the whole kerb line. All granite tops sit at exactly z 0.115 and every face at y 0, with no tilt, so the line looks computer-perfect. The target asks for scatter of plus or minus 4 in the face line and plus or minus 3 in the top, tilt up to 1.5 degrees, and one joint in twelve opened to 15. The notes give this to the engine's placement. That is acceptable, but the review composition should apply the scatter so a reviewer can see it. Note only.

7. **There is no asphalt dish at the grate.** The road is flat at +6 right up to a grate at 0, a 6 mm drop. The target asks for a 15 mm dish over 150 on the carriageway side, and the notes give it to the engine. It does not look wrong at this distance. Note only.

8. **The closer block before the concrete stretch is short.** It is 661 mm long (block.006, about px 200 to 330), below the target's range of 750 to 1050. A cut closer is what a mason lays at a change of kerb, so it is believable. Note only.

## What is right

- **Granite kerb.** The half-batter reads correctly: a vertical foot, then the face set back, then a rounded arris into a flat, wide top. The joint lines kink where the batter starts, as real ones do. Upstand 115 and top 170 sit right against the urban_street_03 kerb-run view and the kerb-end view. Blocks are 0.78 to 1.04 m long with 8 mm open joints, and the long blocks with a soft arris read at street distance like Cordova Street, 1988.
- **Concrete kerb.** The bullnose reads like the Birbeck Street concrete kerb.
- **Where concrete meets granite.** At the kerb line the junction is believable: the same face line and top level, an 8 mm joint, and the granite 45 mm deeper at the back, with the flags cut to each kerb's back.
- **Channel.** Two courses in a true running bond, with no cross joint lined up across the courses. Setts are 77 to 203 long (mean 155). Cross joints are about 12 mm, dark and recessed, and course B sits up to 7 lower in places.
- **Gully grate.** 440 x 290, with eight slots running across the channel at right angles to the kerb. The slots read black over the pot. The grate is flush with the setts, and its kerb-side edge is 90 from the kerb with a strip of cut setts in front, as in the urban_street_03 grate ortho. The end walls and side rails read as a cast frame.
- **Stud cover.** 600 square with 10 x 10 square studs, split on the diagonal, flush in the footway and blank, as the bethnal_green_entrance ortho shows.
- **Round cover.** A 690 frame flush with the road, the lid with its lug tread and plain rim band, blank.
- **Period.** No lettering, no maker's mark, no tactile paving. Nothing floats, nothing is sunk wrongly, and the covers stand in their frames.
- **Colours.** The colours are the right kind: grey granite, iron dark (rust comes from the wear masks), and the setts in three stones. The blue cast on the blue-grey setts is the sky light, not the material.

**Not judged in this view.** The crossover (x 8 to 12.8) is out of frame to the left. The recessed footway and road covers, the double-leaf road cover, the stopcock, gas and telecom lids, and grate B are not in the review composition at all. They need their own views before the whole kit can be called passed.
