Review v1, close view, Quay Street lamp post variant C (precast concrete column, mitred concrete arm, steel spigot, boat-shaped sodium lantern). Fresh reviewer, 12 October 2026, cloud week 42. Render judged: kit-lamp-posts/render/close.png (1600 x 1200; camera (-3.0, 1.2, 3.2) m looking at (0, 0.55, 4.9), lens 38). The render was judged at native size and in PIL crops. To see inside the bowl, four scratch probes were rendered from the same camera with the same tool and sky (long lenses on the nose, the boss, the mitre and a catch; the bowl hidden; the bowl clear). The meshes were also read from build/lamp_post_C.npz. No project file was changed.

The head holds up when the player walks up to it. The concrete head and arm read as one weathered casting, and the spigot, boss, canopy, bowl, holder and lamp are all present, in the target's places, closed and touching what they should. There is no fault a player would see as wrong at this distance. There are four narrow points, each with an exact fix. Two of them decide whether the lantern reads as a real 1980s sodium lantern in the engine (points 1 and 2), so do them before the engine pass.

## Narrow points, worst first (for the builder)

1. **A gap at the lantern's nose.** Where: the left tip of the lantern (close.png about x 545 to 556, y 556 to 580). A black notch with a white sliver beside it shows at native size, and clearly in the long-lens probe (test/p_nose.png). Cause, from the mesh: the bowl's rim and the reflector are cut off flat at y 1069.9. There the rim is 55 wide (x +-27.4) and the reflector 67 wide (+-33.7), while the canopy's lip runs on to a point at y 1079. That leaves a crescent about 9 deep and 55 wide, through which the canopy's dark cavity and the reflector's white end face show from below. What the photographs show instead: the bowl seats in the canopy's lip all the way round, nose included, with no opening (Geograph 8023503, the nose at the right of the picture; Geograph 7503277, both ends). Whose: the build's. The target is also ambiguous: "rim 8 inside the canopy's plan, between y 488 and 1070.5", read as half-width minus 8, gives this blunt 50 mm end. Exact fix: make the bowl's rim a true inward offset of the canopy's plan outline by 8 mm, and the reflector's edge by 2.5 mm, each closing round the nose on the axis (the bowl's at about y 1072). Then start the front long-section curve from that point. Add a check: the gap between the bowl's rim and the lip's inner face is at most 3 mm all round. In the target, write "a true 8 mm offset, closed round both ends".

2. **The lamp's jacket is opaque, so the U arc tube can never be seen.** Where: inside the bowl, the pale tube along the lantern (test/close_nobowl_z.png shows a solid pale cylinder). In the glb, `lamp_glass` has no transmission. By day the lamp therefore reads as a plain white tube. At night the jacket's 0.3 glow would cover the tube's full-strength U. What the photographs show instead: the lit lantern from below (US01, previews/.../us01-bethnal-green-lit-lantern-from-below.jpg) shows the core as separate bright strokes, not one even tube. Geograph 7503277 shows the lamp visible through the unlit bowl. Whose: the target's materials table gives the jacket no transmission, and the build followed it. Exact fix: set `lamp_glass` to transmission 0.92, IOR 1.5, roughness 0.05 and tint (246, 242, 232), and keep its glow at night as an emissive at 0.3 of the tube. Add the transmission to target.json's `lamp_glass` row.

3. **The bowl renders as dark smoked brown glass, and nothing inside it shows.** Where: the whole bowl. Cause, from two things. First, the target's (190, 182, 160) went into the glb as the base colour under transmission, which tints the light passing through. Blender treats the closed 3 mm shell as two surfaces and tints it twice. Second, in this render the inside of the lantern is lit only through the glass. This is mostly the render's light (the street reviewer saw the same). I list it because this view is meant to judge the holder and tube through the bowl, and here it cannot. What the photographs show instead: the unlit bowl is pale and translucent, with the reflector and lamp seen inside (Geograph 7503277 and 8023503; the 1989 Orrell and Appley Bridge photographs). Whose: the target's (one number used both as the look and as the tint) and the review render's. Exact fix, checked in a scratch probe (test/close_fixed_z.png): it reads pale and translucent with the holder and lamp showing, as in the photographs. The fix has three parts:
   - give the bowl a transmission tint of (240, 236, 220), and keep (190, 182, 160) as the check on how it looks;
   - keep roughness 0.15 and the prismatic normal map;
   - in review_render or render_lamp, set `visible_shadow = False` on the bowl and the jacket, so the inside gets the sky as the engine's translucency will.

4. **The canopy's nose and the bowl's ends are faceted.** Where: the lantern's left end. It is faint at native size and plain in test/p_nose.png: the lip's outline turns about 90 degrees in four straight segments (stations 1020.9, 1032.7, 1041.6, 1062.3, 1071.2, 1078.8), and the bowl's end curves are five-point polylines. What the photographs show instead: smoothly moulded ends (7503277, 8023503). Whose: the build's, because the target's stations were used as the vertices. Exact fix: put a smooth interpolating spline (Catmull-Rom or monotone cubic) through the target's stations, for the plan and for both end curves, at no more than 6 degrees of turn per segment. That is about 24 segments round each end, and it fits the budget (9,508 of 12,000 triangles).

Note, not a fault in this view: **the arm is level and meets the shaft in a square L.** The two concrete-arm photographs (GG3 1485479, GG6 1485521) rise about 17 to 18 degrees in the picture from a mitre wider than a right angle. This is the target's recorded choice (PW8, rake 0 +-5). No close photograph of a concrete arm junction of any date was found to settle it (search below). If it is ever changed, rake the arm +5 degrees about the mitre and keep the spigot and the lantern coaxial with it.

## What is right

- **The mitre.** The arm is one casting with the shaft's head, flush with its top. The outer corner is rounded R20 and the inner corner has an R10 fillet. The arm's 10 mm chamfers meet the shaft's 15 mm chamfers in a small clean fillet facet. At 5x (test/p_mitre.png) there is no gap, no step and no overlap. It reads as cast concrete of the shaft's own thickness, as GG3 and GG6 show.
- **The shaft.** A square section with 15 mm chamfered arrises that read as thin light strips. No faceting, no seams, and an even taper.
- **The arm's end and the spigot.** The end face is flat with a 5 mm chamfer. The black 42 spigot comes out of its centre and shows a 30 mm neck before the boss, coaxial with the lantern. This is the side-entry arrangement of 8023503.
- **The boss and canopy.** The cast boss sits on the canopy's rear tip with its lower part below the flat soffit, and the soffit closes the canopy's rear underside with nothing showing through. The canopy's lip band and its low dome read right. The plan is a boat lying in line with the arm.
- **The bowl's form.** Deep, with a flat refractor base and sides bulging out to the rim, the kind of deep bowl 7503277 shows under a slimmer canopy.
- **The furniture.** The two catches are small tabs on the lip in the front third, and the hinge knuckles are at the rear (seen with the bowl hidden). The lamp holder is at the rear on its bracket to the ceiling, with the jacket lying along the lantern. Nothing floats.
- **Construction.** All 18 parts are closed meshes, with no open or non-manifold edges.
- **The plates.** No door or number plate is in this frame, which is correct: both are lower on the shaft.
