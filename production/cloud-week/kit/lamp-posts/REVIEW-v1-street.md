Judged on form, proportion, profile, junctions and construction at street distance; light, ground and wear set aside as the brief says.

At street distance this reads as a believable British postwar precast concrete lighting column carrying a 1970s/80s side-entry low-pressure sodium lantern, as it would have stood in 1990. Nothing a player would see as wrong at this distance. No build fault found. The three points below are narrow, and none is a fault at this distance. Two are the target's choices and the photographs do not settle them. One is a note for the engine's material pass.

## Faults

None at this view's distance.

## Narrow points and notes (worst first)

1. **The arm meets the shaft at a square 90 degree L. Both concrete-arm photographs show an obtuse, rising mitre.** Where: top left of the figure, at the shaft head (about x 735 to 800, y 295 to 330). In the render the arm looks level: its slight rise to the right is perspective, and it measures about 7 degrees in the picture where a level arm should come out at 7 to 8. What the photographs show: GG3 Birchington (1485479) has its arm rising about 17 degrees in the picture from a mitre wider than a right angle. GG6 Essex Gardens (1485521) rises about 18 degrees. The 1989 steel columns (823762, 823771) run near level. Whose: the target's. It chose rake 0 (+-5) from the US01 3D fit and the 1989 steel columns, wrote down the disagreement (PW8, second review point 6), and found no period photograph of a concrete arm. Neither did I (see the search below). At street distance the square L does not read as wrong, so this is not a fault. If the PC wants the concrete look without new evidence, the exact fix inside the target's tolerance is to rake the arm +5 degrees about the mitre, with the spigot and the lantern kept coaxial on it. Otherwise leave it until a period photograph settles it.
2. **The arm reads short against the lantern.** Where: the arm between the shaft's road face and the spigot, about 43 px against the lantern's 92 px in the render, a ratio of about 0.47. What the photographs show: in GG3 Birchington the straight arm is about 0.75 to 0.8 of its lantern's length in the picture. GG6's arm is longer still, but it carries a pendant lantern. Whose: the target's. The reach of 430 to the spigot is Read from the research (406 to 457), so this is not a build fault. At street distance it reads as a short bracket, which real columns also had. A note only.
3. **The bowl reads as a dark smoked brown under the pale canopy.** Where: the lantern's underside (about x 805 to 897, y 295 to 325). What the photographs show: both dated 1989 photographs (823762 Orrell, 823771 Appley Bridge) show the unlit bowl pale and translucent against an overcast sky, with the lamp visible inside. This is the render's light: the build follows the target's material (yellowed clear, transmission 0.85, roughness 0.15), and the target's refractor grooves are left to a normal map. For the engine's material pass: check, against those two photographs, that the bowl reads pale and slightly hazed in daylight, using the prismatic normal map and the target's "slightly hazed", and not dark. No geometry change.

Checked and not a fault: the small dark notch at the lantern's front top edge (about x 893, y 290) is the spring catch on the long side, seen edge-on. The 30 mm black spigot shows as a short dark neck between the arm's end and the boss, which is right.

## What is right

- **Proportions.** The column's height against its foot matches GG5 Coundon's concrete column (foot about 0.04 to 0.045 of the height). The shaft is neither stocky nor spindly, and the lantern sits at the top at about mounting height.
- **The shaft.** A square section with a straight, even taper from foot to head, visible in the view (about 46 px at the foot to 23 px at the head; target 220 to 117.5). The 15 mm chamfered arrises show as thin light strips between the faces, so the column reads as cast concrete with softened corners, not a sharp box and not round. No faceting, no seams.
- **The foot.** Plainly planted in the ground with a clean contact: no base plate, collar or bolts, as GG5 shows.
- **The plates.** The number plate shows as a small pale square on the road face at about 2.2 m, as numbered columns carried them. The door plate is on the face toward the building line, as the target places it, so it is correctly out of this view.
- **The mitre.** The arm is the same thickness as the shaft head and flush with its top, with a rounded outer corner and a clean inner fillet. Zoomed to pixels: no gap, no overlap, no step. It reads as one casting, which is what GG3 and GG6 show.
- **The spigot and lantern.** The lantern lies along the arm, in line with it, entered at its narrow rear end through a boss on the spigot, as in US01 from below, the 1989 stations and GG3. Its plan is a boat: narrow at the rear, full and rounded at the front. A slim dome-topped canopy with a lip sits over a deep bowl with a flat base. Size against the column is right for a 35 W SOX lantern on a 5 m column. Nothing is floating; the parts are joined.
- **Construction.** No holes, gaps, overlapping faces or faceting where it should be smooth, at any zoom I tried.
