Material slots are named by what they are: `paint_green` (K1, the condition's colour), `gel_green` (K2), `zinc` (D1, weathered or worn-dirty by condition), `zinc_liner`, `zinc_galv` (K1's bolts), `lock_steel`, `bin_sack_black`, `zinc_new`; the albedo is the target's for each condition. No texture, no emission. The lettering (LITTER on K1 at azimuth 90 and on K2 at z 470, the K1 post's LB n, the dustbin's house number) is the engine's decal; the overflowing litter, the sacks beside D1 b, cigarette ends and gum are the dressing set (instances, never baked in); the chalk, scuffs, rust, white rust, soot, stickers, scorch and the dents under 5 mm are masks and normal maps.

**Departures, each small and named:** the dustbin is a solid of revolution closed at the rim (the sheet's 0.6 is not modelled); K2's burnt hole is at azimuth -58 and z 790, not the target's -28 and 770 (with the aperture now 230 wide, -28 would run the two together); the dents are a smooth bump on a coarse mesh (at 36 sides a 55 mm dent is two vertices across: only the dents 5 deep or more are geometry); the three K1 dents that face the post (azimuth 140 to 230) are in the mesh but cannot be probed from outside; the dustbin's flutes and lid ribs are normal maps (the budget).

**For the builder:** K1's bin is on +x; the engine mirrors the model for B2 (a bin on the other side of its post: bin_dir). K2's aperture faces +x and the engine turns it to the building line (+-15). D1 b's lid is a separate object standing off the can: the engine may move it. The placements, the three conditions' picks (B1 K1 A, B2 K1 B, B3 K2 c, D1-1 a, D1-2 b) and the dressing sets are the target's.
