# The brief every target writer gets (cloud week 42)

You write the exact target for one family of Quay Street's pieces. You do not build it; someone else will build it by script from your target alone, and a third person will judge the build against your photographs. So everything the photographs show must be in your target, in numbers or in plain words that a script can follow.

## The game, in short

LEDGER: a third-person PC game set on Quay Street, in the Hook, the old port of Meridian, a fictional British port town, in 1990 (canon's window 1988 to 1992). Photoreal, wet, overcast, grimy Britain: weather and grime are the strategy. The street is 48 m long, a 6 m road with 2 m pavements; the east side a six-bay Victorian shop parade (Mickey's minicab office at the quay end), the west side a shop block and plain terraces. The game camera stands about 1.6 m above the pavement. The engine is Unreal 5.8; pieces arrive as .glb in metres, z up, scale 1, the pivot at the base's centre on the ground (or as the target says for wall-mounted pieces). Read canon.md (the world's facts and the content rule) and RULINGS.md before you start.

## The rules you must keep

- **Photographs win over books** (Jafar, 8 October). Where a book, a standard or a drawing disagrees with the photographs, the photographs win, element by element: proportions, edges, profiles, fixings, furniture, finish. Write each disagreement down with what you chose.
- **Every detail the photographs show goes into the target.** The front door of 8 October failed its review on mouldings, door furniture and the frame's edges, which its target never wrote down. Go element by element through each photograph and ask: is this in my target, with a number or a profile? Edges (square, rounded, chamfered, and by how much), mouldings and their profiles, joints and seams, fixings (bolts, screws, rivets, straps), furniture (handles, plates, locks, hinges), bases and roots, caps and finials, drips and throats, paint and finish, how it meets the ground or the wall, and the typical wear and damage.
- **Only sources you actually reached.** A page that refused you is not evidence; list it as unreached and use nothing from it. Search summaries are leads, never numbers.
- **Licences.** Photographs for measuring only from free licences (Wikimedia Commons, Geograph: CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, public domain), with author, licence and date read on the file page; never placed in the game, never traced into a texture, never fed to an image model. Books public domain by age (archive.org, HathiTrust full view). Nothing marked NoAI. No real brands, logos, royal cyphers, crowns, operator marks, council names or makers' names on anything: canon owes the pillar box's cypher and the council's name, so those stay blank or generic ("LITTER" is fine; "POST OFFICE", "ER", a crown, a real maker's casting mark are not).
- **Dated.** Prefer photographs taken 1975 to 2000 of British streets; a later photograph of an object unchanged since the period is acceptable when you say why it is unchanged. Say each photograph's date, and whether it shows the period object or a replacement.
- **1990, Britain, a port town's old quarter.** Not London-smart, not heritage-restored, not modern replacement stock (no 2000s street furniture).

## What you hand over (in production/cloud-week/targets/<family>/)

1. **TARGET.md**: one summary line at the top; the sources (each with URL, date read, author, licence, date taken, what it shows, and whether you used it); then the target, part by part, every number with its source and kind (Read: printed; Scaled: measured off a drawing; Photo: measured on a photograph, with method and error; Derived; Judgement), the photographs-win disagreements, the variants the street needs (how many, how they differ), materials and colours (paint colours as sRGB and as a plain name; roughness in words and a 0-1 value; metal or not), wear and damage as the photographs show it, and what the target could not settle.
2. **target.json**: the same numbers for a script: overall dimensions, every part with its dimensions and position (metres or millimetres, say which), profiles as point lists, variants, materials, and a list of `checks` the builder's automatic check must pass (each: a name, what to measure, the expected value and the tolerance).
3. **target_drawing.py**: draws the target from target.json alone as filled polygons in millimetres (an elevation and the sections that matter) into a JSON file, and as pictures to a folder given on the command line (pictures never go into git outside production/previews/).
4. **self_check.py**: tests the target against its own sources before anything is built: every printed number used comes back from target.json; every photograph measurement overrides as stated; the drawing's projected edges fall on the main photograph within the stated error (laid on it at a scale fitted on one dimension only); internal consistency (parts add up, nothing overlaps that should not, nothing floats). It prints a result line and writes its result into target.json under "self_check".
5. **Previews of the photographs you measured**, reduced (JPEG, at most 1200 px on the long side, under 300 KB), in production/previews/cloud-week/refs/<family>/, named <ref>-<place>-<what>.jpg, plus an overlay of your drawing on the main photograph (…-target-on-photo.jpg). These are for the reviewers; credit each one in TARGET.md.

Python: run scripts with /home/user/.bpyenv/bin/python (numpy, scipy, pillow, shapely, bpy are there). Do not commit or push; do not edit files outside your family's folder and its previews folder. About 30 to 45 minutes. When done, reply with your summary line, the self-check's result, and what the target could not settle.
