## Faults, worst first

1. Panel mouldings, all four: flat facets (a square-edged raised strip, then a straight bevel into a sunken flat panel), so from the front they read as thin scored outlines, like a modern moulded door. Photo 2 shows bold projecting mouldings with a curved face, deep shadow under the top member and a bright lower lip; photo 1 shows the same lit lip.
2. Lock-rail band: a plain square-cut plank, flat top and flat face. Photo 1 shows a moulded band with a rounded, lit top and a shadow line beneath.
3. Weatherboard: a flat-faced wedge that reads as a dark strip at the foot, not a projecting moulding. Photo 1 shows a rounded, lit top over a hollow, shadowed face.
4. Transom bar: a flat board flush with the frame. Photo 2 shows it projecting, with a moulded sloped top throwing a shadow onto the door head.
5. No letter plate, knob, lock or number, which both photographs have; the leaf reads as a blank.
6. Frame edges so rounded that, at an angle, the jamb reads as a pole ending in a soft rounded stub on the sill (oblique view). Photo 2 shows flat-faced jambs with a small bead, standing square on the step.
7. The square-cut sides of the panel mouldings show as near-black hairlines down one side of every panel in the close views. Neither photograph has them.
8. Stone lintel and sill stop at the sides of the opening instead of running into the brickwork; photo 1's step runs past it.
