# Four-panel front door and frame: sources, numbers and choices

Read on 8 October 2026. Every number says where it came from:

- **Read**: printed on the page (page image opened, not only the OCR text).
- **Scaled**: measured off a printed drawing against its own printed dimensions, plus or minus 3 mm unless a wider error is given.
- **Derived**: worked out from printed numbers.
- **Photo**: measured on a photograph; the method and the error are given.
- **Judgement**: nothing else fits.

Nothing here comes from memory. 1 inch = 25.4 mm.

Jafar's ruling applies: where a book and the photographs disagree, the photograph wins. A book value is kept where the photograph agrees with it within the photograph's error.

Page images are in F:\LedgerTools\lab\door\refs\ (named by book and page). Previews (JPEG, 1200 px at most, under 300 KB) are in production\previews\lab\door-refs\ under the same names, plus crops named "...-crop-...". Drawings are in F:\LedgerTools\lab\door\target\ (target_drawings.json, elevation.png, section_h.png, section_v.png, all at 1 mm a pixel).

**How P1 was measured.** Edges were read on 6x zooms and from colour and brightness profiles along rows and columns, at plus or minus 2 px an edge. The scale comes from the visible width between the frame's stops (149.9 px), set equal to the target's clear width between stops (772.0 mm). That gives 5.15 mm a pixel. The same scale is used vertically.

The photo cannot fix absolute size. Brick courses measure 15.3 px, so the visible leaf height is 24.4 courses, but no course height was found printed. Its proportions are therefore used, not its size.

The error on each measured length is plus or minus 2.8 px, about 15 mm. The height-to-width ratio is 2.50 plus or minus 0.04.

**The game's present door** is 838 by 1981 by 60. It is a plain box with no frame and no fanlight, and brick is laid straight above it.

- **What the evidence supports.** No source prints 2 ft 9 in by 6 ft 6 in for a front door. A height of 6 ft 6 in is printed only for small-house, attic or common doors (Hasluck p.333, OCR text; Riley p.353, both 2 ft 6 in wide). The front-door examples are 2 ft 8 in by 6 ft 8 in (Hasluck) and 3 ft by 6 ft 10 in (Riley), and Ellis gives 7 ft and up.
- **The size.** The game's leaf, seen between frame stops, would show a ratio of 2.48. That sits inside P1's error, so the size is not contradicted by P1. It is supported by no book.
- **How the target differs.**
  - The leaf is 25 mm narrower (812.8) and 33 mm lower (1948).
  - The leaf now sits in a 101.6 by 127 frame set back 114.3 behind the brick.
  - A 267 mm fixed fanlight sits over a 65 mm transom.
  - The brick opening becomes 882 wide by 2330 high from the sill. The game's opening is 838 by 1981.
  - The leaf is 50.8 thick, not 60.

## 4. Unreached and gaps

- No dated 1880 to 1905 terrace door with confirmed original joinery was found. P1's build date is not documented, and its fresh paint may hide a renewed door. P2 is undated and six-panel.
- Geograph's own site was blocked by a bot check, so its photos were reached only through their copies on Commons. The Commons API rate-limited several searches. The categories "Victorian doors", "Front doors in the United Kingdom", "Doors in London", "Front doors in England" and "Four-panel doors" were therefore not listed, and county door categories were not browsed. Most period terrace doors seen had glazed upper panels and were rejected.
- No book read prints the door frame's rebate, stop, ovolo size, moulding sizes, weatherboard or lock-rail band. Those values are scaled or taken from P1, and flagged.
- No brick course height was found printed (Rivington's OCR gives the brick as 2 1/2 in thick, page image not checked, joint not given). So P1 cannot give absolute size.
- Riley's *Building Construction* (1905), named in the brief, was not opened. His *Manual of Carpentry and Joinery* was used instead.
- Hasluck's Fig. 1147 elevation is not drawn to its printed proportions, so nothing vertical was scaled from it.
- Ellis's door frame figures (355 to 358) carry no dimensions or scale.
- Not drawn: the inside linings and architraves, the iron plugs, the wall's inner face, and the step below the sill (P1 shows a projecting stone step about 144 below the sill top).
