# The Hook sheet, checked against the research and canon

WHY THIS EXISTS, Jafar 2026-09-22: the sheet was generated from a prompt built
on the research, then approved by eye, and nobody had ever checked the IMAGE
against the research or canon. Everything on the street has been matched to it
for a week.

WHAT IT IS AND IS NOT. `hook-sheet.png` is the approved reference and stays
the approved reference. It governs MOOD, PALETTE and COMPOSITION, which is
what it was approved for and what it is good at. It does NOT govern what
things looked like: it was made by an image model, and the three lists below
are what happened when it was asked to invent the parts the prompt did not
pin. Where it and a period photograph disagree, the photograph wins
(`photographs.md`).

HOW IT WAS READ. The sheet is 688 x 1024 and the two panels are about 620 x
327 each. Read at 2x whole and at 4x to 7.5x on the water, the lamp, the
shopfronts and the near west block. **Anything smaller than about four pixels
on this sheet is not evidence of anything** - the research README already
records three traces of one lamp crop giving three answers - so the fine
detail the period photographs are strongest on (fluorescent strips, tiled
thresholds, patterned stallrisers, paper notices, till types, door furniture)
is simply not in this picture, and a brief that cites the sheet for one of
them is citing noise.

## 1. Consistent with the research and canon

- **Brick party-wall terrace in a continuous run**, with varied roof heights
  and stepped eaves. Morphology rule 3 keeps the 6 m modules intact; the
  Kasbah statement's facade pages record varied heights and patched repairs.
- **Welsh slate roofs, dark grey to near-black, with brick stacks and pots.**
  `art-direction.md:305`, "Roofs are Welsh slate, dark grey to black."
- **Tall narrow first-floor sashes**, roughly 1 : 2.3, evenly spaced, flat
  lintels and projecting sills, white painted.
- **The street lighting column**: slender near-black steel, a plain shallow
  swan-neck, a small head aimed down the street, no fluting, no scroll, no
  finial. `art-direction.md:346-347`, "sodium lanterns on swan-neck steel
  columns", and R07's "plain bent-arm lighting". (First read at 2x it looked
  ornamental; at 7.5x it is plain. The 2x read was wrong.)
- **Wall bracket lamp** under the eaves, short curved arm off the wall
  junction. `content-sourcing.md:322-323`.
- **Stone flag pavement, pale kerb, crowned tarmac carriageway, white centre
  line, double yellows.** R09: "slab paving, carriageway markings".
- **Wet overcast daylight**, low contrast, desaturated. Canon's stated target.
- **Grime where water, hands and deliveries put it** - foot of walls, around
  downpipes, under eaves - and not spread evenly. The bible says exactly that.
- **Stone quay wall and an enclosed basin** with mooring rope and a working
  figure. Morphology rule 4, "Hook: working stone quay and enclosed basin".
  (What is floating in it is another matter: see list 3.)
- **Galvanised dustbin** in the object studies. `art-direction.md:347`,
  "wheelie-less metal dustbins".
- **Nothing visibly post-1992.** No CCTV, no mobiles, no coordinated wheeled
  bin sets, no modern paving pattern.

## 2. Invented by the image model, no basis either way

- **The wooded hill that closes both views.** A green, tree-covered hillside
  with scattered detached houses loose among the trees, and a pale scar in the
  street panel. The bible does want "flat low harbour ground, then a
  deliberate inland rise", and Fairview does keep villas - but it says the
  rise is EXPRESSED by "contour-following terraces, retaining walls and stair
  shortcuts", and not one of those is in the picture. A wooded valley side
  with loose villas is the model's idea of a hill. **This one is load-bearing
  right now**: the far end of our street is still sky, and when stage 6 puts
  something there, the sheet and the bible want different things.
- **Retractable canvas awnings** over several shopfronts. Nothing in the
  research mentions blinds either way.
- **The shopfront colour rhythm** - cream, bottle green, navy, oxblood. This
  is the sheet doing its own job (palette) and it is fine to follow; but it is
  not evidence, and R06 warns that a black-and-white photograph "cannot
  establish paint colour". Cite it as the sheet's palette, never as period.
- **Arched-head glazing in the ground-floor frontage.** No research line.
- **Rope coiled over the quay edge, tyre fenders, a figure working timber.**
  Quay work in general is D04; this particular staffage is invented.
- **Two of the six material swatches** read as dry cracked earth, which is an
  odd thing to hand a wet overcast British port. The coiled rope and the
  wooden chest in the object studies have no research line either.
- **Clothing establishes nothing.** Every figure is a dark coat at 6 to 20
  pixels. R09 asked for casual printed tops, trainers and light jackets; the
  sheet neither gives them nor refuses them.
- **No signage on any shopfront.** Every fascia in the street panel is blank.
  The bible calls for "fictional signage; ordinary paper notices" and the
  sheet supplies none, so every sign on this street is unreferenced.

## 3. Contradicting a ruling

- **Mickey's is drawn as a pub.** Oxblood painted frontage, gold serif
  MICKEY'S across the fascia, arched pub glazing, a gold date on the
  stallriser. **D19, 14 September, made it a minicab office** and said in
  terms that it applies to "canon, the atlas, the plans and THE HOOK SHEET".
  Canon adds "a rank outside", and the sheet runs continuous double yellows
  past the door, which forbids one. The known contradiction, confirmed, and
  the sheet was never revised for it.
- **Not one metal shopfront anywhere.** Every frontage that can be read is
  painted timber with small-paned display windows and a plain painted
  stallriser - the conservation-area refit, not the 1989 working street.
  **D01 gives Quay Stores "a plain metal shopfront"** and **D06 says "Hook
  shops receive metal frames, practical light fittings and repair patches
  within older masonry"**, both decided FROM R05, whose photograph is a metal
  shopfront with fluorescent strips and a patterned tile stallriser. This is
  the contradiction that has cost the most, because our own fascias and
  joinery in `terrace-front.py` were built to match the sheet.
  (Honest limit: the near west block's ground floor on the far left of the
  street panel is too close and too dark to read. Every frontage that CAN be
  read is timber.)
- **The water carries canal narrowboats.** Long black hulls with red
  gunwales, low cabin roofs, moored stem-to-stern in a narrow stone channel
  with tyre fenders. Meridian is a PORT; the Hook is its old port; D04 has the
  basin doing "small landing, chandlery and coastal trade" and packing fish;
  R08's photograph is a liquid-cargo barge and working craft on a chain-edged
  quay. A narrowboat in 1990 is inland leisure craft and can do none of it.
  The quay and the basin survive the check; the craft in them do not.
- **Mickey's and the fish shop share one frontage on a free-standing corner
  building.** The street panel letters one continuous gold fascia and the
  harbour panel puts FISH on a return elevation under a gable.
  **`MICKEYS.md` is explicit: "The fish shop is the next bay north"** - a
  separate 6 m module in a party-wall run - and morphology rule 3 keeps those
  modules intact. The dimensioned drawing wins on geometry anyway; what this
  costs is that the LOOK brief has been reading a corner pub with a return,
  and this street does not have one.

## What follows

The sheet is not withdrawn and nothing built to its mood, palette or
composition is wasted. What changes is that four things on it stop being
citable: the pub, the timber-only shopfronts, the narrowboats, and the corner
building. Where a brief needs one of those, it goes to `photographs.md` and to
the ruling, in that order.
