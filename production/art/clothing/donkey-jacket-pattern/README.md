Step 2, the body, done (tools/meshgen/blender/sew_jacket.py --body; body-stage.jpg): the back (one piece across its fold) and two fronts, cut from the pattern's seam lines with matching points along every seam and filled with even 10 mm triangles (13,000 points), rolled round an elliptical cylinder of the pattern's own girth (a flat piece rolls onto a cylinder without stretching, so the first frame, which Blender takes as the cloth's natural shape, holds the pattern's lengths), bent in over the shoulders, sewn weightless and draped: every seam shut by frame 40, a smooth straight box from the shoulders to mid-thigh. What it took: a rest shape key started the cloth from the flat layout itself; 0.6 kg a point (the old jacket's) tore it through the body; the armhole segment ran the long way round (chosen by points, not length); a cylinder of the pattern's girth ran through the arms of the rest pose, so the body stage collides with the torso alone.

Step 3, the sleeves, tried (sew_jacket.py --from the body stage's settled points; sleeves-stage-tried.jpg), not yet right: with the arms colliding again they pushed into the body shell, which had settled into the space the arms fill (the body stage ignores them), the side seams tore 19 cm open and the jacket rode up to the hips; the sleeves, started from the settled armholes, collapsed into strips along the top of the arms. The rest pose's arms, 45 degrees down, close the armpits. Next, the usual answer: pose the body's arms out (towards a T) with its own skeleton, sew and drape the whole jacket there, then bind it to the skeleton and return the skeleton to its rest pose, carrying the jacket with it; then the collar, the overlapping front, the yoke band and the pockets on the render mesh, and the meshes for Unreal.

Step 3 again, the arms raised (sew_jacket.py --pose 20 --lift 0.04, and --noreturn to stop before they come down; sleeves-raised-tried.jpg, above with the arms still raised, below after they came down): not right either. The shoulders, the front armholes and the sleeves' own seams closed, but each sleeve started about 18 cm from the armhole it is sewn to, and the seams pulled the sleeves up off the arms into crumpled strips before anything draped; the side seams and back armholes stayed about 20 cm open, and when the arms came down the strips stayed where they were. (Also fixed on the way: the sleeves were rolled onto a cylinder 4 cm wider than the pattern, which stretched them by half from the first frame.) That is the second method to fail on the sleeves, so under the two-attempt rule the jacket waits for Jafar's choice of route. If the pattern route goes on, the next try starts each sleeve on its armhole, as the second stage did, with the arms raised: the sleeve's top on the placed armhole's own points and the rest run out along the arm.

## The one-day attempt, 29 September (Jafar: Blender stays; drape with the arms held out, then pose; research first)

Research: production/research/clothing-pipeline/SLEEVES-2026-09-29.md. Run on Ron's current body (F:/LedgerTools/tmp/dcc/MH_RoccoP2/MH_RoccoP2_Body.fbx; the older bare body gave a wrong torso) with sew_jacket.py's new sleeve stage, about two hours in by 17:40:

- The body stage with the arms held out 15 degrees below horizontal (--body --pose 15 --noreturn), colliding with the torso alone: every seam closed by frame 20. Against the whole posed body instead, the panels slid off the shoulders to the chest.
- The sleeve stage (--from that drape --pose 15): the body pinned outside a 7 cm band round each armhole, the sewing force ramped, only the sleeves colliding, and with the arms alone; each sleeve a cylinder round the arm's two segments, blended from the armhole's own points over its first 15 cm. At frame 1 every seam is closed but the underarms (1.6 to 5 cm). Found on the way: self-collision on made the coincident cap and armhole repel (off now); ramping the sewing from nothing let the tube unroll; the sleeve's first 20 cm started inside the shoulder (599 points, up to 7 cm deep), now moved out from the arm's axis (pushing along the surface normal blew the cloth apart).
- Still failing: by frame 20 the back armholes open 24 cm and the side seams' tops 16 cm. The cause is the body drape: with the arms absent, the panels' armholes and the side seams' tops lie inside the raised arm's root, where the sleeves cannot follow. Next within the day's cap: form the body's armholes round the arm root (the arms present in the body stage, the panels started where the rest-pose drape put them), then the sleeve stage again, then the arms lowered.
