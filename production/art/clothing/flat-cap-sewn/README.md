# A flat cap, sewn round Ron's head (the clothing session, 29 September)

CLOTHES.md item 4, the second half. Jafar rejected the cap of 25 September ("not like this"; ../flat-cap-2026-09-25): draped on an egg of the pattern's girth, its band stood up and its crown domed, an eight-panel cap's shape. The research (production/research/clothing-pipeline/TROUSERS-AND-CAP-2026-09-29.md) and the photographs (production/reference/work-trousers-and-flat-cap-1990.md: Peter Fryer's Smith's Dock, 1990 to 1991, 149-012 and 149-005) say a flat cap's crown is pulled forward and fixed to a short, stiff peak, so its front is nearly level, and it sits low and close.

## The pattern

FreeSewing's Florent (MIT), drafted for the girth of Ron's hat line (the second attempt: 649 mm round, just above his brow at the front and lower on the skull behind, tipped 7 degrees, taken over the ear tops; Florent for a 636 mm head gives a 648 mm opening): two crown halves with a centre seam, one band on the fold (77 mm tall at the front, running out to nothing behind the ears), the peak. The numbers that make it a flat cap: the band's upper edge is 422 mm a side against 245 at its lower, so it flares right out under a crown about 30 cm across.

## How it is made (tools/meshgen/blender/sew_flatcap.py)

- The opening (the band's lower edge, then the crown's back edges) held on the hat line, 3 mm out.
- The peak rigid (it is stiffened with buckram): its inner edge along the hat line across the forehead, out and 12 degrees down, its depth running out to nothing at its tips.
- The band laid flared out 45 degrees, the crown as a wide shallow disc over the head; the crown's front edge sewn onto the peak's top 8 mm behind its edge for 13 cm each way from the middle (the research's fix: the crown pulled forward and fixed to the peak).
- Sewn by projection (tailor.relax), welded, settled with a small weight, pressed.

What went wrong on the way, each fixed: the hat line tipped the wrong way (the first cap sat at the eyes); the crown laid over the head the wrong way round (down through the face); the peak mapped by height on the pattern (it wrapped the sides like a halo); the band's mirrored half flipped across the wrong axis (its fold carried 30 cm lengths and threw a strip of cloth out sideways); the band's upper edge taken the short way round its outline (its lower edge and fold, so the crown was sewn to the wrong edge); the band laid upright (its extra length crumpled into a ridge, a pillbox's wall); blocking the crown's middle level made a ridge, not a flat top.
