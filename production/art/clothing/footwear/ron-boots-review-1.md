# Ron's boots, blind review 1 (30 September, night): PASS

A fresh reviewer judged the first attempt (tools/meshgen/blender/model_footwear.py --kind boot: rings lofted along his foot, each his section set out 7 mm, a superellipse toe box 16 mm beyond his toes, the shaft to 15 cm, a flat 20 mm sole with a 5 mm welt, six pairs of eyelets, weights foot and ball across a slanted ball line and the shaft into the calf) against his casting sheet and concept, the Science Museum's NCB steel-toe boots of 1981 and the V&A's black work boots of about 1982.

Verdict: PASS; no obvious faults; "the trouser hems will cover the top of the boot most of the time".

Narrow points, most visible first (the reviewer's):
1. The collar's top edge ragged: small spikes and torn-looking flaps, two little horns at the back corners; shows most when he sits.
2. The sole a flat slab of even thickness, no heel (the references: a heel thicker than the forepart, the NCB boot a step under the arch); the heel corners cut off at angles from behind.
3. The lacing looks painted on: no bow or ends, no tongue, no gap between the facings.
4. The toe low and slightly long (a steel toe is taller and rounder); about 10% long for his feet.
5. The join of upper and sole wavy and lumpy, the upper soft in places.
6. Stiff across the ball at push-off (acceptable for a work boot).

Right: clearly a black, ankle-high, lace-up work boot of the period, close to his concept; a broad rounded toe; a welt all round; a sensible height; six pairs of eyelets; on his feet in every pose, nothing through, no tearing or spikes, bending with the ankle; the size right against his frame.

Handed to the builder 30 September with these notes (NOW.md, Handovers): under the gate's rule, once a fresh reviewer fails nothing obvious the refining stops and his eye decides the rest.
