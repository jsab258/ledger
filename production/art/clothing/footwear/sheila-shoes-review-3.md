# Sheila's shoes, blind review 3 (30 September, night): FAIL, set aside

The last try, after research (production/research/clothing-pipeline/SHOE-TOE-AND-LACING-2026-09-30.md): built as the trainers' last try (the toe drawn as a last is, a 13 mm nose; an open throat with four pairs; the toe cap and counter as panels; the upper meeting the sole on one line). A fresh reviewer judged it against her concept and the V&A's Rayne brogue of March 1989.

Obvious faults:

1. The heel from behind like a deflated bag: bulging at mid-height, tucking in above the heel block, a notch at the bottom centre, a crease across (a smooth firm cup rising almost straight from the heel block). The view the third-person camera shows most.
2. The forefoot too broad and flat, like a duck's bill: its widest about 2.5 times her lower leg against 2 in the concept; the toe box spreading, low and flat, the toe cap knobbly: clumsy, oversized, a man's shoe (the concept: a neat, gently rounded toe with a little lift; the Rayne slimmer again).

Narrow points: no tied bow or loose ends; the lacing as if pasted on, the facings' edges not showing, the tongue a small point; no lift at the toe; flat faces showing on the sides and toe cap; a step where the upper sits on the sole at the waist; the top edge off the ankle at the back inside; perhaps 5 to 10 per cent long.

Right: the type and period; a sensible sole and low heel block; the lacing over the instep with four pairs; the top edge below the ankle bone following the leg; clean in every pose, the heel height and the way the shoe meets the floor believable in motion. "Fix the heel cup and slim and round the front of the foot, and this is close."

Set aside under the two-tries rule (three reviews, the last after research). The best candidate is kept in F:/LedgerTools/tmp/clothes/feet/sh19.
