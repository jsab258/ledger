Obvious faults:

2. The wrist pushing through both cuffs arms down and walking: a V-shaped notch up the inner side of each sleeve end; the sleeve ends wide with a thin rolled edge (the concept: deep ribbed cuffs snug at the wrist, the sleeve blousing above).

Narrow points: the collar a low band at the side and back, standing only at the front (the concept: a stand collar all round); the teal a stripe on the upper arm, the forearm all purple (the concept: teal down most of the sleeve); the hem at the low hip, hiding the belt and pager (the concept: ending at the belt); the rest renders showing a second band and a tab at the side seam; the zip stopping above the band; the T-shirt with no crew neck, close to the body's grey; a dimple at the upper back, dents at the hip walking; the hem not lifting with the arms raised.

Right: a loose, blousy nylon top on a thin frame gathered at the hem, the shell-suit shape; raglan teal upper sleeves on a purple body; the zip open to mid-chest over the T-shirt at the concept's depth and width; the slanted pockets; arms raised and walking with no tearing or spikes, the sleeves not riding up.

## Set aside (the two-tries rule)

Two failed reviews, the research, and a third failure: set aside, 30 September. The research's method fixed the collar, zip and panel edges (none of them obvious faults now); what fails is the sitting pose and the wrists in the skinned test, as on every garment before it.
