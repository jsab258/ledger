# Blind review 3 (29 September, night): FAIL. Set aside.

A third fresh reviewer judged the final attempt, made after the research (production/research/clothing-pipeline/FIT-AND-STIFFNESS-2026-09-29.md): the jacket settled and carried at melton's true weight (it had weighed 84 kg in the simulation), the body pieces without the arms' skin weights, the simulation mesh cut from the pattern and welded seam by seam, the yoke one piece of the jacket's own surface, the back 2 cm shorter above the armhole.

Obvious faults:

1. Arms raised: the cloth tears at the armpit and side (stretched streaks, a hole, fragments behind the shoulder), a white fleck of the body through the left chest.
2. The side seams gathered like shirring with the arms down (the idle pose), walking and sitting; the sitting front crinkled like thin cotton. The reference: melton hanging flat from the shoulders with a few broad creases.
3. The back: below the yoke a large horizontal sagging fold across it, like a blouson; the back yoke's left edge ragged; its lower edge stepping down part way across.
4. A horizontal gathered ridge from armpit to armpit under the yoke, the cloth above it moulding over the chest.

Narrow points: the front yoke notched at the collar, its edge sloping; the collar a shirt's, long soft points; the buttons small, the lowest gap larger; the pockets taller than wide and out towards the sides; the back hem standing off behind; arms raised the hem barely lifts; sitting, the back a crumpled bag (no chair); a small line across the front at mid-chest.

Right: navy-black with a black yoke front and back, the back yoke to about armpit level; the length; boxy and unshaped from the front, square corners, no vent; dark buttons to the neck, stopping above the pockets; two flapless patch pockets low; sleeves to the wrist in every pose, set-in and fairly straight; a pointed turn-down collar, no lapels; no quilted puffiness; no flare walking.

## Set aside (29 September, the two-tries rule)

Two failed reviews, the research, and a third failure: the jacket is set aside and the clothing list moves on (CLOTHES.md). Jafar's ruling of 29 September then asks whether Marvelous Designer can be driven entirely by its own scripting: partly, not entirely (production/research/clothing-pipeline/MD-SCRIPTING-2026-09-29.md: no command-line or headless mode, a click from a person each time it starts, online and signed in; US$39 a month, a 14-day free trial). That goes to him as a decision in the day's summary.

What the attempt leaves that works, for the garments after it: the measuring fixed (the armpit, shoulder to shoulder round the back, the seat, the crotch, the trousers' measures); the pattern placed, sewn with unlimited force and welded (seams of 10 to 25 cm closed in 20 frames); the cloth at its true weight; the arms brought down on the body's own skeleton (tailor.unpose); pressing; the game mesh cut from the pattern and welded seam by seam; the finishing by the pattern's own coordinates; the four-pose test on the game mesh. What it does not solve: how a set-in sleeve's armpit behaves when the arms move (the gathering at the side seams, tearing with the arms raised), and a back that bags below the yoke.
