# Ron's donkey jacket, sewn from a pattern round his body (the clothing session, from 29 September)

CLOTHES.md item 2, **set aside 29 September** under the two-tries rule: three blind reviews failed it (review-1.md to review-3.md), the third after research. The builder's pattern route (../donkey-jacket-pattern) made a clean body but lost the sleeves three ways; this carries on its one-day attempt (Jafar, 29 September: drape with the arms held out, then pose; research first).

## What was wrong, and what changed

- **The pattern was cut for the wrong body.** The armpit was taken 9 cm under the arm's joint in MetaHuman's reference pose, whose arms are raised: 15 cm under Ron's shoulder, where it is 22 (a ray from the torso out towards the arm, the highest level where it meets air before the arm). Brian then drew a 17 cm armhole whose foot sat inside the root of his arm, and to match it a sleeve 54 cm wide with a 4 cm cap. Shoulder to shoulder is now taken round the back as FreeSewing takes it (520 mm, not 446 straight across). tools/meshgen/blender/body_measurements.py; ron-measurements.json.
- **The draft** (brian-ron.json, pattern.png; the last one, after the research, with the back 20 mm shorter above its armhole: tools/meshgen/alter_brian.py --back-tuck 20; 30 collapsed a shoulder): Brian at Ron's measurements (biceps 442, chest 1175, hpsToBust 375, hpsToWaistBack 471, neck 442, shoulderToShoulder 520, shoulderSlope 5, waistToArmpit 251, waistToHips 120, shoulderToWrist 636, wrist 217), chestEase 20%, lengthBonus 55%, bicepsEase 12%, cuffEase 60%, armholeDepth 6%. The shoulder slope is 5 degrees, his shoulders in the pose the garment is exported in (13, a man standing, put the shoulder seam 3 to 4 cm under his raised shoulders). The armhole 25 cm under the shoulder's high point, 3 cm under his armpit; the sleeve 52 cm round with a 12.4 cm cap, a work jacket's; the back 92 cm from the shoulder's high point (at 86 the hem came out at his crotch: the raised shoulders take up length).
- **The sewing** (tools/meshgen/blender/sew_donkey.py, tailor.py, brian.py), after the research (production/research/clothing-pipeline/SLEEVES-RECIPE-2026-09-29.md) and twelve runs:
  - the body pieces laid round the posed body, arms held 40 degrees below the horizontal (his rest pose is 52.6): each column of a piece at its pattern height, as far out as the torso reaches there or anywhere above (a curtain from the shoulders), the arms left through the armholes; the full body with the head, so nothing reads as inside it at the open neck;
  - the sleeves as unstretched tubes hanging on the top of each arm;
  - every piece remembers the shape it was laid in (its lengths within 7% of the pattern's);
  - sewn weightless with the sewing force as good as unlimited (the builder's cap of 10 stopped pulling once a gap passed a few millimetres), slippery on the body: the shoulders close from 25 cm and the armholes from 10 to 14 cm within 20 frames;
  - the seams welded, then settled under gravity, the cloth remembering the pattern's own lengths (tailor.pattern_rest: once welded it would otherwise remember every crinkle of the sewing);
  - the arms brought back to the rest pose by skin, not by shoving: the jacket takes the posed body's skin weights and a second skeleton, whose rest is the sewing pose, carries it down, the cloth on top settling it (run 14: pushed down by the arms alone, the stiff sleeves levered the whole jacket up to his crotch and the body came through at an armpit);
  - pressed (press_garment.py, tailor.press): the sleeves come out of the sewing crinkled all over, their caps stretched about 40% to reach the armholes, and neither the pattern's lengths nor bending eight times as stiff settled them out; each round moves every point a little towards its neighbours, draws each edge back to its pattern length, and keeps everything 4 mm off the body.
- **What did not work**, in order: the pieces remembering a straight wrap (the fold over the shoulders fought it and the jacket slid down to his chest, weightless, run 3); the sleeves remembering their tube while blended into the armholes (pulled 6 cm back off them, run 4); the sleeves relaxed with their tops held (still stretched by half, run 6); true lengths with low bending (the right sleeve fell in at the armpit, run 9); a tube moved whole onto its armhole (pushed into the arm, run 10). The armhole wraps the arm's root at an angle, its top over the shoulder 7 cm inward of where a tube round the arm begins: no unstretched tube meets it; sewing from a gap does.

## After the reviews (runs 15 to 19)

- **The arms down by the body's own skeleton** (tailor.unpose): the jacket takes the posed body's skin weights and is carried back to the rest pose by inverting them, then the Armature modifier and the cloth together. A second skeleton "applied as rest" silently did nothing in a background Blender.
- **The cloth's true weight** (tailor.mass_per_point): Blender's mass is per point; at 0.006 kg a point the jacket weighed 84 kg. Now melton's 0.7 kg/m2, about 1.3 kg in all, settled and carried at that weight, bending 10.
- **Torso weights** (tailor.torso_weights): the body pieces keep no arm weights except within 6 cm of the sleeves, smoothed, so a raised arm no longer drags the side into a sail.
- **Pressed and spanned** (press_garment.py, tailor.span_hollows): the hollows between chest and arm, and under the shoulder blades, bridged by the hull's straight line, bounded (at most 4 cm, below 17 cm under the neck) and smoothed.
- **Finished** (finish_donkey.py): the yoke one piece of the jacket's own surface where the pattern says yoke, starting 60 mm off the centre front, set 2.5 mm proud and 1.5 mm thick; the front lap 40 mm, flush at the centre line; four buttons; two 190 by 200 mm patch pockets; a collar from the neckline. The game mesh cut from the pattern at 25 mm and welded seam by seam: 3,195 points, one closed piece. The render mesh is far too heavy for a game (521,000 triangles): the subdivision would come off before any handover.
- **Pose tests** (pose_test.py): the game mesh as cloth, the render mesh following it, arms down, raised, walking and sitting, the sleeves and body held towards their skinned places.
- Review 3's faults (review-3.md): the armpit tears with the arms raised; the side seams gather like shirring in every pose; the back bags below the yoke; a ridge across the chest under it. Unsolved: how a set-in sleeve's armpit moves with the arm.
