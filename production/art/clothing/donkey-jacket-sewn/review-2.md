# Blind review 2 (29 September, evening): FAIL

A second fresh reviewer judged the jacket after review 1's fixes (the hollows spanned, the sleeves pressed again, a broader collar, a deeper front yoke, larger buttons) against the same references, the V&A's photograph seen.

Obvious faults:

1. Spikes and tears at the armholes in every pose (a thin spike out of the left armhole arms down and sitting; the underarm a comb of shards arms raised).
2. The yoke torn at both shoulders: the front panels' outer ends jagged, a navy sliver over them.
3. The yoke not reading as going over the shoulders: navy on top from collar to sleeve, black only as a chest patch and a back strip.
4. The back broken under the yoke: ragged side edges, a notch at the centre, a thick horizontal roll of wool below it.
5. Body showing through at the armhole in the walk.
6. Soft, clinging cloth: the front wraps the chest and belly with horizontal ridges; a crease under the chest suggesting a bust.

Narrow points: the collar's points long and narrow, a shirt's; the raised strip down the front reading as a placket; a seam down the middle of the back yoke; the back standing off like a tent; arms raised, the body hangs like a sail; the upper sleeves crumpled; small dark slits on the sleeve caps; the hand catching the hem walking.

Right: the colours; the length, about 87 cm; boxy, straight, no waist, straight hem, square corners, no vent; four dark buttons about 16 cm apart to the neck; two large flapless patch pockets low; the front yoke's depth and straight lower edge; the back yoke to about armpit level; set-in straight sleeves to the wrist; a pointed collar, no lapels; sitting, the front drapes over the thighs.

## What followed

Two failed reviews, so the two-tries rule: research first (production/research/clothing-pipeline/FIT-AND-STIFFNESS-2026-09-29.md), then one more attempt, and if that fails the jacket is set aside.

- My own faults, fixed without research: the game mesh had small holes at the armpits where sliver faces were dropped, and the render mesh tore at them (faults 1 and 5): it is now cut from the pattern at 25 mm and welded seam by seam as the drape is, one manifold piece of even triangles. The yoke was four panels laid through the pattern (faults 2, 3 and the back's ragged edges): it is now the jacket's own surface where the pattern says yoke, front and back joined over the shoulder as one piece, its outline straightened, set a constant 2.5 mm off the wool. The overlapping front now lies flush at the centre line.
- The research's first finding: Blender's cloth mass is per point, and at 0.006 kg a point the simulated jacket weighed 84 kg (1.81 m² of melton weighs about 1.3 kg); that fits the clinging, the cape, the sail and the sag (fault 6 and the narrow points). The jacket now settles and is carried at melton's true 0.7 kg/m², with a bending that keeps a stiff line at that weight; the body pieces carry no arm weights except near the sleeves, so raising an arm no longer drags the side into a sail. The hem, measured, was level within 2 cm, so the pattern's balance stands.
