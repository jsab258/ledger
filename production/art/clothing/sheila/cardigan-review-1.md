# Sheila's cardigan, blind review 1 (29 September, late): FAIL

A fresh reviewer judged the first attempt against her approved concept (production/casting/sheila-dunn/full.jpg) and her casting sheet.

Obvious faults:

1. The hem the wrong shape and too short: a waistcoat's or shirt-tail's, up to the waist at the sides, dipping to points front and back; the skirt's waist showing at both hips (the concept: a straight, level hem at the hip, a deep ribbed welt over the skirt's band).
2. Not hanging like a knit: shaped in at the waist, tight to the ribs, a bodice (the concept: loose and boxy, straight from the bust).
3. The neckline a wide boat or off-the-shoulder scoop, a white band across the shoulders and back (the concept: hugging the base of the neck, the V from the side of the neck, the back neck high with a rib band).
4. No blouse collar: a flat, jagged white strip, notched flaps at the back, a split, a stray tab (the concept: a small round collar, two rounded points over the cardigan's neck at the front).
5. The bands wrong: each side of the V a stiff plank with a square top, no back-neck band, a step where they meet the button band (the concept: one band from the hem up both fronts and round the neck).
6. No ribbed welt or cuffs as shapes; the cuffs ragged.
7. Holes in the poses: white at both hips below the hem (the hidden body reaching past the cardigan), at the shoulder by the neck, between cuff and hand; seated, a void at the back.

Narrow points: the third button half sunk; a lumpy crease under the bust, ragged side edges; the body not lifting with the arms raised (acceptable skinned); the V a little deep and narrow (the concept's ends at the first button, mid-chest); the sleeve head dropped, following the wide neck.

Right: the beige and the cream in the V; long sleeves to the wrist; single-breasted, seven buttons evenly down a centre band; no body through at the chest, back or arms; the sleeves following the elbows walking and sitting without tearing.

## What followed (the second attempt)

The first attempt took her skin by the bone that moves it, which left out the neck (the boat neck, the shoulder holes) and the hips (the shirt-tail hem). The second takes the neck and the hips as well; hangs straight from the bust and the shoulder blades all round, with more ease; a level hem at the hip with a 5 cm welt drawn in and ribbed, and ribbed cuffs; one band from the hem up both fronts, round the neck and down again; the collar laid on the knit by projection, smoothed, its fronts rounded at the throat; the V at mid-chest; the body hidden only well inside the cardigan's edges.
