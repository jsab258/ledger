1. live talk too slow;
2. the look;
3. clothing;
4. animation without NoAI assets;
5. the way of working on one PC and a weekly allowance?

## The answers in short

1. **Live talk.** Nothing found reaches two seconds under his rulings: no cloud voice, no third attempt at the voice, no prepared openings.
   - **The free voice sets the first sound today, not the check.** On 7 October it took 2.2 to 4.4 seconds to make its first piece while the check ran, and the first sounds came at 3.6 to 5.7 seconds [I, from LEDGER's own measurements].
   - **New voice models that talk and listen at once** all speak before anything is checked, and are cloud services or research-only.
   - **Small fast checkers exist,** but are less exact than the slow ones [SHOWN, paper]. They would matter only with a faster voice.
   - **Choosing the facts before writing** was already built and measured here on 30 September: no fewer "that's all I know", a quarter of a second slower.
   - **One new risk:** canon forbids real song lyrics, and nothing in the talk program says so (proof 4).
2. **The look.** Nothing new makes it.
   - **Generated worlds** give video or clouds of soft points ("splats"), not buildings, and their licences exclude Europe or are research-only.
   - **Picture-to-3D** waits by his 22 September ruling, needs NVIDIA cards, and its licences raise questions.
   - **"Photoreal" filters** do not run on this card, and training one on Unreal's frames is barred by the Unreal licence.
   - **What can help is the gate.** Studies this period report that AI judges spot faults less reliably than they confirm what is right, and that comparing with the last good frame helps [ABS]. Nobody has measured LEDGER's own reviewer (proof 1).
3. **Clothing.** **No tool or paper shows a tailored jacket with a notched lapel.** The research field's common base cannot express a jacket's front opening [SHOWN]. His ruling to wait "until the tools catch up" stands; they have not.
4. **Animation.** His rulings name the sources: MetaHuman's own clips and Mixamo, with sitting from the Mixamo harvest. The method was set on 6 October. Two free sources LEDGER has not tried would matter only if those clips fail the floor:
   - **Epic's capture from one phone camera** (inside Unreal 5.8). Its Fab NoAI flag is unread, and this card is below Epic's recommended one.
   - **NVIDIA's Kimodo,** trained on licensed studio capture. It was tested mainly on NVIDIA cards, and its weights' licence was unreached.
5. **The way of working.**
   - **Epic now ships a connection that lets Claude work inside the Unreal editor:** lights, materials, screenshots through the game camera. Untried here (proof 5).
   - **The nightly tester's "what broke the illusion" line has had no pictures to look at** on any night from 4 to 7 October (proof 2).
   - **Much of the town's newer code is not exercised by its comparison tests.** The independent review of 1 October broke it eleven times, one line each, and the tests still passed. A method LEDGER already uses for the gossip code caught all 37 faults planted there (proof 3).
6. **Decompiling, mods and mashups.**
   - Wave Race 64 rebuilt for PC with Claude Code, a Fable 2 port and Universal Modder's plugin were read in their own code and notes [SHOWN]; none was run.
   - The wider wave of games inside other games is press and clips only [SS].
   - **None of it is a route for LEDGER** (below). One method carries over without touching anyone's game: proving a screen capture is live before trusting it.

## The legal position, plainly

**Not legal advice. Every statute and court site was unreached** (3-LEGAL.md).
- **Allowed:** playing other games, watching them, measuring, screenshots for notes, and reading what their makers published. In Europe a lawful user may also observe and test a game while it runs to find the ideas behind it. Ideas, mechanics and genres are free.
- **Not allowed:** decompiling, unpacking or ripping another game to learn how it was made.
  - Switzerland, the UK and the EU allow decompiling only for narrow purposes, such as making programs work together.
  - The US has allowed it as fair use where there was no other way, but enforces game licences that forbid it [SS].
  - Their code, art, models, animations, sounds, text and a confusingly close look are protected.
  - Feeding their material to an AI to "rewrite" it is not a clean room.
- **Patents are the exception to "ideas are free".** Warner Bros' Nemesis patent covers characters who remember the player, in a combat setting [SS]. LEDGER's gossiping town is in the same broad territory; only a patent attorney can say how close.
- **Three risks that apply to LEDGER:**
  - **Song lyrics.** A Munich court reportedly found lyrics reproduced by a chatbot infringing [SS]. Proof 4 tests whether live talk keeps to canon.
  - **The AI notice.** The EU's AI law reportedly requires telling players they talk to an AI [SS]; Anthropic's policy asks for it at the start of each session [SHOWN]. His ruling already puts a notice before the first talk.
  - **The title.** "LEDGER" is also the name of a well-known hardware-wallet company [I].
