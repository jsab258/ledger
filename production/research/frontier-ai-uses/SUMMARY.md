# Frontier AI uses, July to October 2026: what is new, and what it means for LEDGER (7 October 2026)

**The question.** What is new in how people use frontier models (Claude Opus 5.5, OpenAI's and Google's latest, the best open models), and does any of it help LEDGER's five problems: live talk too slow, the look, clothing, animation without NoAI assets, and the way of working on one PC and a weekly allowance?

**What was done.** This cloud session read CLAUDE.md, CHARTER.md, PLAN.md, the pre-production risks and the asset plan's summary. Seven read-only helpers each took one strand for about thirty minutes, given the problem and not a theory (notes/). The deciding facts were then re-read here at their source (SOURCES.md). Nothing was run. No code or rule was changed.

**What could not be reached.** OpenAI, Google and DeepMind, Hugging Face, NVIDIA, Fab, unrealengine.com, Reddit, YouTube, X, the games press, Steamworks and every legislation and court site refused this session. **Nothing is concluded from them.** Anything only a search summary reported is a lead, never evidence.

## The answers in short

1. **Live talk (problem 1).** One new route attacks the floor itself: the writer names the facts it will use before the line, and a small local checker clears the first sentence against only those facts in about a tenth of a second.
   - The parts exist and were read: Claude's fixed-schema structured outputs; a published checker at 0.11–0.13 s (Enoki, hardware unstated); MIT-licensed small detectors (LettuceDetect, TinyLettuce).
   - **Arithmetic on LEDGER's own 7 October measurements, not a run:** "cleared to speak" would move from 2.96 s to about 1.45 s. First sound would be about 1.65 s with a paid streaming cloud voice, and still above 2 s with today's free voice.
   - Every new speech-to-speech and full-duplex system speaks first and checks later. None fits a design that checks before a line is heard.
   - **No shipped game with live AI characters was found with measured reply times or a check before speech.**
2. **The look (problem 2).** Nothing new makes it.
   - World models give video or splats, not geometry, and their licences exclude the EU and UK or are research-only.
   - Image-to-3D needs NVIDIA cards with 24 GB or more, and its licences or training data fail the rules.
   - Neural "photoreal" filters do not run on the RX 6700.
   - **What does help is the gate:** studies this period report that model judges miss most planted faults (6.6–42.3% found, against 83.4% for people) and lean towards passing work, and that comparing with the last clean frame helps [ABS: abstracts only].
3. **Clothing (problem 3).** **No tool or paper shows a tailored jacket with a notched lapel**, in research or for sale. The research field's common base cannot express a front opening. His 3 October ruling to wait "until the tools catch up" stands; they have not.
4. **Animation (problem 4).** Two new free routes with no dataset problem:
   - Epic's own markerless motion capture from one phone camera, inside Unreal 5.8, processed on the PC. **Its Fab NoAI flag is unread**, and the RX 6700 is below Epic's recommended card.
   - NVIDIA's Kimodo with its "RP" weights, trained on 700 hours of licensed studio motion capture. Code Apache-2.0; the weights' licence text was unreached. Its SMPL-X version is non-commercial and stays out.
5. **The way of working (problem 5).**
   - Epic now ships an MCP server inside the Unreal 5.8 editor and a Claude Code plugin (MIT) [SHOWN: Epic's documentation and the plugin's licence, read here]. The same server can run inside a cooked build, so the nightly tester could read the town's memory instead of judging video.
   - Agents writing C++ beat agents wiring Blueprints by 30–43 points [ABS].
   - **Code that passes every test still behaved differently under random inputs** in this period's decompilation research [ABS]. LEDGER's own review of 1 October found 12 one-line faults its golden table passed.
6. **Decompiling, mods and mashups.** Wave Race 64 recompiled to PC by Claude Code, a Fable 2 port and Universal Modder's plugin were read in their own code and notes [SHOWN]; none was run. The wider mashup wave is press and clips only [SS]. **None of it is a route for LEDGER in any form** (below). Two of its methods carry over without touching anyone's game: a live check that the screen capture is not frozen, and stopping after about three identical failures.

## The legal position, plainly

**Not legal advice; every statute and court site was unreached** (3-LEGAL.md).
- **Allowed:** playing other games, watching, measuring, screenshots for notes, and reading what their makers published. Ideas, mechanics and genres are free.
- **Not allowed:** decompiling, unpacking or ripping another game to learn how it was made. Switzerland, the UK and the EU allow it only for narrow purposes such as making programs work together, and learning methods is not one of them. The US has allowed copying for compatibility as fair use, but lets a game's licence forbid even that [SS]. Their code, art, models, animations, sounds, text and a confusingly close look are protected. Feeding their material to an AI to "rewrite" it is not a clean room.
- **Patents are the exception to "ideas are free".** Warner Bros' Nemesis patent covers characters who remember the player, in a combat setting. LEDGER's gossiping town is in the same broad territory; only a patent attorney can say how close.
- **Three live risks for LEDGER:**
  - characters quoting song lyrics: a Munich court reportedly found lyrics reproduced by a chatbot infringing [SS];
  - telling players they talk to an AI: the EU AI Act's rule reportedly applies from 2 August 2026 [SS], and Anthropic's policy asks for it at the start of each session [SHOWN]. The game already shows a notice before each launch's first conversation (read in its code);
  - the title "LEDGER", which a well-known hardware-wallet company also uses [I].

None is on the plan. Each would join the builder's list only by his order at a stated place. **Not worth a proof now:** world models, image-to-3D, neural filters, full-duplex speech, AI tailoring, and anything that takes from another game.

## What needs him (money, licences or scope)

1. **The 2 s goal needs a paid streaming cloud voice** whatever the check does. That decision is already on his page; this research adds only that a faster check would make it sufficient (about 1.65 s).
2. **Authored lines picked live** (a September paper: 0.38 s against 2.6 s, preferred by listeners 63% to 12% [SHOWN, paper]) may count as the "prepared openings" he ruled out on 7 October. Recommended: keep his ruling; nothing here argues it should change.
3. **One review by a Swiss IP lawyer before release**: the title search, the Nemesis patent's claims, the store and AI notices. Money, so his.
