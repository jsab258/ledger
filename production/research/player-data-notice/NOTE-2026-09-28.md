# Players' words: what the notice before the first conversation should say (research, 28 September 2026)

The question: what one short line added to the notice must or should tell players about where their typed words go and what is kept, and whether each statement in it is true today. This is not legal advice. Sources were opened on 28 September 2026 unless marked otherwise. "No date" means the page shows none.

## 1. Anthropic, commercial API (primary sources)
- Retention: "For Anthropic API users, we automatically delete inputs and outputs on our backend within 30 days of receipt or generation". The exceptions are features the customer keeps data in (the Files API), custom agreements (zero retention), enforcing the Usage Policy, and the law. Anything flagged by the automated trust and safety systems is kept longer: "inputs and outputs for up to 2 years and trust and safety classification scores for up to 7 years". Source: Privacy Center, "How long do you store my organization's data?", updated 1 July 2026. https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data
- Training: "By default, we will not use your inputs or outputs from our commercial products ... to train our models." The exception is when the customer explicitly sends feedback (thumbs, bug reports) or otherwise chooses to allow it; that data is kept for 5 years. Source: Privacy Center, "Is my data used for model training?", updated 18 August 2026. https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996868-is-my-data-used-for-model-training
- The developer docs say "Retained data is never used for model training without your express permission". They also say conversation content "is not retained by default", except on Covered Models, and "if a chat or session is flagged, Anthropic may retain inputs and outputs for up to 2 years" even under zero retention. Source: platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention (no date).
- Covered Models: since 9 June 2026, Claude Fable 5 and 5.1 and Mythos 5 and 5.1 keep prompts and outputs for 30 days for safety work, and this overrides zero retention. Source: Privacy Center article 15425996. The game uses claude-sonnet-5 and claude-haiku-4-5 (LlmClient.cs), so it is not affected.
- Where the data is: "Currently, "us" is the only available workspace geo" (storage at rest). Inference defaults to "global", meaning any geography. Source: platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency (no date).
- Transfer basis: "Anthropic's DPA with Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) is automatically incorporated into our Commercial Terms of Service", and it covers the Claude API. Source: Privacy Center, DPA article, updated 16 March 2026.

## 2. The law
- GDPR Art. 13(1)-(2), with the same article in UK GDPR, requires the following "at the time when personal data are obtained": who the controller is and how to reach them (and any EU or UK representative); the purposes and legal basis; the recipients, named or by category; any transfer abroad and its safeguard; how long data is kept; the person's rights; and the right to complain. Art. 12(1) requires "clear and plain language". Source: Regulation (EU) 2016/679.
- A layered notice is allowed. Source: WP29 Transparency Guidelines, WP260 rev.01 (11 April 2018, endorsed by the EDPB). Para. 36: the first layer should give the purposes, the controller's identity and the rights, plus the processing with "the most impact" and processing "which could surprise them". The rest can sit in a linked notice, and the layers must not conflict (para. 35). Para. 39 endorses just-in-time notices.
- Recipients: processors count as recipients, and "In practice, this will generally be the named recipients" (WP260, Annex, Art. 13(1)(e)). CJEU C-154/21, Österreichische Post (12 January 2023): on an access request, the actual recipients must be named. So name Anthropic.
- The ICO's page "What privacy information should we provide?" (no date) gives the same list. Recipients may be named or given by category, but "as specific as possible".
- Swiss FADP Art. 19 (in force 1 September 2023). The minimum at collection is the controller's identity and contact details, the purpose, and the recipients. Art. 19(4) adds that for data sent abroad, the notice must name "the State or the international body" and the safeguard. This is stricter than the GDPR: it means naming the USA, and Hetzner's country. Art. 60: a wilful failure carries a fine of up to CHF 250,000 on the person responsible. Sources: swissrights.ch Art. 19 text; FDPIC, "Duty to provide information" (no date).
- EU AI Act Art. 50(1): people must be told they are talking to an AI. This has applied since 2 August 2026 and was untouched by the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744). Source: secondary, aiactblog.nl and usercentrics, 2026. The existing notice already meets it.

## 3. Steam
- The Content Survey (partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey, no date) says Live-Generated AI must be disclosed along with its guardrails against illegal content. It says nothing about player data or privacy.
- The Steam Web API Terms (July 2010) apply only if the relay uses Steam IDs or Steam data. They say "You will post a privacy policy regarding the use of nonpublic end user data" and require storing that data in the countries the policy names.
- I found no Valve rule that requires an in-game privacy line. The note of 24 September agrees (runtime-ai-business/notes/stores.md).

## Is it true today? (checked in the repository)
- The relay's log holds sizes, costs and the copy's hash, and no words (ledger/Relay self-test). True.
- A report is kept with the first 8 characters of the copy's hash and a timestamp, and nothing ever deletes it. So the current notice's "and nothing else does" is slightly off, and no keeping period can be promised yet.
- The current notice's "nothing else does" can also be read as "my typing goes nowhere". In fact every line passes through the relay and on to Anthropic. The new line corrects this.
- The relay is not hosted yet (decided 28 September: Hetzner once friends test). The line becomes true when the game talks through it. Until then only Jafar plays.

## Suggested line (to go before the report sentence)
"What you type is sent through our server to Anthropic, the US company whose Claude model writes the replies. Our server keeps none of your words unless you report a line, and Anthropic deletes them within 30 days (longer only if flagged for misuse or required by law) and does not train its models on them; who we are, your rights and the rest are in the privacy notice [link]."
Also change the report sentence's ending to "...and your note are kept for the developers, with your copy's code." The old ending was "and nothing else does".

## The fuller privacy notice (linked from the line, and from the store page)
- Who the controller is: Jafar or his trading name, with a postal address and an email. Also the EU and UK representatives, if they are needed.
- What is handled. Typed words and the character's context, in transit only. A per-call log: time, sizes, cost, hashed copy code. Reports: the line, the player's line before it, the note, the copy code and the time. The IP address the server sees, and whether the host logs it.
- Purposes and legal basis. Writing the replies: contract, Art. 6(1)(b). Spending limits and abuse checks: legitimate interests. Reports: legitimate interests (moderation, and Steam's guardrail duty).
- Recipients by name: Anthropic PBC (processor, USA) and Hetzner Online GmbH (host; state the country). Nobody else, no sale, no advertising.
- Transfers: the USA, under Anthropic's DPA with SCCs. Add the UK and Swiss addenda once verified.
- How long each thing is kept. Our log: X days. Reports: X months (a period must be set first). Anthropic: 30 days, or 2 years if flagged, and safety scores for 7 years.
- No training on the data. No automated decisions with legal effect.
- Rights: access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection and portability, and how to use them with only a copy code. Complaints go to the FDPIC, the ICO, or the authority in the player's own EU country.
- A plea not to type real personal details. The notice's date, which must be kept in step with the in-game line.

## Uncertain
- Whether a Swiss one-person seller needs EU and UK representatives (Art. 27). The "occasional" exemption looks unlikely to fit continuous chat. This costs money, so it is a decision for Jafar, or a question for a lawyer.
- Anthropic's Data Privacy Framework status: secondary sources conflict (compound.law, 2026). The SCCs suffice either way.
- The docs say nothing is retained by default, while the Privacy Center says "within 30 days". The line uses 30 days, which is the safe upper bound.
- Whether the hashed copy code is personal data. CJEU C-413/23 P, EDPS v SRB (4 September 2025), makes pseudonymous data depend on who can re-identify it. We can link a code to a buyer, so treat it as personal data.
- The legal basis for reports (consent or legitimate interests).
- The line stays true only while four things hold. The model is not a Covered Model, or the 30-day figure still holds. The relay stays wordless. Player conversations are never sent to Anthropic as feedback. Jafar joins no data-sharing programme.
