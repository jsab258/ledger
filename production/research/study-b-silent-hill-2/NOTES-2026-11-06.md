# Study B: Silent Hill 2 (2024 remake), research notes

6 November 2026. Public sources only. The game's files were not opened. All links read 6 November 2026. "Unverified": no primary source read confirms it. "(inference)": this note's own reasoning. The brief (order of 2 November) names study B without questions, so the four given questions are used.

## Questions

1. How does the remake achieve its streets' look in fog: Lumen settings, fog, wet surfaces, decals, density of props?
2. Its characters' clothes and faces: pipeline, cloth or skinned, close-up quality.
3. Frame budgets and known performance problems, including stutter, and what caused them.
4. What of its method fits a small team with one street, and what does not?

## Summary of reach

No developer talk (GDC, Unreal Fest, Epic case study, Bloober or Konami technical post) was found by two searches. Digital Foundry's own article (Eurogamer) could not be fetched. ## 1. Fog, Lumen, wet surfaces, decals, props

- Engine: Unreal Engine 5; Lumen and Nanite named by the developer at announcement. PlayStation Blog (Konami producer Okamoto, Bloober creative director Lenart), 19 Oct 2022: Lumen is "a fully dynamic global illumination solution that immediately reacts to scene and light changes"; Nanite is "an amazing tool for level designers"; the team says both raise the graphics "while intensifying the series' unsettling atmosphere" [1]. This is marketing-level text; no settings.
- Engine version 5.1 is claimed by one PC review (Neowin) seen only in a search summary; its page returned 403. Unverified.
- Hardware ray tracing is optional on PC, switched on with one click (via a secondary summary of Digital Foundry [2]). Ray tracing used for bounce lighting and reflections, most visible in puddles and water (GamingBolt [3]). PS5 uses Lumen with a software approximation of ray tracing in both modes (search summary of GamingBolt; the fetched article did not state it; unverified).
- Fog as design: Bloober used "light, darkness, objects and fog" to hide enemies and keep dread once the fixed camera was gone (GamingBible interview, Aug 2024 [4]). Fog is a design tool for hiding distance, not a stated performance trick; whether it also saves cost is not stated anywhere read (inference only).
- Smoke volumes and volumetric light shafts are prominent (GamingBolt [3]).
- Fan fog-removal mods exist ("Sunny Hills", an HD Collection fog mod) showing streets and shops that the fog hides (80.lv [5]). That page gives no technical detail on how the fog is built.
- Lumen settings (scene quality, final gather, reflection mode), the fog type (exponential height fog, volumetric fog, fog cards), the wet-surface method (clear coat, decals, screen-space or Lumen reflections) and prop density: not published in anything read. Unverified.

## 2. Clothes and faces

- GamingBolt graphics analysis (undated on the page; read 6 Nov) [3]: high polycount models with good skin meshes; hair with fine strands, physics-driven movement, few artifacts; clothing uses physically based materials with good texture and geometric detail; depth of field in Angela's close-up shows no artifacts along clothing and hair. The author finds the cleaned-up faces lose some charm of the original, and soft skin makes Angela and Harry look "rather offputting". Taste statements, not pipeline facts.
- Patch 1.04 (21 Oct 2024) lists "Improved the deformation of Nurses' skirts" (The Sixth Axis [6]; same item in other outlets). So skirts deform by a simulation or a skinning fix; which is not stated. Unverified.
- Digital Foundry (via Wccftech [2]): frame rate drops to 30 FPS in cutscenes and "when viewing cloth objects". The wording is the summariser's; the original was not read. Unverified as to cause.
- Pipeline (skinned or simulated cloth, rig, facial system, scan or sculpt, texture sizes, strand-hair tool): not published in anything read. Unverified.

## 3. Frame budgets and performance

- Digital Foundry, October 2024, as summarised by Wccftech [2]: traversal stutter on PC, from loading and unloading objects as the player moves; no shader compilation stutter found; described as an engine-wide Unreal pattern that Epic has not fully resolved, even in Fortnite; hardware Lumen "not well refined"; grass textures and reflections flicker; DLSS supported; PC visuals scale beyond PS5. Read only through a third party; DF's own numbers (resolutions, frame times, hardware) not reached.
- Conflicting claims: Tom's Guide lists shader stutter as a PC con (search summary only; page text did not load [7]); DSOGaming (403) said menus were fine once shaders compiled (search summary, unverified). The sources disagree with DF on shader stutter; not resolved here.
- TechPowerUp handheld review: traversal stutter not very noticeable on high-end PC, noticeable on Steam Deck and ROG Ally outdoors (search summary only; page 403). Unverified.
- Patch 1.04, 21 Oct 2024 (The Sixth Axis [6]; MP1st [8]):
  - Fixed stuttering tied to sky map generation.
  - New toggle for HZB culling, "may reduce stuttering on some AMD and Intel GPUs". Bloober did not say which setting helps (search summary of a secondary outlet; unverified).
  - Intel Nanite support updated; Steam Deck performance improved; DLSS frame generation and Reflex; FSR 3.1.1 and Fluid Motion Frames; graphics settings save locally.
  - Streaming fix: looking at the Grand Market's inside walls stopped the surroundings loading (The Sixth Axis [6]).
  - PS5: crash fixes in AI, texture binding, streaming, audio portal [8].
  - MP1st notes players report a new "Scene Occlusion" setting not in the official notes. Unverified.
- Cause list from patch notes only: sky map generation, a culling path (HZB) on AMD and Intel, object streaming. Root causes beyond those: not stated.
- Frame budget (target ms per pass, PS5 modes, PC resolution scale): not reached. Unverified.
- Later patches after 1.04: not read.

## 4. Fit for a small team with one street

Facts only; a fit is shown only where a read source supports it. Not a recommendation.

Fits:
- Fog and light as a way to hide what is not built: the developers used it for dread and concealment [4]; a one-street game also shows only what the camera is allowed to see (inference).
- Unreal's Lumen, Nanite and optional hardware ray tracing are stock features; the developer names them as its base [1]. Our project is on Unreal 5.8, so the same switches exist (inference).
- Reflections in puddles needed ray tracing to look precise [3]. Our wet street faces the same trade (inference; see the project's own record that the ground's water is a clear coat).
- Stutter cause class, streaming on the move [2], is a place a 30 m street avoids if all of it stays loaded (inference).

Does not fit or is unproven:
- Open streaming town with traversal loading [2][6]: a bounded corner does not stream the same way.
- Studio-scale character pipeline, strand hair, facial capture: not described by any source read, so nothing can be taken from it.
- Hardware Lumen "not well refined" with flicker [2]; our card is an AMD RX 6700 and the patch shows AMD/Intel culling stutter [6][8], so ray-traced settings are a risk to measure, not a method to copy (inference).
- Console-targeted modes (PS5 two modes) [3] do not apply to a PC-only game.
