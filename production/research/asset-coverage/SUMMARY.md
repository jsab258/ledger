# Nobody has ever drawn any of your characters

Research topic: an asset coverage audit. You asked what a finished game of this
shape is physically made of, and what we actually hold.

I listed 84 kinds of material a game like this contains, working backwards from
the same five games the systems audit used, and checked every one against the
repository. **11 exist, 47 are part-done, 26 are absent.** Nothing is
recommended out, because a game cannot skip its own materials. So the answer
you want is the absent list and what it costs.

Full working is in `DELIVERY.md` beside this.

## First, the host check you asked for

**All five of the newly opened hosts are still refused.** dev.epicgames.com,
docs.blender.org, huggingface.co, arxiv.org and datashare.ed.ac.uk all answer
403 at the gateway, each tested directly this session, and the proxy's own log
names all five with the same refusal. I retried one of them through the other
channel we have and it refused too. Nothing changed. It costs this delivery one
specific thing, in the last section below.

## The thing you suspected is worse than you suspected

**There is no character concept art. Not one image, on any branch.**

Beside that zero: 35 written cast cards, 60 generated residents, **17 characters
already cast with a chosen voice clip each**, and 8 concept images of places. So
we have decided how these people think, we have decided how seventeen of them
sound, and nobody has drawn a single face.

And it is not that it was deferred. The environment had a commission structure,
a five-station line and a review document. The cast had a voice pipeline and a
consent rule. **Neither structure has a drawing station in it, so nobody was
ever late.** The category was never named. That is the failure this audit was
meant to catch, and it caught the same one twice more: costume sheets, and body
language, which you yourself ruled was moat rather than polish on 14 September.

## On bodies and clothes, the real problem is not the clothes

You asked about the 18 Mixamo bodies in modern casual dress. They are a problem.
They are not the front line.

**Everybody in the town except the player is a box figure.** The code
instantiates one bought body, on the player, and gives the crowd a mannequin of
ten boxes and a sphere, because a proper body per person has never been costed
on a machine with no graphics card. The code says both will be live together for
a long time.

On the clothes themselves, the earlier clothing research already settled the
route: MetaHuman, free, already allowed, bodies and clothes arrive together. The
decision was taken eight days before you asked me this. The gap is that nobody
has run it once.

## One thing I found that breaks your own content rule

You ruled on 10 September that alcohol is out entirely, never shown or served,
and that tobacco stays. The animation library we have downloaded holds the
opposite of both:

- `Characters/C/drink__Drinking....fbx` is sitting there, live, and the picker's
  record says it was an exact match for a slot literally called `drink`.
- A `Bartending` clip is live too, filling the counter-work slot. That one is a
  judgement rather than a fact, since it could dress a shop counter.
- The smoking clip, which your rule keeps, is the one that got dropped.

The picker still has `drink` and `sit_drink` as slots it goes looking for.

The reason nobody caught it is worth more than the clips. Your content rule is
enforced at five places, and every one of them reads text: dialogue, barks,
character cards, the brand bible, image specs. Nothing in the project reads the
assets we download. The only tool that even looks in that folder is the one
that checks licences. **A downloaded asset is outside every content check we
have.**

I have not touched any of it. That is your call and the studio's, not mine.

## Two I got wrong, and I am telling you because the answers got better

**Sound.** I first reported five sound categories as missing, on a hard
measurement: this repository contains 2,111 audio files and every single one is
a voice clip. That number is true. The conclusion was wrong, and reading the
audio code instead of counting files is what caught it.

**The game is not silent. It makes its own sound.** Footsteps, cloth, keys, rain
in two layers, a day bed and a night bed, four kinds of impact, five interface
clicks and a four-part score are all generated by arithmetic at runtime. The
real finding is better than a hole: **every sound in this game that is not a
human voice is synthesised, and none of it was recorded by anybody.** Which is
exactly why you made sound a lane on 14 September and said CC0 libraries. And in
the Unreal build, which is the one that ships, all of it is missing anyway.

**The voices.** I wrote that thirteen voices had auditions on disk, from
counting folders. Our own voice checker says seventeen characters are cast, each
with a chosen clip, and all seven of the principals reach one. What is actually
missing is the recorded lines, not the casting. The inventory says no voice is
picked for any character; that is out of date, and I have noted it without
touching it.

## What the absent things cost

Three of them break something you have already said is essential:

- **Faces that move.** No lip sync exists anywhere. A photoreal face talking
  with a still mouth is the one thing that makes a person look dead, and real
  spoken conversation is the reason this game exists.
- **Body language.** Your ruling: the recognition ladder is invisible except in
  dialogue. A posture is the cheapest way the town shows it knows you.
- **Paper you can read.** No mobiles, no internet, so letters, the local paper
  and the book of fares are how information moves. That is the mechanic, not
  dressing.

Eight make the town look like a film set: no parked cars, no vegetation of any
kind, **no water in a port town**, no interiors, no loading screen, no graffiti
despite you minting five crew tags, no landform, no radio.

The rest cost nothing today and a lot later: garments, carried things, getting
into a car (which is currently a message reading "You get in"), objects to hold,
containers to search, and an icon set.

Four are genuinely your call rather than mine: whether Meridian has a map at
all, and whether key art, a store page and a credits screen ever need to exist,
which depends entirely on whether anybody outside this house ever sees this.

## The three I would do first

1. **Draw the cast.** Cheapest of the three, the pipeline already exists and has
   produced eight district concepts and five street sheets, and every day it
   waits we make more decisions in words that will have to be re-made in
   pictures. Nine or ten faces, four costume pages by trade rather than by
   person.
2. **Record the sound.** One lane, your ruling, your constraint, and it moves
   "the town feels alive" further per hour than anything else on the board. It
   starts the day the street's light is right, which is your judgement to make.
3. **The face that moves.** Biggest of the three and the only one that touches a
   pillar head on. Approved in August, driver already chosen, nothing built.
   Weeks rather than days, which is why it should start before it is urgent.

I left interiors out of the three only because the visual ladder already orders
it after the street, and water only because it is stuck behind the sky, which
the last Unreal run rendered as nothing at all.

## What this cost us, being blocked

One thing, and it lands on recommendation 3. Our licence allowlist says the
face-animation tool is MIT and free to use. The search channel suggests the
code is MIT but **the actual model files may be under a different NVIDIA
licence**. The page that settles it is on huggingface.co, which is one of the
five hosts that did not open. I am not claiming the allowlist is wrong. I am
saying it is a ten-minute check on a machine that can reach the page, and it
should happen before anyone builds on it.
