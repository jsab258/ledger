# Making a smashed shop window read as smashed (Unreal 5.8, 1990 Britain): research note, 29 September 2026

**The problem.** The player can put a shop's plate-glass window in. The game hides the pane and lays a few small shard boxes on the pavement, and the AI tester, looking afterwards, said the window "still looks whole": the frame, the mullions and the opaque backdrop a metre behind the glass look exactly as before. It has to read as smashed at a glance, from the pavement and from across the road, by day and under sodium light, at 60 fps at 3440 by 1440 upscaled (Lumen, software ray tracing). About thirty minutes of research by a separate helper given the problem; saved by the builder (the helper cannot write files). "Mine" marks the helper's own inference. All sources accessed 29 September 2026.

## 1. What a freshly smashed float or plate window looks like

- **Cracks and hole.** A low-speed impact (a brick) sends cracks outward from the point of impact; with the pane held on all sides, rings of straight segments form between them. A fast impact leaves a cone-shaped hole. SWGMAT, "Glass Fractures", July 2004: https://www.asteetrace.org/static/images/pdf/02%20Glass%20Fractures.pdf
- **Where the glass lands.** Fragments thrown back towards the thrower were found up to 3 m from the window, their density falling off roughly exponentially with distance. Pounds and Smalldon, J. Forensic Sci. Soc. 18:197-203, 1978: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/753918/. Mine: most goes inward onto the display; the top of the pane drops and bursts against the stall riser and the pavement.
- **Photographs (links only).**
  - Sam Hood, Joe Gardiner's shoe shop, Sydney, about 1934, no known copyright restrictions (State Library of NSW): most of the pane still in the frame, a ragged dark hole low down, bright white cracks across the pane, a small spill on the pavement. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shop_window_of_Joe_Gardiner%27s_Shoe_Store_smashed_in_George_Street,_Sydney,_c._1934,_by_Sam_Hood_(4066640411).jpg
  - "Kaputtes Schaufenster nach Fasching", Bruchsal, 27 February 2011, CC0: at night the pane nearly gone, a few long slivers with bright edges left at one side, glass on the display floor, and a glittering band of fragments about 2 m deep along the whole front (mine: the strongest single cue). https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kaputtes_Schaufenster_nach_Fasching.JPG
  - Oslo riots, 8 January 2009, Egil Fujikawa Nes, CC BY 2.0: modern laminated glass, milky and still in the frame; a look to avoid for 1990 plate glass. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shop_windows_smashed_Oslo_riots.jpg
- **Boarding up.** Emergency glaziers board with OSB today and claim under 90 minutes (Able Group, undated): https://www.able-group.co.uk/glazing/boarding-up-service/. A boarded UK shop after the 2011 riots: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:499_tottenham_high_road_-_After_the_riot_-_The_Ship_boarded-up.jpg. Mine: in 1990, plywood or chipboard over the opening that night or next morning, loose glass knocked out first; no dated 1990 source found.

## 2. How games make it read

- Unreal Tournament 2003/2004 (Unreal wiki, 30 May 2006): the pane goes, a burst of shard particles and a sound; nothing is left to read afterwards, which is what LEDGER does now. https://beyondunrealwiki.github.io/pages/breaking-glass-ut2003.html
- Half-Life 2 (2004): the pane breaks in chunks and leaves pieces stuck to the frame (TWHL, 20 September 2024: https://twhl.info/wiki/page/Tutorial:_New_Glass); a 2025 remake: a grid of cells each picking one of 16 jagged-edge textures, cells no longer joined to the frame fall (dev.to, 19 October 2025: https://dev.to/justind/technical-showcase-breakable-glass-4k5h).
- CryEngine breakable glass (manual, undated; search excerpts only): a spiderweb crack decal, clouding round the cracks, particles. https://docs.cryengine.com/display/CEMANUAL/Breakable+Glass
- Receiver 2 (Wolfire, 1 July 2020): a hand-drawn crack pattern cut into the pane; shards touching the frame stay, the rest fall. https://www.wolfire.com/blog/2020/07/wolfire-monthly-update---june-2020/
- Mine, ranked for reading after the event: (1) jagged glass left in the frame, whose outline makes the hole; (2) a bright band of glass on the ground; (3) white crack lines and glints on what remains; (4) sound, only at the moment.

## 3. The cheapest robust way in Unreal 5.x, without Chaos

- **See-through glass is weak in Lumen.** Sharp translucency reflections cover only the front-most see-through surface; lit translucency gets lower-quality indirect light from a coarse volume (64 cells, fine zone 15 m). Epic, Lumen GI and Reflections and Lit Translucency, UE 5.8 docs. Mine: see-through shards would light flat and not glint under sodium.
- **The upscaler smears see-through edges** (no depth or motion written). Epic, TSR docs, UE 5.8.
- **Nanite takes only opaque and masked materials.** Epic, Nanite docs, UE 5.8.
- **Decals:** deferred only; the default screen-size cut-off (0.01) can pop them out from across the road, so set it to 0. Epic, Decal docs, UE 5.8; the fade note from forum threads, undated.
- **Versions:** 5.5 added a reflection clean-up and refraction in reflections, mostly for hardware ray tracing (5.5 release notes); nothing in 5.8 changes this approach (Tom Looman, 27 August 2026).
- **Method (mine):** make the remaining glass OPAQUE or masked (full Lumen reflections, shadows, stable under the upscaler): very low roughness, near-black grey-green, strong edge reflection, and a thin pale green strip along each broken edge (the glass's thickness catching light, which is what reads at a distance). Author the broken state in Blender (remnants in the frame, glass on the display floor) and export it hidden, switched visible from C++; on the pavement one decal plus 15 to 30 small shard meshes, no Nanite.

## 4. Recommendation (the helper's)

1. **Jagged glass left in the frame:** an uneven band of teeth 5 to 25 cm deep and one or two long daggers hanging from the top, opaque, with bright edge strips and a few white cracks.
2. **A glittering fan of glass on the pavement:** out to 2 to 3 m, dense at the wall and thinning fast, a few large pieces by the stall riser, low roughness; glass on the display floor too.
3. **Boards by the next scene:** a board mesh swapped in, nearly free and impossible to misread.

**Look risks (mine):** starburst or spiderweb cracks read as bullet holes or laminated glass; thin masked teeth shimmer when upscaled (keep them chunky); a pavement decal can vanish at distance; if the intact pane showed little reflection the difference is weak (check the unbroken glass visibly reflects the street); an even spill looks like a texture.
