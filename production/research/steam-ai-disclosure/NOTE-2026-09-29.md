# Steam's AI content disclosure for live-generated talk (research note, 29 September 2026)

For ROADMAP's "a thirty-minute build for his friends" (Steam's safeguards
description; checklist AI07, open since 24 September). A separate helper was
given the problem, not a theory, with about thirty minutes and web sources
only. It could not see the survey form itself, which needs a sign-in; the
wording comes from Valve's public documentation and from press that reproduced
the form. "Inference" marks the helper's own reasoning.

**The problem.** LEDGER's characters' replies are written live by Anthropic's
Claude through the developer's own server, and a local text-to-speech voice
speaks them. Before the game is shown beyond friends, the Steam content
survey's AI section and the store-page disclosure must describe this
accurately.

## Findings

1. **What the survey asks today** (Steamworks Content Survey,
   https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey, undated,
   fetched 29 September 2026):
   - Its third part covers generative AI in what ships and is "consumed by
     players"; development tools are exempt.
   - It has two categories: Pre-Generated, made with AI during development, and
     Live-Generated, made by AI while the game runs.
   - For Live-Generated content, the developer must tell Valve what guardrails
     stop the AI producing illegal content.
   - Valve rewrote the form on 16 January 2026. It now has a text box for
     AI-made game and marketing content, and a checkbox for images, audio, text
     and other content generated during play (Game Developer, 16 January 2026,
     https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam;
     TheGamer, 17 January 2026).
   - Some answers lock once the game is approved for release (the doc's FAQ).
   - Much of the disclosure goes on the store page (Valve, 10 January 2024,
     https://store.steampowered.com/news/group/4145017/view/3862463747997849618).
     The page shows an "AI Generated Content Disclosure" block with the
     developer's text. The guardrails answer was not seen on any page.
   - Some live-AI store pages also carry "Connects to 3rd-Party Service for AI
     Content Generation" naming the provider (Suck Up!, AikoMate). No Valve
     documentation was found on when it started or whether it is required.
2. **Guardrails and reporting:**
   - Valve built a report button into the Steam overlay for live-generated
     content players think the guardrails should have caught (Valve, 10 January
     2024; PCGamesN, 10 January 2024). No requirement was found for a game's own
     report tool, and no programming interface for Valve's.
   - Per-use AI costs are the developer's to manage; any payment goes through a
     Steam-supported method.
3. **Changes, 2025–2026:**
   - The January 2026 rewrite.
   - Rule 15 (16 July 2025) bans content breaking payment processors'
     standards.
   - One unconfirmed report (Tech Insider, 28 July 2026, citing Remio.ai) says
     AI tags became mandatory for every asset type in June 2026; no Valve
     source was found.
   - No documented question asks which AI model is used.
   - No documented case was found of Valve refusing a game with live AI
     characters.
4. **Published examples** (store pages fetched 29 September 2026; each AI block
   is 20–60 words):
   - Vaudeville: its dialogue comes from a conversational AI and may not suit
     every audience.
   - Whispers from the Star: an AI-driven voiced conversation made with an
     actor. Its mature-content description says player input can steer the
     tone, and that filters block explicit sexual content, self-harm and hate
     speech.
   - AI2U: art, story and music made by people, the chat generated live by a
     language model and text-to-speech; filters named in its content
     description.
   - AI Roguelite and Suck Up!: a short description, with no safety detail.
5. **Sexual content, minors and real people:**
   - Valve will not ship live-generated Adult Only sexual content "at this
     time" (the survey FAQ).
   - Steam's rules ban explicit images of real people, libel, content
     exploiting children, content illegal where sold, and content breaking
     payment-processor standards; the survey does not exempt live output.

## What it suggests (inference)

- **The categories:** tick Live-Generated for both the replies' text and the
  spoken audio. Anything AI-assisted that ships goes under Pre-Generated.
- **Public text:** about 40 to 80 words. The full guardrail list goes in
  Valve's guardrails field.
- **The provider:** name Anthropic's Claude on the third-party service line if
  offered.
- **The overlay:** keep the Steam overlay working in the packaged game. Never
  ask for a player's own key or for payment outside Steam.
- **Mature content:** put the steering risk and the filters in the
  mature-content description. Jafar approves the wording before the first
  public build, since some answers lock.
