## 1. How AAA games build varied crowds

- Hitman: Absolution (IO Interactive, GDC 2012): crowds of up to 1,200 at 30 fps on consoles, Glacier 2 engine [1]. Memory and animation-sharing detail not in the pages reached: unverified.
- Assassin's Creed Unity (Ubisoft, GDC 2015, "AI Recycling"): AI level of detail with a pool swapping low-resolution people for high-resolution ones; limit of 40 real AIs and 120 high-resolution models, 10,000 crowd NPCs in one scene [2]. Distant vertex animation is a forum guess: unverified.
- Mafia (local study 12, from setting names): set-piece crowds are vertex-animated meshes with animation sharing; animation budget 3 ms; civilians freeze when unseen 3 s and beyond 70 m. Kingdom Come II: 70 people in full detail within 150 m, 3.5 ms update budget.
- Cyberpunk 2077: only design intent found (crowds "where it makes sense", about 1,000 routine NPCs); no technical method reached [3]. Unverified.
- Common shape across the above: few full-detail people near, many cheap ones far, with a pool swapping between them; no memory numbers per person published in anything reached.

## 2. Epic's route, 5.6 to 5.8

- MetaHuman Crowds (Experimental; the page advises caution when shipping): a Collection with a crowd pipeline, instances for each appearance, Mass Entity Config, Mass Spawner. It builds optimised assets, drops higher-detail LODs (retained indices set in the Collection's build settings), converts strand hair to card-textured skeletal meshes (no strand detail or hair physics), and uses the Instanced Skinned Mesh Component at distance, which skips the post-process Anim Blueprint (no correctives) [4]. Plugin classes include head, groom, outfit and skeletal-clothing pipelines and an appearance provider [5]. A third-party article says the system shipped in 5.8 on 17 Jun 2026 and that MetaHuman Creator (browser) shuts 5 Nov 2026; both unverified here [6].
- Epic's page gives no memory or per-person numbers [4].
- Animation Budget Allocator: caps skeletal animation game-thread time, default 1.0 ms (1.0 to 2.5 ms by view-distance quality in Epic's example); throttles tick rate by significance. It saves CPU, not card memory [7].
- Nanite skinned meshes: Epic staff, 31 Aug 2026: little changed; experimental Nanite translucency in 5.8 could serve MetaHumans; most other missing features (per the original post: morph targets, grooms, cloth) still missing; progress hoped for Q1 2027, no ETA [8]. Not a route for MetaHumans now.
- Open fault: a 5.8 forum report says Mass skinned-mesh instances never release animation tracks when entities die, so card memory grows with entities ever spawned (20 spawned and destroyed twice: 39 tracks); a level reload resets it. User report, no Epic reply, unverified [9].
- Not read (page content did not load): Epic's Mass Crowd page, MetaHuman optimisation page, 5.7 release notes, Nanite docs.

## 3. AI-native and open-source tools

- NVIDIA RTX Neural Texture Compression SDK, v0.10.1 beta, "NVIDIA RTX SDKs LICENSE" (not an OSI licence; terms not read). One example: a 2K material bundle 32 MB raw, 12 MB BCn, 2.5 MB when decoded at sample time. Inference recommended on RTX 4000 and newer; on RX 6000 only a slow fallback; a known AMD driver incompatibility; no Unreal integration mentioned [10]. Not a fit for an RX 6700 target now (reader's reading).
- Neural LOD and generated-variant research: no primary source reached. Unreached, unverified.

## 4. Small and AI-assisted developers

- Forum reports (anecdote, unverified): Mass with static or vertex-animated meshes handles thousands; skinned instances cost more; character movement components can dominate cost [11]. No RX 6700-class results found. A vendor article advises capturing a baseline, adding agents in steps and watching memory beside frame time [6].

## 5. What the sources say about our case

- 20 to 40 visible people fit the "few near, many far" shape; the number is small beside any figure above, so the issue is memory per distinct person, not agent count. Epic publishes no such figure; MEMORY-PLAN's own forum figure of 2.3 GB is unverified.
- Levers named by the sources: fewer distinct people with shared skeletons and animation; hair cards beyond a near range; dropped high LODs and capped textures at distance; a pool swapping full to cheap people (Unity); MetaHuman Crowds' pipeline as a possible builder, experimental, untested here.
- Rough cost to find out (reader's estimate, not sourced): measuring MB per person on one build, half a day to one day; trying the MetaHuman Crowd pipeline on 5 to 10 people, two to three days; hand-built pooling, longer.
