# Field scan: parked fictional 1990 cars (8 November 2026)

One-hour scan for his note of 8 Nov ("one car first, the hatchback"). Not repeated: N09-13-CAR-COSTS-2026-11-08.md and NOTE.md (1 Oct). All links read in full on 8 Nov 2026 unless marked.

## 1. How studios build period and fictional cars
- Background and traffic cars: 5,000-25,000 triangles on shared atlas textures (the same page also says 5,000-30,000 in another answer); hero cars 40,000-80,000 in open-world games [1]. Texture size for parked cars is not given; the page says maps "should follow camera reality", with mirrored body halves, packed ORM channels, trim shared between cars, dark simplified interiors behind tinted glass, and baked emissive cards for far lamps [1].
- Fictional designs: no studio talk on trademark-safe generic design was reached. [1] says only "keep badges as separate pieces". The GTA/Watch Dogs blended-class-model point rests on 1 Oct sources not re-read now (unverified this pass; Sportskeeda returned HTTP 405).
- A senior vehicle artist [2] gives no numbers: judge each LOD at its viewing distance; basic PBR materials suffice for pristine cars.
- Mafia (Definitive Edition) car triangle counts, textures and LODs: none found in any public source (search only; unverified).

## 2. Epic's route
- Vehicle template: only a sports car and an off-road car in the project, nothing said on licence or reuse [3].
- City Sample Vehicles (free, Epic): 13 driveable vehicles (sedan, coupe, SUV, hatchback, sports car, taxi, vans, trucks, bus). Licence field blank; description "UE-Only Content - Licensed for Use Only with Unreal Engine-based Products". The page shows "Allows usage with AI: No", "Generated with AI: No", no triangle counts [4]. Press: licensed for Unreal-based products "including commercial projects" (5 Apr 2022) [5]. A forum reply (16 Dec 2023, community member, not Epic) says use is fine if not sold stand-alone [6]. Shapes are modern American (1 Oct note, not re-read), and they are modelled on real makes; whether the NoAI tag conflicts with the project's Epic exception is the session's call.

## 3. Open-source and AI-native routes
- Car Primitives (Blender add-on): Geometry Nodes sliders for wheelbase, overhangs, bonnet and roof height, window angles, tyre size; licence field "GPL"; paid, $19.95 commercial; Blender 3.5+ [7]. Builds a bare body, not a finished car.
- NPR Car Generator (stylized, Geometry Nodes, Blender 4.x/5.1): licence and price not stated on the thread [8]; stylized, wrong look.
- Street Generator V5 (free, traffic): licence not stated [9]; modern cars.
- Poly Haven: CC0 confirmed; no AI-use restriction on assets [10]. Its only car is the covered yard car (1 Oct).
- No CC0 or unmarked CC-BY 1980s hatchback was found (search only; CGTrader hits are paid and some tagged "No AI"). Not exhaustive: Sketchfab was not browsed by filter.
- Image-to-3D: TRELLIS (MIT; Linux only, 16 GB NVIDIA) [11]; TripoSR (MIT; ~6 GB VRAM, textured output option) [12]. The local card is AMD, so neither is confirmed to run here (unverified). No vehicle-quality evidence was found beyond N09-13. Output has no interior, wheels or clean topology (generic reading; unverified for cars).

## 4. Solo and AI-assisted developers
- Only one usable devlog: a solo Blender developer built three new car types in about 1.5 hours from the game's existing standard car; no polycount, no AI or free-asset mention [13]. No solo UE5 devlog on parked 1980s fictional cars was found (searches only; unverified).

## 5. Fastest route to ONE hatchback, per the sources
- Build one parametric body in Blender (existing script; optionally Car Primitives' parameter set as a model for what to expose), mid-poly with bevels, 15-25k triangles, one shared atlas, separate badge-free pieces [1], Epic Automotive Materials for paint and glass, interior blockout, invented plates, right-hand drive built in from the start. Estimate: 24-40 h (N09-13/NOTE.md's own figure, est.); no money.
- Main risk: the shape reads as no era or no country (1980s British boxiness, stance) or as a recognisable real model; a second risk is Nanite or LOD drawing a coarse stand-in (seen 1 Oct).
