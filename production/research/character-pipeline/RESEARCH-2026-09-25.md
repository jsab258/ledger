**Cast the MetaHuman first, audition voices offline, and build clothes from patterns.** Research checked **25 September 2026**. Confidence: high on documented capabilities, prices and licence exclusions; medium on quality comparisons; low on RX 6700 performance and garment labour estimates. I did not run these models on your PC. No published test I found measured expressive TTS sharing your exact GPU with Unreal.

**1. Faces: approve an achievable face before building the character**

| Route | What it actually delivers; requirements and licence |
|---|---|
| **UE 5.8 From Custom Mesh / Mesh to MetaHuman** | Accepts a head scan or sculpt, including arbitrary topology; fits MetaHuman geometry, offers landmark correction and surface refinement; texture transfer uses an external baking tool. This converts an existing likeness into a riggable character; it does not reconstruct a portrait by itself. Included in UE’s commercial licence. AMD is supported, although Epic recommends RX 6800 XT or better, above your RX 6700. Work with the game/models closed. [1](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-creator-from-custom-mesh-tool-in-unreal-engine) [2](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-hardware-requirements-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US) [3](https://www.metahuman.com/license) |
| **Blender sculpt → From Template** | Free, direct artistic control. Preserve MetaHuman topology or supported UV correspondence; compare matching camera views while sculpting. Expect an artist’s likeness pass, not automatic matching. Runs on AMD; Blender’s GPL does not impose GPL on exported artwork. [4](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-creator-from-template-tool-in-unreal-engine) [5](https://www.blender.org/about/license/) |
| **KeenTools FaceBuilder** — paid | Fits a head to one or several photographs through landmark pinning; projects textures and provides a MetaHuman workflow. More useful with consistent front, three-quarter and profile views. Blender/Windows, no CUDA requirement. Current individual Freelancer quote: **$19.99/month or $191.88/year**, excluding tax; companies require a different licence. No independent portrait-likeness score found. [6](https://keentools.io/products/facebuilder-for-blender) |
| **Headshot 3.1 + Character Creator 5** — paid | Single-image head generation, lens correction, shape editing and texture reprojection. **$329 advertised bundle**, Windows perpetual; AMD RX 5500 XT/8GB minimum is below your card. Exporting its head into UE’s custom-mesh route is a plausible additional conversion, not a verified one-click MetaHuman pipeline. Standard content licensing permits embedded commercial game characters. [7](https://www.reallusion.com/character-creator/headshot/) [8](https://manual.reallusion.com/Headshot_Plugin/ENU/1.0/ID_SysReq.html) [9](https://www.reallusion.com/Content/EULA/EULA.htm) |

A single portrait does not determine the unseen skull/profile; generating additional views can invent different anatomy. Texture projection can improve frontal resemblance while preserving the wrong silhouette or baking lighting into skin. Use neutral lighting, then judge geometry, skin, hair and expressions separately. Epic’s fitting controls and Headshot’s correction tools do not supply measured guarantees of likeness. [1](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-creator-from-custom-mesh-tool-in-unreal-engine) [7](https://www.reallusion.com/character-creator/headshot/)

Free single-image reconstruction is a weaker route: **TripoSR (March 2024, MIT)** produces generic objects, with approximately 6GB default GPU use and a CPU fallback. I found no convincing head-likeness benchmark or RX 6700 measurement. **MICA’s head reconstruction is non-commercial and excluded.** [10](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSR) [11](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSR/blob/main/run.py) [12](https://github.com/Zielon/MICA/blob/master/LICENSE)

Production evidence supports two approaches: 3Lateral’s **April 2020 Hellblade II trailer** used an actor’s 4D-derived face and authored rig; Treehouse’s **2 March 2022** account describes selecting MetaHuman designs before custom hair/clothing. The latter is a small film team. The Chinese Room confirms MetaHuman use for **Still Wakes the Deep**’s workers (**7 November 2024**), but does not document its casting decisions; I cannot infer those. [13](https://www.3lateral.com/portfolio/senuas-saga-hellblade-ii) [14](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/metahumans-star-in-treehouse-digital-s-animated-short-horror-film-the-well) [15](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/making-waves-developing-realistic-water-mechanics-for-still-wakes-the-deep-in-ue5)

**Try, in order:** **1)** Free: cast several MetaHumans with the intended voice, approve front/profile/speaking views, and use the chosen render as the face concept. **Yes, this is more realistic than enforcing an arbitrary generated portrait.** Expect the approved character to survive into the game. **2)** If that portrait is essential, spend one FaceBuilder month on one likeness pilot; expect manual corrections. **3)** Reserve a manually corrected sculpt/scan for indispensable likenesses; expect more control and substantial artist work.

**2. Voices: separate voice identity from acting direction**

Calm references can transfer calm rhythm and delivery, but there is no defensible percentage assigning your flatness to reference versus model without an A/B test. Original Chatterbox’s `exaggeration` is not a semantic anger/sadness instruction: its own advice suggests roughly **0.7 exaggeration / 0.3 CFG** for drama and warns that exaggeration also speeds speech. Turbo/Nano have different controls, including vocal-event tags; do not assume that slider recipe applies. Chatterbox is **MIT**. [16](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox)

| Free model | Useful control; evidence and hardware limits |
|---|---|
| **VoxCPM2**, April 2026, Apache-2.0 | Both description-based voice design and reference cloning **with per-line directions**. Its **5 June 2026** paper reports instruction-following **4.50/5**: 50 listeners, approximately 100 samples across several tasks, randomized and blind, but an author-run study. This is not a test of restrained British acting. Official full-model requirement is approximately **8GB VRAM**—leaving only 2GB of your card for everything else. [17](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM) [18](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06928v1) |
| **Qwen3-TTS**, 22 January 2026, Apache-2.0 | VoiceDesign creates voices from descriptions; 1.7B CustomVoice follows directions with nine supplied voices. **Base clones your voice but does not provide that instruction interface.** Designing then cloning does not fix this separation. No verified RX 6700 concurrent-game speed or peak-memory measurement found. [19](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS) |
| **CosyVoice3 0.5B**, December 2025, Apache-2.0 | Clone plus `inference_instruct2` direction, including emotion, pace and vocal events. Smaller candidate, but the English character-acting evidence is weaker than VoxCPM2’s; language support is not accent validation. No verified Windows/RX 6700 speed or peak-memory measurement found. [20](https://huggingface.co/FunAudioLLM/Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B-2512/blob/main/README.md) | 
| **LAION MOSS 8B voice-acting**, 2026, Apache-2.0 | Directions plus reference cloning. Demo grids select the best of 64 takes; they do not establish first-take reliability. Authors measured **24.3GB peak VRAM at batch one** on an 80GB GPU. This is not a 10GB deployment; no AMD timing found. [21](https://github.com/LAION-AI/moss-8b-voice-acting) |

Stock GPU examples for these models largely target CUDA. VoxCPM now points to an **MIT llama.cpp-omni CPU/Vulkan implementation**, providing an AMD experiment, not a performance guarantee. A firsthand **9 April 2026** Linux/Strix Halo test measured VoxCPM2 Q4 at **1.55 seconds computing per second of speech**, across three runs; its Vulkan path crashed then. That old failure does not establish the current port’s status. Your own September 24 findings already report Nano taking approximately **four seconds for three seconds of speech while the game runs**. [22](https://github.com/tc-mb/llama.cpp-omni) [23](https://sleepingrobots.com/dreams/voxcpm-strix-halo/) [24](https://github.com/jsab258/ledger/blob/6de07a0/FINDINGS.md)

All support English; none of the cited studies establishes northern-English authenticity. VoxCPM2, Qwen VoiceDesign, Eleven Voice Design and Hume can propose voices from descriptions, but a prompt naming Yorkshire is not evidence of a Yorkshire accent. Audition a specific locality and age, not “British.” Retain licensed reference recordings and reject accent drift. **Breeze TTS 2 is excluded:** its Apache code licence does not cover its non-commercial weights and self-hosted outputs. **Fish Audio S2 also requires a separate commercial licence.** [33](https://github.com/breezeblue-ai/breeze-tts) [34](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/blob/main/LICENSE)

**Try, in order:** **1)** Free, one afternoon: same speaker, calm versus acted references, identical lines and settings; blind-score threat, embarrassment, warmth and humour separately. Expect improvement in energy, not reliable nuanced acting. **2)** Try VoxCPM2 offline, with a two-hour installation limit; expect selectable performances requiring retakes. **3)** Spend $5 on Sonic or $6 on Eleven, comparing the same lines; use performed voice conversion for difficult authored scenes. Cache approved speech; retain Nano on CPU for uncached live lines until a replacement passes the combined performance test. Do not promise a larger live model on the shared GPU before measuring latency, memory and game frame times together.

**3. Clothing: make sewn surfaces, then a game garment**

The production path is **period reference → measured pattern pieces → sewing/drape → topology/UV/material work → skinning → fitting → collision/cloth tests**. Treehouse’s published MetaHuman workflow used Marvelous Designer, further sculpting, weight transfer and Unreal simulation; the garment generator was only one step. [14](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/metahumans-star-in-treehouse-digital-s-animated-short-horror-film-the-well)

| Route | Result, reuse, hardware and licence |
|---|---|
| **FreeSewing + Blender cloth** | FreeSewing’s MIT-licensed designs include **Florent flat cap, Charlie trousers, Carlton coat** and shirt/jacket blocks; commercial use is allowed. These are patterns, not ready MetaHuman assets. Blender sewing springs can join actual panels on your exported body; CPU simulation works without NVIDIA. Re-drape for substantially different bodies, then skin/export. Expect plain correct construction before convincing fabric detail. [35](https://freesewing.eu/designs/) [36](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing) [37](https://freesewing.eu/docs/about/faq/commercial-use/) [38](https://docs.blender.org/manual/es/4.5/physics/cloth/settings/shape.html) |
| **OpenSew-2** | Free **GPL-3.0** Blender 5.2 add-on: pattern blocks, sewing and weight transfer. Very young—seven commits when checked; its own tests, not independent production evidence. Author reports **8–15 seconds for a tank-top drape**, not a finished garment. No verified MetaHuman compatibility; limit the experiment. Exported garment art need not be GPL. [39](https://github.com/MarcelloMorettoni/opensew-2) [5](https://www.blender.org/about/license/) |
| **Marvelous Designer** — paid | Dedicated pattern/drape workflow with documented MetaHuman production use. **Personal commercial licence $39/month or $280/year**, per **5 August 2026** support page. Works on your PC through **CPU simulation**; its GPU simulation requires NVIDIA. Patterns can be adjusted/re-draped onto other bodies. Export still needs game finishing. [40](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358269350041-What-kind-of-licenses-does-Marvelous-Designer-offer) [41](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358219834649-System-Requirements-April-2026) |
| **Free libraries** | MakeHuman’s **Suits01** jacket/trouser pack is **CC0**, but needs MetaHuman fitting, materials and weights. **GarmentCodeData, 4 September 2024**, supplies patterns and draped meshes under **CC-BY 4.0**; attribution required. Neither is a verified finished library of 1990 British workwear. [42](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/suits01.html) [43](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/9d16a4da-0d30-4963-8842-af20fcf82899) |

UE 5.8’s **Outfit Assets** can resize garments using source body/garment pairs; extreme bodies need additional fitted examples. A render mesh or transferred skin weights **does not automatically become simulated cloth**: supply a suitable simulation mesh, weight maps and collision setup. Test walking, sitting and raised arms. Use simulation selectively for loose hems; a flat cap needs none. [44](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/getting-started-for-creating-parametric-clothing-in-metahuman) [45](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/building-a-metahuman-parametric-outfit-with-a-render-mesh)

Image generation is not ready to replace this process. **TRELLIS.2, MIT**, can generate open surfaces, including clothing, but supplies neither approved patterns nor fitted, rigged Unreal outfits; its documented local route requires **Linux/NVIDIA ≥24GB**. It is not a next-week solution on your card. [46](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)

**Planning estimates, not measured studio averages:** allow **8–20 hands-on hours** for a first simple garment, **20–40 for a first jacket**, and **1–4 per body variant**, including cleanup and engine checks. A first-ever cloth workflow can exceed these budgets. Tool demos timing only draping conceal most of this work.

**Try, in order:** **1)** Free: prove the Florent cap and trousers on one approved body, then adapt a jacket block to period references. Have the AI construct open pattern panels and seam constraints, adding thickness after draping. Expect recognisable but initially plain clothes. **2)** If sewing/draping blocks progress, buy one $39 Marvelous month and finish one donkey jacket. **3)** Reuse that approved pattern on a few standard bodies through Outfit Assets; vary fabric, wear and colour. Use distinct patterns for anoraks and cardigans. Expect a coherent wardrobe from a small number of finished garments, with manual clipping fixes.
