# Shop hours in a Humber port town, 1990

Research note, 29 September 2026, by a research helper (about twenty minutes). Numbers are sources; "inferred" means my reasoning.

## Summary

- Caff: about 6–7am to mid-afternoon, Monday to Saturday (inferred). No early closing; may open Sunday.
- Newsagent: from about 5–6am for the papers; post office counter about 9am–5.30pm weekdays. Sunday morning: papers, sweets, tobacco only.
- Pawnbroker: about 9am–5.30pm Monday to Saturday (inferred); shut one afternoon a week and Sunday.
- Steam laundry: not found (counter probably ordinary shop hours, inferred).
- Fish shop: after the market to afternoon; no early closing; shut Sunday. Chip shops could not sell fish and chips on Sunday.
- Fish market: landing through the night; auction about 7–7.30am, working days.
- Kiosk (papers, sweets, tobacco): any day, Sunday included.
- Street market: Hull's historic days Tuesday, Friday, Saturday; 1990 days not found.
- Catholic chapel, boarding house, minicab office: not found.
- Early closing: every shop not exempt shut by 1pm on one weekday of the shopkeeper's choosing, posted at the door. All shops shut by 8pm, or 9pm on one late day. Thursday is the only day found for Hull and Grimsby, from the early 1900s.
- Sunday: only listed goods (papers, tobacco, sweets, meals, milk, fruit, vegetables, flowers, medicines) and post office business. Big stores openly broke it by 1991.

## Findings

Law, repealed 1 December 1994 [5]:

- Shops closed "not later than one o'clock in the afternoon on one week day in every week" (s.1, at 1 February 1991) [1]. Since 1965 the occupier chose the day, displayed it visibly from outside, and could change it only after three months [2]. Exempt: shops selling only refreshments, newspapers, tobacco, or perishables such as fish and bread [1].
- General closing: 8pm, 9pm on the late day; meals eaten in and post office business allowed later [1].
- Sunday (Fifth Schedule): meals or refreshments "but not including the sale of fried fish and chips at a fried fish and chip shop", plus the goods in the summary. Fresh fish is not listed. Markets were covered too, but fishermen could sell freshly caught fish (s.58) [1].
- Pawnbroking law was replaced from 19 May 1985 [3]; no later pawnshop hours found.

Practice:

- Only 550 convictions for Sunday opening in nine months of 1985 (Commons, 14 April 1986) [4]. Hull City Council tried to stop B&Q and faced a £1.2m cross-undertaking (Commons, 12 December 1991) [6]; Tesco sought £2m from Hull before Christmas 1991 (Lords, 17 January 1992) [7].
- Early closing "less widespread since the mid 1980s", though independents still shut Wednesday afternoons in the 80s and 90s (forum, 2020) [8].
- Hull: Thursday was early-closing day in East Park's early decades [9]. Grimsby: a department store's Thursday half day began at 4pm, early 1900s (snippet; paywalled) [10].
- Hull's market days date from the early 19th century (1969) [11]. Grimsby's Freeman Street market today: Tuesday, Friday, Saturday, some listings add Thursday [12].
- Hull fish dock, early 1970s: round the clock; bobbers from 2am; selling from 7.30am each working morning (2023) [13]. Grimsby today: 7am auction, Monday to Friday [14].
- Newspaper work "from 5 am to 11.30 am" (undated) [15]. Post office counters 9 to 5.30 weekdays (snippet) [16].
- Early-1900s pawnshops: open till midnight Saturdays (snippet) [17]. Hull's H&T today: 9am–5.30pm, Monday to Saturday [18].
- A London minicab sent to a pick-up just after midnight (Commons, 15 February 1988, snippet) [19].

## Not found

- Hull's or Grimsby's early-closing day in 1990; any council exemption.
- 1990 hours for cafés, laundries, boarding houses, minicab offices (all night plausible, unsourced); period mass times; Grimsby market days; sub-post office Saturday hours.
