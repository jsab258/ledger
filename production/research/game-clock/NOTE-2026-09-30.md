# The game clock, the whole method (research note, 30 September 2026)

For Jafar's list of 30 September, item 1 (one continuous route; the clock running as the first week needs it). A separate helper was given the method question (time scale, catch-up, waiting, scheduling, save, where the clock lives in Unreal) with about thirty minutes; its notes follow as it kept them, with its dated sources. The waiting design itself was researched on 29 September (production/research/waiting/NOTE-2026-09-29.md).

## What LEDGER took (30 September)

- The clock is engine-free (LiveClock.h), stepped by play, a wait or a black screen through the same hourly step, each hour handed back once; saved and restored exactly, never re-simulated on a load.
- Held while he talks (the box open or a reply on its way) and during Sheila's walk-round; each exchange costs a few fixed game minutes instead.
- The scale stays the town's two game minutes a real second (its first hour, which Jafar approved) as one constant; the helper found it faster than every reference (1:15 to 1:86), so a 100 m walk costs over two game hours: for the AI tester to measure and Jafar to hear.

# Game clock research notes (30 Sep 2026)

## Time scales (sources, dates)
- Skyrim: default timescale 20 (1 real min = 20 game min; 3 real min per game hour; 72 min per day). Menus/journal pause time. Wait/sleep count as time passing; cells reset after 10/30 days. UESP Skyrim:Time, last modified 21 Feb 2026.
- Stardew Valley: 0.7 s per game minute (7 s per 10 min); day 6am-2am = 20 h = ~14 min real. Single player: clock stops in cutscenes, dialogue, menus, some animations. Saves only at sleep/new day. Stardew Wiki Day_Cycle, last edited 16 Sep 2025.
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance: 15x (1 game hour = 4 real min; day = 96 min). Search summaries of KCD forum/Steam; exact primary not fetched (Nexus 403).
- Shenmue (1999): ~1 game hour ≈ 4 real min?? active 08:00-23:00; NPCs visibly follow daily routines. (search summaries; conflicting — some say ~1 min per hour). Check.
- Majora's Mask (2000): 72 h cycle = ~54 real min; 45 s per game hour (Inverted Song: slower). Wikipedia/Zelda wiki.

## Off-screen and catch-up
- Oblivion Radiant AI: four process levels (high in loaded cells, then high-middle, low-middle, low). Low levels: coarse, travel resolved cell to cell with estimated durations, teleport when enough time passed; no combat, no actor-actor interaction. paavo blog "What was Radiant AI, anyway?", 7 Jun 2025.
- KCD1/KCD2: all NPCs keep routines when player is away; KCD2 ~2400 NPCs; AI LOD introduced with scripting oblivious to LOD. GDC Festival of Gaming 2026 session "Supporting Thousands of NPCs in KCD & KCD2" (abstract via search; page 403).
- Schedule-as-function-of-clock: entry active = last transition at/before now (wrap midnight) -> NPCs correct instantly after skip. csaf.itch.io Township devlog (date to check).

## Unreal (engine source, UE 5.8 installed on this PC)
- BaseGame.ini: MinGlobalTimeDilation=0.0001, MaxGlobalTimeDilation=20.0, MaxUndilatedFrameTime=0.4 (2.5 fps floor), MinUndilatedFrameTime=0.0005.
  -> global time dilation cannot be 0 (it clamps) and cannot fast-forward more than 20x; not a wait mechanism. And a frame hitch caps delta at 0.4 s.
- UWorld has TimeSeconds (paused-aware, dilated), UnpausedTimeSeconds, RealTimeSeconds, AudioTimeSeconds.
- UTickableWorldSubsystem exists (WorldSubsystem.h) = UWorldSubsystem + FTickableGameObject; IsTickableWhenPaused default false.
- Epic Subsystems doc (UE 5.8, undated): GameInstance subsystem lives with the GameInstance (survives level loads); World subsystem per world.

## More time scales
- Fallout 3/NV: default timescale 30 (1 real min = 30 game min; 48 real min/day). Lowering it breaks quests (GameDaysPassed discrepancy). Nexus/Steam threads (search summaries, undated).
- The Sims 4: speed 1 = 1 sim minute per real second (1:60, 24 real min/day); speeds Normal/Fast/Ultra; Ultra only when all Sims on lot asleep or away (sleep = fast forward through the same simulation). Sims wiki/fandom via search summary.
- Majora's Mask (2000): 72 h cycle ~54 real min (45 s per game hour, ~1:80); Inverted Song slows; Double Time skips to next dawn/dusk; three-day cycle chosen to "make the game data more compact". Wikipedia (fetched 30 Sep 2026).
- KCD: pre-release plan "1.5 hours = day" (~1:16), KCD forum 25 Feb 2014; players cite 1:15.
- Shenmue (1999): active 08:00-23:00; sources conflict (~1 real min per game hour per a GameFAQs summary; others 4 min). No skip in Shenmue I (GameRevolution 22 Aug 2018); Shenmue II offers "wait until" for story events.
- Persona 5: time slots (after school, evening), time advances only on actions; calendar with deadlines. (Game Rant 29 Jun 2025, in waiting note.)
- Like a Dragon: time of day changed by story, free choice only late (Infinite Wealth guides, 2024).
- Bannerlord: campaign map time pauses when player not moving; 1 pause, 2 play, 3 fast forward; campaign behaviours subscribe to HourlyTick/DailyTick events (API docs apidoc.bannerlord.com v1.2.9) -> fast forward = same ticks faster.
- Stardew: clock stops in dialogue, menus, cutscenes (single-player). Events = preconditions (Time 600 1000, DayOfWeek) checked when the event's location is entered (Modding:Event_data, last edited 10 Sep 2026; Events for Everyone, 27 Jun 2026). Saves only at night.

## Scheduling / catch-up
- Skyrim Papyrus RegisterForSingleUpdateGameTime / OnUpdateGameTime: event "may come in much later than you expect if the player is sleeping, waiting, fast traveling, or serving jail time" and is sent once after the period (CK wiki via search summary; page 403). Coalescing: handlers must cope with hours having passed.
- Skyrim quest dialogue conditions poll GameDaysPassed >= X (WICommentNextAllowed). CK wiki example scripts.
- Discrete-event simulation: priority queue by time; jump to next event time (next-event time progression). Wikipedia DES.
- Shadows of Doubt devblog 8 (15 Sep 2018): day's schedules precomputed at dawn (10-15 s); deviations live. Player claim (Steam, 4 Dec 2025) that skipped time is actively simulated - not a dev statement.
- Township devlog (~Aug 2026): schedule as ring of transition points; active = last at/before now; position depends only on clock -> correct instantly after a skip; no stored state.
- Oblivion: 4 process levels; low levels teleport actors cell to cell when estimated travel time elapsed (paavo blog 7 Jun 2025).

## Unreal (UE 5.8 source on this PC)
- LevelTick.cpp: DeltaSeconds *= EffectiveTimeDilation; FixupDeltaSeconds clamps; TimerManager.Tick only when !bIsPaused and not TimeOnly. Timers are transient, not saved (timers doc UE 5.8 says nothing on saving).
- BaseGame.ini: MaxGlobalTimeDilation=20 -> cannot fast forward hours by dilation.
- GameState doc (UE 5.8): for replicating to clients; GetServerWorldTimeSeconds "reliable time to use for replication". AGameState has ElapsedTime int32 replicated.
- UTickableWorldSubsystem: shares the UWorld lifetime and ticks with it; IsTickableWhenPaused false by default -> stops on pause.
- Fixed timestep: Fiedler "Fix Your Timestep!" 10 Jun 2004 (accumulator).

## Project state seen
- ue-probe LiveClock.h (30 Sep): 2 game min per real second (1:120, 12 real min/day), held while paused/conversation, hands back each hour crossed in order once, wait jumps hour at a time, saved as whole minutes + fraction. FixedClock.h: accumulator, 0.1 s step.
- 1:120 is faster than every reference found (KCD ~1:15, Skyrim 1:20, FO 1:30, Sims 1:60, MM ~1:80, Stardew ~1:86).
