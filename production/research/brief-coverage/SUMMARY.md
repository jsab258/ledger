# Brief coverage: what the project has to decide, and what nobody asked

STATUS: SPEC (stocktake). Written 2026-09-19 by the research lane. This asks
whether the briefs were the right ones, which is a different question from the
self-audit's, which asked whether each delivery answered its brief.

METHOD, and it is the coverage audits' method pointed inward. I did not start
from the topic list. I started from what this project has to decide and worked
back: `ledger-v2/respec/vision-pillars-v2.md`, the seven deliberately open
questions in `ledger-v2/open-questions.md`, the 18 decided records plus the
rulings log in `ledger-v2/respec/decision-register/`, `production/ladder.md`,
`production/quality-ladder.md`, `production/decision-queue.md`,
`game-design/decisions-pending.md`, the asset audit's 84 kinds and the coverage
audit's CROSS-CUTTING synthesis. Then, for each decision, I asked what a person
would need to know to decide it well and searched all 39 delivered summaries for
it.

## The pattern, stated first because it explains every gap below

**All thirty-nine topics are about the game or about other people's games. Not
one is about the studio.** The set was generated by one person thinking about
what he was building, so it is dense where the game is and empty where the
method is. That is the same blind spot the systems inventory had, and it has the
same cause.

Jafar's own example is the closest the set ever came: the postmortem and
small-team topics studied developers, and still studied human ones who shipped
before agentic tools existed. The lane looked at people and found the wrong
people, which reads as covered from the topic list and is not.

## The gaps, ranked by how much they would change a decision

**1. How one person directs a studio of agents.**
Bears on: the second of the project's two quieter goals, prove the method, and
on whether the structure in `ledger-v2/studio-v2/` is right at all. This is the
method by which every other decision gets executed, and it is the thing most
likely to fail.
Covered by: nothing, in 43 deliveries.
Commission: the agentic-studio-attempts topic, commissioned 2026-09-19.

**2. Content mass, as distinct from code mass.**
Bears on: whether the game can reach the breadth the asset audit says it needs.
That audit found 11 of 84 asset kinds exist, 47 part-done, 26 absent. The
project's strong opinion is that the assembly line generalises from kerbs to
everything, and `clothing-assembly-line` extended it to clothes on paper.
Nobody has asked what an agent-made content unit actually costs, what fraction
survives review, or where the line stops working.
Covered by: partially, and only for code-shaped content.
Commission: "Who has produced game content at volume with generative pipelines,
what share survived review, and what did they stop doing?"

**3. Which conversation model, doing exactly what job.**
Bears on: pillar 2, the live half of the moat, and the largest uncertain line in
the hardware floor. The project has a strong opinion with no evidence behind it:
that the model classifies and never adjudicates, and can therefore be small. The
only measured datapoint the lane has found points the other way (the RECHECK on
arXiv 2511.10277: a 1.1B model at 55 percent factual accuracy, a 130 MB model
its own authors will not recommend for dialogue).
Covered by: size and price, in hardware-floor and llm-inference-economics.
Never the capability question.
Commission: "What is the smallest model that reliably does closed-set
classification in a fixed persona, on measured evidence, and has anyone shipped
one in a game?"

**4. What happens when the simulation first meets the named cast.**
Bears on: D4, which puts combat before driving, and on the risk the lane itself
named as one of two that threaten us. `systemic-game-postmortems` found the
Oblivion failure and said the fix we must not copy is marking people
unkillable. It did not find what anyone did instead.
Covered by: the risk is named once. The solution is researched nowhere.
Commission: "How have shipped simulation games kept a story-critical cast alive
without lying to the player, and what did each approach cost?"

**5. How the Meridian Test is actually administered.**
Bears on: the project's only definition of success. Three of its four conditions
need a stranger to play for thirty minutes and describe the town as alive
without being prompted. Nothing addresses recruiting, running or scoring that,
and `production/stranger-test/` is a simulation sweep of gossip propagation
rather than a protocol for a human.
Covered by: nothing. Zero of 39 summaries mention a playtest, a tester or
recruiting.
Commission: "How do small teams run a thirty-minute first-impression test with
strangers, and how do they avoid coaching the answer they want?"

**6. Persistence at the scale the moat claims.**
Bears on: consequence persistence 95, the highest number in the moat, and on
whether a thirty-hour playthrough's memory survives. Internal soak and
save-chaos tests exist. No delivery asks what a save that remembers everything
costs, or what others have done about schema migration and corruption over a
long game.
Covered by: nothing outward-facing.
Commission: "What have long-memory simulation games done about save size,
migration and corruption across a long playthrough?"

**7. Music and radio.**
Bears on: open question 4, which is explicitly unresolved, has a moving legal
landscape and a named trigger (re-verify at the moment radio music is produced).
A 1990 British town with radio in it is a content class nobody has scoped.
Covered by: one incidental mention, in a list of allowlist categories.
Commission: "What are the licensing and production routes for period radio
music, and what changed after the Suno ruling of 2026-07-31?"

## What is genuinely well covered, so the list above is fair

The moat's design (rumour propagation, eyewitness testimony, group standing,
holding information, emergent legibility), the period (policing, crime fiction,
forensics, film stock), the visual route (photoreal on a budget, frame budget,
hardware floor), the voice (architecture, licensing, economics), and five
competitor audits with a cross-cutting synthesis. That is a serious body of work
on the game. The gaps are all on one side of a line, and the line is the studio.

## What this stocktake could not establish

- **Whether Jafar would rank these the same way.** The ranking is mine, by how
  much a finding would change a decision, and he owns the decisions.
- **Whether the seven are all of them.** I worked back from the decision
  documents that exist. A decision nobody has written down is invisible to this
  method, exactly as an unrecorded brief was invisible to the self-audit.
- **Whether gaps 2 and 3 are one gap.** Both are about what agents can be
  trusted to produce unsupervised. They may collapse into one commission once
  gap 1 is answered.
