# Grounded replies, measured before and after (30 September 2026)

Jafar's list after the adversarial audit, item 1: research the method first, test
picking the facts and intent before wording, and measure it against a fixed set
of newcomer questions, labelled independently, the fallback rate before and
after.

## The fixed set and its labels

- **The questions.** A newcomer's twenty first questions, each put to Sheila,
  Ron and Darren: sixty pairs (ClaimBench `firsts`, the real engine and its
  check, through Claude Code on the subscription; no key).
- **Can the character answer it?**
  - Two labellers labelled each pair apart (Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5, ClaimBench
    `answerable`). They agreed on 44.
  - A third (Fable 5.1) settled the 16 where they differed, by majority, so the
    labels never rest on whoever is testing a method against them.
  - Result: all sixty are at least partly answerable from what the character
    knows (38 yes, 22 partly), so every "that's all I know" on this set is a
    failure.
  - Files: bench/firsts-answerable.jsonl and firsts-answerable-rulings.json.
- **Each reply** was labelled for invented details by the two labellers apart,
  under the bench's LabelRule (ClaimBench `firsts-label`):
  - invented when both find one;
  - disputed when one does;
  - empty when it is the fallback line.

## What came back (out of 60 each run)

| version | runs | "that's all I know" | invented (both) | disputed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| this morning, before | 1 | 36 | 1 | 4 |
| today's version | 3 | 23, 23, 19 (mean 21.7) | 0, 3, 1 | 5, 5, 9 |
| the facts and intent planned first | 3 | 18, 18, 19 (mean 18.3) | 2, 2, 1 | 1, 1, 8 |

- **Today's version:**
  - the one to three facts that bear on his line are chosen by code before the
    reply is written;
  - a detail stated word for word in its cited item is cleared by code;
  - a people line clears who somebody is, in the present;
  - the second look has worked examples (the second and third runs).
- **Planned first:**
  - the writer sees what it knows as numbered facts, and plans in one tag
    (intent, one to three fact numbers) before the words;
  - the check also looks at the planned facts alone.

**Question by question**, over three runs each: planning fell back less on 16
questions and more on 15, and was the same on 29 (sign test p = 1.0).
Inventions over the 180 replies: 4 for today's version, 5 for planning.

## What it says

- **The day's change is real.** "That's all I know" fell from 36 of 60 to a mean
  of about 20, with inventions unchanged at about one or two in 60, labelled
  independently.
- **Planning first is no better than choosing the facts in code,** on this set.
  It costs more:
  - a numbered list of everything the character knows on every call;
  - a look more per flagged detail;
  - about a quarter of a second before the first word.

  By the rule "of two fine ways, the cheaper", it stays off behind its switch
  (ConversationEngine.PlanFirst; ClaimBench `firsts --plan`).
- **The rest are failures the check makes.** On every question the character
  can at least partly answer, what still falls back is a reply the check
  refuses twice: an embellishment it rightly stops ("diesel chits", "Father
  Walsh took the service"), or a paraphrase it still misses. That is where any
  further work lies.

## Afternoon: the second try narrowed (measured and set aside)

- **Tried:** when the first reply is refused, the second is told to answer in one
  or two short sentences from what bears on his line and nothing else
  (ConversationEngine.NarrowRedraft; ClaimBench `firsts --narrow`). Saying the
  chosen facts word for word was ruled out first: they include the character's
  secrets, notes on how to speak, and lines about them in the third person.
- **Result, three runs labelled the same way:** "that's all I know" 30, 26, 24
  (mean 26.7) against today's 23, 23, 19; question by question 14 better and 30
  worse (sign test p = 0.02); inventions 2, 0, 4. Worse, so the switch stays off.
- **What it says:** two ways of prompting the writer (planning first, a narrower
  second try) have not beaten today's version. The refused details are texture
  nobody has written for the character (who took the funeral, how Mickey ran
  things, last week's takings, the drivers' hours), which the writer fills in and
  the check rightly stops. The lever left is the knowledge itself, written ahead,
  as Valve's talk has it: the method changes from prompting to writing.
