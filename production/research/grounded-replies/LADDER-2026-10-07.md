# The ladder before "that's all I know", measured (7 October 2026)

The talk task of 7 October, item 1: characters too often say "that's all I know"
while they still know relevant things. Give them a ladder they must climb before
giving up (the next relevant fact; then where to ask; then who told them; only
then a refusal in character, with a reason), track what each has told each
listener, and measure on the existing question bench, before and after: far
fewer empty answers, with no invented facts.

## What was built (ledger/Assets/Scripts/Core/TalkLadder.cs; handover production/handovers/talk.md)

- **When it runs:** only where both drafts were refused by the claim check, the
  one place "that's all I know" is said. Everything before it is unchanged: a
  first reply's prompt is today's word for word (tested), so **one run measures
  both**: the engine keeps what today's talk would have said (`LastWouldHaveSaid`)
  beside what the ladder says. Each comparison below is paired, turn by turn.
- **What is relevant:** the rule table's facts where a rule chose them; else the
  facts the refused drafts cited for what they got right (the check's own list
  says which known item backs each detail), and of those only what the check's
  model, in one short call, reads as answering his line (`JudgeLadder`; if that
  call fails, a fact must share a distinctive word with his line). Code says the
  fact; the model only classifies. Never facts chosen by shared words alone:
  measured on 30 September, 84 of 123 such lines missed the question.
- **What can be said:** only the street's facts in their written plain words, and
  who told them a story, read from the memory of the telling. Never a card's own
  facts or secrets. Somebody else's job ("Sheila keeps the books") is a pointer
  ("You'd want Sheila for that"), unless he asked who they are.
- **The refusal** comes only at the top of a climb, when he presses on what they
  have already given. A first question they hold nothing on keeps their honest
  "that's all I know": a reason given to an innocent question (the first run's
  "People remember who was asking", to "Where's the nearest phone box?") claimed a
  secret nobody keeps.
- **What was told** is kept per listener, saved with the talk, never said plainly
  twice, and the facts already told are listed for the writer of later replies.

## How it was measured

- **The bench:** ClaimBench `firsts`, the real engine and its check, with the talk
  program's own switches (the rule table, the plain line, the ladder) and each
  line's model by the kind of moment, as the game chooses it. Three sets, as on 30
  September: **the fresh sixty** (20 questions to each of the three, written after
  the talk was tuned; the brief's "23 to 25 empty"), **thirty nobody can answer**,
  and **the sixty the talk was tuned on**.
- **Through Claude Code on the subscription**, the bench's own way and the way its
  30 September numbers were taken (a key run of the fresh sixty would have cost
  about $1.40 at API rates; the task's key went to the two checks).
- **Where the character could answer** is the bench's fixed label (two labellers
  apart and a third, 30 September, before the ladder existed).
- **Every line the ladder said** was labelled apart by two labellers (Opus 5.5,
  Sonnet 5): does it answer what he asked (answers, partly, declines, beside the
  point; the lower reading stands), and does it state anything the character does
  not know (invented when both find a detail).
- **A fourth, never-seen set**, written after the ladder was built: 20 more
  questions (firsts-fresh.txt) to each of the three, labelled for who could
  answer by the same two labellers and a third (46 of 60 settled; the third
  labeller ruled only 2 of 16 disagreements, so 14 pairs count neither way).
- **The versions.** v1 refused with a reason on a first question; changed to the
  honest "don't know" there. v2 added job facts as pointers and kept every turn's
  leads; it ran live on the three sets. Its lines, labelled, were half beside the
  point, so two ways of choosing were tried on **exactly v2's turns**: the ladder
  is code, acting only where both drafts were refused, so each turn is replayed
  through it with the same drafts and citations (ClaimBench `ladder-replay`). v3
  chose by shared distinctive words; **the shipped version chooses by the
  model's judgement** (`--judge`, Haiku 4.5, on LEDGER's key). The never-seen set
  ran live with v3 and was replayed with the judgement.
- **Labelled the same way every time:** the labellers' answers-or-partly,
  declines and beside-the-point for each ladder line; invented when both
  labellers find a detail the character does not know.

## What came back: the shipped ladder (the judged one)

"Empty" is "that's all I know" or the ladder's refusal; "could answer" is the
bench's fixed label, set before the ladder existed.

| set | where they could answer | empty today | empty with the ladder | the ladder's lines: answer / partly / decline / beside | invented |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **the fresh sixty** (the brief's bench) | 48 | **22** | **12** | 4 / 4 / 2 / 0 | 0 |
| the sixty the talk was tuned on | 60 | 5 | 2 | 3 / 6 / 0 / 0 | 0 |
| thirty nobody can answer | 8 | 1 | 0 | 0 / 1 / 0 / 3 | 0 (1 disputed) |
| twenty more, never seen (46 labelled) | 29 | 7 | 5 | 1 / 0 / 1 / 4 | 0 |
| **all four** | **145** | **35** | **19** | **8 / 11 / 3 / 7** | **0** |

- **Empty answers where the character could answer fell by nearly half**, 35 to
  19 (on the brief's fresh sixty, 22 to 12), and **nothing was invented**: every
  ladder line is a written fact or a written line, and the labellers found no
  invented detail in any of the 29.
- **Where nobody could answer** (51 questions), today's honest "that's all I
  know" (15 of them) became a fact beside the point 3 times (to "What did Mickey
  do after he locked up on his last night?", Ron and Darren said how Mickey died).
- **Seven of the 29 ladder lines are beside the point**, four of them on the
  never-seen set: asked how the drivers are paid, or how busy the rank gets at
  night, Sheila says there are two drivers, one by day and one by night.
- **The judgement costs one short call** on the turns the ladder climbs: median
  0.54 s, slowest 1.05 s over 18 (LEDGER's key, logged), and about $0.0002. It
  changed its mind once in 18 between two runs.

## The versions beside each other (all on the same turns)

|---|---|---|
| today (no ladder) | 22 / - | 28 / - |
| v2: every cited fact | 6 / 9 of 18 | 7 / 13 of 35 |
| v3: a shared distinctive word | 14 / 1 of 9 | 20 / 2 of 17 |
| **shipped: the model's judgement** | **12 / 0 of 10** | **14 / 3 of 23** |

- Words could make the ladder careful or broad, never both: "What is this
  place?" shares no word with the office's address; "How long has the office
  been here?" shares one and is not answered by it. The judgement reads both
  right.

## What is left, and the lever

- **Most of the 19 still empty have no written fact that answers them** (the
  weather, whether he may smoke, which cars run, a greeting): the gap is the
  knowledge, as the 30 September note found, not the ladder.
- **What they know of the street's people** (the P lines: "Father Walsh, who says
  mass and calls on the old") is never said plainly yet; "Who's the priest round
  here?" stays empty though every character knows. Giving those lines plain
  words is the next cheapest gain.
- **The beside-the-point lines** are the judge passing a fact about the same
  thing that does not answer the question; a stricter judgement prompt, measured
  on these turns, is the next try.

## Who told me (item 2), counted and tested

**Counted** (ClaimBench `whotold`, no model, nothing spent): a seeded town of 14
of the street's people passes three witnessed stories round for a day, three
seeds. Of 177 second-hand copies, who told the holder was known by none before
the change and by all 177 after, each matching the holder's own memory of the
telling, and all 177 kept through a save and a load. Of 115 heard accounts, the
holder's reason named who told them in none before ("I heard that ...") and in
all 115 after ("Sheila told me that ...").

**Tested,** ledger/TalkTests: every retelling records who told the hearer (Tick,
CompareNotes), a sighting of their own clears it, saves keep it (written only
beyond the first witness, so older saves and the golden table read as before),
and a heard account's reason names the teller ("Sheila told me that ...", through
the talk program's `toldBy`). The ladder's third rung reads the teller from the
memory of the telling. Port to the game: production/handovers/talk.md.
