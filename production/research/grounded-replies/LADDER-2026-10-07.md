# The ladder before "that's all I know", measured (7 October 2026)

The talk task of 7 October, item 1: characters too often say "that's all I know"
while they still know relevant things. Give them a ladder they must climb before
giving up (the next relevant fact; then where to ask; then who told them; only
then a refusal in character, with a reason), track what each has told each
listener, and measure on the existing question bench, before and after: far
fewer empty answers, with no invented facts.

## What was built (ledger/Assets/Scripts/Core/TalkLadder.cs; handover production/handovers/talk.md)

- **When it runs:** only where both drafts were refused by the claim check, the
  one place "that's all I know" is said. Everything before it is unchanged: a
  first reply's prompt is today's word for word (tested), so **one run measures
  both**: the engine keeps what today's talk would have said (`LastWouldHaveSaid`)
  beside what the ladder says. Each comparison below is paired, turn by turn.
- **What is relevant:** the rule table's facts where a rule chose them; else the
  facts the refused drafts cited for what they got right (the check's own list
  says which known item backs each detail). The writer judges what bears on his
  line; code says it. Never facts chosen by shared words: measured on 30
  September, 84 of 123 such lines missed the question.
- **What can be said:** only the street's facts in their written plain words, and
  who told them a story, read from the memory of the telling. Never a card's own
  facts or secrets. Somebody else's job ("Sheila keeps the books") is a pointer
  ("You'd want Sheila for that"), unless he asked who they are.
- **The refusal** comes only at the top of a climb, when he presses on what they
  have already given. A first question they hold nothing on keeps their honest
  "that's all I know": a reason given to an innocent question (the first run's
  "People remember who was asking", to "Where's the nearest phone box?") claimed a
  secret nobody keeps.
- **What was told** is kept per listener, saved with the talk, never said plainly
  twice, and the facts already told are listed for the writer of later replies.

## How it was measured

- **The bench:** ClaimBench `firsts`, the real engine and its check, with the talk
  program's own switches (the rule table, the plain line, the ladder) and each
  line's model by the kind of moment, as the game chooses it. Three sets, as on 30
  September: **the fresh sixty** (20 questions to each of the three, written after
  the talk was tuned; the brief's "23 to 25 empty"), **thirty nobody can answer**,
  and **the sixty the talk was tuned on**.
- **Through Claude Code on the subscription**, the bench's own way and the way its
  30 September numbers were taken (a key run of the fresh sixty would have cost
  about $1.40 at API rates; the task's key went to the two checks).
- **Where the character could answer** is the bench's fixed label (two labellers
  apart and a third, 30 September, before the ladder existed).
- **Every line the ladder said** was labelled apart by two labellers (Opus 5.5,
  Sonnet 5): does it answer what he asked (answers, partly, declines, beside the
  point; the lower reading stands), and does it state anything the character does
  not know (invented when both find a detail).
- **Two versions were run.** The first (v1) refused with a reason on a first
  question; it was changed to the honest "don't know" there, and a job fact made
  a pointer, after reading its lines. The second (v2) is the code on the branch,
  and its runs are the measurement of record.
