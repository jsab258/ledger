# Why each "that's all I know" was empty (30 September 2026)

Jafar's list of 30 September afternoon, item 1, in the order of the research
note production/research/grounded-dialogue-selection: for each empty answer, the
cause: the wrong facts chosen, a real invention, or a true paraphrase refused.

## How it was found

- **A fresh run of today's version** on the sixty fixed newcomer questions
  (ClaimBench `firsts`, the real engine and its check through Claude Code on the
  subscription; no key): 21 of 60 ended in "that's all I know", in line with
  the three runs this morning (23, 23, 19).
- **The engine now keeps each turn's evidence:** everything the character knew
  for the check, the facts chosen as bearing on his line, and what each draft
  was refused for (ConversationEngine.LastKnown, LastBearing, LastInvented,
  LastRefusedAgain).
- **Labelled apart** by two models (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5), each saying which
  known items answer the question and which item, if any, states or plainly
  implies each refused detail; a third (Fable 5.1) settled the 15 of 21 where
  they differed on a point the cause turns on (ClaimBench `causes`). That much
  disagreement means the per-answer names below are rough; the counts under
  them are the firmer finding.
- Each answer: F:/LedgerTools/town-scratch/arm-C1/causes.jsonl.

## What came back

| cause, one per empty answer (code's order of precedence) | of 21 |
|---|---|
| every refused detail was true (a true paraphrase refused) | 1 |
| the wrong facts chosen: something they knew answers it, none of it chosen | 11 |
| a real invention: the answer was chosen, the draft added what nobody gave | 9 |

The causes overlap, so counted each way:

- **Something the character knew answers every one of the 21** (by the
  labellers' reading), so none of these should have been empty.
- **The choice missed in 12 of 21:** none of the answering facts was among
  those chosen, and in 7 nothing was chosen at all. The choice is by shared
  words (ClaimCheck.Bearing), and it fails on plain questions: for "Who can I
  trust?" it chose Sheila's secret about the real book; for "Do you work for me
  now?", the dispatcher and a locksmith; for "Where can I get something to
  eat?", nothing, though she knows the cafe.
- **The check refused true details:** 37 of the 126 refused details (29%) were
  backed by an item the character held ("one comes in at night" by "two cab
  drivers on the rank, one by day and one by night"), and 16 of the 21 empty
  answers had at least one true detail refused among the invented ones.
- **The writer invented the rest,** 89 details: texture nobody wrote for them
  (who took the funeral, how Mickey ran things, the phone ringing this morning,
  the ledger open on the desk).

## What it means for the list

- **Item 2 (say the chosen facts plainly)** needs the choice fixed first: with
  the choice as it is, the plain statement would say the wrong fact in about
  half the cases, and could say a secret. The statement also has to keep
  secrets out and speak as the character (the items are written in first,
  second and third person).
- **Item 3 (tune the checker)** has a clear target: nearly a third of what it
  refuses is true.
- **Item 4 (the rule table)** is where the choice gets its structure: the kind
  of question decides which facts can answer it.
