# Grounded replies without the fallback (research note, 29 September 2026)

For town list T1, after two attempts at the fallback rate moved nothing: the
street's plain facts, then the character's own plain opening. The rule (Jafar,
29 September) is research after two failed attempts, and one more try. A
separate helper was given the problem, not a theory, with about thirty minutes
and web sources only. Its note is kept here as it gave it; "inference" marks
the helper's own reasoning.

**The problem.** LEDGER's characters may state only what the simulation gives
them. A second model call rejects any reply with an unsupported specific, and
after one failed redraft the character says a fixed "that's all I know" line.
Over half of a newcomer's first twenty answers end in that line: partly the
model inventing colour, partly the check rejecting close paraphrases.

## Findings

1. **Wording alone changes little.**
   - Prompted refusal still left role-play agents weakest on questions outside
     the role's knowledge (Tell Me What You Don't Know,
     https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16913, September 2024).
   - In grounded question answering, refusal accuracy fell below 50% on
     multi-document tasks; neither a bigger model nor longer reasoning helped
     (RefusalBench, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10390, 12 October 2025).
   - Characters often recognise a question is outside what they know and
     answer anyway (CHARM, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01352, 1 September 2026).
   - With retrieved context added, Claude 3.5 Sonnet declined to answer less
     often (84.1% fell to 52%) and answered wrongly instead (Sufficient
     Context, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037, November 2024, ICLR 2025).
2. **Temperature and length.**
   - Temperature 0, 0.6 and 1.0 differed by under a point in hallucination, and
     replies with more claims hallucinated more (HalluHard,
     https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01031, 1 February 2026).
   - But telling models to be concise cut their resistance to hallucination by
     up to 20%: no room to say what is wrong or unknown (Phare, Giskard,
     https://www.giskard.ai/knowledge/good-answers-are-not-necessarily-factual-answers-an-analysis-of-hallucination-in-leading-llms,
     30 April 2025).
3. **Choosing the facts before speaking works.**
   - A detective game picked the permitted fact, then wrote the line, then
     checked it: hallucinated turns fell from 17.8% to 6.3%, at the cost of
     some abrupt reveals (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23043, 19 September 2026).
   - Select content, then plan each sentence, then write: tighter citations
     with equal or better attribution (Attribute First, then Generate,
     https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17104, ACL 2024).
4. **Checkers err both ways, and missed inference is a named cause.** In
   AttributionBench, 39% of wrong "not supported" verdicts came from the checker
   failing a needed inference. Step-by-step reasoning mostly swapped one error
   for the other (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15089, 23 February 2024).
5. **The usual standard accepts paraphrase and direct inference.**
   - The AIS standard counts a statement as supported when "all parts can be
     directly inferred from the source". Human raters agreed only moderately,
     alpha 0.69 to 0.79 (https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/, Computational
     Linguistics, 2023).
   - FACTS Grounding labels each sentence supported, unsupported, contradictory
     or "needs no support". The best single checker scored a macro-F1 of about
     70, so the benchmark averages three (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03200,
     6 January 2025).
6. **Worked examples help checkers.** FaithJudge puts human-labelled answers to
   the same prompt into the checker's prompt: balanced accuracy up 2 to 10
   points with the same model, and 75.1% to 85.1% on data-to-text
   (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04847, 7 May 2025).
7. **Partial support is the hard case.** The best metric told full from no
   support at ROC-AUC 92.6%, but full from partial at only 82.3%
   (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15264, June 2024).
8. **"Cite, then verify" designs exist, but still check content with a model.**
   - Anthropic's Citations feature takes each known item as a block; pointers
     are valid, stream with the reply, and are not billed as output. It cannot
     be combined with structured outputs
     (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations, read
     29 September 2026).
   - A valid pointer proves little: up to 57% of citations were added after the
     fact (Correctness is not Faithfulness, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18004,
     23 December 2024).
   - VeriCite checks each claim against its cited passage with a small
     entailment model; removing that check badly hurt citation quality
     (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11394, October 2025).
9. **Games.**
   - Generative agents added embellishments in 1.3% of answers about
     relationships (Park et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442, UIST 2023).
   - Inworld limits knowledge through filters, with no published rates
     (https://inworld.ai/blog/improved-contextual-mesh, undated).
   - An in-game coaching agent driven by live game state rose from 61–70% to
     96.7% grounded turns, first words at 1.5 s
     (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27606, 23 September 2026).
   - No studio found publishes how often its characters fall back.

## What it suggests for LEDGER (inference, by expected effect)

- **Change the check's standard and give it examples:** stated or directly
  implied, as a plain listener would take it; four labels (stated, implied,
  unsupported, no claim); 10 to 20 labelled examples from our own flagged
  turns. Measure on the bench's paired set.
- **Repair the reply instead of replacing it:** drop only the sentence that
  failed, or redraft with "you don't know X; say so, then say what you do
  know". The fixed line only when nothing supported is left.
- **Make the speaker cite, and let code do the first pass;** the model check
  then sees only the sentence and its cited items, a shorter input.
- **Have the speaker choose one to three items before drafting,** with
  in-character examples of not knowing that still offer something known; cap
  specifics per reply, not words.
- **Leave temperature alone;** more "only what you know" wording is unlikely to
  help.

## What LEDGER did with it (29 September)

The third and last attempt under the rule was the repair: drop the sentences
carrying what the check flagged, and say the rest without a second draft.

- **Offline,** on the bench's saved checks, it left something standing in 125
  of 137 flagged replies.
- **Live,** on a newcomer's sixty first questions through Claude Code,
  "that's all I know" fell from about 32 to 4. But some answers were nonsense,
  kept sentences pointing back at dropped ones ("That's the measure of it.").
- **Kept only up to the first flagged sentence,** it fell to 20.
- **An independent check failed it:**
  - A kept opening can affirm what was dropped: "Yes." before the invented
    names; "Seen it myself." before an invented van.
  - Matching a flag to its sentence is unsafe, since 41% of the check's flags
    are not worded as in the reply. In one streamed path, the very sentence
    that failed its own check would have been said.

Set aside, as Jafar's rule has it after three attempts (the street's facts, the
character's plain opening, the repair). The route left is the check's own
standard and examples (town list q), measured on the bench.
