# BRIEF: content mass, and what happens to judgement when generation stops being the constraint

topic on the studio side of the line.

## Why this topic exists

From the commission:

> The agentic-studio-attempts delivery already found the shape of this: prompt
> volume widens the decision surface faster than reviewers can evaluate it, so
> the bottleneck moves from creation to judgement, and generated meshes still
> need a human pass for topology, UVs and rig readiness. A studio that can
> produce a thousand assets and review ten has not solved content mass, it has
> moved the queue. Our asset audit says twenty-six of eighty-four kinds are
> absent and forty-seven are part-done, so this is about to be load-bearing.

## The question, in his words

> So the question is not whether generative pipelines produce assets. It is what
> teams do about the reviewing problem once they do.

Specifically:

> - How is generated content actually judged at volume: by whom, against what,
>   and how fast.
> - What can be checked mechanically and what cannot.
> - Who has built automated acceptance for generated assets and what does it
>   catch.
> - What fraction of generated work is rejected in practice, and what happens to
>   the rejects.
> - Where did teams decide the pipeline was not worth it and go back to hand
>   work.
> - And the second-order problem that delivery named, which nobody here has
>   considered: generated assets arriving without meaningful metadata, and the
>   searchability problem that grows across hundreds of sessions.
