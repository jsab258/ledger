# Period print and fonts: Britain 1988–1992

Research, 1 October 2026, by a separate research helper.

What British print, paperwork and signage looked like around 1990, and which free fonts can carry that look in a game that will be sold. Each fact carries a source number (list at the end). **JUDGEMENT** marks my own opinion, not a source. **HOLE** means not found. Note: most design, photo and paint-chart sites were blocked by this session's network proxy. Wikipedia, GitHub and one Sheetlines PDF were opened; everything else is from search snippets.

## (a) The looks

### 1. Council notices and public information

- **Planning site notices.** Under the Town and Country Planning General Development Order 1988, art. 12B, a site notice stayed up for at least seven days in the 21 days before a decision. If it was torn down through no fault of the applicant, that was excused provided reasonable steps had been taken to protect and replace it [S1 SNIPPET]. So paper notices on posts and railings were normal street furniture in 1990.
  - What they looked like: typed or word-processed A4, photocopied, often in a plastic sleeve, taped or tied to a lamp column, with the council's name and crest at the top in capitals. **JUDGEMENT / HOLE**: no 1990 photograph could be opened.
- **Typefaces of the period.**
  - Transport, designed 1957–63 by Jock Kinneir and Margaret Calvert. It comes in two weights: Medium for white on dark signs, Heavy for black on white [O2]. Britain's road signs went over to it on 1 January 1965. It is a softened refinement of Akzidenz-Grotesk [S2 SNIPPET].
  - Rail Alphabet (1965, the same designers) was British Rail's, and was later used by the NHS and British Airports Authority [O3].
  - Gill Sans: the LNER from 1929, British Railways' standard lettering from 1948 until the 1965 rebrand, Penguin covers from 1935, and Ordnance Survey [O5].
  - Helvetica and Univers also appear: Helvetica in British Telecom's 1981 identity by Banks & Miles [S3 SNIPPET]; Univers on OS maps (see Maps).
- **Street name plates.** Black capitals on white. Many British street signs used Kindersley's lettering [O6; also the project's street-clutter-1990 note]. The project already renders these in Marcellus SC.
- **Parking.** Yellow lines, with small time plates on posts. Today's regulations require a yellow background with a black border for parking control signs [S4 SNIPPET, current rules]. The exact 1981 plate design was not confirmed (HOLE).
- **Police appeal boards** (the yellow "Appeal for witnesses / Can you help?" A-boards). The only dated photograph found is London, December 2007 [S5 SNIPPET]. Whether these boards existed in 1990, and in what layout, is a **HOLE**. Do not rely on them without a period photograph. A typed or handwritten appeal poster in a police station window is the safer choice (JUDGEMENT).
- **Post Office and BT.**
  - In 1986 the Post Office split into Royal Mail Letters, Royal Mail Parcels and Post Office Counters; Parcels became Parcelforce in 1990 [S6 SNIPPET].
  - British Telecom's 1981 identity used a yellow circle with a purple "T"; the "piper" replaced it on 2 April 1991 [S3 SNIPPET].
  - Post boxes in 1990 said "POST OFFICE" (project street-clutter note).
  - These are real brands and logos: under canon, use only their colours and the feel of their type, never the marks.

### 2. Newspapers, 1988–1992

- **Nationals.**
  - The Sun went tabloid in 1969 and, copying the Daily Mirror, put its title in white on a red rectangle. That is the origin of "red tops" [O10].
  - Its masthead is a heavy italic Franklin Gothic [S7 SNIPPET].
  - One snippet says its headlines were set in Ludlow's Tempo, a 1930 geometric sans by R. Hunter Middleton [O7; S8 SNIPPET, **uncertain**]. Tempo has never been fully digitised [O7].
  - Tabloid pattern: bold sans headlines with serif body text on cheap paper [S7 SNIPPET].
  - The Times went from Times Europa (1972) back to Times Roman on 30 August 1982, then to Times Millennium in 1991 [O11a].
- **Local evening papers.**
  - The Grimsby paper was the *Grimsby Evening Telegraph* from 1932 to 2002. It first printed full colour across its editorial pages in 1995 [O8], so in 1990 it was black and white with at most spot colour (inference).
  - The *Hull Daily Mail* went tabloid in 1986 [O9].
  - 1990 front pages exist in the British Newspaper Archive (e.g. Hull Daily Mail, 21 June 1990), behind a paywall [S9 SNIPPET].
  - Masthead and headline faces of local papers in 1990: **HOLE** (the logo-archive site was blocked). JUDGEMENT: many old regional mastheads were blackletter or heavy serif; headlines in condensed grotesques or Century-type serifs; body in a Times-like face.
- **Contents bills.** Four- or five-word "contents bills" were posted outside newsagents to sell the evening paper [S10 SNIPPET]. A very period street and loading-screen device.
- **Newsprint.** Off-white; it yellows and goes brittle in air and light because of lignin [O12]. No measured colour was found (HOLE). Hex values below are JUDGEMENT.

### 3. Typewritten and printed forms, ledgers, notebooks

- **Typewriters.**
  - Courier was designed for IBM by Howard Kettler in 1956 [O20, Courier Prime description]. Prestige Elite (Clayton Smith, IBM, 1953) was, with Courier, "extremely popular" on electric typewriters, especially the Selectric [O13].
  - Selectric type styles included Courier 72, Prestige Pica 72, Prestige Elite 72, Letter Gothic, Orator (labels), Artisan, Light Italic and Script [S11 SNIPPET].
  - By the 1980s offices used daisy-wheel electronic typewriters. The daisy wheel was invented at Diablo in 1970 and survived into the early 1990s in typewriters [O14]. Office electronic typewriters had their heyday from the early 1980s to the early 1990s (Triumph-Adler, Canon, Olivetti ET) [S12 SNIPPET]. Olivetti's ET personal 55 dates from 1987 [S13 SNIPPET].
  - So a 1990 office letter or form is a crisp, even daisy-wheel impression in Courier 10 or Prestige Elite 12, not a worn manual typewriter (JUDGEMENT from O14). A cheap back-office machine could still be an old manual (JUDGEMENT).
- **Ledgers and cash books.**
  - Bound account books with ruled "cash columns": Collins Cathedral books use "azure ledger paper" in a red leather-grain cover; Guildhall books have a dark blue vinyl hard cover and a sewn spine [S14 SNIPPET, current products]. That both ranges were sold in 1990 is likely but unverified (JUDGEMENT).
  - Ruling (JUDGEMENT, standard British account-book practice): pale blue horizontal "feint" lines; red vertical lines dividing £ and p columns; a double red line under the headings; a date column and a narrative column on the left.
- **Memo books.** Silvine's red cover exercise book dates from 1960 [S15 SNIPPET]. Small red memo books were common in 1990 (JUDGEMENT). It is a real brand: evoke the red card cover, never the name.
- **Police pocket notebooks.**
  - Each has a serial number on the cover and numbered pages that must not be removed.
  - Errors are struck through with a single line. Gaps at line ends are ruled off, and each entry ends with a line across the page and the officer's signature [O15; S16 SNIPPET].
  - These rules are a good model for Mickey's fare book (JUDGEMENT).
- **Booking records.** The minicab operator had to record every booking in the form the council set (Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1976 s.56, from the project's cab-office notes). A small 1990 office most likely ran on paper and voice radio (project note cab-office-interior-1990).
- **Embossed labels.** Dymo was founded in 1958. Its plastic tape is embossed so the letters turn white by stress-whitening [O16], typically white capitals on coloured tape [S17 SNIPPET]. Popular from 1958 to the late 1980s [S17 SNIPPET]; so in 1990 they would be on older box files, shelves and switches (JUDGEMENT).
- **Rubber stamps, carbons, VAT forms.** Not researched to source (HOLE). JUDGEMENT: violet or red stamp ink, and a blue-violet blur on carbon copies.

### 4. Shop, street, bus and rail signage

- **Shop fascias.**
  - Letraset (founded 1959; dry-transfer lettering from 1961) was being replaced by desktop publishing in the late 1980s [O17].
  - Hand signwriting faced stiff competition from computer-cut vinyl in the 1980s (North American source) [O18].
  - So in 1990 a provincial street mixes old hand-signwritten fascias, plastic light-box signs and new cut-vinyl lettering (JUDGEMENT).
  - A small 1980 minicab office painted its phone number large on the window; another, photographed in 2007, has a boarded cabin with a sign of phone numbers (project note cab-office-interior-1990, Alamy A55REH).
- **Buses.**
  - The National Bus Company identity was by Norman Wilson, 1972: poppy red (about 24 companies) or leaf green (about 14), with white relief; a double-N symbol; fleet names in Wilson's National lettering, based on Akzidenz-Grotesk [O19].
  - Deregulation came in 1986 and privatisation in 1986–88. Companies returned to their own liveries and the double-N disappeared [O19]. A 1990 street shows faded NBC red or green next to new local liveries.
  - Destination blinds: linen, later Mylar film, white on black. London's used Johnston. Machine-made, outsourced blinds came in the late 1980s [S18 SNIPPET, London]. Provincial blind lettering: HOLE.
- **Rail.** In 1990 British Rail stations still used Rail Alphabet [O3]. The BR double arrow is a real logo and must not appear.

### 5. Maps (for the paper map in the Ledger)

- **Ordnance Survey lettering** [O1]:
  - Univers adopted in 1969 with metrication, for the 1:50,000 and 1:10,000;
  - Times Roman on the 1:25,000 Second Series;
  - Gill Sans for map margins from about 1964;
  - a blackletter called "Lutheran" for antiquities;
  - the 1:250,000 relettered in Univers in 1985.
  - Geograph adds that on the 1:50,000 (Landranger) towns are in Univers capitals, sized by population, with villages in upper and lower case [S19 SNIPPET].
  - Water names in italic: JUDGEMENT, general OS practice.
- **A–Z street atlas.** First published in colour in 1985. It contains deliberate "trap streets" to catch copying [O21].
  - Today's A–Z road atlases colour roads as: motorways blue, primary routes green, A roads red, B roads orange, C roads white [S20 SNIPPET, current].
  - The 1985–90 street atlas key is a **HOLE**.
  - A–Z is a real brand: copy the feel (hand-drawn street grid, condensed capital street names, a coloured road hierarchy, a grid index), never the cover or name.

## (b) Font table

**How each was checked.** Each "verified" entry was checked in three places on 1 October 2026:
- the google/fonts repository folder (ofl/ or apache/), plus the `license:` field in its METADATA.pb;
- the licence string inside the font file itself (name table);
- the presence of the £ sign (U+00A3); all fonts below have it.

**Labels.** "Var only" means google/fonts holds only a variable-font file for that family. "RFN" means the font has a Reserved Font Name and must be renamed if we modify or rebuild it (OFL FAQ 5.9 [O22]). Closeness ratings are JUDGEMENT.

**OFL in a sold game.** The OFL FAQ (v1.1-update7, November 2023) [O22] says:
- 1.4: you may sell software that bundles OFL fonts, and it names "games and entertainment software".
- 1.10–1.12: embedding inside a program is allowed, in full or as a subset, and only then may the licence text be left out.
- 1.1 and 1.13: pictures made with the font (such as the name-plate images) carry no restriction.

The project's own rule, shipping the licence text beside the font, is stricter than required, which is fine.

## (c) Kits

All hex values are screen approximations. Paint colours are from paint-chart snippets, whose figures vary between sources; everything else is JUDGEMENT. Check against a photograph in the game's own camera and exposure before using.

### Kit 1: Council and Transport

For menus, prompts, subtitles, notices and signs.
- **Fonts:**
  - Work Sans (Medium or SemiBold) for headings and prompts;
  - Arimo or Liberation Sans for typed council notices;
  - Tinos for notice body text;
  - Marcellus SC for street plates.
- **Colours:**
  - notice paper #F2F0EA; toner black #1E1E1E;
  - BS 381C 538 Post Office red #A23B3C (sources range #9C2629 to #C41C22) [S25];
  - BS 381C 227 deep Brunswick green #254432 [S25], or rgb(30,62,46) = #1E3E2E [O23];
  - BS 381C 309 canary yellow #EEC626 and 356 golden yellow #F5A920 [S25], for any yellow warning board.
- **Materials:** A4 photocopy in a polythene sleeve, Sellotape gone yellow, cable ties on a lamp column, rain-run ink, black-on-white enamel or aluminium plates.

### Kit 2: Evening paper

For the title screen, loading screen and newspaper props.
- **Fonts:**
  - masthead: UnifrakturMaguntia, or Old Standard TT Bold capitals, for a fictional *Meridian Evening* paper;
  - headlines: Libre Franklin Black, or League Gothic or Oswald for condensed;
  - contents bills: Anton;
  - body: Tinos or PT Serif.
- **Colours:** fresh newsprint #E9E4D8; yellowed #DCD2BC; ink #262626; spot red #C41C22. Do not copy a real red-top masthead.
- **Materials:** five or six columns with fine column rules, coarse halftone photographs, ink set-off and the central fold, black-and-white with only spot colour (no full colour before 1995 [O8]), and a contents bill clipped to a newsagent's A-board.

### Kit 3: Typewriter and ledger

For the Ledger notebook, the fare book and the typing box.
- **Fonts:**
  - typed: Courier Prime, or Cutive Mono as a lighter alternative;
  - Special Elite only if Apache 2.0 is allowed;
  - handwriting: Caveat for the bookkeeper, Reenie Beanie for drivers' ballpoint, Patrick Hand for neat figures.
- **Colours:**
  - azure ledger paper #E4ECEF; feint blue rule #8FAACC; red cash-column rule #B8443A;
  - cover red #8E2B2B or dark blue vinyl #1E2A44;
  - ballpoint blue #1D3C8F; carbon-copy blue-violet #3A3F8C; ribbon black #2A2A2A; stamp violet #5B3F8C.
- **Materials:**
  - £ and p columns, numbered pages, crossings-out with one line and lines ruling off gaps (as in police notebooks [O15]);
  - carbon-copy blur, staple holes;
  - ash smudges (tobacco is allowed).

### Kit 4: Minicab card and Dymo

For the office front, the phone and in-game labels.
- **Fonts:**
  - League Gothic or Anton for phone numbers and "24 HOURS";
  - Libre Franklin Bold for small print;
  - Abril Fatface for a signwritten window or fascia.
- **Dymo labels:** no labelling font has a verified licence. Set capitals in Oswald or Archivo Narrow and emboss them with a shader, or draw our own 40-odd glyphs (JUDGEMENT).
- **Colours:** tape red #B22222, blue #1F4E99, black #1A1A1A, green #2E6B3A, with white letters; card #F5F3EE or #F2D64B.
- **Materials:** rounded tape ends, tape curling at the edges, uneven letter spacing, a phone number painted large on the window glass.

### Kit 5: Map, optional

For the paper map in the Ledger.
- **Fonts:**
  - Work Sans for place names in capitals, sized by importance (the Univers role, after O1);
  - Tinos italic for water;
  - UnifrakturMaguntia for old sites;
  - Archivo Narrow for condensed street names.
- **Colours:** roads in the modern A–Z order (blue, green, red, orange), unverified for 1990.
- **Materials:** a fold grid, an index grid, a hand-drawn street plan.
