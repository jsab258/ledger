## 1. The method: how a game's identity is made, in order

The published sources agree on what a brand is made of. The order of the stages is mostly my JUDGEMENT, put together from those sources.

**What a brand is now.** PlayStation Studios Creative Arts gave a talk at GDC in March 2026, "Beyond the Logo: Crafting Comprehensive Brand Systems for Games" (Matt Redway and Alanna Cervenak, on Horizon). Their point: a logo plus key art is no longer a brand. A brand is a system of parts:

- typography
- colour
- shape
- language
- texture
- motion
- tone of voice
- editing style

They also said rules must be set at the start for what may change and what stays fixed, because the brand passes through many hands. Sources: GDC schedule, session 914434, 9–13 March 2026, SNIPPET; Thumbsticks, "How PlayStation built a brand system for the Horizon franchise", 2026 (exact date not seen), SNIPPET; Inven Global article 25078, undated, SNIPPET.

An older GDC talk, "Brand Development for Video Games" (GDC Vault, date not seen, SNIPPET), says the identity should carry through everything: the packaging, the menus and the end credits.

**The stages, with what each produces and who approves it (JUDGEMENT, unless a source is given):**

1. **Strategy and positioning.**
   - Produces: one sentence saying what the game is and who it is for, three to five comparable games, the tone words, and a mood board.
   - Approved by: the owner.
   - Presskit.gg's branding guide recommends a one-sentence positioning statement as the test for every later choice (undated, SNIPPET; the page itself was blocked).
2. **Naming and clearance.**
   - Produces: a shortlist of names, a quick "knock-out" search, then a full clearance search with a lawyer's written opinion, and checks of domains, social-media handles and Steam.
   - Approved by: the owner, on the lawyer's advice.
   - US law firms say this must happen before the game is announced, a trailer is commissioned or advertising is bought (Snell & Wilmer; Legal Moves Law Firm, 2026; SNIPPET).
3. **Logo, or "title treatment".**
   - Produces: thumbnails, then three or so directions, then one refined, then the finished set: the full logo, a short version for small sizes, one-colour, reversed (light on dark), a transparent PNG and vector files, plus the clear space around it and the smallest size it may be shown.
   - Approved by: the owner, judging it at real Steam sizes.
4. **The brand system.**
   - Produces: the typefaces, colours, graphic devices (for LEDGER: halftone, the rule lines, the spot red), tone-of-voice rules, and a short brand guide.
   - A typical guide holds the logo and its rules, icon rules, approved fonts, key art and how it may be used, colour swatches, and social-media avatars and headers (Game Developer, "The Big Game Branding 101", date not seen, SNIPPET).
5. **Key art.**
   - Process: references, thumbnails, a refined line-and-value pass, colour, then the finished painting. Often it is painted over a render from the game.
   - There is an art-director review at each step. "Two rounds of art director review per asset" is a vendor's figure (game-ace.com, undated, SNIPPET; it is a vendor's own marketing).
   - Key art is "the singular, iconographic image" a campaign is built on (Wikipedia, "Key art", OPENED).
6. **Store and platform assets.** Cut from the key art and the logo; the Steam list is in section 3.
7. **Trailer title cards and the in-game title screen.** Same logo, same type, same motion rules. I found no published source on how studios hand this over (a HOLE).
8. **Press kit and social.**
   - The press kit holds a fact sheet, the logo pack, key art, screenshots, the trailer and a description.
   - Rami Ismail's presskit() set this standard layout in 2013; later tools copy it (dopresskit.com; presskit.gg, 2026; SNIPPET).

**Steam's own timings, which fix the latest dates** (Steamworks Release Process, Coming Soon and Review Process pages, undated, SNIPPET):
- A Coming Soon page must be up for at least two weeks before release.
- The store page review takes 3–5 working days; Valve asks you to submit at least 7 days before you want the page live.
- The build review takes 3–5 working days; plan for 7.
- Every store asset must exist before the Coming Soon page goes up.

**JUDGEMENT on the pattern.** The strong cases (Red Dead Redemption 2, Disco Elysium) make the logo out of the game's own visual language and carry it into the interface, so each one sells the other. LEDGER's chosen interface, a 1990 local evening paper, can be the brand system itself: a masthead logo, condensed headlines, black ink with one spot red, halftone photographs. Each of those then reads as LEDGER on Steam, in the trailer and in the menus.

## 3. Steam's requirements today

Valve's pages were refused by the network. The figures below come from search summaries of Valve's own pages (SNIPPET), except where another source is named. They match one another and Game Developer's August 2024 report.

**Store assets**

| Asset | Size | Rules seen |
|---|---|---|
| Header capsule | 920 × 430 | Top of the store page, "Recommended for you", Big Picture, daily deals. "Should immediately tell the customer something important about the game", art first, "some sense of the gameplay" |
| Small capsule | 462 × 174 | Required. "Should contain a readable logo", which in most cases "should nearly fill" it. May use different art from the other capsules. A third-party guide says Steam makes 120 × 45 and 184 × 69 copies from it; not confirmed at Valve |
| Main capsule | 1232 × 706 | Front-page carousel; same wording as the header |
| Vertical capsule | 748 × 896 | Seasonal-sale front pages; same wording |
| Screenshots | at least 1920 × 1080, 16:9 | At least 5. "Should exclusively show the gameplay" |
| Page background | 1438 × 810 | Optional; subtle, not bright or busy (third-party guides; Valve wording not seen) |
| Bundle header | 707 × 232 | Branding, logo clearly legible |

**Library assets**

| Asset | Size | Rules seen |
|---|---|---|
| Library capsule | 600 × 900 | No quotes or text beyond the game's title |
| Library hero | 3840 × 1240 | **No text at all.** Central safe area of 860 × 380 |
| Library logo | 1280 wide and/or 720 tall, transparent PNG | Laid over the hero. Its position is chosen in a preview tool: bottom left, top centre, centre, or bottom centre |
| Library header | 920 × 430 | Third-party guides only |
| Community icon | 184 × 184, JPG | Used in compact lists and notifications |

- **When the sizes changed.** Valve doubled the asset sizes in August 2024, and the old sizes stopped being accepted on 1 November 2024 (Game Developer, "Steam increases Store image requirements", August 2024, SNIPPET).
- **New Steam hardware** (Steam Machine, Steam Frame; announced 12 November 2025) brought new compatibility checks. No new store-art size was found tied to it.
- **Event assets** have their own Valve page, but I did not see their sizes. The client icon's specification was not seen either.

**Rules on what a capsule may show** (Valve's Graphical Asset Rules, in force since 1 September 2022, SNIPPET; PCGamesN 2022, SNIPPET):
- Only game artwork, the game's name and any official subtitle.
- No review scores, no award names or logos, no discount wording, no promotion of other products, no other text.
- Any other text must go up as an "Artwork Override" that expires after at most one month, translated into every language the game supports. It may only announce a major update, a seasonal event, DLC or similar new content.

**What Valve's guidance says makes capsules work:** a readable logo that nearly fills the small capsule; art that "immediately tells the customer something important"; art first, not text. Zukowski's evidence on clicks is in section 1. Third-party "data studies" of capsule click-through rates turned up in the searches, but they look like low-quality or invented case studies, so they are left out.

## 4. The legal side

**Which classes matter** (Verna Law and Revision Legal, undated; WIPO's Nice classification page; SNIPPET):
- **Class 9:** downloadable game software. **This is the main one for a game sold on Steam.**
- **Class 41:** entertainment services and online games that are not downloaded.
- **Class 28:** physical games and consoles; only relevant for merchandise or board games.
- Class 16 (printed matter) would matter for an art book.

**How studios check a name.** First a knock-out search: the register plus the web, looking for the same or a near-identical name on related goods. Then a full search: national and foreign registers, names used without registration, game stores, domains and social handles, with a lawyer's opinion. Owning a domain or a handle gives no trade mark right. Sources: Snell & Wilmer; Morse Law; Legal Moves Law Firm, 2026; SNIPPET.

**Warning case.** The Day Before, then Steam's second most-wishlisted game, had its Steam page blocked in January 2023 at the request of someone who had filed the name as a US trade mark after the game was announced. It was delayed eight months, although the developers later said the delay was already planned (PC Gamer and NME, January 2023, SNIPPET). Two different games called "Piece by Piece" launched within 48 hours of each other in 2025 and settled it with a joint bundle (Notebookcheck, 2025, SNIPPET).

**Official filing fees** (SNIPPET, several law firms agreeing; the registers' own fee pages were refused):

| Office | Fee |
|---|---|
| UKIPO, from 1 April 2026 | £205 for one class online, £60 for each further class (was £170 and £50). Hindles; Taylor Wessing, March 2026; Mathys & Squire; IP Law Watch, 26 November 2025 |
| EUIPO | €850 for one class, €50 for the second, €150 for each class after that. Several firms, 2025–26 |
| USPTO, from 18 January 2025 | $350 per class. Extra charges: $100 if the application is incomplete; $200 per class for wording not taken from the USPTO's standard list of goods; $200 for each further 1,000 characters. Reed Smith; Fish & Richardson; Buchalter |

**Is "LEDGER" taken?** I could not open any register. The search summaries below quote registrations from official decisions:

- **Real newspapers have used the name:** *The Ledger* of Lakeland, Florida (founded 1924 as the *Lakeland Evening Ledger*), and Philadelphia's *Evening Ledger* (Wikipedia titles, SNIPPET).

**JUDGEMENT, not legal advice:**
- **The bare word LEDGER is a real risk.** It is identical to the mark of a well-known company that is registered in class 9 in all three places, actively enforced (a domain taken in 2026, a UK opposition claiming reputation), and covers "multimedia" software.
- Whether a video game is close enough to those goods to cause confusion is a lawyer's call. A company claiming reputation can reach beyond its own goods.
- Separately, searching for "Ledger" brings up the crypto company, so the bare word is also weak for being found in search.
- A longer title (LEDGER plus a subtitle) lowers the risk but does not remove it.
- **This must be settled before any Steam page, trailer or announcement.**

**Fonts under the SIL Open Font Licence in a logo for a game that is sold** (OFL FAQ version 1.1-update7, November 2023, OPENED via SIL's own copy on GitHub):
- Section 1.1: "Can I use the fonts … to create logos or other graphics…? Yes … No additional license or permission is required." Logos are named in its list of examples.
- Section 1.1.1: "You remain the author and copyright holder of that newly derived graphic." The licence's conditions apply only when the font file itself is redistributed, bundled or changed.
- Section 1.1.2: no credit is required.
- Section 3.7: "The OFL does not grant any rights under trademark law."
- **So an OFL blackletter may be the base of the LEDGER logo**, and the outlines may be redrawn as artwork.
- **JUDGEMENT:** a logo drawn or heavily reworked by hand is easier to protect and harder to copy than plain type set in a free font that anyone can download.
- If a font file ships inside the game, the licence text must ship with it. The project already does this (production/design/ui/FONTS.md).

**Valve's AI disclosure for store art:**
- The form was rewritten on 16 January 2026. Anything a player sees counts, including "marketing assets" and the store page: AI-generated key art or store text must be described in the text box. AI tools used only during development are exempt (Game Developer, 16 January 2026; Generation Amiga, 17 January 2026; SNIPPET). See also this project's note of 29 September 2026, production/research/steam-ai-disclosure/.
- **How players react** (all SNIPPET):
  - PC Gamer (2026, date not seen) calls AI-made capsule art "spammy", recognisable by its "AI sheen".
  - *Crimson Desert* shipped AI-made placeholder art, then removed it and added a disclosure (GameSpot, 2026).
  - Shrine's Legacy drew negative Steam reviews accusing it of using generative AI after its October 2025 launch.
  - UK Games Expo banned games and merchandise made "completely or in significant part" with AI on 24 September 2026 (Northeast Times, 26 September 2026).

## 5. What a small team can do itself, and where a specialist matters

**What the team can do itself (JUDGEMENT, unless sourced):**
- The positioning sentence and the brand system rules.
- The type choices: the OFL faces are already checked.
- The halftone and spot-red treatment, which is a mechanical process.
- Screenshots and the trailer cut from the game itself.
- Cutting the Steam asset set from finished masters.
- The press kit.
- The in-game title screen, built from the logo files.

**Where a human specialist pays:**
- **The capsule and key art.** This is the strongest published evidence (Zukowski 2020 and 2022, above). For a 3D Unreal game, a paintover of an in-engine render by a key art illustrator is the usual route (JUDGEMENT).
- **The wordmark.** Lettering is a specialist craft, and blackletter is hard to keep readable at 120 × 45 pixels. A lettering designer can make a full masthead version plus a simpler small-size version (JUDGEMENT).
- **The trade mark search and opinion.** A lawyer is the only real way to clear a name.

**Published examples:**
- Disco Elysium's look, logo and key art came from one in-house painter (Rostov); the small team had a fine artist at its centre.
- Rockstar's logo came from a type designer (Larabie) who once worked for the company.
- I could not open any post-mortem in which a developer compares a logo they made themselves with a commissioned one (a HOLE).

**What it would take for LEDGER to reach a 2026 AAA standard (JUDGEMENT):**
1. A one-line positioning and a two-page brand guide built on the evening-paper system.
2. A cleared name.
3. A hand-finished masthead wordmark in full and short versions, one-colour, reversed and transparent.
4. One key art master painted at high resolution over a real game frame, judged against the Hook sheet and the KCD2 frames like every other visual.
5. The full Steam set at the sizes above.
6. A logo animation for the trailer and title screen.
7. A press kit.

A rough budget, assembled by me from the ranges above and not published as a package:
- a lawyer's clearance across the UK, EU and US: low thousands of pounds;
- the wordmark from a lettering specialist: a few hundred to a few thousand;
- key art and capsules from an experienced illustrator: about £1,000–5,000;
- filing fees after clearance: UK £265 and EU €900 for two classes, US $350 per class.

## Decisions for Jafar

1. **The name (scope and money).** Before any Steam page, trailer or public mention, should a trade mark lawyer check whether LEDGER is clear in the UK, EU and US?
   - (a) Yes, now, with two fallback names checked at the same time. **Recommended:** a well-known crypto company holds LEDGER in class 9, the class that covers games.
   - (b) Keep LEDGER and accept the risk.
   - (c) Rename now.
2. **Who makes the logo and the key art (money).**
   - (a) We draft the brand system and the in-game title screen ourselves, and hire a specialist only for the wordmark and the key art and capsules. **Recommended.**
   - (b) All in-house, from free fonts and game renders.
   - (c) An agency does the whole package.
3. **Generative AI in store art (licences and reception).**
   - (a) None in anything on the Steam page or in marketing. **Recommended:** nothing to disclose, and no backlash.
   - (b) Allowed, with Valve's disclosure.
4. **When (scope).**
   - (a) Only the name check and the brand guide's rules now; logo and key art after the playable route works, under the current stop on visual work. **Recommended.**
   - (b) Start the logo now.
5. **Canon.** The in-world local paper's name is still owed in the brand bible. Should the game's paper-style masthead read LEDGER while the town's own evening paper has a different name?
   - (a) Yes, keep them apart, so the logo is the player's notebook dressed as a paper and not an in-world masthead. **Recommended.**
   - (b) Name the in-world paper the Ledger.
6. **Filing a trade mark once the name is cleared (money).**
   - (a) UK and EU in classes 9 and 41 straight away (about £265 and €900 in fees), the US before launch. **Recommended.**
   - (b) UK only.
   - (c) Don't file.
