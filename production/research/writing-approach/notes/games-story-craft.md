GAMES STORY CRAFT: how the most praised game stories get made
Researched 2026-11-23 from this PC's web access. Every claim below rests on a page actually fetched (listed at the end). Where a page did not cover a point, that is said. Not reached: the 2018 Vulture/New York Magazine Houser piece itself (only second-hand reports), any GDC video or slides, writers' books, Rob Wiethoff or Roger Clark interview pages beyond the Hollywood Reporter extract, Matt Sophos and Richard Gaubert (God of War writing), Szamałek GDC material, Argo Tuulik beyond one summary. Treat those as gaps, not as negatives.

== WHAT SEPARATES GREAT FROM COMPETENT ==

1. Writers who also design, and designers who also write. BG3's lead writer Adam Smith credits the writing staff's dual role as designers with keeping hundreds of storylines from contradicting each other (80.lv). CD Projekt Red's side quests mostly came from quest designers, while main-quest ideas often began with writers; both then ran the same pitch-scenario-draft pipeline (Shacknews). Kasavin is creative director and also writes Hades (Game Developer). The pattern is one shared pipeline, not a script handed over a wall.

2. Many passes, with throwing away allowed. Disco Elysium's Cuno was rewritten 20 times (Tuulik, GamesHub). The Witcher 3 team made several passes on each scenario, "reinforcing some drafts and scrapping others to start over"; the Bloody Baron line went through several iterations before approval, with Karolina Stachyra writing and Tomaszkiewicz and Blacha giving feedback (Shacknews). Druckmann describes TLOU2 as repeated creating and cutting, with emotional scenes that "didn't work for a long time" (USgamer). Rockstar reportedly made about 70 versions of each RDR2 trailer and ad, a sign of the studio's polish habit (Kotaku).

3. A fixed spine lets everything else be extravagant. Disco Elysium has one linear critical path and one answer to the mystery; writer Justin Keenan calls it a backbone that lets the team be "quite extravagant" elsewhere (GamesHub). BG3 rejected a branching tree for a "spiderweb" in which paths converge, with true endings in the "very low double-digits" and thousands of variations from the route (GamesRadar). Convergence, not infinite divergence, is the cost control.

4. Find the ending, then work back; know the theme first. Druckmann and Gross advise "finding your ending" and working backward; Druckmann started TLOU2 from a theme (tribalism and cycles of violence, as against love in the first game) and says pressure reveals character (Arc Studio). Theme came before scenes.

5. Characters are made by specific people and tested by specific actors. Firewatch's Cissy Jones was cast before Vanaman began writing, and Rich Sommer, about the fiftieth actor auditioned for Henry, "reshaped" Vanaman's idea of the character (Red Bull). RDR2's Alex McKenna said she took about two years to understand Sadie fully, and credits the writing for her arc (Hollywood Reporter). Casting is part of writing, not after it.

6. Writing continues through production. RDR2 started with a basic plan, "fencepost scenes" and key characters, then "write as we go", adding lines where the story needed complexity (Hollywood Reporter citing Edge). Roger Clark's first day was August 2013, over five years before release. The final push on narrative and dialogue was three weeks of intense work by the four senior writers, "three weeks, not years" (Kotaku). A long open structure with a hard closing edit.

7. Cut what does not work, including things the team liked. RDR2: Arthur's romance options cut from two to one because one "didn't work"; missions dropped because they could not be made technically slick enough; about 5 hours trimmed late from a 65-hour story (GamesRadar). TLOU2: the Seattle sections cut from five days to three each, a Seraphite island encounter and a boar hunt removed for pacing and production reasons (USgamer). Cuts were made for pacing and feasibility, not just quality.

8. Players should be made to stop and think; hard choices are deliberate. Marcin Blacha: a big choice "must be constructed in such a way that the player puts the controller away, stands up, and starts walking" (PC Gamer). The Witcher team also set out to remove ordinary fetch quests and "compensate with the narrative" where they remain (PC Gamer 2014 preview).

9. Characters who react, not worlds that change. Smith: the aim is characters who react to every choice, and even small reactions "mean something"; even short exchanges should differ by what the player said earlier (GamesRadar, Den of Geek). Making the player feel heard is the quality marker, not branch count.

10. Unnecessary detail is what people remember. Kasavin: "dumb stuff" unnecessary to the game is what makes it special, because developers cannot predict which detail a player will connect with (Game Developer podcast summary). Disco Elysium's hidden interactions served the same end (GamesHub).

11. Moral complexity, no pure villain. Tuulik: no pure evil, everyone has something interesting (GamesHub). Gross and Druckmann aim for characters both good and bad, "not sugar-coated" (Arc Studio). RDR2 critics single out the gang's layered members as the story's strongest asset (The Next Web).

12. A team that has worked together for years. Rockstar's four senior writers (Dan Houser, Mike Unsworth, Rupert Humphries, Lazlow) had worked together at least 12 years (Kotaku). Supergiant had worked together nearly 10 years by Hades (Game Developer). BG3's character writers had lived with their characters for six years and could write new lines at short notice (80.lv).

13. Scale of talent in the room matters less than discipline. Hades was made by fewer than 20 people (Game Developer). Disco Elysium's writing team was about ten of 35 in-house staff (GamesRadar). God of War 2018 was about 300 people (GamesBeat). Great stories came from teams from 10 to 500.

14. What goes wrong: too much text, too little editing. Disco Elysium broke the Articy branching tool (Hindpere: it "got quite janky" and froze), and the team lacked time to edit everything, so cut parts (PC Gamer). Houser admitted RDR2 was "behind schedule" and "over budget so much" (GamesRadar). Process cost is real.

== PROCESS FACTS BY STUDIO ==

Rockstar, Red Dead Redemption 2
- Senior writing team of four: Houser (lead), Unsworth, Humphries, Lazlow; together 12+ years (Kotaku).
- About 7 years on the game; over five years from first cast day (Aug 2013) to release; two delays (GamesRadar, Hollywood Reporter).
- About 500,000 lines of dialogue, 300,000 animations, 1,000+ speaking roles; 1,200 SAG-AFTRA actors, about 500 on motion capture (said by Houser to have taken six years), 700 recording dialogue (GamesRadar).
- Plan, fencepost scenes and key characters first; written as they went; lines added where complexity needed (Hollywood Reporter).
- Performance capture: full scenes played together on a soundstage in mocap suits, face and body at once, roughly 60 to 70 cameras; sets matched in-game dimensions; pre-visualisation showed the surroundings and time of day. Actors secretly read each other's script sides to learn the story (Hollywood Reporter).
- Houser: "100-hour weeks" several times in 2018, later clarified as the writing and dialogue work, not the whole studio (Kotaku). Not reached: the original New York Magazine piece, or any account of actor-led rewrites by Clark or Wiethoff. Clark said only that he built his own Arthur rather than copy Wiethoff's Marston (Hollywood Reporter).
- Critical reception (The Next Web, one critic): gang is the strongest asset; Arthur's tuberculosis (Chapter 6) drives his redemption; pace steady but slow, with the tropical island chapter out of place; Hosea's death marks the turn; Micah a telegraphed traitor; Arthur's death powerful, no fourth-wall winks; epilogue touching but weaker since the real ending has passed. One critic only. Do not generalise.

Naughty Dog, The Last of Us Part II
- Director Druckmann and narrative lead Gross wrote it; Troy Baker suggested how Joel's death scene should play (USgamer).
- Theme first (tribalism), find the ending and work back (Arc Studio).
- Cuts as above. Team size, drafts and playtest method not found in what was read.

Santa Monica Studio, God of War (2018)
- About 300 people (GamesBeat). Writer-specific process (Sophos, Gaubert, Barlog) not reached: the pages fetched did not cover it. A gap, not a finding.

CD Projekt Red, The Witcher 3
- Quest designers: original game 4, sequel 6, Witcher 3 about 14 (Tomaszkiewicz, PC Gamer 2014).
- Pipeline: idea (writers for main story, designers for most side quests), brief pitches, narrowed to strongest, full scenario ("foundation behind the events, specific scene ideas, what should happen where"), several drafts, asset list, prototype with temporary assets, mood tuning of lighting, sound, music and item placement (Shacknews, 2016 forum Q&A by Pawel Sasko and Mateusz Tomaszkiewicz).
- Hardest quest to write: Bloody Baron (parallel between two fathers who lost daughters; won a 2015 Golden Joystick Best Gaming Moment). Hardest technically: Battle for Kaer Morhen, with 9 to 16 supporting characters in any combination (Shacknews).
- Targets: about 100 hours total, 50-hour main story (PC Gamer 2014).

ZA/UM, Disco Elysium
- Origins in a Tallinn artist and writer collective formed around 2005; Kurvitz's 2013 novel (five years to write, about 1,000 copies); one-page synopsis pitched an isometric CRPG mixing AD&D and 1970s cop show (GamesRadar).
- 35 in-house plus about 20 contractors; writers nearly a third in-house; writing team about ten (GamesRadar).
- Method: each writer starts with a character or scene; intersecting writers add a layer; an editing layer follows; sometimes back to the start; no single owner (GamesHub).
- Some characters took one to two months each to write (Rattasepp, PC Gamer). Scope moved from huge world to one coastal town to the capital; late 2017 target slipped to 2019 (GamesRadar).
- Dialogue and skill systems were the hardest problem; the writing had to feel reactive and be usable by a large team (GamesRadar).

Supergiant, Hades
- Fewer than 20 people; planned Early Access from the start; story released serially, resolution arriving through updates (Game Developer).
- Monthly milestones: early in the month code changes allowed, then code locks and only data (narrative events, voice-over) continues, then test and polish. Earlier games used 2.5 to 3 month milestones. About 3 years total (Game Developer).
- Kasavin: narrative systems should soften difficulty walls for story-first players; bosses remember earlier fights (Game Developer podcast summary).

Larian, Baldur's Gate 3
- Divinity: Original Sin about 40 developers, story done in "three days or something"; Original Sin 2 about 120; Larian now approaching 500; BG3 team spends years on story (GamesRadar).
- Each companion has a dedicated writer who also does other work; cutscenes over 170 hours; Early Access from 2020 with one of three acts (80.lv).
- Smith needed 18 playthroughs to test every branch of one origin character (80.lv). Testing question: "Have we seen this aspect?"
- Playtest failure: Jaheira killed by three critical hits before recruitment, so story-critical characters can be dead and still give information; goal that players can always recover (GamesRadar spiderweb).
- Smith frames the writer as a Dungeon Master filling unfinished corners with detail that happened just outside the scene (Den of Geek).

Campo Santo, Firewatch
- Founded September 2013, four co-founders (Vanaman, Rodkin, Anderson, Moss); first six months spent learning to work together; Vanaman: 18 uninterrupted months is enough "if you know what you're doing" (Red Bull).
- Began from one image (a lookout tower); about two years planned; research trip to Yosemite; humour used to make players care about Henry and Delilah; characters written to respect each other when they disagree (Game Developer).
- At least 40 hours recording dialogue; the game picks the fittest next line from earlier choices; script visualised by a tool, not drawn as trees (Red Bull).

Warhorse, Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2
- About 250 staff, roughly 100 more than the first game; added pipelines to track how systems fit together (GamesRadar). No writing-specific process found.

Remedy, Alan Wake 2 (Sam Lake)
- 13 years from first game to sequel, stop-start; shared world with Control shaped tone and structure; live action on sets remade to match the game, part of the critical path in The Dark Place (Xbox Wire). No drafts or writer-designer process found.
