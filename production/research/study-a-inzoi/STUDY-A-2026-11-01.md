# Study A: inZOI, a small language model on the player's card

1 November 2026; revised that night after its fresh review (STUDY-A-REVIEW-2026-11-01.md). Public sources only; nothing installed. Tags like [5] point to the source list, all read 1 November 2026; project records are cited by path. "Unverified": no primary source confirms it. "(inference)": this study's reasoning.

## Questions

1. Smart Zoi's model: name, size, quantisation, format; since when; optional; which cards?
2. Its card memory, speed and frame-rate cost?
3. What it decides; what stays scripted; shown or spoken lines?
4. How it avoids inventing facts?
5. How it shares the card with the renderer?
6. Changes since launch; what players and reviewers reported?
7. Other games' on-device language models, sizes and speeds?
8. For LEDGER: could a small local model check sentences faster than the cloud check on this AMD RX 6700, about 4 GB free? Does NVIDIA's tooling work on AMD?

## 1. The model

- NVIDIA: "a .5B billion parameter Mistral NeMo Minitron small language model", "GPU-accelerated exclusively on GeForce RTX GPUs" [1]. That is 0.5 billion parameters. No public model card for it was found; NVIDIA's published Minitron sizes are 2B, 4B, 8B [2][3].
- Quantisation and format: not published. Unverified.
- Built by: NVIDIA supplied the on-device model so the game would not drop frames; Krafton fine-tuned it on a hand-made dataset and wrote the inference code; NVIDIA optimised it for CUDA [4].
- Since the early-access launch, 28 March 2025, as "experimental", off until the player turns it on [1][5].
- Krafton's tier table [5]: minimum RTX 3060 8 GB, 12 GB RAM; medium RTX 4060 8 GB; recommended RTX 5070 or 4070 Super 12 GB; high RTX 5070 Ti or 4080 Super 16 GB, 32 GB RAM. Every tier: "Internet connection required". Only NVIDIA graphics cards named.
- AMD: no official support statement found. Yet players with an RX 6650 XT (2025) and RX 9060 XT / 9070 (2026) report turning it on, with stutter or crashes [6][7][8]. One reports it working on an RX 9070 under Linux and with an older Windows driver [7], so in practice it is not tied to CUDA. Whether it then runs on the card or the processor is unknown. Since v0.8.0 (29 April 2026) the game warns when Smart Zoi is enabled on 7 GB of video memory or less [9]: the gate looks like memory, not maker (inference). Unverified.
- Mac (from August 2025): reported to use Apple's Core ML. Unverified.

## 2. Memory and speed

- No official memory figure, tokens per second or time per decision; NVIDIA says only "low latency in a very compact footprint" [1]. "About 1 GB" (PC Gamer, a fan wiki): unreadable, unverified.
- A guide measured a 12.1 GB peak at 4K maximum with frame generation and Smart Zoi, against "up to 10 GB" without it [10]. A player measured inZOI alone at about 5,004 MiB on a 10 GB RTX 3080 (Linux); Smart Zoi then switched itself off [11].
- Frame rate: no controlled test found. Krafton's known issue: five sleepy Zois on screen after 11 p.m. "may cause lag" [5]. Players lower graphics settings, and report stutter on an RX 6650 XT [6][12].

## 3. What it decides

- Krafton (Kang-Wook Lee): "Every action sequence is made by language model inference"; "Planning is also done by language model inferences" [13]. At day's end a Smart Zoi reviews its day and adjusts its schedule [1][13].
- The player sees inner thoughts live and steers them with free text (the Zoi Pen), English only at launch [1][5].
- v0.10.0 (3 September 2026): Smart Zois use location, group activity, social event, job or business "to decide its interactions"; their chosen interactions carry a symbol [14]. So the model picks among the game's existing interactions; animations, needs and the actions themselves stay scripted (inference).
- It writes thought bubbles, journal and schedule entries [5][15][18]. No source says it writes spoken dialogue. Unverified.

## 4. Keeping to the world

- Nothing published on grammars, structured output, filters or retrieval for Smart Zoi. Unverified.
- Visible: output is a choice of existing interactions plus short thoughts (inference); a hand-made fine-tuning set [4]; a model per language (Korean added v0.5.0, restored v0.6.0) [15][16].
- NVIDIA's general ACE design adds retrieval memory and "reflection" on past actions [3]; not confirmed for inZOI.

## 5. Sharing the card

- Not published for inZOI; use of NVIDIA's In-Game Inferencing SDK (NVIGI) is unverified [4].
- NVIGI: the game hands over its D3D12 device and queue so AI runs beside rendering ("compute in graphics") [2]. Three hints: prioritise graphics, prioritise compute, or balance (default). Priority needs NVIDIA driver 575.00 (CUDA beside D3D12) or 580.88 (CUDA beside Vulkan, or D3D compute); "Vulkan compute: not optimized". Advice: run asynchronously across many frames; latency targets are hundreds of milliseconds; measure by toggling the feature and comparing total frame time [17].
- inZOI: off by default; a low-memory warning and a game-wide "Performance Safeguard" (v0.8.0) [9]. One player says he had set it to "only One Zoi" [8], a choice of his, not a known limit. No published fallback when the card is busy.

## 6. Since launch

- 2025: v0.4.0 (30 Oct) fixed Smart Zoi overwriting schedules [18]; v0.5.0 (23 Dec) "Improvements", Korean added [15]; v0.5.2 (30 Dec, build dated 29 December) "Rolled back SmartZoi feature to October version for stable service" [19].
- 2026: v0.6.0 (12 Feb) action selection "further optimized" [16]; v0.8.0 (29 Apr) Unreal 5.6, the 7 GB warning [9]; June and September crash fixes, Mac still crashing [20][21]; v0.10.0 (3 Sep) context-aware choices [14]; October, the director (signed "Kjun") promises to fix Zois "repeatedly having the same thoughts" [22].
- Players: mostly thought bubbles, actions little changed, costs performance, "vendor locked" [12]; AMD crashes that came and went with drivers [7]. No professional benchmark found.

## 7. Others, for comparison

- **PUBG Ally** (Krafton, NVIDIA ACE) [23][24]: a 2B model per language (Mistral-NeMo-Minitron-2B English, kanana-1.5-2.1b Korean, Qwen3-1.7B Chinese), 4-bit GGUF through NVIGI, 1.6 to 2.1 GB with cache, for 8 GB cards. On RTX 4060-class cards: recognition 0.12 s (processor), model 0.80 s median (0.91 s p90), speech 0.70 s; a whole reply about 1.6 s against 3.4 s with a cloud model (its model step 2.54 s). One inference at a time; about 5,000 tokens of context; 90.7% prompt-prefix cache reuse [23]. Grounding: 16 tools, an availability check before actions [23]; the prompt makes tool results "the only ground truth for the current match" [24]; distilled from Gemma 4 31B through 8B intermediate teachers [23], the teacher's prompt carrying a "deterministic PUBG specification" [24]; a keyword filter before speech [23]. Its "Fact" score 0.932, against the teacher's 0.944 [23].
- **Total War: PHARAOH advisor** (experimental, Jan 2026) [25]: "up to 6 GB"; fed from 1,200+ game tables; text only; NVIDIA says ACE can fall back to the cloud on Radeon if the developer chooses. **Dead Meat**: Minitron-8B-128K locally on RTX 50-series, no speed published [3].

## What this means for LEDGER's speech (inference, against the project's records)

- **Where speech stands tonight.** Re-measured beside the packaged game, the first sound comes at a median 1.17 s, 11 of 20 under 2.0 s (production/research/voice-latency/REMEASURE-2026-11-01.md). Eight one-sentence replies wait for Haiku's whole check (2.00 to 2.32 s). The tail is the check's second look (3.34 s) and a redraft (6.62 s).
- **The fast path is already local.** A 17M checker sits before Haiku, on by default since 28 October (RULINGS.md, the speech line; production/orders/2026-11-02.md, o27-checker-open). Beside the game with the voice running it answered in a median 0.095 to 0.099 s, 3 to 4% of answers over 0.15 s, with no frame stall (production/research/invented-claims/LOCAL-CHECKERS-2026-10-26.md, last sections). So a language model would not replace "the cloud check"; at most Haiku's second look.
- **His bar for a local checker** (26 October): 0.15 s or less beside the running game, no frame stalls, no more invented details through than Haiku, a licence for a sold game (production/orders/2026-11-02.md, o26-speech-checker). PUBG Ally's 0.80 s, on NVIDIA with CUDA, is over five times that.
- **Small models were already weighed** (LOCAL-CHECKERS-2026-10-26.md): Qwen3-0.6B estimated at 0.8 to 2.1 s on this processor, Granite Guardian 2B several seconds; 0.15 s only on the card, unmeasured there; five untrained checkers measured, none passed. On this card, game off: Qwen3-4B, 4-bit, llama.cpp on Vulkan, 85 tokens a second, a median 432 ms per short routing answer (production/research/local-models/DELIVERY.md, 8.2); local writers 2.7 to 3.3 s a line (production/research/local-writers/README.md).
- **Sharing this card was measured, and it stalls.** On 25 October another process's AI work (the cast voice, on the card) held the card for 487 ms of a 504 ms frame; card memory peaked at 9.5 to 10.1 GB of 10. Two tries (the voice at idle card priority; the game off async compute) cut the worst frame only to 118 ms (production/research/frame-headroom/VOICE-ON-CARD-TRACE-2026-10-25.md). This driver has no hardware scheduling, and a high queue priority ranks only within one process (production/research/voice-latency/GPU-QUEUE-PRIORITY-2026-10-01.md). inZOI's stutter reports fit.
- **Memory.** The game alone peaks at 5.6 to 5.9 GB (production/research/frame-headroom/FINDINGS-2026-10-23.md). About 4 GB is free only because all three voices run on the processor (production/specs/voice-engines.json). The project's budget for a model on the card is about 2 GB (DELIVERY.md, 8.2). NVIDIA's multi-vendor language models start at 4B, the smallest listed build 4 GB [27].
- **AMD routes.** CUDA, compute-in-graphics and NVIGI's priority hints do not apply. NVIGI's D3D12 and Vulkan plugins came at release 1.2.0 as "experimental and not intended for production use"; later notes do not restate the status; its core tests do not support AMD [26]. NVIGI also lists an ONNX DirectML language plugin for "ONNX-supported GPU or CPU" [2], but DirectML "is in sustained engineering" [30]. Windows ML's AMD card provider (MIGraphX) needs RDNA 3 or later and "is not supported for GenAI scenarios today"; its WebGPU provider is experimental [31]. AMD's HIP SDK 7.2 marks the RX 6700 (gfx1031) unsupported on Windows [29]. That leaves llama.cpp on Vulkan, as the project found (DELIVERY.md, 8.1). On an RX 6700 XT, alone, it reads a prompt at 1,051 tokens a second and writes 83.88 (7B, 4-bit) [28]; for a checker, the reading matters more.
- **Conclusion.** Nothing on record suggests a language model on this AMD card meets 0.15 s beside the game without stalls; a model in a separate process is what stalled the card on 25 October. The cheaper lever comes first: asking the 17M about whole one-sentence replies (REMEASURE). A trial of a 1 to 2B model is worth it only as a replacement for Haiku's second look, judged against his 0.15 s bar or a bar he sets for that step, with the 25 October stall weighed. It would measure check time, a frame trace with it running, peak card memory, and agreement with Haiku on the 617 held sentences; its licence must pass the allowlist.
