# Study A: inZOI, a small language model on the player's card

1 November 2026. Public sources only; the game was not installed or downloaded. Tags like [5] point to the source list; every source was read on 1 November 2026. "Unverified": no primary source confirms it. "(inference)": this study's reasoning, not a source.

## Questions

1. Which model runs Smart Zoi (name, size, quantisation, format)? Since when? Optional? Which cards?
2. Its card memory beside the game, its speed, its frame-rate cost?
3. What does it decide, what stays scripted? Does it write shown or spoken lines?
4. How does it avoid inventing facts?
5. How does it share the card with the renderer?
6. What changed since launch; what did players and reviewers report?
7. Which other games run a language model on the player's machine, at what size and speed?
8. For LEDGER: could a small local model draft or check first sentences faster than the cloud check on an AMD RX 6700 with about 4 GB free? Does NVIDIA's tooling work on AMD?

## 1. The model

- NVIDIA: "a .5B billion parameter Mistral NeMo Minitron small language model", "GPU-accelerated exclusively on GeForce RTX GPUs" [1]. That is 0.5 billion parameters. No public model card for it was found; NVIDIA's published Minitron sizes are 2B, 4B, 8B [2][3].
- Quantisation and format: not published. Unverified.
- Built by: NVIDIA supplied the on-device model so the game would not drop frames; Krafton fine-tuned it on a hand-made dataset and wrote the inference code; NVIDIA optimised it for CUDA [4].
- Since the early-access launch, 28 March 2025, as "experimental", off until the player turns it on [1][5].
- Krafton's tier table [5]: minimum RTX 3060 8 GB, 12 GB RAM; medium RTX 4060 8 GB; recommended RTX 5070 or 4070 Super 12 GB; high RTX 5070 Ti or 4080 Super 16 GB, 32 GB RAM. Every tier: "Internet connection required". Only NVIDIA cards named.
- AMD: no official support statement found. Yet players with an RX 6650 XT (2025) and RX 9060 XT / 9070 (2026) report turning it on, with stutter or crashes [6][7][8]. Since v0.8.0 (29 April 2026) the game warns when Smart Zoi is enabled on 7 GB of video memory or less [9]: the gate looks like memory, not maker (inference). Unverified.
- Mac (from August 2025): reported to use Apple's Core ML. Unverified.

## 2. Memory and speed

- No official memory figure, tokens per second or time per decision. NVIDIA says only "low latency in a very compact footprint" [1].
- "About 1 GB" (PC Gamer, a fan wiki): articles unreadable. Unverified.
- A settings guide measured a 12.1 GB peak at 4K maximum with frame generation and Smart Zoi, against "up to 10 GB" without Smart Zoi; its "2-4 GB" extra is stated, not tested [10].
- A player measured inZOI alone at about 5,004 MiB on a 10 GB RTX 3080 (Linux); Smart Zoi then switched itself off [11].
- Frame rate: no controlled test found. Krafton's launch known issue: five sleepy Zois on screen after 11 p.m. "may cause lag" [5]. Players report lowering graphics settings, and stutter on an RX 6650 XT [6][12].

## 3. What it decides

- Krafton (Kang-Wook Lee): "Every action sequence is made by language model inference"; "Planning is also done by language model inferences" [13]. At day's end a Smart Zoi reviews its day and adjusts its schedule [1][13].
- The player sees inner thoughts live and steers them with free text (the Zoi Pen), English only at launch [1][5].
- v0.10.0 (3 September 2026): Smart Zois use location, group activity, social event, job or business "to decide its interactions"; their chosen interactions carry a symbol [14]. So the model picks among the game's existing interactions; animations, needs and the actions themselves stay scripted (inference).
- It writes thought bubbles, journal and schedule entries [5][15][18]. No source says it writes spoken dialogue. Unverified.

## 4. Keeping to the world

- Nothing published on grammars, structured output, filters or retrieval for Smart Zoi. Unverified.
- Visible: output is a choice of existing interactions plus short thoughts (inference); a hand-made fine-tuning set [4]; a model per language (Korean added v0.5.0, restored v0.6.0) [15][16].
- NVIDIA's general ACE design adds retrieval memory and "reflection" on past actions [3]; not confirmed for inZOI.

## 5. Sharing the card

- Not published for inZOI; use of NVIDIA's In-Game Inferencing SDK (NVIGI) is unverified [4].
- NVIGI: the game hands over its D3D12 device and queue so AI runs beside rendering ("compute in graphics") [2]. Three hints: prioritise graphics, prioritise compute, or balance (default). Priority needs NVIDIA driver 575.00 or 580.88; "Vulkan compute: not optimized". Advice: run asynchronously across many frames; latency targets are hundreds of milliseconds; measure by toggling the feature and comparing total frame time [17].
- inZOI: off by default; a low-memory warning and a game-wide "Performance Safeguard" (v0.8.0) [9]; reportedly a limit of one Smart Zoi [8] (unverified). No published fallback when the card is busy.

## 6. Since launch

- 30 Oct 2025, v0.4.0: fixed Smart Zoi overwriting schedules, and thought-bubble faults [18].
- 23 Dec 2025, v0.5.0: "Improvements", Korean added [15]. 30 Dec, v0.5.2: "Rolled back SmartZoi feature to October version for stable service" [19].
- 12 Feb 2026, v0.6.0: action selection "further optimized" [16]. 29 Apr, v0.8.0: Unreal 5.6, the 7 GB warning [9].
- Jun and Sep 2026: crash fixes on enabling Smart Zoi; Mac still crashes [20][21]. 3 Sep, v0.10.0: context-aware choices [14].
- Oct 2026: the director (signed "Kjun") promises to fix Zois "repeatedly having the same thoughts" [22].
- Players: mostly thought bubbles, actions little changed, costs performance, "vendor locked" [12]; AMD crashes that came and went with drivers [7]. No professional benchmark found.

## 7. Others, for comparison

- **PUBG Ally** (Krafton, NVIDIA ACE) [23][24]: a 2B model per language (Mistral-NeMo-Minitron-2B English, kanana-1.5-2.1b Korean, Qwen3-1.7B Chinese), 4-bit GGUF through NVIGI, 1.6 to 2.1 GB with cache, for 8 GB cards. On RTX 4060-class cards: recognition 0.12 s (processor), model 0.80 s median (0.91 s p90), speech 0.70 s; whole reply about 1.6 s against 3.4 s with a cloud model (its model step 2.54 s). One inference at a time; about 5,000 tokens of context; 90.7% prompt-prefix cache reuse. Grounding: 16 fixed tools, an availability check before actions, live tool results as "the only ground truth", distilled from a 31B teacher given fixed rules, a keyword filter before speech. Grounding score 0.932 against the teacher's 0.944.
- **Total War: PHARAOH advisor** (experimental, Jan 2026) [25]: "up to 6 GB"; fed from 1,200+ game tables; text only. NVIDIA: ACE can use the cloud on Radeon or short-memory systems if the developer chooses.
- **Dead Meat**: Minitron-8B-128K locally on RTX 50-series [3]; no speed published.

## What this means for LEDGER's speech (inference)

- Nearest case: PUBG Ally's 2B 4-bit model took about 0.8 s per call against 2.5 s in the cloud [23]. LEDGER's check takes 1.1 s, or 2.3 to 2.8 s with a second look. A local check under a second is plausible; only a measurement on this card can say.
- Memory: about 4 GB free. A 2B 4-bit model needs 1.6 to 2.1 GB with cache [23]; 0.5B less. It fits, leaving about 2 GB for Unreal's peaks.
- AMD: CUDA and its priority hints do not apply. NVIGI's D3D12 and Vulkan plugins run "on general GPU architectures/vendors" but shipped "experimental and not intended for production use", and its core tests "do not support AMD hardware" [26]. NVIDIA's newer ACE models are listed as multi-vendor GGUF files; its Unreal language plugin covers 5.5 to 5.7, not 5.8 [27]. The plain path is llama.cpp's Vulkan backend: 83.88 tokens a second on a 7B 4-bit model on an RX 6700 XT, alone [28]; the RX 6700 is not listed. A 2B model runs several times faster, less beside the game.
- The processor already carries Pocket TTS; a model there would compete with the voice. No figures for this PC.
- Best use: a checker, not a writer. Both inZOI and PUBG Ally keep the small model to fixed choices and the game's own facts. A local checker answers one short question ("does this sentence state anything not in this fact list?"); a fixed fact-list prefix lets the cache carry the prompt.
- Risks: missing inventions the cloud check catches (bench it through the whole check on the claim-check goldens first); frame hitches with no priority control on AMD; driver updates breaking it, as with inZOI on AMD; memory peaks past 10 GB; the model licence against the allowlist; GPU load during the build machine's frame-rate step.
- Needs: one trial of a 1 to 2B 4-bit model under llama.cpp Vulkan beside the packaged game, measuring check time, frame time on and off, peak memory, and agreement with the cloud check.
