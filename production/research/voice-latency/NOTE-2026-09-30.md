## Where today's 5.4 seconds go (project records, not the web)

- **Real path, in the finished game, 30 September** (production/playtest/real-talk-2026-09-30.md, 30 lines): words on screen 1.9 s median; first sound 5.4 s median (2.2 to 10.2); the voice's own share 3.7 s median (1.0 to 8.2).
- **The words' 1.9 s splits in two** (production/playtest/talk-cost-2026-09-30-after.md, same day, the game's talk program on the real key but outside the game, 24 turns): the first sentence is *written* at 0.9 s median and *passes its check* at 2.0 s median. The check of the first sentence costs about 1.1 s, in series.
- **The game asks the voice only after the check.** It starts the talk program with `--early` but not `--pending` (CrimeProbe.cpp, LiveHelperStart), so "voice asked" equals "words" in every one of the 30 rows. `--pending` already exists in the talk program; the same file measured that it would hand the voice its sentence about 1.0 s sooner (median).
- **The voice makes each sentence whole before any sound.** voice-server.py calls Nano's `generate()` per sentence on the card, writes a WAV, then reports it; nothing streams. Beside the game Nano does about 1.3 s of work per second of speech (24 September).
- **Sheila's first sentence is slower than the others'.** In the 24-turn file her first sentence was written at about 2.0 s median against about 0.8 s for Ron and Darren; that sample shows 9 Sonnet 5 calls for her 8 turns, so she appears to be the character on the larger model (my reading of the call counts). In the game run her words came at about 2.2 s median against Ron's 1.75 s.
- **A likely cause of the 8 to 10 s tails (hypothesis, not measured):** the voice server takes one request at a time and finishes every sentence of it before reading the next. With `--early` the game sends a reply's first sentence and then its rest as a second request; if the rest of one reply is still being made when the next line's first sentence arrives, that sentence waits behind it. The spread of the voice's share (1.0 to 8.2 s for short first sentences) fits this, but no record shows it.
- **Already built and kept:** each cast voice is learned once and cached, and warmed before the first line (`--prewarm`); the game plays a pre-made acknowledgement ("well-now", "let-me-think") while the answer is made and cuts it when the answer starts (LedgerAck, 28 September); the game can already add streamed pieces onto a playing sound without a gap ("joined" pieces, 26 September).
- **The August graphs** (F:\LedgerTools\voice-graphs, noted by the coordinator): ONNX exports of the *original* Chatterbox (about 0.5 B parameters, the one with the mood control), not Nano. game-out holds a prefill graph and a step graph of about 2.1 GB each at fp32, plus a chunked decoder with a seam cache, which is a streaming design already built (game-design/live-speech-latency.md). Measured then, on the card alone: 17.2 ms per step with the memory kept on the card, 68 to 77 ms per step on the processor, fp16 broke the stop decision. Its first sound was never measured at 17.2 ms; the note's own arithmetic says "well under a second" alone on the card. Beside the game it would need about 5 GB of card memory at fp32 next to the game's 3.5 GB, and it is card-only. It is the only route that keeps the mood control live; it is not the fast route.

## How others get to the first words, end to end (the method)

**1. A budget, split by stage, measured at the median and the tail.**
- Twilio's reference for a spoken voice agent (Nov 2025, https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/developers/best-practices/guide-core-latency-ai-voice-agents): mouth to ear 1,115 ms target, 1,400 ms upper limit; of it, speech recognition 350 ms, the model's first token 375 ms (upper 750), text-to-speech first byte 100 ms (upper 250).
- A measured production agent (Hugging Face blog, 15 Mar 2026, https://huggingface.co/blog/dvalle08/voice-agent-latency-playbook): model first token 566 ms median, 2,246 ms at the 95th percentile; speech first byte 243 / 296 ms; the whole 1.39 s median, 3.38 s at the 95th. Its advice: the model's first token is "the biggest variable", so measure each stage before optimising.
- ElevenLabs (23 Jun 2026, updated 6 Sep 2026, https://elevenlabs.io/blog/voice-agent-latency-optimization): target under 800 ms median and 1.5 s at the 95th percentile; buffer the model's words to a sentence end and make that sentence while the next is written; cut the player's audio buffer to 50 to 150 ms.
- A summary of the classical split (29 May 2026, https://thepromptbench.com/voice-and-realtime/latency-budgets-for-realtime-voice/, citing Retell): about 600 ms, of which the model's first token 150 to 400 ms and speech 100 to 200 ms.
- **What differs for LEDGER:** typed input needs no speech recognition or end-of-turn wait (that is 350 to 550 ms of their budget we do not spend), but our reply goes over the network to a larger model and passes a fact check none of these agents has in series (talk-helper/METHOD-2026-09-30.md found no product that checks every reply before it is spoken; they check alongside).

**2. Stream the model's words and start speech on the first sentence.**
- The usual unit is the sentence: Pipecat's speech services collect streamed words to a sentence end by default, with a word-by-word mode for engines that accept streaming text (https://docs.pipecat.ai/pipecat/learn/text-to-speech, undated; search summary). The quoted cost of waiting for the sentence is 200 to 300 ms (search summary, not verified).
- Word-by-word input needs an engine built for it; a July 2026 on-device benchmark found only one engine (the vendor's own, paid) that takes raw words without waiting for a sentence end (Picovoice, 14 Jul 2026, updated 18 Aug 2026, https://picovoice.ai/blog/on-device-tts/; a vendor's benchmark).
- Keep the first sentence short. LEDGER already does, in the talk program and in the voice server (a long first sentence is cut at its first comma).

The spread in Sopro's figures (0.3 s on a Mac, 3.1 s in a C++ port on a PC processor, about 1 s in Python here) is the single most important unknown: it must be measured on this processor, beside the game.

**4. Checks run beside the speech, not before it.** Covered in talk-helper/METHOD-2026-09-30.md (NeMo Guardrails checks streamed chunks in parallel; product safety checks run after writing). The equivalent for LEDGER is the town's `--pending`: make the sound while the sentence is checked, play it only if it passes.

**5. Opening words.** Acknowledgements played instantly are the cheapest cover (Imoto, https://dev.to/kenimo49/your-voice-agent-is-slow-here-are-5-tricks-to-hide-it-3pcb, undated): keep them neutral, use them sparingly, never one that could be read as agreement. "Progressive disclosure", beginning the real answer with a short clause while the rest is written, is the same article's third trick. Two cautions: streaming speech models often garble one- or two-word phrases, so very short openers are better pre-made (https://dev.to/azrty/low-latency-text-to-speech-for-ai-voice-agents, search summary, not verified); and the owner's target counts only the reply's own words, so a pre-made "Well, now" is cover, not a solution. LEDGER already has the pre-made beats.

**6. Voice conditioning prepared once.** Standard practice, and done here for Nano (the cache and `--prewarm`) and for Sopro (the reference is prepared once per character and kept in memory). PocketTTS.cpp goes further and caches the model's state after the voice prompt, about 4 ms to restore (voice-in-the-game DELIVERY).

**7. Processor or a card shared with the game.**
- NVIDIA's in-game SDK sets priorities between the game's drawing and the AI's compute through its own driver interface, for NVIDIA cards only; it says Vulkan compute beside Vulkan graphics is not supported (NVIGI 1.7.0, updated 14 Aug 2026, https://docs.nvidia.com/nvigi-sdk/1.7.0/docs/nvigi_core/docs/GpuSchedulingForAI.html). Nothing equivalent was found for AMD cards.
- On this card Nano beside the game ran slower than speech and worsened the slowest frames from 12.9 to 16.0 ms (24 September).
- The one found report of a cloned voice beside a running game chose the processor: Chatterbox Turbo at 4-bit, 796 ms to first audio, no dropped frames (d47, 9 Sep 2026).
- So: the voice on the processor, with its threads capped (2 to 3 of the 5600X's 6 cores, voice-in-the-game DELIVERY; Sopro's worker today uses PyTorch's default, all cores).

**8. Unreal.** A procedural sound wave takes queued PCM through a first-in-first-out buffer read on the audio thread, and plays silence if the buffer runs dry rather than failing (USoundWaveProcedural, Epic API docs, undated; forum posts; search summary). LEDGER already queues each piece on one and joins streamed pieces. Handing PCM over directly instead of through a WAV file per piece would save little (tens of milliseconds, my estimate); it matters later, when the voice moves inside the game.

## The recommended pipeline for LEDGER, in order

| Stage | Today (median) | Target | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Enter to first sentence written | 0.9 s (Ron, Darren 0.8; Sheila about 2.0) | 0.9 s; Sheila under 1.2 s | talk-cost 30 Sep, real key, outside the game |
| B. Its check, beside the voice | ends at 2.0 s, in series | ends by 1.8 s, or not needed | same file |
| C. Voice: first audio after the sentence arrives | about 3.7 s (whole sentence, Nano, card) | 0.3 to 0.8 s (streaming, processor) | real-talk 30 Sep; Sopro figures above, unmeasured here |
| D. Into the game's sound and heard | under 0.1 s (estimate) | under 0.1 s | my estimate |
| **Heard = max(B, A + C) + D** | **5.4 s** | **1.5 to 2.0 s** | |

1. **Measure each stage in the game (free, half a day).** The game's timing line gains: first sentence written (the pending line), first sentence passed, voice asked, voice received it, voice's first audio ready, first sound heard; the voice server logs whether it was still busy with an earlier request.
2. **The newest line first (free, half a day).** The voice server takes a new turn's first sentence ahead of any earlier reply's unfinished rest, and drops pieces of a turn the player has already moved past. This addresses the tails if the hypothesis holds.
3. **Turn on `--pending` (free, a day).** The game hands the first sentence to the voice at 0.9 s and plays it only when the same words pass. Heard then = max(check, sentence + voice). With today's Nano that is still about 0.9 + 3.7 = 4.6 s; with a voice that is ready in about a second, about 2.0 s: the check becomes the limit.
4. **A streaming voice on the processor (free, one to two days for the worker).** Sopro's `stream()` for the characters Jafar gives it, first chunk at 64 frames, three threads, sent to the game as joined pieces; Nano stays for the others. The proper route (Nano or Sopro converted to ONNX inside the game) remains voice-in-the-game's two weeks, not needed to reach the target.
5. **Take the first sentence's check off the path (the town session's side, one to two days plus the ClaimBench rerun).** Two ways, both already half there: (a) the model is asked to open with a short reaction of its own that names nothing specific ("Depends who's asking.", "Not that I've seen, no."), which the check already passes at once as "plain" (4 of 24 first sentences did on 30 September); (b) a leaner check for the first sentence only, aiming to end within about 0.8 s of it being written. Either puts heard at about 0.9 + 0.5 + 0.1 = 1.5 s median for Ron and Darren.
6. **Sheila's first sentence.** At about 2.0 s written she cannot make 2 s heard whatever the voice does. Caching her card and rules (prompt-caching note, already recommended for cost) should shorten the model's first token (Anthropic states that caching cuts latency; by how much at her prompt size is not verified); otherwise her first sentence needs a faster model, which is a decision for Jafar.
7. **Keep the pre-made acknowledgements** as cover for the tail, cut the moment the answer starts, as now.

Expected, if steps 1 to 5 land and Sopro streams on this processor as its maker says: Ron and Darren about 1.5 s median; Sheila about 2.5 s until step 6; the 90th percentile still set by the model's slow first sentences (up to 4.7 s in the 24 turns), which only a faster or hedged first request moves.

## What to measure first

A replay beside the running game, free and without the key: the street running at Jafar's settings; the 24 real first sentences from 30 September fed to the voice at their measured "written" times; three voices in turn: Nano on the card as today, Sopro whole-sentence on the processor, Sopro streaming on the processor with three threads; for each, the time from sentence to first audio, and the game's median and slowest-1% frame times.
