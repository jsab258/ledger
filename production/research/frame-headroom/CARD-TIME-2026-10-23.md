# The card's spare time: what grew between 7 and 21 October (23 October 2026)

Question 3a of QUESTIONS-2026-10-23.md. The card's spare time against the 14.0 ms planning line fell from 2.65 ms by day and 2.95 at night (7 October, build 67c51f5) to 0.54 and 0.28 (21 October, build 00f81b1). Which passes grew, by how much, and from what?

Analysis only. Nothing was run in the game or in Unreal, and nothing was changed. The kept frame files were read with small scripts of my own (scratch, not kept).

Labels used below. **Measured**: read from the frame files or runs.jsonl. **Lead**: likely, from the history or the code, not proven. **Estimate**: a number reasoned from measurements, not measured itself.

## The answer in short

1. **Most of the loss is not new content. It is the picture setting.** The 21 October captures ran the game at the engine's Highest picture. The 7 October ones ran at the project's High, the picture a player's first launch gives. Highest costs the card about 2.1 ms a frame, 1.75 to 1.8 ms of it in the upscaler (Temporal Super Resolution). **Measured**, three ways (below).
2. **By day, standing: the picture explains all of it.** 2.11 ms lost; the picture accounts for about 2.1. The content added since 7 October nets out to about zero by day. **Measured** (difference of two measured differences; the noise is about ±0.2 ms).
3. **At night, standing: the picture explains about 2.1 of the 2.67 ms.** The remaining ~0.5 ms is content, net. Inside it, the street lamps' and Rita's shadow maps grew by 1.36 ms; the deferred lighting fell 0.40 and Lumen's reflections 0.22. **Measured** sizes; the shadows' cause is a **lead**.
4. **Walking: not like with like.** The GPU median moved 1.03 ms, but the walk covered 30.7 m on 7 October and 34.6 m on 21 October, and the card's work changes along the street. A same-place comparison is given below.
5. At High, the 21 October content would have had about **2.6 ms spare by day and 2.4 at night** (**estimate**: measured room plus the measured picture cost). That meets PLAN's 2.5 by day and 1.4 at night.

## How the picture level was found

- **The upscaler's time is the High or Highest value.** 7 October afternoon, every capture at High: 1.03 to 1.27 ms. 7 October morning, the copy still at Highest: 2.58 to 2.97. 21 October, every capture: 2.72 to 3.13. 22 and 23 October: 0.99 to 1.18. (Measured, runs.jsonl.)
- **The frame files say which picture ran.** Each capture's closing line records the texture pool: 1000 MB in all 19 kept captures of 21 October, 800 MB in the four of 22 and 23 October checked. In the engine's own scalability file the texture pool is 800 at High and 1000 at Highest. (Measured; Engine/Config/BaseScalability.ini.)
- **What Highest changes, from the engine's file.** The upscaler keeps its history at 200% of the screen instead of 100%, and updates it at a higher quality. Also: post-processing at Epic, scene colour at 64 bits instead of 32, skin's subsurface at full resolution, a larger translucency lighting volume, local-light shadow maps up to 2048 instead of 1024, and smaller objects casting shadows. The project's High already keeps Epic's reflections and lighting (ue-probe/Config/DefaultScalability.ini), so Lumen is the same at both.
- **Its cost, measured three ways:**
  - Same place, same frame path: the 21 October walk (00f81b1, Highest) against the 22 October walk (35e10a9, High), both with the card voice, compared metre by metre in 13 bins of 2.5 m. Highest costs **+2.12 ms GPU**: upscale and post +1.87, everything else +0.03 to +0.11 per group. Draw calls +16 of about 1,500, so Highest adds almost no drawing. (Measured. The 22 October PC was not quiet, which if anything shrinks this difference.)
  - 7 October, standing by day: the morning's Highest (a32eeeb) against the afternoon's High with the old room shadows (c2f17fb, -RoomShadowsAll, which undoes that build's other change to the frame): **+2.13 ms**; upscale and post +1.81, shadows +0.13. (Measured, runs.jsonl.)
  - 3 October, in the editor, standing by day: Epic against High-with-Epic-reflections-and-lighting: **+2.24 ms**; upscale and post +1.78. Small samples (34 and 167 frames). (Measured, p1-runs.)
- **Why the 21 October runs were at Highest. Lead, not proven.** Each new build's copy replaces the played copy whole, its saved settings with it (.github/workflows/ledger-probe-unreal.yml, the stable-copy step). The capture starts the game with -NoTitle (tools/p1-packaged.ps1), and the picture budget is applied only from the title screen (TitleScreen.cpp: FirstLaunchSettings and PictureBudgetOnce). So a fresh copy measured before anyone opens its title runs at the engine's default, Highest. On 21 October three builds were copied and measured in a row. On 22 and 23 October the nightly tester's walk had opened the title first and saved High. How the 7 October afternoon captures came to be at High is not on record: their command lines were not kept. The script's -Picture switch ("scalability 2"), added that day, is the likely way.

### The night's shadows: what the files show, and the leads

Measured:
- On 21 October at night, the game names four lights casting shadows within 40 m. Three street-lamp pools (spot lights, 500 lm, 18 m reach, at 12, 21 and 31 m from the camera) and Rita's window spill (a rect light, 350 lm, 9 m reach). None casts from "cinematic" objects only (the capture's own log). On 7 October there were five: the three pools, Mickey's tube and Rita's spill (P1-PACKAGED-2026-10-07.md). So fewer lights now cost more.
- The night's shadow maps take 2.37 ms for 527 shadow draws. The day's sun takes 1.99 ms for 902. So the night's maps are heavy for their draws: large maps, or large areas to fill.

Leads (not separable with the kept files):
- **Rita's spill draws its shadows six times.** In the engine's source a rect light's shadow is a one-pass cube: all six directions, everything within its reach (RectLightSceneProxy.cpp). It now stands in front of the cloud's display (189,416 triangles, 51 parts) and the cloud's front (new; 56,155 triangles, 24 parts), and it takes every other mesh within 9 m too.
- **The pools are wider.** Their cone went from 42 to 46 degrees on 8 October (unreal-look.json). That is about 32% more ground in each pool's shadow map.
- **More things stand in the pools' reach:** the lamp posts themselves, Mickey's row's sashes, the houses' sides, Mickey's back room, and the people placed at Mickey's door.
- **Highest's own shadow settings:** local-light maps up to 2048 instead of 1024, and smaller casters drawn. Their share at night was not measured on 21 October. The one night pair on record (3 October, editor, only 23 frames at Epic) put Epic's shadows 0.17 ms above High's.
- **The engine's shadow cache cannot help here.** For a still spot or point light, Unreal keeps the shadow of static meshes from frame to frame (r.Shadow.CacheWholeSceneShadows, on by default: ShadowSetup.cpp). But the street's meshes are all spawned movable (VignetteShot.cpp), so every shadowing light redraws every caster every frame.

## What could be cut or tuned (savings are estimates)

1. **Measure the picture the player gets.** Not a cut. The capture should ask for High, or the game should apply its picture budget under -NoTitle too. Each runs.jsonl line should record the picture (the frame file's texture pool gives it free: 800 is High, 1000 is Highest), and a capture at a picture nobody asked for should fail. **Restores about 2.1 ms** of reported spare time. This is the measured picture cost, not an estimate. It also means the 21 October card-memory figures were taken at Highest. Highest's larger texture pool (+200 MB) and the upscaler's 200% history would have added to the game's own share: an estimate of 0.2 to 0.5 GB, not measured. Question 1a should check it.
2. **The sun's shadow maps by day.** By day no local light casts shadows (the game's own count: 0 within 40 m). So the day's ShadowDepths, about 1.9 to 2.0 ms and 902 draws, is almost all the sun's cascades (a lead: lights beyond 40 m are not counted). The sun stands at a tenth of the scene file's strength under an overcast sheet that asks for no sun shadow (unreal-look.json, sun_gain_note). Fewer cascades, a shorter shadow distance (the distance-field shadows already cost 0.21 ms beyond it), or no sun shadow maps: **estimate 0.8 to 1.6 ms by day.** It touches the look, so it needs his page.
3. **The night's local-light shadows (2.37 ms):**
   - Rita's spill casting only from its own display and front, as the rooms' lights have done since 7 October, or casting none: **estimate 0.2 to 0.6 ms.**
   - The two far pools (21 and 31 m) at a lower shadow resolution, or their shadows fading with distance: **estimate 0.2 to 0.5 ms.**
   - The street's fixed meshes made static, so the engine's cache keeps their shadows and each still lamp redraws only people: **estimate 0.5 to 1.5 ms at night**, and it would hold as more shop spills arrive. Unverified whether the cache works for meshes spawned while the game runs. It costs card memory (the cache's default budget is 150 MB), and card memory is already short. Needs research first.
4. **Low priority:** the upscaler at High is about 1.0 to 1.2 ms. The 55% picture depends on it. Small knobs might save 0.1 to 0.3 ms (estimate).

## Worth knowing beside the question

- **The voice's place moves the card's numbers.** Same build (00f81b1, Highest, standing by day): GPU 13.46 ms with the voice on the card, 12.67 and 12.73 with Pocket on the processor. Mostly in Lumen's reflections (2.41 against 1.98). Measured. Question 3c's runs must say where the voice was.
- **The shadows are still growing after 00f81b1.** The memory re-measure of this afternoon (23 October, build of 22 October 13:45, High, voice on the processor, read from runs.jsonl at 15:31) shows ShadowDepths at 2.50 by day (shadow draws 1,135 against 902) and 3.19 at night, with five lights shadowing at night (two new 60 lm window spills). GPU 11.41 by day and 11.68 at night. Not like with like (another build, the voice elsewhere), but the direction is plain: each new shop adds casters to the sun's cascades and to its own spill's cube.
- **The frame is set by the render thread, not the card.** In every kept capture the render thread's median is within 0.1 ms of the frame's (21 October, day: 15.17 against 15.19; GPU 13.46). Measured. Reading it as the frame's limiter is a lead: the engine's exact meaning of RenderThreadTime was not checked. More card spare time alone may not buy frames.
