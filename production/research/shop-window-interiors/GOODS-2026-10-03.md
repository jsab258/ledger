# Shop-window goods that read as real at 1 to 3 m

Research note, 3 October 2026, by a separate helper, about 35 minutes. All sources were accessed that day.

- OPENED means the page or data record was read.
- SNIPPET means only a search summary was seen.
- UNREACHED means the page refused us. Nothing is concluded from an unreached page.
- "Mine" marks my own inference.

**The tool reads text, not pictures.** Every asset below was found by its title, its data record and its licence. Nobody has looked at it yet. Each one must be checked by eye, against the reference photographs, before it goes into a window. That is the gate's first check.

Fab and Sketchfab were searched from this PC through the browser pane, without signing in. Their data records were read directly.

## 0. The answer in brief

1. **The bar is met with scans, not modelling.** The one window that passed used scanned goods. Studios that aim at photoreal food scan it, or take it from a scan library. Hand-modelled or hand-painted food is the stylised route, and against a photoreal bar it reads as toys.
2. **There is far more free, allowed scan material than we are using.**
   - **Fish, crabs and prawns:** a Japanese natural-history archive publishes hundreds of CC0 scans on Sketchfab. It includes a cod, a mackerel, a herring, flatfish, crabs and a close cousin of the British prawn.
   - **Fruit, veg and baking:** Quixel's free Megascans on Fab cover apples, pears, bananas, lemons, cabbage, carrots, grapes, breads, a tea cake, muffins, pork pies and a smoked fish.
   - **Rope:** Megascans also has rope coils.
3. **Things with print on them are not scanned anywhere:** newspapers, magazines, boxes of chocolates and paint tins. Studios make them as simple shapes and spend the effort on the printed graphics. We must design the graphics ourselves, all fictional (no real brands).
4. **Some goods have no allowed source at all:** sweets in jars, the sou'wester, scones, tomatoes, fillets, dressed crab and crushed ice. Each needs a built method. Section 4 gives the cheapest method that should read at 1 to 3 m.
5. **Glass jars:** do not rely on the importer, and do not rely on true transparency behind the shop window. Make the sweets real, opaque geometry. Give the jar a thin shell with a faked rim highlight, and make the lid and label solid. The importer's black glass is replaced by script.
6. **Licence traps:**
   - On Fab, "free" sometimes means only the "UEFN – Reference only" tier is free. Clementine is an example. Those listings are money.
   - Many free Fab and Sketchfab models are CC BY. CC BY is not on our allowlist for 3D.
   - Public-domain NOAA photographs are not named on the allowlist either. Both need his ruling before use. Neither is needed for the plan below.

## 1. The method: how studios dress windows, stalls and shelves

**1. Scanned hero goods, then instancing with variation.**
- Photoreal games build food from photogrammetry:
  - Final Fantasy XV's team cooked its dishes, then photographed and scanned them (Engadget, 13 February 2017, SNIPPET).
  - In Resident Evil 7, more than half of the assets were photogrammetry, including scanned meat (CEDEC 2016, reported by cgrecord.net in September 2016 and Kill Screen; SNIPPET).
  - A DiGRA paper surveys photogrammetry in game environments from 2014 to 2019 (SNIPPET).
- Quixel's Megascans library exists to sell exactly these food scans to studios. Its listings give each item's real physical size (section 3).
- Mine: a scan carries its own colour variation, cavity, roughness and silhouette noise. A modelled apple has to fake all four, and that is where "toy" comes from.

**2. Hand-painted food is the stylised route.**
- 80.lv's Medieval Market guide hand-paints its fruit, vegetables, pastries and fish in Substance Painter (SNIPPET). That works for a stylised game.
- Mine: it is the look the reviewer failed.

**3. Packaged and printed goods are simple meshes carrying good graphics.**
- Naughty Dog says its "one-offs such as signs, branding, and unique assets" were outsourced, and kept in a tagged library (80.lv, 8 December 2020, OPENED).
- The shape of a tin, a box or a magazine is trivial. The print is what reads as real (mine). One common way to get many products from a few meshes:
  - all labels go into one texture atlas;
  - each instance picks its tile through per-instance custom data;
  - Unreal's instanced meshes and PCG both support this. PCG's spawner packs attributes into per-instance custom data that the material reads (Epic docs on custom primitive data, and the Epic forum on PCG's instance data packer, SNIPPET).

**4. Flat things have real thickness at close range.**
- Mine:
  - A newspaper or magazine seen at 1 m needs a visible edge (2 to 4 mm), a slight curl and a soft fold.
  - A flat alpha card is fine only for things seen face-on from further away: a poster, a postcard pinned to a board, or the back rows of a rack.
  - At 1 to 3 m, the front rows are thin meshes and the back rows can be cards.

**5. Piles are a few distinct pieces, never one copy.**
- A crate of apples is three to six different scans. Each is instanced with random rotation, a ±10% scale and a slight tint per instance, through Unreal's PerInstanceRandom (mine).
- This also answers the reviewer's "copy-pasted" note on the pawnbroker's window.

**6. Glass props: the contents are solid, and the glass is faked.**
- Practice on Polycount is plain. The contents are made opaque. The glass is either a translucent shell or a reflection-only layer. Where true transparency costs too much, it is an unlit translucent layer with a cubemap reflection (SNIPPET; one thread, polycount.com/discussion/198225, was UNREACHED, 403).
- Section 4 gives the Unreal 5.8 detail.

**7. Real geometry in the near metre, the interior image behind it.**
- This is unchanged from the 1 October note (NOTE.md, sections 2 and 4).
- The goods in this note are the near-metre display.

**8. Kingdom Come: Deliverance II.**
- No public source was found on how Warhorse makes its market goods. Searches found only face scanning and a historian who checks food.
- We judge against its frames, not its method.

**A pipeline for one window (mine, from the above):**
1. List the goods from the period photographs.
2. Take scans for everything organic.
3. Decimate the heavy scans and bake their normals in Blender.
4. Build the printed goods as simple meshes with our own graphics.
5. Lay them out by script with variation.
6. Light them with the window's own light.
7. Judge through the game camera against the Hook sheet and the KCD2 frames.

## 2. The allowlist test

Allowlist: ledger-v2/research/license-allowlist.md, SHIP-SAFE 2, "CC0 libraries (Poly Haven, ambientCG, Sketchfab CC0 filter)" and "Fab purchases under the Fab Standard License".

- **Poly Haven (CC0): passes.**
- **ambientCG (CC0): passes.** It has materials only, no models of goods.
- **Sketchfab CC0: passes, item by item.**
  - Each ffishAsia scan is labelled "CC0 Public Domain", and its own description says "License: CC0" (OPENED, data records).
  - A download needs a Sketchfab account (mine, from how the site works; not tested). We do not know whether he has one. Signing in is his to do, not ours.
- **Fab, Quixel Megascans, price 0 on the Personal or Professional tier: passes** under the Fab Standard License. The project already takes free Megascans (production/specs/fab-free-megascans.md).
  - The Fab EULA (last updated 1 October 2024, OPENED) allows the Personal tier only if the buyer has had no more than $100,000 of revenue in the last 12 months. Where both tiers are 0, take Professional.
  - **Trap 1.** Fab's "free" filter also lists items whose only free tier is "UEFN – Reference only", with Personal and Professional paid. Clementine is CHF 1.64 Personal and CHF 16.56 Professional. Each item in section 3 was checked: both its Personal and Professional prices are 0.
  - **Trap 2.** Every Megascans record checked carries `isAiForbidden: true`. Mine: do not feed these assets into AI tools (TRELLIS, image generators). The exact terms behind the flag were not read.
- **CC BY, on Fab or Sketchfab: not on the allowlist for 3D.** Needs his ruling. None is recommended.
  - Examples: EFX's "Paint Can Scan", gpz3d's "Glass Jar", and plaggy's "CC0 – Jar", which is labelled CC0 in its title but licensed CC BY.
- **Fab "UE Marketplace License": not plainly on the allowlist** (the 1 October note). Not recommended.
- **Real brands: never ship** (NEVER SHIP 5). Examples: Louey's free scanned "Whitman's" and "Rawleigh's" tins.
- **NOAA photographs: not on the allowlist by name.**
  - Images "created by NOAA are not subject to copyright in the United States and may be used without permission", crediting NOAA. Images credited to others need their owners' permission. (NOAA Fisheries website policies, last updated 02/04/2026 in US style, so 4 February 2026, OPENED.)
  - Using them as textures is a licence decision for him. **The plan below does not need them.**
- **Smithsonian Open Access 3D (CC0): not searched directly.** The Smithsonian items in Sketchfab's CC0 results were vases, garments and a mantis shrimp, none of them shop goods. Treat this as unchecked, not as empty.

**A limit on the Sketchfab search.** The anonymous API returned a short, unpaged list for each search: "cake" gave 12 results, and "candy" none. It probably searches only part of the CC0 catalogue. The website's own CC0 search was not checked, so more CC0 goods may exist there.

## 4. The hardest cases: the cheapest method that should read at 1 to 3 m

Everything in this section is mine unless a source is named. Each method is a proposal to try once, judged in the game camera.

**The heavy scans: process them first.**
- The ffishAsia scans are 0.5 to 2.9 M triangles each.
- In Blender, decimate each to about 20,000 to 50,000 triangles. Bake normal and AO maps from the full scan onto the result.
- If a scan carries vertex colour instead of a texture, bake that to a base-colour map. Whether these scans come textured or vertex-coloured is not known until one is downloaded.
- Alternatively, Nanite in 5.8 can take the full mesh. But two dozen fish at 1 to 2 M triangles each is a lot of disk and memory for one window.

**Whole fish on ice.**
- Take decimated scans: cod, mackerel and herring.
- The material carries the wet look: roughness about 0.15 to 0.25 on the skin, a little higher on the fins, specular a little up, and a glossy eye.
- Lay the fish in rows by script. Each gets a few degrees of random roll and is half sunk into the ice so its belly does not float.
- Recolour a flounder scan for plaice: brown-grey with orange spots, as a decal or in its base colour.

**Crushed ice.**
- Two layers, both opaque:
  - a displaced bed mesh that follows the slab, with a white-blue base colour and low roughness (ambientCG Ice or Snow maps as a start);
  - 200 to 400 instanced small chunk meshes (three or four shapes) on top, around the fish.
- The chunks need no true transparency. A subsurface profile with a bright, slightly blue scatter and strong speculars reads as ice at 1 m. What reads as real is the many small glints under the window light.

**Fillets (cod, smoked haddock).**
- No scan exists. Make each one as a relief mesh: a flat, tapered fillet outline, lightly displaced, with a procedural flake pattern (stretched cells, slight translucency or subsurface).
- Cod is off-white. Smoked haddock was dyed yellow-orange.
- Laid overlapping in a white tray, at 1 m the tray and the overlap carry most of it.
- If it still reads as plastic after one try, set it aside and use whole fish and smoked fish instead.

**Kippers.**
- Take the herring scan, flatten and split it in Blender into a butterflied shape, and tint it copper-brown and glossy.
- Or use the Megascans Smoked Fish, if it reads as a kipper by eye.

**Dressed crab.**
- No source shows a British brown crab.
- Take the carapace of a CC0 crab scan, the most oval one, recoloured to brown-red.
- Turn it into a dish: fill it with two displaced patches, white flaked meat and a brown paste, plus a parsley sprig.
- Seen from above at 1 m, the filling is what reads.

**Prawns.**
- Decimate the Pandalus scan to about 5,000 triangles and tint it cooked pink.
- Instance 40 to 80 in a tray with random rotations, so it reads as a heap.

**Sweets in jars.**
- **Contents:**
  - Model four or five sweet shapes (boiled-sweet ovals, humbugs using ambientCG Candy stripes, cola cubes, pear drops), a few hundred triangles each.
  - Fill each jar by script, by dropping them in a quick Blender rigid-body run, then merge each jar's contents into one static mesh.
  - Give the sweets a glossy, slightly translucent-looking surface: subsurface, low roughness.
- **The jar:** a lathe in Blender, a straight-sided jar with a coloured screw lid and a printed paper label (fictional maker). The lid and label are opaque and carry most of the "jar".
- **The glass, the cheapest version that should read:**
  - a thin shell in a translucent, unlit, additive material;
  - it adds only a Fresnel rim and a soft, fixed reflection: a small baked picture of the street, or the sky cubemap;
  - the sweets stay fully visible and fully lit through it.
- **Why not true glass here.** The shop window is already translucent, and Lumen's high-quality reflections apply only to the front translucent layer (NOTE.md, section 4). So a physically correct glass jar behind the window gets no proper reflections and will look flat or murky. The faked rim does better there and costs less. Translucent behind translucent also invites sorting faults.
- **If true glass is wanted** (the cab office, say, which is entered and has no window between): use the Thin Translucent shading model.
  - Translucent blend mode, shading model Thin Translucent, lighting mode Surface ForwardShading (UE 5.8 Shading Models page, OPENED; setup lines SNIPPET).
  - It renders tinted glass with white highlights in one pass.
- **Furthest rows only:** an opaque cylinder whose material shows a sweets texture under a clearcoat sheen. It passes from across the road, not at 1 m.

**The glTF importer's black glass.**
- Poly Haven's glTF files mark glass with the transmission extension. Unreal's glTF importer maps each glTF material to one of a set of engine material instances listed under Project Settings, glTF Settings (for example MI_Default_Blend) (UE 5.3 Interchange reference, SNIPPET; the 5.8 page, OPENED, does not describe it).
- Ours came out opaque black. So the transmission was not mapped to a transparent archetype (mine).
- **The fix games use anyway: never trust an importer with glass.** After import, the import script (tools/ue/import_*) replaces every material whose name contains "glass", or whose glTF material has transmission, with our own glass material instance. That is M_LedgerGlass, or the jar shell above.
- Overriding the glTF archetype in Project Settings may also work. It is not verified for 5.8.

**Boxes of chocolates.**
- Simple box meshes with a slightly bevelled lid, stood on end as the spec says.
- All the effort goes on the lid art. Design six to eight fictional 1990-style lids:
  - a painted country scene, a ribbon band, a gold script name;
  - set in OFL fonts;
  - no real maker, no children in any picture.
- Add a thin sheen for the cellophane, done as a clearcoat on the lid, not as transparency.
- Put the lids in one atlas and pick a tile per instance.

**Magazines and newspapers.**
- Thin meshes, not cards:
  - a folded newspaper is a slab 3 to 4 mm thick with a soft fold and a slight curl, laid in a stepped row;
  - a magazine is 2 to 3 mm, fanned with a slight bend.
- Pages are generated by script: an invented masthead and headline readable at 1 m, columns of grey text, and halftone pictures.
- Content rules apply to every page: no children on any cover, no pools coupons or betting, no drink adverts, no real titles.
- Only the back rows of the rack are alpha cards.

**Paint tins (new stock).**
- A lathe tin with a lid rim and a wire bail, about 0.18 m.
- A clean printed label: fictional maker, a colour swatch band, "gloss" or "emulsion", the size in litres and pints.
- A slight dent and edge wear from a roughness map.
- The Megascans Dirty Paint Can is right for the ironmonger's back shelf or the chandler's varnish, not for the window pyramid.

**Coiled ropes.**
- Use the Megascans Rope Coil and Bundled Rope Coil as they are, in natural fibre.
- For the four colours, make a Blender curve spiral with a three-strand twisted profile, using ambientCG Rope textures, hue-shifted per coil to blue, orange, white and green.
- At 1 m, the twist in the strands is what reads, so it must be real geometry or a strong normal map, not a flat texture.

**The sou'wester.**
- No source exists in any allowed licence. It is a garment, so it follows the clothing pipeline:
  - a simple sewn pattern, a crown of four to six panels with a long back brim, made in Blender;
  - stiffened, with a yellow oilskin material: slightly glossy and rubbery, with darker seams and creasing at the brim.
- Hang it from a peg so only the outside shows.
- Mine, and the dimensions are unverified: crown about 18 cm deep, front brim about 5 cm, back brim about 10 to 12 cm. Check against a photograph before making it.
- It is a prop, not a worn garment, so no fitting to a body is needed.

**Scones.**
- Take a bread-roll scan, squash it to about 6 to 7 cm across and 4 cm high, and lighten the crust.
- Slice a few open with a dab of jam and cream, as small separate meshes, on a doily.
- If it does not read after one try, show tea cakes and pork pies instead.

**Tomatoes.**
- No allowed scan was found. Model a tomato from dimensions: a sphere flattened at the stem, with a green calyx.
- Use a glossy, slightly translucent red with fine surface noise, varied per instance.
- A heap of glossy tomatoes reads better than one close up. Keep them behind the apples in the crate order.
