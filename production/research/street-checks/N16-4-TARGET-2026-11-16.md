**Decision recorded (the session, 16 Nov):** the course pitch of 86 mm applies to the brick texture only; the kit's geometry grid stays as it is (75 mm course, 225 mm brick, TERRACE-KIT-TABLE section 3). **Period sources (R).** Flemish "predominated domestically" in Victorian work (buildingconservation.com Brickwork, via READING B4). Burnt or vitrified headers are described for Tudor to Georgian diaper and chequer work (RICS; buildingconservation.com; Historic England list text for an 18th-century Wiltshire house, via READING B4). Wikipedia Brickwork (read 16 Nov): "Some examples of Flemish bond incorporate stretchers of one colour and headers of another"; headers exposed to the fire turn "grey-blue" or vitrified "deeper blue"; some salt-glazed; sometimes Staffordshire blue headers. No source read ties a regular burnt-header pattern to Victorian northern terraces (U).

**Recommendation on "burnt headers": occasional darker, over-fired headers, randomly placed and loosely clustered; not a chequer, not black, not glossy.** Support: Hull 1986 (P) shows darker headers irregularly; Hull 1982 (P) shows almost none; the chequer diaper is attested only for earlier work (R); the Hook sheet, his approved look, shows scattered dark headers. A clear chequer would be a period error for c.1870-1900 fronts (I, M confidence).

## 2. Method

So the bond is our own method, with Mafia's variation and corner-piece methods.

**Recommended route (sources named per step):**
1. The procedural Flemish tile of 10 Nov (MaterialCraft SVG bond, PROOF-2026-11-10), redrawn at the 86 mm texture course, given a **per-brick ID channel with a header flag** (BRICK-2026-10-04 method 2; built for scans as tools/brick_ids.py, DECISIONS 3 Nov 20:31). The shader darkens headers by frac(id + house seed) under a world-noise cluster mask, so each house differs and no grid appears. Fixes fault 2.
2. Mortar: lower chroma, recessed by height and normal, soot from the generator's eave and sill masks (BRICK-METHODS 4). Arris and face wear from photographed bricks pasted into the bond (BRICK-METHODS 1) or a coarser edge-wear mask, judged at 5-10 m. Fixes fault 1.
3. Grade to hue 12-18° and today's near-wall mean (BRICK-COLOUR; review.json). Fixes fault 3.
4. Corners: the kit's Corner_225x2775 (TERRACE-KIT-TABLE #8) carries the quoin pattern, as Mafia's corner pieces do; or the wall's UVs start at the arris with the return offset by a header plus closer. Fixes fault 4.
5. Variation over metres as built (macro 0.08 at 4 m, DECISIONS 3 Nov 21:14); repointing patches and spalls as masks or decals by source.

## 3. The three street checks

**(a) Width, front to front (s04-01).**
- Reads production/specs/vignette-scene.json `street.carriageway.half_width_m`, `street.kerb.width_m`, `street.footway.width_m`. Width = 2 x (3.0 + 0.125 + 2.0) = 10.250 m, matching study 04's 10.25 and SliceCharacter's 5.125 per side.
- Assumptions, stated in the tool's output: the channel lies inside the carriageway (3.0 - 0.255 = 2.745, the cam_hook note's "the channel starts at 2.745"); the facade stands at the back of the footway, since the blocks carry no frontage z. Once placed fronts exist (the placement spec), the tool reads their faces instead.
- Later streets: the same fields per street, or sampled every 5 m along a street's spline if widths vary.
- Pass 9.000 ≤ w ≤ 15.000 m at 1 mm. A missing field fails, never skips.
- Output: `street-width: quay_street 10.250 m (9.0-15.0; frontage = footway back) PASS`.
- Tool tests: pass on today's file; fail with footway 0.5 (7.250 m); fail with half_width 6.0 (16.250 m); boundary 9.000 passes; missing kerb field fails.

**(b) Kit reuse (s04-04).**
- Reads the placement spec (MAFIA-KIT 5.4, the PCG export: one record per placement with mesh, family, kind, street, one_off flag). None exists today: Quay Street exports one mesh per material (quay-street.json "what").
- Failure trigger: when a street in vignette-scene.json carries `"facade_source": "kit"` (a new field, written when the shells step starts, PIECES-DRAFT step 3) and no placement spec names that street, the check fails. Without the field it prints `kit-reuse: no placement spec (facade_source not kit)` and passes.
- Counts per street: distinct facade meshes, facade families, placements, uses per kit mesh (placements / meshes, kit families only), kit share of facade placements; one-offs (shops, Mickey's, flagged) counted apart.
- Mafia (studies/04, M): the measured street, 18-19 facade pieces from 2 families, about 150 meshes, about 2,350 placements, about 15 uses per mesh; the whole main-street group (about 100 buildings), 88% kit elements. They fit only in kind: Mafia's 73 m street has 4-5 storeys and balconies; Quay Street has 36 m of frontage and 2 storeys.
- Bars (I, Prov.): facade families ≤ 3 per street; kit share ≥ 70% excluding one-offs; no kit mesh used once unless flagged one_off; uses per kit mesh ≥ 4, printed beside Mafia's 15.
- Pass fixture, worked: the front wall modules of houses A, B, C in TERRACE-KIT-TABLE section 4 (A: G 7,1,6,2,4,2 and T 7,2,5,1,1,4,2; B: G 7,2,4,2,6,1 and T 7,2,4,1,1,1,2; C: G 7,2,4,1,6,2,4,8 and U, T each 7,2,4,2,5,1,1,4,8). 52 placements of 7 meshes (#1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8): 7.4 uses per mesh; fewest uses 3 (#5, #6, #8); one family; kit 100%: PASS. C's side wall and gable (#9, used once) are left out, or #9 is flagged one_off.
- Fail fixtures: the same strings with each house's meshes renamed unique: 18 meshes, 52 placements, 2.9 uses per mesh, and A's #6 used once: FAIL; any fixture with an unflagged single-use mesh: FAIL; `facade_source: kit` with no spec: FAIL.
- Output: `kit-reuse: quay_street facade meshes 7, families 1, placements 52, uses/mesh 7.4 (Mafia 15), kit 100% PASS` (the fixture's numbers).

**(c) The street's ends (s04-06).**
- Cameras: vignette-scene.json `cameras` whose yaw lies within 30° of the street axis: cam_A (yaw 0), cam_hook (20.4), cam_reverse (200.4).
- By data, in plan: from each camera, rays every 1° across ±10° **about the street axis** (+x for cam_A and cam_hook, -x for cam_reverse), clipped to the camera's horizontal field (46° vertical at 16:9 gives about 74° horizontal, so for cam_hook the fan runs 10.4° to 30.4° left of the view centre, inside the frame). Each ray must hit a "closer" (a block footprint, or an entry in a new `street_ends` list: end building, turn, hillside houses, quayside sheds, each with footprint and top height) at or before the road's modelled end (no cut), and the closer's top must stand above the eye (elevation angle > 0°), so no sky or fog sits at the vanishing point (no void).
- By picture, on the morning pictures (hook by day, reverse view): a box 10% of the frame width around the street's vanishing point, from the horizon to 8% of the height above it, may hold at most 20% sky or fog-white pixels (luminance within 5% of the sky sample, saturation < 0.06). Then one fresh reviewer per view: void, cut, closed vista.
- Output: `street-ends: cam_hook 21/21 rays closed (nearest 38 m) PASS; cam_A 9/21 open past x 48 FAIL`.
- Tool tests: pass fixture: a closer across the road from z -6 to +6 at x 47 to 48, 8 m high (inside the road's end at 48); fail fixture: closers removed (rays reach x 48); fail fixture: the same closer only 1.2 m high (its top below a 1.6 m eye).
- Expected today (U, not run): cam_A and cam_reverse likely fail; the scene file says the road "runs past" the blocks into fog, and the reverse view looks toward the quay (the corner gate listed "the quay end fading to white", GATE-MIDWEEK-2026-11-04).
