# The kit's master material: surface detail without unique UVs (21 October 2026)

Why: lamp post C, the first cloud kit piece in the street, renders as a clean, flat, pale block (all three gate reviewers listed it as a shortfall; the concrete reads about sRGB 186 to 196 in the day views against the target's 146). His order of 21 October, item 5: "shared Unreal master materials made once from the material slot names ... the look is made in Unreal against the Hook sheet." The kit's glTF files carry only flat colours and auto-generated UVs.

Questions: (1) how shipped games give modular props detail without unique UVs; (2) what UE's world-aligned functions are and cost; (3) how a tiled detail keeps the authored colour in charge; (4) cheapest weathering; (5) albedo ranges, and whether Lumen explains the clean look.

Method: one research helper, 21 October, read-only; the two engine functions opened directly in the installed UE 5.8; web pages through a summarising fetch (a digest of the full page, not the raw text). Marks: UNVERIFIED where only a snippet or a third party was seen; my inference marked "inference".

## Findings

1. **Detail without unique UVs.** No GDC talk or Epic sample read names a default for small street furniture. Artist write-ups: tileables on UVs or in world space, the latter "ignores your UVs"; a world-space dirt or detail layer makes identical pieces differ; decals for one-off marks; few materials a prop (Beyond Extent, Kronenberger, 14 Sep 2020, https://www.beyondextent.com/articles/balancing-modularity-and-uniqueness-in-environment-art, read 21 Oct 2026). Trim sheets plus world-position moss and decals (80.lv, Zhukov, 30 Apr 2026, https://80.lv/articles/making-of-a-modular-gothic-environment-with-procedural-systems-trim-sheets-custom-shaders, read 21 Oct). Triplanar is "most often used" on landscapes and modular wall and floor systems (https://80.lv/articles/ue5-triplanar-deep-dive-from-worldalignedtexture-to-high-quality-normals-part-1, read 21 Oct). Sunset Overdrive's trim sheet and tileables (GDC 2015, https://archive.org/stream/GDC2015Olsen2/GDC2015-Olsen-2_djvu.txt, OCR garbled, read 21 Oct); Dishonored 2's world-offset grunge (third-party analysis, UNVERIFIED). Inference: with auto-generated UVs, world-aligned tiling plus a detail normal is the safe default.
2. **The engine's functions** (installed UE 5.8, opened 21 Oct): `/Engine/Functions/Engine_MaterialFunctions01/Texturing/WorldAlignedTexture` (inputs TextureObject, TextureSize in world units per repeat, WorldPosition, Export Float 4, World Space Normal, ProjectionTransitionContrast; outputs XY, Z, XYZ Texture) and `.../WorldAlignedNormal` (TextureObject, TextureSize, Normal, WorldPosition; outputs XY, XYZ, XYZFlatTop, Z). The XYZ output takes 3 texture samples against 1 for an ordinary one; WorldAlignedNormal's high-quality switch "Adds ~20 instructions" (Epic's note in the asset). Blend weights come from the vertex normal. Epic's docs give no cost figures (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/texturing-material-functions-in-unreal-engine, read 21 Oct).
3. **The authored colour in charge.** Epic's DetailTexturing function (in the asset): the detail map "Should have highpass run on it and have sRGB unchecked", "Multiply against your diffuse", the normal "Add to your normal"; 2 samples. Unity's Standard shader multiplies albedo by the detail times 2 so mid-grey is neutral (unofficial source mirror, UNVERIFIED). A high pass pulls a texture to mid-grey (https://gamedeveloper.com/art/the-power-of-the-high-pass-filter, read 21 Oct). Inference: store the detail with a mean of 0.5, sRGB off, apply lerp(1, 2 x detail, intensity).
4. **Weathering, cheapest first:** a height gradient (a few instructions, no texture; https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/paulareb-smart-height-dirt-procedural-mask-educational-breakdown/2708321, read 21 Oct, internals not shown); a baked vertex-colour channel; one shared grunge sample to break the gradient's edge; decals for one-off marks.
5. **Albedo and Lumen.** Epic's table: fresh concrete 0.51, worn asphalt 0.08 (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/physically-based-materials-in-unreal-engine, read 21 Oct; colour space not stated). Non-metals between about 30-50 and 240 sRGB (Adobe's PBR guide part 2, read 21 Oct). Concrete about 0.2-0.35 aged (search summaries, UNVERIFIED). Lumen gives infinite diffuse bounces (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-global-illumination-and-reflections-in-unreal-engine, read 21 Oct). Inference: the clean look comes from flat albedo and missing contact shading, not a Lumen quirk.

## Measured here

The lamp's concrete is 0.287 linear (sRGB 146), inside the range; it renders brighter because a free-standing column sees more open sky than a wall (the reviewers found the lantern's canopy over its target by the same ratio, so the exposure, not the material).

## Recommendation (to build, then a fresh review against this note)

One opaque master (and the glass master kept separate): world-aligned detail on by default; parameters BaseColor (linear), Roughness, Metallic, DetailSize (cm), DetailIntensity, NormalIntensity, dirt colour, dirt height and falloff; one detail map with a mean of 0.5 (sRGB off) and one detail normal, from the street's own surface photographs (high-passed offline), so no new fetched input. Grime band about 0 to 40 cm at the foot, its edge broken by the detail. Starting values: concrete sRGB 140-165, white enamel at most about 235, black paint at least about 35; cast iron and painted metal are non-metals.
