# Brick bond and material (research, 4 October 2026)

O = opened, S = search summary only, I = inference; [n] = sources below.

## Bond verdict: not stretcher; Flemish and English garden-wall. Confidence: medium

- Leigh, Lancashire: "English garden wall and later stretcher" are the commonest bonds [1 O]. Wigan: an 1870 house in garden-wall bond, 1850 terraces in Flemish [2 O].
- The North: tens of thousands of 9-inch walls in garden-wall bond, a header course every five to seven courses, "usually at the rear" [3 O]. Garden-wall bond often on Victorian terraces, especially northern; 3 or 5 stretcher courses [4 O, 20 O]. Sunderland "5 and one" [7 S]; Batley [5 O].
- Stretcher bond did occur: Princes Buildings, Preston (c.1860, S), shops with dwellings over, stretcher bond, mostly stuccoed [6 O]. One front so is defensible; a whole street is not (I).
- Flemish on better fronts: Preston, Grimsby [7 S]; called the common Victorian bond [20 O].
- **Share:** no source counts it. Estimate (I): fronts about half garden-wall, a third Flemish, under a tenth stretcher, English rare; rears garden-wall in commons.
- **For Meridian (I):** parade fronts Flemish (pressed facing brick); cottages garden-wall, 3 or 5 courses by house; rears 5-course in a duller common.

**Colour, mortar, wear**
- Hull: red and light buff commonest [8 O]; yellow fronts banded red, or the reverse [9 O]. Grimsby: red-brown, buff, yellow; blue-brick bands [10 O]. Blue engineering plinths: I.
- Lime mortar before about 1919 [11 S]; joints flush, struck or weather-struck [11 O]. Black ash mortar in industrial towns: I, unchecked.
- 1990 (I): brick still darkened from coal smoke, heaviest under eaves and sills, lighter where rain runs; some fronts cleaned, painted or rendered; grey cement repointing in patches.

## Why every house looks the same (I)

The tile is 7.2 m square; a front is about 5 m wide and 6 to 7 m high. If each front starts at the same UV origin (unchecked), each shows nearly one whole tile: the same bricks, the same tones, the same places. A tint moves the whole wall; the eye still matches the pattern.

## Material method, ranked by fit and cost

1. **Per-house values in Custom Primitive Data**: stored on the primitive, so similar meshes still instance (Epic's test: 46 draw calls, against about 94 with dynamic material instances); 32 floats [13 O]. Seed, tint, mortar, soot, UV offset. About 0 ms (I).
2. **Brick-ID channel, tone in the shader**: the tile stores a random value per brick and a header flag, not baked tone; the shader reads a small palette at frac(id + seed) [17 O]. A different tone layout per house; one sample, a few instructions (I). Fade it far off, where mips blur IDs (I).
3. **Per-house UV offset in whole bricks**: removes the identical patch at no cost (I). The engine's TextureVariation offsets randomly [16 O] and would break the bond's grid (I).
4. **Macro variation**: world-space noise at 2 to 10 m (engine MacroUVs, LowResBlurredNoise [16 O]); UT3 multiplied a second pattern at another scale [18 O]. One or two samples (I).
5. **Wear masks from the generator**, which knows every sill and eave: soot, rain-wash and repointing into a second UV. Vertex paint needs vertices a flat wall lacks (I); texture-colour paint needs virtual textures [15 S]. A UE London brick scene used dirt and weathering vertex channels [19 O].
6. **DBuffer decals** for specifics: cost grows with screen coverage and material complexity; no prepass penalty with Lumen; enable only the channels used [14 O].
7. **Fully procedural bond**: no repeat, no texture; the engine's brick functions do running bond only [16 O], so custom math (I). Fallback.

Costs are unmeasured: stat gpu on the hook frame.

## What to build, in order

1. Generator: Flemish and garden-wall (3- and 5-course) tiles with height, per-brick ID and header flag.
2. One master material: palette by ID and seed; Custom Primitive Data; whole-brick UV offset.
3. Seeds: parade Flemish, red with one buff; cottages garden-wall, courses mixed; no neighbours alike in bond, palette and soot.
4. Macro noise; soot masks from sills and eaves.
5. Decals last.
6. Judge on the hook frame; a fresh reviewer hunts the repeat.
