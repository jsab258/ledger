# Why our wet road read dry, and what a mirror road does (3 October 2026, evening)

The proof view, step 2.3. Jafar's bar: "dark, glistening, reflecting the sky and the buildings, puddles that look like water." Two fresh reviews failed it (wet4, wet9). This note sits beside PUDDLES-2026-10-02.md, whose sources it builds on.

M = measured on our frames; I = my inference; [n] = a source in PUDDLES-2026-10-02.md.

## What the frames showed

- **The puddles were mirrors all along [M].**
  - A frame of the roughness buffer (`-ExecCmds="viewmode VisualizeBuffer,r.BufferVisualizationTarget Roughness"`) shows every puddle black, i.e. roughness about 0. So the decal's roughness does reach the road, as [5] says it should.
  - The same view shows Mickey's glass white, roughness 1. That is why it catches no sky; it belongs to step 2.6.
- **The stains came from the road's own brightness [M].**
  - Water's F0 is 0.02 [6]. At the hook camera's 16° to the near road, Schlick gives about 0.21 of what the water mirrors.
  - The water mirrors the hill: a darker wall of houses. The road beside it was brighter under the overcast sky (puddle 101, road 124 on 0 to 255).
  - Darkening the road (gain 0.62 to 0.22) and making it near a mirror (0.06) made the frame read wet. It mirrored the pillar box, the facades and the posts, cleanly, with no grain.
  - Seven earlier tries had a rougher road (0.10 to 0.35) on a light road. They read damp, grainy or matte.
- **A perfect mirror road splits along the camera's own plane [M, I].**
  - A flat mirror keeps the view ray's direction across the street. Each lane therefore mirrors what lies on its own side of the camera.
  - The camera stands above the crown, so the boundary between "sky over the low cottages" and "the tall parade" projects onto the road as a straight line down the centre line. We measured 155 against 40 at that line.
  - It is not the crossfall: the mesh's normals are right, at 1 in 40, and that tilts a grazing reflection by under 1°.
  - Most of the near road's mirrored point lies above the top of the frame. So it comes from Lumen's world traces (the sky, the surface cache), not its sharp screen traces [2, 11]: an even grey sheet on the left, a dark one on the right.
  - Real wet asphalt is no perfect mirror. Its micro-roughness and the stone chips spread that boundary over the whole lane. That is the glistening: many small highlights rather than one sheet.

## The professional method, as it applies here

- **Wet road:** darker (we found ×0.35 of the dry gain is needed under this sky, more than Lagarde's ×0.68 [6], because our dry road was graded light). It is glossy but not a mirror, and keeps its stone normal. Water sits in its cavities [6, 7].
- **Puddles:** the only true mirrors: roughness 0 to 0.02, a flat normal, an opaque core [5, 6]. They read as water only where they mirror something brighter than the road: the sky and the mist, the shopfronts. Ours mirror the hill, so the hill step (now next, by his order) is part of the puddles' fix.
- **Kerbs:** wet granite darkens and shines like the flags. A pale matte kerb reads dry.
- **Wall bases:** a darker splash band. This is in the brick step's list (2.4).
