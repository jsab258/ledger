## 0. Canon decides the main question

canon.md "Brands and law": every vehicle is fictional; "no real ... logos ... car models" [27]. So every bought model of a real car (Sierra, Volvo 240, Transit, Routemaster) is out, whatever its licence. TurboSquid also sells real-brand cars as "Editorial Use Only", with three restrictions [6] (D):
- a game is commercial use;
- that use needs the maker's permission;
- the model may not be modified.

Such models serve as reference only, and are not to be traced (I).

**Pattern (I).** Big publishers license real cars; everyone else invents a maker and blends real cars into its range. Teams that copy single cars have escaped suits by luck, not by right.

## 3. Law in brief (not legal advice)

**UK**
- **Design rights:**
  - Unregistered design right ends 10 years after first marketing (15 at most).
  - A registered design lasts at most 25 years [19] (D).
  - So any car sold before about 2001 is free of design rights (I).
- **Car shape trade marks rarely hold:**
  - The black cab's shape marks were held invalid: the shape does not depart from the sector's norms and gives substantial value. *London Taxi v Frazer-Nash* [2017] EWCA Civ 1729 [20] (D).
  - The Defender's shapes were refused registration. *JLR v Ineos* [2020] EWHC 2130 (Ch) [21] (D).
- **What remains protected:** names, badges and emblems, and passing off where endorsement is implied (I).

**US**
- **Car shapes can be trade dress,** where they have secondary meaning and confusion is likely:
  - *Ferrari v Roberts* (6th Cir. 1991): kit-car replicas [22] (D).
  - *GM v Lanard Toys* (6th Cir. 2006): a toy Humvee [22] (D).
- ***AM General v Activision*** (S.D.N.Y., 31 March 2020): Humvees in Call of Duty were protected under the Rogers test, as artistically relevant and not explicitly misleading [23] (D). **No 2023 or 2024 ruling found.**
- ***Jack Daniel's v VIP*** (Supreme Court, 8 June 2023): Rogers does not apply where a mark identifies the source of one's own goods [24] (D). So a car inside the game is probably safe. A car used as the game's logo, key art or merchandise is not (I).
- **EA v Textron** (Battlefield 3 helicopters) settled in 2013 on secret terms [25] (D). Disputes cost money even when the law favours the game (I).

**What "inspired by" needs (I, from the above and GTA's practice):**
1. No real names, badges, emblems or hubcap logos; an invented maker and invented plates.
2. Never one car. Blend three or more cars of a class, and change the signature parts: lamps, grille, window line, C-pillar.
3. From blueprints, take only class dimensions, as ranges. Never trace one car.
4. Avoid cars whose makers defend the shape: Mini, Defender, black cab, Beetle, Porsche. The test: if a British enthusiast would name the model, change it.
5. Keep cars off the key art, the store capsule and the title.
6. Keep one provenance line per vehicle: its source cars and what was changed.

## 4. Building one from scratch

- **Blueprints.** the-blueprints.com has 261 Austin, 289 Vauxhall and 197 Volvo sets, some free; getoutlines.com has free Vauxhall and Volvo 240 sets [26] (D, summaries).
- **Time.**
  - A AAA vehicle takes 2–6 weeks; a Project CARS car about 4 months [26b] (D, forum).
  - A parked background car is far less: the earlier note's 3–5 days for the first scripted car, then 1–2 days per class (I).
- **Polycount.** Background cars run 5–25k triangles (earlier note). Zhabotinsky's sedan is 19.7k; ArmA 2 vehicles reach 20k (D).
- **AI generators.**
  - **Failures on cars,** from the vendors' own blogs [28] (D): wheels melt or differ; arches close into solid blocks; panel gaps and lamps blur. The fix is to split the parts, clean one wheel, copy it, and retopologise.
  - **Licences:**
    - TRELLIS.2: MIT licence, December 2025 [29] (D). Earlier research here says it needs CUDA and an NVIDIA card with 16 GB (production/research/clothing-pipeline/TRIED-2026-09-24.md; corrected by the research session from the helper's 24 GB); this PC has an AMD RX 6700 with 10 GB, so it cannot run locally.
    - Hunyuan3D: its licence excludes the UK and EU [30] (D).
    - Meshy: free output is CC BY 4.0, and the paid tiers are subscriptions.
    - Tripo: the free tier is non-commercial [31] (D).
  - **Verdict (I):** these tools give a shell with roughly the right massing, not a car with symmetry, panel gaps and clean glass, so use them for blockout reference at most. Feed them an invented design sheet, never a real car's photo, or they copy its trade dress.
