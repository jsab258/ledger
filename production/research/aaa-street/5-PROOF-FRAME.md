# One view of Quay Street beside KCD2: what it needs and what it costs (research note, 1 October 2026)

About 30 minutes, separate helper. D means documented, with the source number. I means my own observation of the images or my own inference. **Nearly every website was blocked: 80.lv, ArtStation, Polycount, Epic, Fab and Flickr. Every web claim below therefore rests on a search-engine summary, not a full reading.**

## 1. What separates today's hook frame (day) from the sheet and the bar

**Composition (the first thing anyone reads).**
- **The frame is the sheet mirrored.**
  - The sheet has Mickey's and the parade on the LEFT. The road bends right and rises, and the street opens onto cottages, trees and a misty hillside.
  - The game has Mickey's on the RIGHT and a continuous wall on the left. The road is dead straight and ends at a flat brick-and-cream block whose windows are black holes with no frames.
  - The sheet gets its depth from the bend and the rise. The game's street ends in a wall.
  - I found no ruling that chose the mirror (I searched DECISIONS.md for it). (I)
  - **Correction by the research session:** canon rules it. Jafar ruled on 22 September that the parade is Quay Street's east side (canon.md, "Quay Street's three sides"), and the hook camera stands at the quay end looking north, so the parade must be on the right. The game follows canon; the sheet, generated by an image model, has the sides the other way round. Keep canon's sides. What the frame should take from the sheet is its depth: the bend, the rise, the far end opening onto the hillside, and the near shop large in the frame.
- The sheet's camera is about 1.9 m up. Its vanishing point is at x 1502 of 2048 (D19).

**Geometry.**
- **Repetition.** In street-B every bay is identical: six windows, five chimney stacks, the same pots, equal spacing. The sheet varies from house to house: a stone-faced house, darker bricks, different heights, clustered pots. (I)
- **Windows.** On the right-hand parade:
  - the windows sit nearly flush with the wall;
  - the heads are a painted white arc, not a brick arch;
  - the sills are thin slabs with no drip or stain;
  - there are no sash boxes, meeting rails, nets or curtains.
  
  Real sashes sit behind the front 4½ inches of brick (about 100-115 mm), on a sill that projects about 40 mm (D14). KCD2's windows have deep, shadowed reveals with grime under the sill (I).
- **Roof, chimneys and downpipes.** The roof is barely visible. The chimneys are plain boxes with no flashing, soot or lean. The downpipes are plain black tubes with no brackets, hoppers or staining. (I)
- **Mickey's.** The shopfront is a flat box fascia with no pilasters, consoles, cornice, recessed door or threshold. In street-A its glass is a black void. The near corner shows the brick stretching and repeating about every 2 m. (I)

**Materials and wear.**
- The brick is one texture at an even saturation.
- It has no per-house tint, soot, streaks under sills, algae, splash band at the base, repointing or wetness.
- Painted timber is flat colour with no chips or dirt.
- By contrast, KCD2's plaster has large-scale tonal variation, cracks, and dirt gathered in the reveals.

(I)

**Ground (the bottom third of every third-person frame).**
- **Road.** Light grey with red speckle; it reads dry and new. The sheet's road is dark, wet asphalt with a sheen and a camber. The puddles are hard-edged mirror blobs that look like stickers, and the road lines are pristine. (I)
- **Kerb.** One continuous grey extrusion: no separate blocks, chips, drain grates or dark gutter band. The sheet shows drain grates in the gutter. (I)
- **Pavement.** The flags are orange-brown, saturated and spotless; the sheet's are grey-buff with patches. There is no litter, no leaves and no gum. KCD2's ground has puddles, mud, straw and ruts. (I)

**Set dressing.**
- The hook frame has about 10 props. KCD2's arcades frame has 40-plus in the market alone.
- Missing for 1990:
  - A-boards and a newsagent's headline board
  - shop blinds
  - posters and stickers in windows
  - letting boards
  - a bus stop and a street name plate
  - bollards
  - burglar alarm boxes

(I)

**Vehicles.** The cars are boxes with no reflections, wear or interior (I). The gap is materials, stance and reflections, not polygon count (D20).

**People.** The MetaHumans stand rigid, facing outwards; one stands with legs apart (street-B). They have no contact shadows. The people in KCD2 and in the sheet are walking or busy. (I)

**Hillside.** Identical stepped boxes with flat window rectangles, one roof grey and blob-shaped trees, all at the same contrast as the foreground. In the sheet the hillside is soft in mist and varied, with gardens, retaining walls and mixed buildings. (I)

**Sky, light and atmosphere.**
- **Day.** The sky is flat white. There is no fog, no aerial perspective (distance haze) and no chimney smoke, and little contact shadow. (I)
- **KCD2.** It layers depth with a graded sky, smoke and haze (I), on top of dense volumetric fog and voxel-based bounced light (D16).
- **Night.** One orange hue over everything, with no pools of light and no dark gaps. The hillside windows are flat white and the shops are unlit. (I; the method is already in D21.)

**Grading.** Mid-grey, low contrast, and a sky close to clipping (I).

**What carries over from the bar.** KCD2 is lit by a low sun; Quay Street is overcast. Take KCD2's material richness, dirt, clutter density and depth haze, not its sun (I).

## 2. How others build one convincing view, and how long it takes

- **The "beautiful corner".** Studios take a small area to final art polish before the rest of the game. It is usually not playable and exists to show what the game will look like at launch (D15). This proof frame is exactly that (I).
- **How long one artist takes on one realistic UE5 environment:**
  - a student's Japanese street: 5-6 weeks (D2);
  - "Necropolis" (not a street): about 4 months (D5);
  - a Venice street: about 1,000 hours over a year (D1);
  - one artist on a London street spent almost half his time on the brick material alone (D3).
- **The method these breakdowns share:**
  - a modular kit on a 1 m grid at a fixed texture resolution (1K per metre), with hero pieces chosen separately from repeated ones (D2);
  - layered materials with grime at world scale (D1);
  - decals for leaks, cracks and grime, plus a few parallax decals (D1, D5);
  - vertex painting to break up repeated modules (D1);
  - photogrammetry (scanned assets) where available (D4, D10, D11).
- **Trim sheets.** Shared texture strips with bevels baked in at 45 degrees let a small team texture a huge world and swap materials without new UVs (D7).
- **The Matrix city is procedural.** A "building DNA" system assembled modular pieces into 7,000 buildings and about 7 million instances, built by a small environment team (D6). Generation scales; the realism came from the kit and its materials (I).
- **Bodycam and Unrecord.**
  - Bodycam's two developers built on Megascans. Degrading the image and "overloading" the maps with assets made it read as hyper-real (D10).
  - Unrecord mixed Megascans, marketplace assets and its own, and matched real lighting in Lumen (D11).
- **Time per item:**
  - a basic tileable material: about 2 days in production (D9);
  - a hero prop: 2-3 weeks from blockout to textured, in a studio (D8);
  - a kit of 30-50 pieces can dress many locations (D18, a weak source).
- **Free sources on the allowlist:**
  - Poly Haven: CC0; about 850 textures, 990 sky images and 520 models (D13). It is already used for the shop interiors (D22).
  - ambientCG: CC0; 2,000-plus materials (D13).
  - Megascans: about 1,500 stay free on Fab under the Fab Standard Licence (D12).

## 3. Ranked list for the proof frame

Effort is in builder-days, with agents scripting Blender and Unreal; the limit is looking and judging, not typing (I). The list is ordered by realism gained per hour, which is roughly the order a viewer notices things (I).

| # | Item | Done looks like | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Composition**: camera and layout matched to the sheet's depth while keeping canon's sides (road bending and rising, closing block gone, end open to the hillside; see the correction in section 1) | Laid over the sheet with the hook-pair tool, the vanishing point, horizon and masses line up | 0.5-1 day; more if the layout moves |
| 2 | **Atmosphere and sky**: height fog, distance haze, a CC0 overcast sky with cloud structure, a few chimney smokes | Hillside clearly lower in contrast than the foreground; sky not pure white | 0.5-1 day |
| 3 | **Wet street**: darker, glossier road and pavement; puddles from a soft mask laid over the whole street; darker wall bases | Road dark and reflective like the sheet's | 1-2 days |
| 4 | **Brick breakup**: stop the visible tiling; large-scale tonal variation; two or three brick sets with a per-house tint; soot, streaks under sills, algae and splash band, repairs; masks emitted by the generator | No repeat visible at the near corner at 2560x1440; each house distinct | 3-5 days |
| 5 | **Facade depth and variety**: 100-115 mm reveals, brick arch heads, sills with a drip, sash detail, nets, plinth, varied doors, heights and pots, a stone house and a painted house as in the sheet | Street-B no longer reads as copy and paste | 4-7 days |
| 6 | **Shopfronts in view**: pilasters, consoles, cornice, recessed doors, tiled thresholds, blind boxes, posters; interiors in every window (the recipe already exists) | No black void in the frame; Mickey's holds up at 2 m | 3-5 days |
| 7 | **Ground**: separate kerb blocks with chips, drain grates, gutter grime, grey-buff flags varied per flag, cracks, patches, gum, covers, oil stains, worn lines; one decal sheet | The bottom third of street-A holds up | 3-5 days |
| 8 | **Clutter, from about 10 to 40-60 items**: D23's list plus litter, leaves, cigarette ends and A-boards; debris from free Megascans or Poly Haven | Two to four objects at every shop doorway | 4-7 days |
| 9 | **Hillside**: the street's own kit reused at distance, flat stand-ins for the farthest houses, gardens, retaining walls, free trees, all in fog | A town in mist, not stacked boxes | 2-4 days |
| 10 | **Cars up to the bar** (method in D20) | Stance, glossy paint reflections, interior seen through the glass, wear | 3-5 days |
| 11 | **People posed**: walking, looking in windows, contact shadows (clothing is a separate track) | No one standing rigid facing the camera | 2-4 days |
| 12 | **Night** (method in D21): pools of lamp light, dark gaps, lit shop windows, glow in the sky, wet streaks | Not one orange wash | 2-3 days |
| 13 | **Grade**: tonemapping checked, exposure fixed per lighting preset, faint grain and vignette | Midtones and highlights read as photographic | 0.5 day |

**Most realism per hour:** items 1-4 together, about a week, because they change the whole frame at once. Items 5-8 are the bulk of the work, about three weeks.

The full list comes to roughly 30-50 builder-days for one view (I). For comparison, one skilled artist took between 5 weeks and a year on comparable pieces (D1, D2, D5).

**Two cautions (I).**
1. **The sameness comes from the generator.** Give it per-house variation seeds and wear masks now. The earlier research recommended this (D17), and it is how the Matrix city's building DNA works (D6).
2. **The proof frame only proves the method if it is reusable.** Items 4-8 must be made as kit pieces and materials that can be used again, not as one-off fixes by hand.

## What I could not verify

- **No breakdown article could be opened.** Durations, counts and quotes come from search summaries and may be paraphrased wrongly; most are undated.
- **No hours for a comparable street.** I found no published breakdown with hours for a British terraced street at this standard. KCD2's own production methods (scanning, kit sizes, artist-hours for Kuttenberg) were not in any source I could reach.
- **No period photographs.** Peter Marshall's photographs on Flickr could not be opened. Period details were checked only against the sheet and the earlier research.
- **The mirror.** I found no ruling for the mirrored layout in DECISIONS.md. (The research session found it in canon.md: see the correction in section 1.)
- **The efforts are estimates.** Every figure in section 3 is my inference, not a measurement, and assumes agents do the scripting while the builder judges each step.
