# How game writers write ambient lines for a living town, end to end (research note, 30 September 2026)

Asked by Jafar's rule of 30 September: "how game writers write ambient lines for
a living town is itself a method to research first". A research helper was
given the work (a dozen residents of one 1990 street greet, place, react to and
gossip about Tom through his first week, from lines written ahead, enough that
two hours never repeats one) and capped at about thirty minutes, web only.

## 1. Planning: contexts, and the rules that pick a line

1. **Valve**, Left 4 Dead 2, about 10,000 lines.
   - Each event queries hundreds of world facts.
   - The rule matching the most criteria wins, with ties broken at random, so
     specific exceptions sit over a general baseline.
   - Memory is facts written back when a line plays.
   - Sources: Ruskin, GDC, March 2012,
     https://archive.org/stream/valve-publications/2012/GDC2012_Ruskin_Elan_DynamicDialog_djvu.txt;
     Emily Short, 16 Mar 2012, https://emshort.blog/2012/03/16/gdc-2012-talk-on-dynamic-dialogue/
2. **Firewatch** reused that model.
   - Event, sender, target and fact tests.
   - Facts are scoped per object, per player, per day and globally.
   - The most specific rule is tried first.
   - Source: Ewing and Armstrong, GDC 2017,
     https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2017/Presentations/Armstrong_Do_you_copy.pdf
3. **The Last of Us**: rules read individual and shared knowledge, game state
   and surroundings. Source: Gregory, GDC 2014, https://archive.org/details/GDC2014Gregory
4. **Hades**.
   - Conversations are filtered by conditions and weighted by importance.
   - Essential beats come first, with no repeat until the unused options run out.
   - Over 22,000 lines, since players hear so much.
   - Sources: a player's analysis, 11 Mar 2025,
     https://www.christi-kerr.com/post/how-the-dialogue-system-in-hades-rewards-failure;
     GDC 2021, https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026975/Breathing-Life-into-Greek-Myth
5. **At scale.**
   - Red Dead Redemption 2 gave each pedestrian actor a script of about 80 pages
     (GQ, 24 Oct 2018, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Red_Dead_Redemption_2).
   - Ubisoft files each NPC's barks in cells by topic
     (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/here-are-more-details-on-ubisoft-s-narrative-ai-tools-from-gdc-2023, 22 Mar 2023).
   - No published formula for lines per character per hour was found.

## 2. Writing

6. Start from what the character wants to say through the line, not from the
   trigger; two to ten variants per situation.
   https://sarah-beaulieu.com/en/writing-barks-for-video-games (11 Dec 2020)
7. Give each speaker a distinct personality, or the whole town talks with one voice.
   https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/adding-life-to-worlds-with-dialogue-barks (18 May 2020)
8. Watch Dogs: Legion wrote each line about 20 times, once per persona,
   changing the words and not just the delivery.
   https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-watch-dogs-legion-s-play-as-anyone-simulation-works (9 Dec 2020)
9. Review: writers accept, edit or discard each draft; Firewatch's lead marked
   chosen takes in a shared sheet. No published account of table reads specific
   to barks was found.

## 3. Repetition

10. Valve's response groups play every line once before any repeats. Ruskin
    lists "never twice" and "not within 60 seconds" as basic needs.
11. Yarn Spinner 3: line groups carry conditions and a once test; by default
    the best-scoring line of those used least is picked.
    https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/write-yarn-scripts/advanced-scripting/saliency
12. Voice directors want 8 to 20 or more variants of a frequent callout,
    delivered low-key, because big readings grate within hours.
    https://www.toosixmg.com/post/voice-direction-in-games-why-combat-barks-are-harder-than-cutscenes (undated).
    No study of how often players notice a repeat was found.

## 4. Tools and handover

13. Firewatch.
    - Writers worked in plain text.
    - A database gave each line an ID, speaker, notes, type and tags.
    - Recordings were named by line ID.
    - Each line's status ran write, record, wire, localise.
14. Voice-script columns: actor, cue, context, inflection, location, effect, filename.
    https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/organizing-and-formatting-game-dialogue (18 Nov 2005)

## 5. What goes wrong, by their own account

15. The recurring faults:
    - new lines break old ones through shared facts;
    - late rewrites need tags to find every affected line;
    - barks are over-performed;
    - lines are generic.

## What it says for LEDGER's first week (inference)

- **What exists (inventory, 30 September).**
  - Core/StreetVoice.cs holds 505 lines in 49 banks.
  - None belongs to a named character.
  - A line is said once until its bank is used up (RemarkLedger).
  - The Unreal game voices recognitions and faint remarks for Sheila, Ron and
    Darren from those shared banks.
- **What to add.**
  - Each named character gets their own lines per context, written to their
    casting sheet: greeting at each familiarity, recognition, a reaction per
    kind of deed, idle remark.
  - The existing picker and ledger choose among them.
  - These are written ahead, not a new system.
- **How big.** Size each pool from how often it fires in two simulated hours
  (TownReach --two-hours already counts lines heard per bank), plus a margin,
  checked against 8 to 20 for a busy context.
- **"Never repeats"** is measured by logging many two-hour walks with different
  seeds: repeats must be zero, and the silence rate is reported.
- **Order of work.**
  1. One character complete, through the gate.
  2. Then the rest, one at a time.
  3. The logged walk last.
- **Per-line gate:**
  - the casting sheet's voice;
  - words and prices right for 1988 to 1992;
  - the content rule and canon;
  - whether the speaker could know it;
  - not a paraphrase of its pool's other lines;
  - low-key enough to bear a third hearing.
