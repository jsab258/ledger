### KCD2, from "I Stalked Kingdom Come 2 NPCs for 9 Days" (transcript; one player's observation in Miskowitz)
- A housewife's day is many chained tasks (plucks and cooks a chicken, gathers eggs, sweeps, fetches water from the pond, feeds the hens, naps), with clothing changes; she eats before her day starts; the household sleeps together at night. System: schedules attached to a day, not a spot (his words: "attached to a day"). LEDGER: every regular's day is a chain of small jobs across places, not a post.
- Routines overlap at shared places in the evening (vendors, the housewife and guards meet at the one social building), giving a small-community feel. LEDGER: the café, the launderette and the newsagent as meeting points; no pub (content rule).
- Snatches of overheard talk carry private lives (an affair, a refusal) the player can follow up. LEDGER: overheard lines carry the town's gossip; overhearing is how Tom learns.
- Market vendors call their wares all day but no townsperson buys; the stock includes items not shown. Weak spot: commerce only for the player. LEDGER: townspeople visibly buy (the grocer serves a neighbour), stock matches the calls.
- A vendor has a workroom at home tied to her trade (linen room). LEDGER: shopkeepers live over or behind the shop, the back room kit.
- A guard scolds him for walking without a light at night. System: a law the town enforces by time. LEDGER: the town's own unwritten rules (quiet after closing, the cab office's ways) enforced by people, never a fine screen.
- The game puts a moved guard back into his routine the next night ("the game had corrected him"). LEDGER: schedules self-heal after the player's interference.
- He kills a guard in the woods; next morning nobody mentions it, nobody searches, the routines close round the gap. Weak spot named by the player. LEDGER: the opposite is our point: an absence is noticed, asked about, gossiped; this is where LEDGER beats the bar.
- His help is refused: NPCs react to being bumped or followed with complaints and reports ("Do you want to hit me?"). LEDGER: being followed or crowded is noticed and spoken of.

### KCD2, from "The Tiny Details in Kingdom Come 2 You Weren't Meant to Notice" (transcript)
- Heating by class: tiled stoves fed from a servant's room for the rich, fed in the living room for the middle, smoke hoods for ordinary houses, an open fire and soot-stained upper windows for the poorest. System: set dressing by wealth. LEDGER: 1990 heating by household (gas fire, storage heaters, coal fire and its soot, paraffin heater), chimneys and stains telling who has what.
- Dovecotes with droppings modelled at their base (a historical supply chain shown in dirt). LEDGER: wear placed by use (pigeons on ledges and their marks, the bins' stains).
- A rich town painted (murals, frames) against the grey-stone assumption. LEDGER: our period photographs decide, not the cliché.
- The city's night exodus: a quarter of the people live in houses without interiors; far from the player they pass through locked doors at night; near the player they walk out of the city instead, so the player never sees the trick (developer's email, quoted by the creator). LEDGER: a method worth taking: cheats only where unseen, a watched person always keeps character.
- Desire paths: worn tracks across grass where people cut corners beside stairs; cart ruts only where the ground is soft, grass along the middle of the track; less-used spots less worn. LEDGER: wear on the Hook's pavements and verges painted by use (a worn diagonal across the green, a scuffed corner), a direct fit for the grime strategy.
- Cats deliberately drawn like medieval paintings (the creator's theory; the studio's reply joked). Not for LEDGER.
