## The scoring

Each game gets 0 to 3 on three tests, for that verb only:
- A. Fun first and well liked for that verb by critics and players (quotes and counts below).
- B. Long unedited footage at 60 frames a second with the normal camera, with the scenes the verb needs findable; provisional until the clip check.
- C. Close to LEDGER: grounded, everyday people, town streets, no superpowers.

A and C decide what is worth copying; B decides whether timings can be taken.

Content rule: a sample taken in a tavern, a bar or a gambling scene (the KCD2 tavern brawl, Mafia's bar, Yakuza's clubs, L.A. Noire's vice cases) is measured for its timings only, and the scene itself is logged "not for LEDGER", as the teardown brief does. LEDGER shows no alcohol, gambling or children. A game low on A can still be measured as the example to avoid, when it costs almost nothing extra (it is already in the viewing set).

## 5. What to take, already visible from the reading

These are leads, not rules; each becomes an experience rule only after it is seen in the measuring (FEEL-MEASUREMENT-METHOD section 4).
- Fists: Mafia shows what not to do (button mashing into canned finishers). Sleeping Dogs and KCD2 both make the enemy's blow readable and the player's answer matter.
- On foot: weight must not become lag. RDR2 and Mafia are named sluggish for long start and stop animations; GTA IV is praised for detail.
- Being seen: KCD2 and RDR2 show the town remembering a face and telling others later, which is LEDGER's engine; Hitman shows that the cue of a glance can be read from the person (a head turn, a stare) as well as from a meter.
- Chases: catch by closing in at a normal running speed and a tackle (L.A. Noire); never speed the runaway up unfairly; build chases around a hand-made street (Sleeping Dogs); lose pursuers by breaking sight, not by a timer (GTA V); stopping is what loses (Mirror's Edge).
