# Getting a narrow sleeve end or rib cuff onto the limb at frame 0

Date read for every source: 18 Nov 2026. Web research only, from the outside (public docs, papers, forum posts). PRIMARY = the source's own text, as returned by the fetch. SECONDARY = a third party describing it, or my reading of a primary.

Review note (18 Nov 2026, REVIEW-KILN-WRIST-DIRECTION-2026-11-18.md): the Huang et al. 2025 paper does not call its skeleton shrink a heuristic, and its garment is already closed and kept free of penetration by an IPC-type solver; the Marvelous "cuffs arranged spiral" line is primary, the reading that the cuff encircles the arm before simulation is secondary. The summary file KILN-WRIST-ANOTHER-DIRECTION-2026-11-18.md carries the corrected wording.

## The problem restated

The cuff is cut 191.6 mm round; the forearm there is about 219 mm (unverified). A ring that is shorter than the limb can only end up round the limb if, at the moment it first closes, the limb is already inside it. At frame 0 our tool has only about 190 degrees of wrap, so the ring closes beside the wrist. After that no rest-length setting, stiffness or seam force can fix it, because the fault is in the topology (the arm is outside the loop), not in the forces. Every professional answer below comes down to one of two things: (a) build the loop round the limb axis before any physics runs, or (b) make the limb smaller than the loop at the start and grow it back under collision. Shrinking the cloth (rest-length ramps) is only used after (a) or (b) has already put the limb inside the loop.

## 1. Marvelous Designer and CLO3D

- Arrangement points are generated from the bounding volumes round the avatar, and placement follows the volume's curves. PRIMARY. https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358262924185-Arrange-Pattern-with-Arrangement-Points-Flip-Wrap-Direction
- Patterns longer than the bounding volume, "such as cuffs", are arranged in a spiral, and Flip Wrap Direction reverses the spiral. PRIMARY, same URL. Reading: the cuff is wrapped round the arm volume at placement time, so it already encircles the arm before simulation. SECONDARY.
- A bounding volume is defined by two joints (start and end), a height and two radii; an arrangement point has an Offset ("the distance between the Avatar and the Arrangement Point") and a Wrap Direction (up or down). PRIMARY. https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358128221337
- Arrangement properties include Offset ("Adjusts the offset and curvature between the arrangement bounding volume and the pattern") and Shape Style ("Changes the arrangement bounding volume pattern to be flat or curved"). PRIMARY. https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358278939417-Arrangement-Property-Setting
- CLO: "Depending on the shape of the Avatar, the Bounding Volume is set to the Cylinder and Cuboid"; Length X and Length Y set the circumference; reversing joint order flips left and right when arranging. PRIMARY. https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000431228-Add-Edit-Bounding-Volume
- MD Auto Sewing: for sleeves "the cuffs can be used either arm/wrist Arrangement Points"; it works only with the default bounding volumes. PRIMARY. https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358149073305
- Elastic on a seam segment: Elastic on/off, Strength ("the elastic intensity value"), Ratio (relative to the segment at 100%). The page does not say how ratio changes rest length. PRIMARY. https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358239485593-Elastic
- Shrinkage Weft/Warp scales the 3D pattern without changing the 2D pattern; 100% is the natural state; one stated use is temporarily fitting an inner layer against the avatar when dressing several layers. PRIMARY. https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358324821785-Shrink-Pattern
- Strengthen: patterns "temporarily gain stiffness to all properties". No guidance on when to use it. PRIMARY. https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358253657497-Strengthen-Unstrengthen
- Freeze: patterns are fastened "like solid objects"; frozen patterns still collide, deactivated ones do not. PRIMARY. https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358315135897-Freeze-Unfreeze
- CLO community, rib collar smaller than its neckline: users suggest weft-stretchy fabric, Shrinkage in weft; one notes Elastic ratio above 100 did not lengthen the 3D line. PRIMARY (forum users, not vendor). https://support.clo3d.com/hc/ja/community/posts/360020807153-Rib-Collar
- Users whose sleeves do not wrap the arm on a custom avatar are advised to simulate sleeves first, freeze them, then the body pieces, or to use a T-pose avatar. PRIMARY (forum users). https://polycount.com/discussion/181169/marvelous-designer-help-need-assistance-about-bounding-volumes
- Wrapping too tightly round arrangement points is answered by splitting the pattern, freezing and simulating a few pieces at a time. PRIMARY (forum users). https://support.clo3d.com/hc/ko/community/posts/360029482774-patterns-wrapping-too-tightly-around-arrangement-points
- Pose change by OBJ morphing: start from a T-pose OBJ, import the target pose as a Morph Target, and the avatar "slowly changes according to the set frame count" while the garment simulates; garments "may fall off" for big differences, so morph in stages through an intermediate pose. PRIMARY. https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000407347-Change-Pose-by-OBJ-Morphing
- Not found: a vendor page giving a tight-cuff or waistband workflow, or avatar size morph (small body to true body) for dressing. Treat as not evidenced.

## 2. Other tools

- Houdini Vellum Drape: a "sandbox simulation designed around moving stitched points together slowly before fusing them with welds"; Weld Frame Delay sets frames of soft springs before welding; T-pose recommended; panels drawn larger than the body. PRIMARY. https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/vellum/paneling_draping.html
- Vellum Drape Inflate Collisions: "by slightly deflating the collision object and inflating it over time you can recover a non-self intersecting drape"; parameters Initial Deflation, Final Inflation, Inflation Frames. PRIMARY. https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/vellumdrape.html
- A published Vellum dress project used Inflate Collisions to "start the collision geometry smaller and inflate it over time", and lowered rest length scale on edge primitives to shrink frills. PRIMARY (practitioner). https://jorjboi.github.io/projects/vellum_dress

PRIMARY. https://sidefx.com/docs/houdini/vellum/vellumattributes.html

## 3. Published pipelines

- Automatic garment construction (Jung, SNU dissertation 2016; also US patent 9820519): "any garment can be abstracted as a number of cylinders"; panels are placed "on the surface of cylinders ... to surround the avatar"; each pack's 2D bounding box is wrapped onto its tagged (front or back half) cylinder, vertical offset the only free parameter; sleeve to bodice is a "ring match". PRIMARY. https://core.ac.uk/download/300150963.pdf and https://patents.google.com/patent/US9820519 . The text gives no radius rule and no width-to-angle formula. SECONDARY note.
- GarmentCodeData: a body segmentation mask (arms, legs, trunk); panels matched to the nearest segment; "sleeves are rotated to align with arm pose angle"; "cuff elements are placed below corresponding sleeve or pant components"; first frames gravity-free; temporary attachment constraints; success rate about 72%; a named failure is "body-cloth misalignment, leaving cuffs sticking out". PRIMARY. https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17609v3 . So the best public dataset pipeline places panels flat and still loses cuffs, which is our fault. SECONDARY reading.
- GarmentCode: panels have 6D placement; copies "distributed on a circle around the body". PRIMARY. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03642
- Intersection-Free Garment Retargeting (Huang et al., SIGGRAPH 2025): "we shrink the target avatar to its skeleton, making sure that the limbs overlap with the corresponding limb bones" (each vertex moved to the closest point on its bone), then inflate back while keeping no penetration (continuous collision on every step); a single centre point was rejected because limbs would not enter the right sleeve. No formal guarantee that limbs end in sleeves, it is a heuristic; the guarantee is no intersection in the output. PRIMARY. https://cims.nyu.edu/gcl/papers/2025-Garment.pdf and https://cims.nyu.edu/gcl/papers/2025-Garment-Supplemental.pdf
- CLOTH3D: garments fitted in rest pose for a body shape, then the body transitions in shape over frames "to ensure no body-to-cloth penetration", then rest pose to first pose by quaternion interpolation. PRIMARY. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.02792
- ContourCraft: starts with linear blend skinning and no T-pose interpolation; learns to resolve initial penetrations. PRIMARY. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.09522v1.pdf . Needs a garment already 3D and enclosing; not a placement method. SECONDARY.
- DrapeNet: garments defined on a neutral SMPL body in T-pose, displaced and skinned; no initialisation step. PRIMARY. https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/De_Luigi_DrapeNet_Garment_Generation_and_Self-Supervised_Draping_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf
- Dress Anyone: drapes on a static A-pose body; reference pattern uniformly scaled by an area factor before draping. PRIMARY. https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19148v1
- Fuhrmann et al., Interaction-free dressing: "geometric pre-positioning of virtual cloth patterns around human 3D-scans" designed to converge in the sewing simulation. PRIMARY (abstract only). https://cg.web.th-koeln.de/?p=1094
- Not found: a paper stating a "rest length ramp from enlarged to true size" as the dressing method. Closest are Vellum animated rest length, Maya Rest Length Scale and MD Shrinkage (all primary, above).

## 4. Methods compared

A. Cylinder pre-wrap round the limb axis (MD/CLO arrangement on bounding volumes; Jung and US 9820519). Each panel is assigned to a body segment; its width coordinate u maps to angle theta = 2 pi (u - u0) / W(v), W(v) the panel's width at that row, on a cylinder or cone about the bone (elbow to wrist). Guarantee: if the panel's two side seams meet at theta = 0 and 2 pi, the ring is closed round the axis at frame 0, so the arm is inside by construction. At true rest size the cuff radius is 191.6 / 2 pi = 30.5 mm, the forearm about 219 / 2 pi = 34.9 mm, so the ring starts inside the arm and must be combined with B or C. Cost: small to medium (segment assignment, joint axis, per-row width, seam alignment). Weak: panels not tagged to one limb (raglan, yoke) need a rule; wide panels give large radii; spiral or twist if u0 is chosen badly.

B. Collapse the body and grow it back (Vellum Inflate Collisions; Huang et al. skeleton shrink; CLOTH3D shape transition). Body vertices start pulled toward their bones (or deflated inwards by a set distance) and are restored over N frames with collision active. Guarantee: with A, the ring encloses the shrunk limb at frame 0 at true rest length, and the limb stays inside as long as collision never lets it tunnel (Huang uses continuous collision for that). Cost: medium (morphable collider per frame, enough frames, slow growth so XPBD collision does not tunnel; skeleton projection needs skin weights or nearest bone). Weak: a fast or thin limb can tunnel without continuous collision; shrunk limbs meet the trunk, so trunk panels can catch on arms; the no-tunnelling promise is only as good as the collision.

C. Start large, ramp rest length to true size (Vellum animated rest length, Maya Rest Length Scale, Blender shrinking, MD Shrinkage). The cuff is placed at a radius clear of the arm (say 1.1 x 34.9 mm plus thickness) with rest length set to that placed size, then ramped to 191.6 mm. Guarantee: none on its own; it only tightens a ring that already encloses. With A it is an alternative to B. Cost: small. Weak: by itself it is what failed here (an open or misplaced ring just pulls tighter beside the wrist); the ramp must be slow against collision.

D. Pose or shape morph (CLO OBJ morph, CLOTH3D). Dress a wider or smaller body or a T-pose, then morph to the A-pose. Guarantee: only that whatever enclosed at the start stays enclosed if the morph is slow; it does not by itself make a cuff narrower than the limb go on. Cost: medium to large (needs body targets). Weak: CLO says garments can fall off with big pose changes.

E. Seam-first, freeze, then add pieces (forum workflows; GarmentCodeData gravity-free frames and attachments). Guarantee: none; it is the method whose failure mode we see. Cost: small. Weak: depends on luck and tuning per garment.

## 5. Recommendation

Use A plus B as one general rule with no garment cases: every panel is assigned to the body segment nearest most of its vertices (GarmentCodeData rule) and wrapped round that segment's axis at its true rest width, closing its side seams at frame 0; the collider starts collapsed toward the bones (or deflated by at least the gap between limb radius and ring radius plus cloth thickness, here about 5 mm plus thickness) and is grown back over many frames under collision. Keep C (rest-length ramp) as a fallback if body collapse proves hard in our solver: wrap at max(true radius, limb radius plus clearance), set rest lengths to the placed size, ramp to true. Both keep the arm inside the loop from frame 0, which none of our three in-solver fixes did.
