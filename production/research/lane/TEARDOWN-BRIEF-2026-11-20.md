## Why this exists

His question at 21:07, 19 Nov: "everytime we find new things. Is this now the best way to get a complete view of the game?"

Text alone cannot give a complete view. The parts tree is built from DESIGN, canon and his rulings; REFERENCE-MECHANICS-2026-11-20.md adds 481 mechanics read from the five games' wikis and guides. Wikis list what a game has. They do not list what the player sees and hears from second to second: the small reaction when a passer-by is shoved, the sound when a door will not open, the camera easing in before a fight, the prompt that fades after the third use, the cut from a mission to free play. Those are the things that make KCD2 feel immersive and GTA feel busy, and they are where "we find new things" keeps coming from.

So the complete view has three sources, each catching what the others miss:
1. Text: DESIGN, canon, rulings and the reference mechanics list (done 19 Nov).
2. Screen: this teardown, an hour of each game watched and everything on screen listed (from 23 Nov).
3. Play: the AI player types and the panel playing LEDGER's own build (4t and 4l, from the one-shot on).

After all three, new things will still turn up, but from playing our own game rather than from gaps in the list. That is the normal state of a production, and the tree takes them the same way.

## What to list

Every event on screen, one row each, while watching at normal speed and pausing as needed. An event is anything the player does or anything the game does in answer:

- Action: every input the player makes that has a visible effect (walk, sprint, turn, punch, block, enter car, open door, pick up, talk, choose a line, open map or phone).
- Reaction: what the world does back (a passer-by flinches, shouts, films, runs, calls the police; a shopkeeper raises hands; a driver honks or gets out; an enemy staggers; a guard turns).
- Sound cue: a sound that tells the player something (footsteps of someone behind, a siren starting far away, a hit sound, a "noticed" sting, a radio change, music rising).
- Camera cue: any change of camera (zoom in for a fight, pull back in a car, shake on a hit, slow motion, a cut to show a witness, the camera turning to frame a talker).
- Prompt: any text, icon or button hint, how long it stays and whether it returns.
- Transition: any change of mode (free play to mission, mission to cut scene, cut scene to play, fade to black, loading, a fail screen, a save).
- Interface: anything else on screen that changes (meters, wanted stars or eye icons, money ticker, notices, map flashes).
- Empty time: any stretch over 20 seconds where the hands do nothing and nothing new happens (the timing of this belongs to the activity sheet; here just note it).

Each row: game; piece (early, middle, late); time in the video (to the second); kind (one of the eight above); what happened in plain words; how long it took if it can be timed from the video's clock; and, where it matches, the reference mechanic id (M1.26 and so on from REFERENCE-MECHANICS-2026-11-20.md) and the parts tree part id. A row that matches no mechanic and no part is marked NEW.

Two quick counts per piece, written under the rows: events per minute (all kinds), and the longest gap between player actions.

Expected size: about 4 to 8 rows a minute in busy play, so roughly 250 to 450 rows a game. That is the point: the small things are the ones the text missed.

Saved as one file per game under production/research/teardown/ on his PC (a CSV with the columns above, plus a short notes file), and a reduced still per piece only if the main session's preview rules allow it.

## How the findings go into the tree

1. Match: each row is matched to an M id and a part id where it fits (the builder does this as it lists, the second pass below finishes it).
2. NEW rows: grouped (the same event in several games is one candidate). Each candidate becomes a part in PARTS-TREE.json with its standard taken from what was seen (for example "a passer-by who sees a shove turns and stares within about half a second"), or is marked "not for LEDGER" with the reason, by the same rule the lane used for the reference mechanics (decided within canon, the pillars and his rulings; nothing goes to him unless it is canon or scope).
3. Standards: rows that time something already in the tree (a counter warning, a prompt fade, a camera zoom) are added to that part's standard as a measured reference value with two measurers' numbers, the same way the feel measurement keeps numbers (FEEL-MEASUREMENT-METHOD-2026-11-20.md).
4. Check: tools/lane/parts-tree/check.py runs after the merge, and the one-line summary goes to the overview: rows logged per game, how many matched, how many NEW, how many became parts.

Who does steps 2 to 4: the cloud lane if it is still running (the lane ends 21 Dec), or the main session.
