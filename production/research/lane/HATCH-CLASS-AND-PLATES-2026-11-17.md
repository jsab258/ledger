## 3. Plates in 1990

- Year letters never used: I, O, Q, U and Z (Q kept for vehicles of unknown age). https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/British_number_plates ; carreg.co.uk and National Numbers say the same. The parked-cars plan said "I, O, U, Z": it left out Q.
- H began in August 1990 (letters changed each August to 1999): carreg.co.uk/prefix-number-plates. The exact day, 1 August, is UNVERIFIED by a page read.
- Size: 520 x 111 mm standard; rear plates could also be 533 x 152 or 285 x 203 (Wikiwand). Tennants makes 533 x 152 but says nothing on legality (https://www.tennantsuk.com/knowledge-base/legislation/number-plate-size-guide/).
- Characters for cars first registered 1973 to 1 Sept 2001, the government's V796 (https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a758474e5274a1622e22662/V796.pdf): Group 2: 79 mm high, 57 mm wide (except 1 and I), stroke 14, gap between characters 11, between groups 33, margins at least 11. Group 1: 89 / 64 / 16 / 13 / 38 / 13. So 1990 characters were 57 mm wide, not the 50 mm of the 2001 font. Which British Standard edition was in force in 1990: UNVERIFIED.
- Reflective plates, white front and yellow rear, compulsory for most new registrations from 1 January 1973, optional from 1968 (Wikiwand; colours confirmed by V796).

## 4. Tax disc

- Round, about 75 mm across, used 1921 to 2014 (last expired 30 Sept 2015): https://www.regtransfers.co.uk/content/british-tax-discs-2024
- Shown "on or adjacent to the nearside of the windscreen" (1994 Act, quoted at https://honestjohn.co.uk/askhj/answer/29031/display-of-tax-disc). The 1990 wording from earlier law: UNVERIFIED. The lower corner is custom, not law: UNVERIFIED. In a right-hand-drive British car the nearside is the left.
