# Activity shares: how play time splits in the reference games (20 November 2026)

His question of 20:29, 19 Nov: DESIGN's hour budget in section 1 (for example fights and chases 8%) rests on judgement, not on measured numbers. This file says what is published on how play time splits by activity in GTA V, Sleeping Dogs, Mafia: Definitive Edition, KCD2 and Red Dead Redemption 2, how far it can be trusted, the stretches chosen to time ourselves, the hours on his PC, and how DESIGN's budget will be compared once timed. Written by the cloud lane (task 4f). Raw notes: feel-2026-11-20/METHOD-AND-SHARES-raw.md.

Tags: [READ] fetched 20 Nov 2026; [SS] search title only, not evidence; [I] our own judgement.

## In short

Nothing usable is published. No developer talk, telemetry study or analysis found by search on 20 Nov gives a split of play time by activity (on foot, driving, talk and cut scenes, jobs, fights and chases, menus, waiting) for any of the five games, or for any comparable open-world game. What exists measures other things (where players spend time, where they stop playing, how long the whole game is). So DESIGN's hour budget cannot be checked against published numbers; it can be checked against our own timing of the games, which this file sets up: three 60-minute stretches per game, timed by activity on his PC, about 26 hours in all (about 11 hours for KCD2 and GTA V alone). Minute marks are known for 6 of the 15 stretches (Mafia DE and KCD2); GTA V's three come from another video's chapters and must be found again in the chosen one, and Sleeping Dogs and RDR2 have none yet; finding them is priced in the teardown brief, which uses the same starting points.

Caveats, plainly:
- A full-game walkthrough is a story run by a practised player. It over-weights missions and under-weights wandering, compared with an ordinary player. The shares measured are "a story run's shares"; the comparison in section 4 says so. A fourth stretch of free play is not added here; the teardown's town piece (15 minutes) gives a small look at it, timed the same way and reported apart.
- Uploaders cut deaths, loading and sometimes menus. The clip check notes any cut; menus and loading are then under-counted and marked so.
- Mafia DE's late mark falls near the end of a 15-hour game; Mafia and KCD2 marks are chapter starts, so the stretch may open on a cut scene; it is timed as it comes, not moved (moving would bias the share).

## 3. How a stretch is timed

Play at normal speed (or 1.5 times where nothing changes), pause at every change of activity and write the video time to the second. One row per span: start, end, activity. Twelve kinds, which fold into DESIGN's seven rows:

| Kind timed | Folds into DESIGN's row |
|---|---|
| Walking, running, looking around, carrying, following on foot | On foot |
| Riding or driving (horse, car), including as a passenger with control | Driving |
| Talk the player plays (choices, a conversation walked through) | Talk |
| Cut scene (no control) | Talk (reported apart as well) |
| A job with the hands (a break-in, a search, a lock, a craft, a hunt, a chore) | Jobs with the hands |
| A fight (fists, weapons, guns) | Fights and chases |
| A chase (on foot or in a vehicle) | Fights and chases (and driving, see the rule below) |
| Map, menus, inventory, shops, getting ready | The Ledger, the map, getting ready |
| Waiting with nothing to do (riding along without control, sleeping, a fade, a timer) | Watching and waiting |
| Loading and fail screens | reported apart, not in DESIGN's rows |
| Stealth (moving hidden, watching guards) | reported apart, and folded into on foot for the comparison |
| Other | named in the notes |

Rules: a span goes to one kind; a chase in a car counts as a chase, not driving (DESIGN puts tails and getaways under driving, so the comparison shows both ways); a conversation while driving counts as driving, with talk noted alongside (it is how GTA fills a drive). Gun fights are timed as fights but marked, since LEDGER's guns are rare and late.

Check: the second measurer times one of the three stretches per game blind (the same one); if any kind differs by more than 3 minutes in the hour, both look at that kind again and agree a rule, written into this section.

Output per stretch: minutes per kind, the folded shares, the longest span of each kind, the number of activity changes in the hour, the number of distinct kinds in each 30 minutes (ER-CORE-06 asks for about ten distinct activities per 30 minutes; DESIGN's "activities" are finer than these twelve kinds, so the count is a floor, and the notes name the finer ones seen), and any span over 3 minutes with nothing new (ER-CORE-05).

## 4. How DESIGN's hour budget is compared once timed

1. Per game: shares of the three stretches together (180 minutes), and each stretch alone, to show the drift from early to late.
2. Across the five games: the lowest, middle and highest share per row.
3. Beside DESIGN's budget (on foot 25%, driving 22%, talk 19%, jobs 17%, fights and chases 8%, the Ledger and getting ready 6%, watching and waiting 3%). Each row is marked inside the reference range, below it or above it.
4. A row outside the range of all five games by more than 5 points is not wrong by that alone: LEDGER's pillar (verbs first, the town under the hood) means it is meant to differ in places. Each such row gets one line: either the reason it differs on purpose, with the ruling or pillar it rests on, or a change to the budget proposed to the main session. Changing the budget is a design change; it is recorded, and only canon or scope questions reach him.
5. The experience rules are checked the same way: talk under 30% and waiting under 10% (ER-CORE-06) against what the five games actually do.
6. The result goes into DESIGN's hour budget section as a note beside the table (by the main session or the lane, whoever holds DESIGN then), and into the parts tree's research item for activity shares.
