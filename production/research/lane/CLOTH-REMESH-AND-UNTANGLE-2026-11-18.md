# Cloth: carrying state across a remesh, untangling, closed bands, armpits

Date: 2026-11-18. Research helper for Kiln (Rust position-based cloth solver). About 30 minutes. Papers and official manuals only; nothing decompiled, extracted or downloaded beyond reading pages.

Plain summary: when the cloth is rebuilt finer, new points should be placed on a curved surface and any point that ends up inside the body should go back the way it came, not to the nearest skin; cuffs and waistbands should be wrapped round the limb before their seam closes; armpits need the arms out while dressing and a small gap between arm and body. Checked by a fresh reviewer 2026-11-18.

## The problem as given

- Nylon jacket, right sleeve: worst stretched edge jumps to about 6 to 7 times rest length at the wrist at frame 42, the frame where the cloth is rebuilt from the coarse mesh onto the finer mesh.
- Carried points were measured inside the body (8 and 10 points, up to 17 mm deep).
- Moving inside points to their old triangle's heaviest corner did not change the jump (6.14). So inside points are not the whole cause.
- Separately: a cuff or waistband closed into a tube before the limb is inside it stays beside the limb (tangle, not stretch). Armpits also fail, cause open.
- Rule order per substep: links and seams, strain limits, body contact with friction, holds, cloth-cloth every 4th step, body push every 2nd, seam join, layer passes. Body contact pushes from the nearest point or face within fixed reaches.

## 1. Carrying state across a remesh or coarse-to-fine step

### What the sources do

Narain, Samii, O'Brien, "Adaptive anisotropic remeshing for cloth simulation", ACM Transactions on Graphics 31(6), 2012 (ARCSim). Full PDF read 2026-11-18: https://escholarship.org/content/qt5s1775xd/qt5s1775xd.pdf?t=rbpzun (record page https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5s1775xd).
- Rest state lives in material space: every vertex has fixed 2D pattern coordinates, and strain is always measured against those. A split puts the new vertex's material coordinates halfway along the edge, so refinement adds points to the same rest shape and never changes what "rest length" means. The paper states the material coordinates give "a one-to-one correspondence between points on the mesh before remeshing" and after.
- Velocity of a new vertex: interpolated halfway between the edge's two ends.
- World position of a new vertex: not the plain midpoint. It is placed by minimising a cost that combines a curvature term and the strain of the neighbouring faces (a small linear solve), so the new point sits where the cloth would want it, not on the flat chord.
- Collapses are refused if they invert a face, make a sliver, or change panel boundaries. Splits are unconditional.
- The paper says plainly that remeshing "can cause the cloth to penetrate obstacles or itself". It does not test collisions inside the remesh. Instead, after remeshing, for each face that now intersects something, it takes the intersection's midpoint, finds how far that point moved because of the remesh (its displacement from where the same material point was before), and moves the face back along the reverse of that displacement until the intersection clears. Several such pairs are solved together with impact zones (Harmon et al. 2008). Justification given: remesh changes are small and roughly along the surface normal. No guarantee is claimed.
- When continuous collision detection is used, "remeshing must be performed between collision detection intervals" (reviewer re-read 2026-11-18).
- Stated limitation: strain limiting does not know about collision constraints, so collision fixing can push edges past their strain limit in contact regions.

Magnenat-Thalmann and Volino, "From early draping to haute couture models: 20 years of research", The Visual Computer 21, 2005. Read 2026-11-18: https://doc.rero.ch/record/316913/files/371_2005_Article_347.pdf
- Each mesh vertex is stored as a weighted sum of pattern control points. On topology change "the 3D drape position of the new mesh is automatically recomputed from the drape position" of the old mesh, with extrapolation for vertices outside the old surface.
- Their workflow is the same as Kiln's: rough mesh first, then "a high-accuracy drape is automatically produced using topology reconstruction with a refined mesh". Practical accuracy limit about 5 mm elements.
- Fitting is run in two passes: a fast simplified model first, then the full model with all physical parameters.
- Body and cloth are treated symmetrically by collision detection, with a geometric correction of position, speed and acceleration.

Zhang, James, Kaufman, "Progressive Dynamics for Cloth and Shell Animation", ACM Transactions on Graphics 43(4), SIGGRAPH 2024. Abstract and project pages read 2026-11-18: https://research.adobe.com/publication/progressive-dynamics-for-cloth-and-shell-animation and https://pcs-sim.github.io/pd . Coarse-to-fine levels that keep "tight-matching consistency" across levels. The full PDF (https://pcs-sim.github.io/pd/progressive-dynamics-main.pdf) failed to load. A 2025 follow-up on volumes (arXiv 2509.14177, read 2026-11-18, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14177v1, first 100,000 characters only) says the prolongation is linear and barycentric (each fine vertex bound to a coarse element, extrapolated outside it), velocity is carried by the Jacobian of that map, and contact is handled by IPC barrier energies in the fine solve, not by making the transfer itself clean. It says the shell paper built "a custom prolongation operator for curved shell geometry and rest-shape preservation". Details of how the shell version avoids penetrations at transfer: unverified.

CLO 3D, "Particle Distance Setting" (official help), read 2026-11-18: https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000414447-Particle-Distance-Setting . Recommends 20 mm while dressing the avatar, then under 5 mm for quality. Says nothing about how state is carried.

### What this means for Kiln (read against the facts)

- Rest lengths: the sources all keep the rest shape tied to the flat pattern, so a refined edge's rest length is its length in the pattern. If Kiln's fine-mesh rest lengths come from anything else (for example the coarse drape), that alone could make ratios jump. Worth confirming by printing, for the worst wrist edge, its rest length, its pattern length, and its two ends' parent coarse triangles.
- Placement: Kiln's carried points appear to be placed on the flat coarse triangle. Round a thin wrist a coarse triangle spans a large arc, so the flat chord cuts into the wrist. ARCSim avoids the flat chord by placing new points to minimise curvature and strain. A cheaper version used in practice for subdivision is to lift each new point along the interpolated vertex normal of its coarse triangle (a curved patch), which needs no solve. This is a method to test, not a finding.
- Inside points: ARCSim does not push inside points to the nearest surface. It moves them back along the reverse of the displacement the remesh gave them, which uses the known-good pre-remesh position as the "outside" side. Kiln's body push takes the nearest face within a fixed reach. For a point 17 mm deep in a wrist of roughly 25 to 35 mm radius (estimate, not sourced), the nearest face can be on the wrong side, and a point thrown to the far side of the wrist makes a single edge that spans the wrist. That would fit a 6 times stretch that does not go away when inside points are moved to the heaviest corner (that corner can still be inside, or on the wrong side). Unverified: needs the per-point push direction logged at frame 42.
- Velocity: ARCSim interpolates it. A common safety step (not from the sources above) is to zero or damp carried velocities for the first few substeps after a remesh so contact pushes are not amplified.
- Order: ARCSim remeshes between collision passes and then fixes intersections before the next step. Kiln's body push runs every 2nd substep; a refinement landing just before strain limits and links run, with no fix-up pass first, lets links pull hard against stuck points.

## 2. Untangling cloth already intersecting a body or itself

Baraff, Witkin, Kass, "Untangling Cloth", ACM Transactions on Graphics (SIGGRAPH 2003). Pixar PDF link redirected and could not be read. Abstract read 2026-11-18 on the SIGGRAPH history page https://history.siggraph.org/?p=115940 , plus a course summary read 2026-11-18 https://research.cs.wisc.edu/graphics/Courses/777-s2006/TextWeb/Summaries/Week11/krill-untangle.txt .
- History-free cloth-cloth response: every step, find the intersection curves between cloth regions, flood-fill each side of each curve and treat the smaller side as the wrongly placed region, then apply attract or repel forces to pull it back through. Fixes tangled starting states.
- Cloth-solid: "collision flypapering". Pinched cloth should stay still when the pinching solids are still, sit midway between them, and change smoothly when the pinch starts or stops.
- Fit for PBD: the analysis is geometric and can drive position corrections. Size: a robust triangle-triangle intersection, intersection-curve tracing on the mesh and a flood fill. Several days for a careful port. Pixar holds patents titled "Global intersection analysis for determining intersections of objects in computer animation" (US 6970171 and US 7355600, found in search 2026-11-18, PDF text unreadable). Their expiry status is not checked; check before implementing.

Volino and Magnenat-Thalmann, "Resolving surface collisions through intersection contour minimization", ACM Transactions on Graphics 25(3), SIGGRAPH 2006. Only listing pages reached (https://history.siggraph.org/?p=113608 , https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tog/VolinoM06.html). Method from secondary sources, unverified in the primary text: compute a displacement that shrinks the length of the intersection contour between surfaces, iterate until it vanishes. Local and history-free.
Ye and Zhao, "The Intersection Contour Minimization Method for Untangling Oriented Deformable Surfaces", SCA 2012. Abstract read 2026-11-18: https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA12.311-316 . Adds a "repulsive normal" using surface orientation, which fixes cases plain ICM fails on and speeds convergence; extends ICM to a mesh against a capsule. A body made of capsules is directly relevant to wrist and arm.
Ye, Ma, Jiang, Chen, Li, Xiong, Zhang, Tang, "A Unified Cloth Untangling Framework Through Discrete Collision Detection", Computer Graphics Forum 36(7), 2017. Abstract read 2026-11-18: https://diglib.eg.org/items/8f4e4417-9f92-4b61-b013-f748a3a25602/full . Finds edge-face intersections, groups them into four-vertex stencils with a normal each, moves the four vertices to clear the intersection, solves a penetration region together iteratively. Normals come from surface orientation, history, or global intersection analysis. Handles cloth pinched by objects. Its stencil moves are position corrections, so it is the closest published fit to a PBD solver. Full text not reached.
XRTailor (OpenXRLab) technical brief, read 2026-11-18: https://xrtailor.readthedocs.io/en/latest/getting_started/technical_brief.html . A position-based garment simulator that untangles with Volino and Magnenat-Thalmann plus Ye and Zhao's gradient, and uses Baraff's global analysis to speed convergence. Evidence these fit PBD in shipped code. Licence not stated on that page and not checked: do not take its code.
Bridson, Marino, Fedkiw, "Simulation of Clothing with Folds and Wrinkles", SCA 2003. Full PDF read 2026-11-18: https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/cloth2003.pdf .
- Body as signed distance fields, one per part, merged per frame by taking the minimum.
- Inside points are not snapped to the surface. A velocity is added so the point reaches the surface after a relaxation time, and a post-pass maps inside depths into a band outside the body, [epsilon, tau], with a monotone function, so folds are kept instead of flattened.
- Points inside two or more body parts are flagged "caught in between two body parts" and are not post-processed: they stay on the surface of local minima between the parts (citing Baraff's pinch rule).
- Fit for PBD: very good. A signed distance field per body part gives a reliable inside/outside sign and push direction, unlike nearest-face within a fixed reach. Size: a grid SDF per rigid body part plus lookup, roughly a day.
Houdini Vellum "Fixing intersections with collision geometry" (official), read 2026-11-18: https://sidefx.com/docs/houdini/vellum/intersect.html . When the collider intersects itself (upper arm into chest) the cloth "will have no where to go". Their fix: make the collider soft where it intersects, pinned hard elsewhere, optionally thicken it, so a gap opens.
Marvelous Designer: "Intersection Resolution" switch with normal or flipped normal (source above). Their move-garment advice after a pose change is to drag penetrating parts out by hand during simulation (https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358445646489-Change-Pose-by-Collada-s-Joint-Information , read 2026-11-18).

Which fit Kiln:
- Signed distance body with a stored "last outside" sign per point (Bridson 2003, plus inside/outside history): best fit, smallest. Push only toward the side the point was last outside, never toward the nearest face if that disagrees.
- ARCSim's undo-the-remesh-displacement fix: tiny, fits the frame 42 case exactly, because the pre-remesh position is known.
- Pinch rule (Baraff, Bridson): small; points inside two parts are held midway, not pushed.
- Ye 2017 stencils or ICM with orientation: medium to large; only needed if cloth-cloth tangles remain after the above.
- Global intersection analysis: large and patent status unchecked; last.

## 3. Placing closed bands (cuffs, waistbands, collars) round limbs

Marvelous Designer, "Arrange Pattern with Arrangement Points / Flip Wrap Direction" (official), read 2026-11-18: https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358262924185-Arrange-Pattern-with-Arrangement-Points-Flip-Wrap-Direction . Arrangement points "are arranged on the Avatar according to the Bounding Volume's curves": clicking one wraps the pattern onto the volume with its centre at the point. Patterns longer than the volume, "such as cuffs", wrap in a spiral; wrap direction can be flipped.
Marvelous Designer, "Auto Sewing" (official), read 2026-11-18: https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358149073305 . Collars and bands go on neck points, sleeves on arm points, cuffs on arm or wrist points. The page does not itself say the band is wrapped before sewing; that the limb is inside the loop from the start is an inference from the arrangement-point page above (reviewer note 2026-11-18).
Marvelous Designer, "Shrink Pattern" (official), read 2026-11-18: https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358324821785-Shrink-Pattern . Shrinkage Weft/Warp scales the 3D pattern; one stated use is temporarily shrinking an inner layer so it fits against the avatar when dressing several layers.
Houdini Vellum, "Paneling and draping" (source above): panels drawn oversized round a T-posed body; stitches are distance constraints, set Rest Length Scale 0 to pull ends together; Vellum Drape "moving stitched points together slowly before fusing them with welds", with Weld Frame Delay before a stitch becomes a weld; welding early can push fabric through the body; for fabric that struggles to wrap, a second drape with "Inflate Collisions", which "will slightly deflate the collision object and inflate it over time".
Magnenat-Thalmann and Volino 2005 (source above): patterns placed round the mannequin, an automatic snap to the nearest body position, seam gaps kept small, ideally no starting penetration; "The seams force the patterns to pull and bring the matching pattern borders together during simulation."

Summary of the professional order: wrap the open band onto a cylinder or volume round the limb first, close the seam slowly with a pulling stitch, weld only after a delay. Kiln closes the tube before the limb is inside; every tool above puts the limb inside the loop first. A cheap safety check: before joining a band's seam, compute the winding number of the band's loop around the limb's bone axis; join only if it is 1.

## 4. Armpits and other near-touching body regions

Minor (Digital Domain), "Making Space for Cloth Simulations Using Energy Minimization", SIGGRAPH 2018 Talks. Read 2026-11-18: https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Minor_Making-Space-for-Cloth-Simulations-Using-Energy-Minimization.pdf . Production rigs self-intersect at armpits and elbows and cause jitter and bunching; cloth needs a gap to slide. They pre-process the body: minimise an energy that keeps shape, discourages drift, and pushes intersecting parts apart with a margin. About 0.1 to 0.2 s for a 30,000-vertex mesh.
Wong, Eberle, Kim (Pixar), "Clean Cloth Inputs: Removing Character Self-Intersections With Volume Simulation", SIGGRAPH 2018 Talks. Read 2026-11-18: https://www.mat.ucsb.edu/~kim/BAO/clean_cloth.pdf . Characters "frequently self-intersect at armpits, chins, and elbows"; they clean the body with a tetrahedral volume simulation before cloth. Heavy machinery; the lesson is that the body proxy, not the cloth solver, is fixed.
Houdini Vellum intersect page (above): soft-pinned collider plus slight thickening to open a gap at arm-chest intersections.
Bridson 2003 (above): points inside two body parts treated as pinched, held between, not pushed out.
Baraff 2003 (above): flypapering for pinched cloth.
Pose: Houdini drapes in T-pose ("It is ideal to have the character in the T-pose for this workflow"). CLO "Change Pose by OBJ Morphing" (official), read 2026-11-18, https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000407347-Change-Pose-by-OBJ-Morphing : dress a T-pose avatar, then morph to the target pose over a set number of frames while simulating; for large pose changes go in stages. Marvelous Designer's Collada pose change (above) does the same.
Collision thickness: Marvelous default skin offset 3 mm (above). Magnenat-Thalmann and Volino 2005 report "millimeter collision thickness" with stable handling.

For Kiln: drape with arms out (T-pose or wide A-pose) so the armpit is open, then pose down over frames; give the body proxy a gap where arm and torso meet (a small offline pass on the proxy, or simply separate capsules that do not overlap); treat points inside both arm and torso as pinched (hold midway, no push).

## 5. Ranked methods for Kiln

Sizes are rough hours for an AI coder, excluding test runs (estimates, not from any source). Each needs the existing test set before commit.

1. Clean remesh transfer (ARCSim-style). Changes: fine rest lengths from pattern coordinates only; new points placed on a curved patch (interpolated vertex normals) rather than the flat coarse triangle; carried velocity interpolated then damped for a few substeps; after the transfer, any point inside the body moved back along the reverse of its own remesh displacement (from the same material point's pre-remesh position), then one body-contact pass before links run. About 4 to 6 hours. Check: worst edge in frames 42 to 50 under the nylon's strain limit (and no frame-42 jump above 1.2 times the frame-41 worst); body-through count 0 at frame 43; logged rest length of every refined edge equals its pattern length within 1 percent.
2. Body contact with inside/outside history. Changes: per body part signed distance field (or analytic capsule distance) replacing nearest-face within fixed reach; each point stores the sign and side it last had outside; a point found inside is pushed only toward that side, into a small band outside (Bridson's [epsilon, tau] mapping). About 6 to 8 hours. Check: on the jacket run, no point crosses the wrist (count of points whose side flips between consecutive steps 0); body-through count 0 for every frame; worst edge after frame 42 under the limit.
3. Bands placed round the limb before closing. Changes: cuffs and waistbands wrapped onto a cylinder round the limb's bone axis with a few mm offset (arrangement-point style), seam closed by a stitch whose rest length goes from current to 0 over N frames, joined only after a delay and only if the loop's winding number round the limb axis is 1. About 6 to 8 hours. Check: cuff centre within 30 mm of the wrist axis at the end of drape; winding number 1 for every band; no seam join logged with winding 0.
4. Deflate-then-inflate body, or temporarily shrunk garment. Changes: during the first frames of drape the body proxy is scaled in a few mm (or the inner garment's rest shrunk), then restored over N frames (Vellum Inflate Collisions, Marvelous shrinkage). About 2 to 3 hours. Check: body-through count 0 after restore; worst edge during restore under the limit.
5. Armpit handling. Changes: drape in T-pose or wide A-pose then pose over 20 to 40 frames in stages; body proxy arm and torso capsules given a minimum gap; points inside two parts flagged pinched and held midway with no push. About 4 to 6 hours. Check: armpit-region body-through count 0; no armpit edge over the limit; no point flagged pinched for more than a few frames once posed.
6. Cloth-cloth untangle (only if tangles remain after 1 to 5). Ye et al. 2017 stencils, or ICM with Ye and Zhao's oriented normals. About 2 to 4 days. Check: count of cloth-cloth triangle intersections 0 at end of drape on all test garments. Global intersection analysis last, after checking the Pixar patents' status.
