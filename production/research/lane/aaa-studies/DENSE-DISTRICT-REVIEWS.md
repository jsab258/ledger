# Dense district reviews: a small map revisited for dozens of hours (cloud part)

23 November 2026, the lane, AAA studies set A, study 2, cloud part. Opus 5.5 wrote this file from two helper reading passes (Sonnet) and its own check of their notes: notes/dense-district.md (15 sources) and notes/steam-listings.md sections 6 and 7 (Steam reviews hand-coded). Games are read only from the outside: critics, published talks reported in articles, interviews, Steam reviews, one forum post and one reader poll. No video was downloaded and no game file touched. The footage counts for the same games are done on his PC; their place is held at the end.

Games: Yakuza 0 (2015, PC 2018) and Yakuza: Like a Dragon (2020), with Kamurocho, the Tokyo district the series has reused for about twenty years; Sleeping Dogs (2012, Definitive Edition 2014), Hong Kong, a larger map with dense districts.

## In plain words

1. The makers of Kamurocho say its density comes from stacked buildings: many trades on top of each other in one small footprint, so one staircase or wall changes the scene (GDC 2025, reported by PC Gamer and Automaton, 25 Mar 2025). LEDGER's Hook is a British port of low terraces and a six-bay parade, so it cannot stack trades; its density has to come from people, hours and rooms behind doors instead.
2. What keeps a small map fresh, in critics' and makers' words: visible change over time (shops change hands, buildings appear: Kotaku, 8 Jan 2018), revisits that mean something in the story, one new place or way of moving each time, side stories found by walking, and the night.
3. What wears it out: space without content (the larger Yokohama map of Like a Dragon called "three times emptier" by TheGamer), side jobs that repeat a few verbs (Sleeping Dogs, critics in 2012), story missions that run out, and being "never far from a fight" (PC Gamer, 26 Jul 2018), and in Like a Dragon random battles on the streets (Push Square, 9 Mar 2021; one Steam review notes encounters tied to areas).
4. Most who voted preferred small: 57% of 1,228 Push Square readers chose "smaller, densely detailed" worlds, 33% had no preference, 11% chose "massive with huge scale" (9 Mar 2021); the same writer could cross Like a Dragon's Ijincho in under three minutes if he avoided its random battles, and was still finding things 45 hours in.
5. Players say little about the map itself: in 9 Yakuza Steam reviews read, side stories are praised in 6 and the compact, packed city in 2; backtracking is named in none.

How far to trust it: makers and critics are reached (Eiji Hamatsu and Ryosuke Horii at GDC 2025, producer Daisuke Sato in 2018, nine critics' reviews and features). Players are very thin: 9 Yakuza Steam reviews (5 Yakuza 0, 4 Like a Dragon), 1 more Steam review of Yakuza 3 Remastered, 1 forum post, 1 reader poll; no Sleeping Dogs player beyond one Giant Bomb user review. Steam discussions, Reddit and Backloggd could not be read from the cloud. No published block or street count, side story count, encounter rate or share of play in the open district was found.

## Kamurocho

The makers (sources D1, D2, D6):
- Reuse saves time, and the saved time goes into new things where they matter (Eiji Hamatsu, RGG design manager, GDC 2025, reported by PC Gamer, D1; Horii and Hamatsu, reported by Automaton, D2).
- Density from "zakkyo" buildings, stacked multi-tenant blocks; a row of single-storey Western shops needs more space to feel believable (D1). The map is dense and confined yet shows "constant change over time"; new places can be fitted inside the old layout (Hamatsu, D1).
- Reuse draws the complaint of being taken to the same places; the team answers with revisits that carry story meaning and by putting the saved effort into one new thing each time (a new building in one game, sea play in another) (D2).
- Fixed landmarks constrain the story; real changes in the city and real shops are added; sometimes rooftops, an underground mall or an extra area; each new place comes with new play (series producer Daisuke Sato, Game Developer, 12 Jul 2018, D6).

Critics (D3 to D5, D7, D11):
- The change across the years is the praised core: a tower absent in the 1988 game, clubs changing owners, shops rebranded: "genuine, observable history" (Kotaku, 8 Jan 2018, D3).
- Yakuza 0: "the scale and density of these spaces is remarkable"; side stories "arguably the highlight"; the reviewer over 100 hours in and still enjoying it; but "never far from a fight", and few everyday interactions with passers-by (PC Gamer, Phil Savage, 26 Jul 2018, score 90, D5).
- Smaller and denser preferred, with the caveat that the density rests on a decade of reused work (Push Square, 9 Mar 2021, D4).
- Like a Dragon's map, about three times Kamurocho, called "three times emptier", slower to cross, "open-world bloat"; after six Kamurocho games a new city was "a much-needed breath of fresh air" (TheGamer, undated, read 23 Nov 2026, D7).
- A recycled map still held a reviewer over 15 hours when it came with a new hero, a new way of moving (a grappling hook to rooftops), a new fighting mode, and returns that test the player's memory of the place (GamesRadar, 21 Feb 2025, D11).

## Sleeping Dogs

- Critics split on density: the city "alive", streets busy and reactive (Kotaku review, Tina Amini, 14 Aug 2012, D13); a "beautiful backdrop" rather than a bustling, interactive city (PC Gamer, 6 Sep 2012, score 74, D12).
- Side work: the police side jobs beat the triad missions, "better paced, more varied" (D12); the rest of the side activities "sort of dull", done mainly for the standing that buys cars and clothes (D13); favours and busts plentiful, side missions add about 10 hours (GamesRadar, 16 Aug 2012, D14).
- What wore thin: missions "shuffled from a shallow deck" and a triad story that runs out (D12); missions "samey" (D14); smashing and driving repeated (D13).
- Length: about 15 hours for the story, 25 to 30 with side work (D14; a Giant Bomb user review, undated, D15). HowLongToBeat not reached.

## Standards for LEDGER's Hook

LEDGER's map is the Hook and its docks (section 1), crossed on foot in 3 to 5 real minutes by the provisional band (GD-01), with no random fights or random encounters (section 3) and street happenings authored (3.7). These standards take the lessons above into that frame. Status DECIDED unless marked.

- DENSE-01, the street changes over the weeks. Each game week the Hook shows at least three changes a returning player can point to that were not there the week before (a shop's window, a poster, a board over a door, a mark of a deed, a new man on a corner), and each rung of Tom's rise changes three things a newcomer can point to (as 2.12.5); the checker compares seeded captures from the same cameras, at the same hour and in the same weather, a week apart, and does not count changes the routines make every day (lights, who is passing). Rows: 2.12.5, 2.19, 2.17. Basis: change over time as the praised core (D3, D1).
- DENSE-02, depth instead of height. The 24 open rooms (FIT-P34) are spread so that no stretch of street or quay longer than 40 m lacks a door Tom can go through to a place with a person in it at some hour (a shop, a back room, a yard, a hall); the checker walks every street line of the road plan (FIT-G1). Rows: FIT-P34, GD-02, FIT-G1. Basis: Kamurocho's stacked trades (D1), which a British port of terraces cannot copy. The 40 m is a design choice.
- DENSE-03, one new thing each act. Acts II and III each open at least one place or way of moving that the act before did not have (a yard behind the cold store, the Harbour Board's counter, the dock gate at night, men at his side on a job), and the first visit to it comes from a person, not a marker. Rows: 3.1.2, 3.1.3, 2.12.3, 3.5.7. Basis: one new thing per release, across games (D2, D6, D11), here applied to acts inside one game: a design choice.
- DENSE-04, revisits carry meaning. Every return to a place that a job or story sends Tom to again carries something the first visit did not (who is there, what they know, what changed), and no job sends him back to the same spot twice running for the same verb. Rows: 3.5.6, 7.14, ER-JOB-06 (in 3.4.2). Basis: revisits with story meaning (D2, D9); Sleeping Dogs' "shallow deck" (D12).
- DENSE-05, something on the way, never a random fight. Crossing the Hook by day offers a fare, a debt, a person with a word or a street happening within 30 real seconds (7.12, FIT-F32), and nothing on a crossing starts a fight that Tom did not start or a rival's state did not cause. Rows: 7.12, FIT-F32, 3.7. Basis: "never far from a fight" (D5) and Like a Dragon's random battles (D4); LEDGER bans random fights (section 3). PENDING: the 30 s band waits on FIT-F32 and the footage (DENSE-10); the no-random-fight half is his rule (MEASURED).
- DENSE-06, no empty ground. From any point of the playable map Tom can reach on foot, at least one of these lies within 35 m (the shape range of section 40): a person, a door he can use, a prompt for something usable, a vehicle he can take; except where and when the routines empty a place (the docks at night), whose quiet is a window in itself (section 2) and sits inside GD-02's band for that hour. The checker samples the map on a 10 m grid at four hours of a seeded day. Rows: GD-02, 2.1.2, FIT-G9. Basis: the larger map called "three times emptier" (D7).
- DENSE-07, learnt by heart. Every go-to step of Act I can be done by landmark without the paper map (ER-JOB-01, 4.1.3): the tester runs each step with the map locked shut and reaches the place within 3 minutes; and at least 6 of 8 panel players open the paper map less in their second evening than in their first. Rows: 4.1.3, 6.9, 6.3, 7.2. Basis: players who stopped checking the map (D9).
- DENSE-08, the night is its own street. The Hook at night changes who is out, what is open and what can be done (pools of lamp light, lit windows by routine, the landing's window), and at least a third of the jobs and side stories happen after dark. Rows: 4.7, 2.1.4, FIT-G10. Basis: the night praised over the day (D10). The third is a design choice.
- DENSE-09, side stories found by walking. Of the 25 to 40 side stories (3.3), at least half are first met on the street or at a door by walking past at the right hour, not sent by the radio or a letter. Rows: 3.3, 3.5.7, 3.7. Basis: side stories as the draw (D5; 6 of 9 Yakuza reviews). The half is a design choice.
- DENSE-10, the footage band. Things met per street minute, crossing times, the share of play in the open district, side stories per hour and fights met while crossing, counted in the reference games' footage on his PC, set the bands for GD-01, GD-02 and DENSE-05: PENDING the footage counts.

## Built on, and where it differs

Read first (23 Nov run, second pass): the blueprint's PRIOR-RESEARCH-INDEX.md and the studies it points to on this subject.
- aaa-reference/studies/17-yakuza-shenmue (October): Kamurocho modelled on Kabukicho (the real Kabukicho is about 0.36 km2, a figure for the real place, not the game's map), about 8 minutes' walk end to end (marked inferred there), about 100 side stories (unconfirmed); side stories found on foot without markers, many comic; random fights frequent, not to be copied. Its importer notes that none of its numbers has been re-read or freshly reviewed. This study first said no size or side story count was found; the earlier figures stand with their marks, and DENSE-09 and DENSE-05 agree with that study's rows 3 and 7. The Hook is about 0.107 km2 (street-scale-test, 16 Nov, marked partly holding in the index); no size of the game's Kamurocho is known to compare it with, so the footage counts settle crossing times.
- research/hitman-density (Sep): density is connections, not size; no dead ends; more than one way out and a different way back. DENSE-02 adds doors every 40 m; the two hold together.
- The Push Square crossing figure for Ijincho (under three minutes avoiding battles) sits beside study 17's 8 minutes for Kamurocho; both are readings, the footage counts settle them.

## Rulings to revisit

None argued against.
