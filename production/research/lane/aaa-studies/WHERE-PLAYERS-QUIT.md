# Where players quit: story milestones by Steam achievements (study 4, set B)

23 November 2026, the cloud (set B of the AAA studies, Opus 5.5 lead, one Sonnet reader, one fetch helper), one of the ten further studies he chose at 12:12 today before the one-shot's blueprint is closed. Read with the players study (production/research/lane/PLAYERS-KCD2-GTAV-RDR2-2026-11-20.md) and the story-patterns study beside it. Nothing from any game is in this file: only percentages, counts and short quotes. No GTA VI story detail. Mafia is not a reference and is left out.

Marks: [STEAM] Steam's own global achievement page (steamcommunity.com/stats/<app>/achievements), read 23 Nov 2026; [MIRROR] a site that lists Steam achievement percentages (gamerguides.com), read 23 Nov 2026, undated, not proved to be Steam's own figure; [DEV] a developer's own figure reported second hand; THIN where a figure rests on one source or one anecdote; [I] this study's own arithmetic or judgement.

## In plain words

1. The steepest drop comes early, after the guided opening, not in the middle or near the end. Red Dead Redemption 2, the only full curve reached, loses 31 points of its owners between the end of chapter 1 and the end of chapter 2 (72.7% to 41.5%), more than in all its later chapters together (41.5% to 20.4%, 21 points). GTA IV and The Witcher 3 show the same shape on thinner rows, placed in the story by mission or quest name only.
2. Holding grows with each stage passed. Of RDR2 owners who finish chapter 1, 28% reach the end [I: 20.4 / 72.7]; of those who finish chapter 2, about half (49%) [I: 20.4 / 41.5]; of those who finish chapter 3, about 63% [I: 20.4 / 32.6].
3. Big story games are finished by about one Steam owner in four to one in ten: GTA V 23.8% (its owners include many who play only Online), The Witcher 3 22.0%, RDR2 20.4%, GTA IV 9.4% (see its caveat). KCD2 was finished by about 8% of players two weeks after release, by its director's own post as reported (platform and base not stated, so not comparable with Steam owner shares).
4. What this means for LEDGER [I]: the dangerous moment is the hand-over from Act I's guided first week (about 2 hours, DEFINITIONS F15) to Act II's open rise (11 to 14 hours), and the first hours of Act II carry most of the players who will ever see the game. They need the densest story and the plainest next step; the long middle must be cut into felt stages.

## What was read, and how far to trust it

- Steam's own global pages were reached for RDR2, GTA V, GTA IV, The Witcher 3, KCD2, L.A. Noire and Disco Elysium, but the page as fetched from the cloud shows only the 26 most common rows, so most rarer story milestones are cut off. Hitman's page came back whole (83 rows) but with translated names and no descriptions, so its mission rows cannot be told apart. Where Steam's page did not reach, gamerguides.com's lists [MIRROR] were used; the one row checked against both agrees within 0.1 point (The Witcher 3's story finish: Steam 22.0%, mirror 21.9%); the mirror is otherwise unverified (THIN).
- A percentage is the share of the game's Steam owners holding the achievement, at the moment read, over all years since release. [I, unverified] The base seems to include owners who never started, which with early quitters is why even the first milestone is under 100%; bundles, gifts, sales and free weekends move the base. It cannot tell a player who quit from one who is still playing. So read steps and shapes, not absolute rates.
- Not reached from the cloud: the story rows of KCD2, Disco Elysium, Hitman (names came back translated without descriptions), Sleeping Dogs, Watch Dogs: Legion and Yakuza 0 or Like a Dragon (fetches refused or no percentages). GTA V's and L.A. Noire's mid-story rows were reached but their order in the story was not sourced, so their curves are not used for shape.
- What would close the gaps: Steam's public no-key call for global achievement percentages (ISteamUserStats/GetGlobalAchievementPercentagesForApp) returns every row; the players study already reached Steam's review service from the production PC, so this is a short job for the PC session (the full list for all eleven games, about ten minutes). Named as a handover below.

The players study's RDR2 complaints fit the stretch: boredom (34 of 99 unhappy reviews in its first pass), controls and slow animations (33 of 99), riding back and forth between mission givers, a slow opening. No source analyses chapter 2 itself (not reached). [I] This study's reading: the slow opening loses some (12.7 points), but the larger loss comes when the open game must hold players on its own and the next main beat is a long ride and a choice away.

### The Witcher 3 (Steam 292030) [STEAM] and [MIRROR]

The mirror (https://www.gamerguides.com/the-witcher-3-wild-hunt/steam-achievements/overview) gives the early milestones: Lilac and Gooseberries (finding Yennefer, early) 60.3%; Family Counselor (finding the Baron's wife and daughter, in Velen, the first big open region [I]) 37.4%; A Friend in Need 30.4%; Something More (finding Ciri) 24.4%; Passed the Trial 21.9%. The Witcher 3 has no act-completion achievements, so the curve is coarse.

### GTA V (Steam 271590) [MIRROR], used for its ends only

The first story milestone (Welcome To Los Santos) 81.9%; the final mission 23.8%; the Vangelico heist 41.1%. The other mid rows were not placed in the story by a source and are left out. So about 29% of those who start finish [I: 23.8 / 81.9]. The heist's place in the story was not sourced, so no shape is drawn from it. GTA V's owners include many who bought it for Online only, which lowers every story row.

### KCD2 (Steam 1771300): no story curve reached

Steam's page as fetched shows 26 of 83 rows and no act or story-end row, apart from "finished the story in Hardcore mode" at 2.0%. Two early rows: "Déjà Vu" ("You defeated Capon during practice. Again.") 67.8% and "An Old Friend" ("You found Pebbles.", the horse) 63.9%. | The Witcher 3 | Velen, the first open region | 60.3% | 37.4% | 22.9 | mirror, order by quest name |

In RDR2, the one full curve, no step after chapter 2 loses more than 9 points, and from chapter 4 on each loses under 3 [I]. Step sizes depend on how far apart the rows are, so this is not claimed for the thinner curves (GTA IV loses 11.8 points in one middle step).

## The shape of a game that keeps people

From the curves above and the players study's complaints:

1. The guided opening is not where most players go; the stretch after it is. In RDR2 more than twice as many owners are lost between chapter 1's end and chapter 2's end as in the opening [I: 31.2 against 12.7 points]; [I] that is when the game first asks them to find their own next thing among long rides.
2. Commitment builds: after chapter 3 each later RDR2 step loses about 1 to 5 points. [I] Those left are self-selected, so the cause cannot be shown; investment in people and story is the likely carrier (story and characters are the first praise in all three games of the players study).
3. Most players see only the first part. With one owner in four to one in ten reaching the end, the first act and the start of the second carry most of the audience; the ending is seen by a minority.
4. Length shows no clear direction in this data. GTA V (about 31 hours of story, THIN) 23.8%, The Witcher 3 (about 50) 22.0%, RDR2 (45 to 50) 20.4%, GTA IV 9.4% with its caveat; KCD2's 8% is on another base. Nothing here says a shorter game is finished more often.

## Standards for LEDGER (a checker can test each)

LEDGER is 15 to 20 hours (ruling 28 Oct), Act I a guided first week (ruling 29 Sep), Act II the rise of 11 to 14 hours, beats on states, never on days (3.1.2, 7.4), Act III five mornings (3.1.3). Act II is LEDGER's chapter 2: the first open stretch, and the longest act.

The bots named below are the three bot playthroughs of DEFINITIONS F15 (lazy, median, greedy), the same that log act hours.

- **WQ-01 The hand-over never goes quiet.** In the lazy and greedy bot playthroughs, from the end of Act I's first week to the first rung change of Act II, a main-story beat or a person bringing a job reaches Tom at least once every 30 real minutes, and the next main step is always named in the Ledger's Open page with a person and a landmark. This adds to ER-CORE-05 (never more than 5 minutes with nothing new, in 7.12) and does not loosen it. Fail: any gap over 30 real minutes, or an Open page with no main step. Basis: RDR2's largest loss comes after its guided opening; the 30 minutes is a design decision (DECIDED). Rows: 3.1.2, 7.4, RT-04, 7.12, 6.2.3, 3.5.7.
- **WQ-02 Act II is cut into felt stages.** Act II has at least four stages, none longer than 4 real hours in the bot playthroughs, each ending in a rung change that passes ER-RISE-03 (in row 2.12.5: three changes a newcomer can point to). Fail: a stage over 4 hours, or a stage end failing 2.12.5's test. Basis: in RDR2 the share of owners kept grows with each stage passed (28%, 49%, 63% reach the end from chapters 1, 2, 3); stages make the long middle into several shorter climbs (DECIDED on the 4 hours and the count, which follows from Act II's 11 to 14 hours). Rows: 3.1.2, 7.4, RT-04, FIT-F13, FIT-F16, 2.12.5.
- **WQ-03 The first hours of Act II get the best scene.** Within Act II's first 2 real hours (lazy and greedy bots), the player meets one planned job that can be done at least two ways, and the panel playtest (10.5) names at least one scene from those 2 hours unprompted in its debrief, by at least 4 of 8 testers. Fail: either missing. Basis: many more owners see the start of the second stretch than the end (one in four to one in ten finish the games read). Rows: 3.1.2, GD-08, RT-03, RT-02, 10.5.
- **WQ-04 Polish goes where the players are.** The checking order for each release candidate is the first hour (7.1), then the rest of Act I, then Act II's first stage, then the rest; each part is checked in full before the next starts. The gate's own rule (no visible fault) is unchanged; letting a later fault wait would be his ruling, not this study's. Fail: a later part checked while an earlier part's check is unfinished. Basis: the share of owners reaching each part (RDR2: 73% finish chapter 1, 20% the story). Rows: 7.1, 3.1.1, 10.5.
- **WQ-05 The ending is worth reaching but never the only payoff.** Each act ends on a scene that closes something (a debt, a rival, a thread of Act I's five), so a player who stops after Act II has had a whole story arc. Fail: an act end with no thread closed. Basis: more than three quarters of owners in the games read never see the ending. Rows: 3.1.1, 3.1.2, RT-02.
- **WQ-06 Length stays inside the ruling.** The bot playthroughs' main story plus the side stories they meet land inside 15 to 20 hours, and no act is longer than 14 real hours. Fail: outside the band. Basis: his ruling of 28 Oct and DEFINITIONS F15 (this data shows no length effect either way). Rows: FIT-F15, FIT-F16, 3.1.2, 10.2.
- **WQ-07 How LEDGER will know.** LEDGER has no achievements and sends nothing without a yes (10.7, 8.2.1), so its own curve comes from the panel and the tester: in the panel playtest (10.5) each tester's last act and stage reached is written down. Pass: every tester who stops before the end has a recorded stage and a stated reason; a record, not a curve (eight testers cannot make one). Fail: a panel run with no stage record. Rows: 10.5, 10.7, 8.2.1.
