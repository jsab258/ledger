# Money and progression: how wealth and power grow over a playthrough (study 6, set B)

23 November 2026, the cloud (set B of the AAA studies, Opus 5.5 lead, one Sonnet reader), one of the ten further studies he chose at 12:12 today. Its standards feed the numbers run (production/lane/blueprint/NUMBERS-RUN.md when written) and the economy bot (GD-07, DEFINITIONS F24 and G8). Read with the players study, which already found that players punish money with nothing to buy late in GTA V and RDR2 (the basis of GD-07). Nothing from any game is here: figures and short quotes only. No GTA VI story detail.

Marks: THIN where a figure rests on one guide, one post or one review; [I] this study's own arithmetic or judgement. All pages read 23 Nov 2026; the page's own date is given where shown.

## In plain words

1. In all three games money stops being a worry early, and the games are criticised for it. An RDR2 player: money "is not a concern" once chapter 2 is reached (one post); GTA V's heists make earning "effortless" (one article); KCD2's own director said its late economy and combat became too simple (a livestream, reported third hand from a fan translation). All thin, but they point the same way and agree with the players study.
2. The shape that fails is a curve that outruns its sinks. GTA V's story pays about $1.2 million for its first big heist, less for the middle ones, then about $40 million each for its last, and the stock tips on a side line can take a character to about $2 billion, while the dearest property on the one list read costs $10 million [I: with the stocks, cash ends about 200 times that property; THIN]. RDR2 reports at the story's end run from "a few thousands" (a guide) to about $20,000 (one player), against horses near $1,000 and camp upgrades of $220 to $325 [I: a few times to about 20 times the dearest common purchase; THIN].
3. KCD2 shows a second limit: late on, the merchants cannot afford what the player brings, so the bottleneck is the buyer's purse, not the player's (the director's words third hand, and one review; THIN).
4. Power grows fast in KCD2: skills are about two thirds of the way up (about 20 of 30) by the end of the first region in one review's count, and late Henry is "an unstoppable powerhouse" (two reviews say he becomes very strong). LEDGER has no stats or skills (OUT-06), so Tom's growth must come from position and the player's own hands, which is the design already.
5. For LEDGER over about 7 game weeks (his clock, 60 real seconds a game hour: Act I one week, Act II five, Act III one): keep a named thing out of reach at every act's end (GD-07), let running costs rise with the rung, cap any single take against the week's income, and limit what buyers can pay.

## The pattern across the three

| | Early | Mid | Late | Late cash against the dearest purchase found [I] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RDR2 | tight (about $500 from early missions; a forced bounty) | not found ($4,000 to $7,000 carried, stage not stated) | a few thousand to about $20,000 | a few times to about 20 times a top horse |
| GTA V | not found | $1.2 million from the first heist, less from the next two | tens of millions, up to about $2 billion with stocks | about 4 times the cinema (the dearest property on one list) without stocks, about 200 times with |
| KCD2 | nothing, then hundreds | thousands an hour from a trade | merchants cannot pay | the buyer's purse is the limit |

All three rows rest on thin figures; they agree on the direction. [I] What would have kept money meaning something: things to want that grow with the player (GTA V has the properties but they pay for themselves), running costs that grow too, and takes that grow in steps rather than one leap.

## Standards for LEDGER (a checker can test each)

LEDGER's money: Tom starts with £23.50 and five debts, £156 in all; rent £18 every Friday; money buys position, never numbers (GD-07 in DEFINITIONS; the description's section 24; row 2.7.8). G8 (DEFINITIONS) prices what money buys: a driver's wage £90 a week from the book, a driver's own fares about £85 to £95 a week in Act I, from rung 2 a car on a good rank £110 to £140 a week, a man's cut £10 to £40 a job, the brief £50 a week, a bought officer £50 or £200 a time, a used car £600 to £1,200 (the dearest priced item). No stats (OUT-06). Under his clock (60 real seconds a game hour) the calendar is about 7 game weeks: Act I one, Act II five, Act III one (DEFINITIONS clock table, column B); F24 is still written for nine weeks and moves to seven with it. The economy bot (GD-07), greedy and lazy, tests each standard. Numbers marked DECIDED are design choices made here.

Terms, for every standard below. Money held: pocket plus tin plus book. The week's income: all money that comes in during the game week, to any of the three (fares into the book, night money, fence payments, debts collected, a front's share). The week's outgoings: everything paid out that week (rent, wages, the brief, cuts and shares paid, bribes, purchases of running things, not position items). Worked from G8 for Act I's week [I]: income about £170 to £190 from the two drivers' fares plus about £23 of Tom's own (F24) plus whatever of the £156 in debts and the landing's cut he takes, so about £195 to £370; outgoings about £198 (two wages and the rent).

- **MP-01 A wanted thing out of reach every Friday.** On every Friday of Act II while it runs, and at Act I's end, in both bots, at least one named thing on the rung ladder that Tom does not hold costs more than the money held. Fail: any Friday with everything affordable. Basis: GD-07 and the players study; RDR2's late cash runs a few times to about 20 times its dearest common purchase (THIN). This tightens GD-07 from act ends to every Friday (DECIDED). Likely clash for the numbers run: G8's dearest priced item is a used car at £1,200, and a front's share and the round have no price; a greedy bot may pass £1,200 within a few weeks, so the top rungs need dearer priced things. Rows: GD-07, FIT-G8, FIT-F24, 2.7.8.
- **MP-02 Takes grow in steps, not one leap.** No single take (a job, a contract's week, a fence's payment, a debt) in either bot is larger than the greater of £150 or one and a half times the average week's income of the two weeks before it; and the week's income in Act II's last week is between 3 and 10 times Act I's week's (DECIDED; from the worked Act I figure, about £600 to £3,700). Fail: a take over its cap, or the end ratio outside 3 to 10. Basis: GTA V's last heist pays about 33 times its first after flat middle heists, and its heists are blamed for making money worthless (eXputer 2025, one article, THIN). Rows: FIT-F24, 2.7.2, 2.7.6, GD-07.
- **MP-03 Running costs rise with the rung.** Each rung adds at least one cost paid every week that the player sees paid (a driver's wage for a new car, the brief, a weekly retainer to a bought man, DECIDED as a new weekly form beside G8's per-use bribe, or a front's man's wage), and in the greedy bot the week's outgoings are at least 50% of the week's income on every Friday of Act II (DECIDED). With night money counted as income this can fail; on cars alone it is met by construction (a £90 wage against £110 to £140), which is why night money must count. Fail: a Friday under 50%, or a rung with no new weekly cost. Basis: the three games have almost no running costs late, and the cure proposed in print is running costs (eXputer 2025, opinion). Rows: 2.7.4, 2.7.8, 2.11.4, RT-17, FIT-G8.
- **MP-04 The lazy run still pays the rent; the idle run does not.** As ER-MONEY-03: doing nothing misses the first Friday's rent and a week of Tom's own fares pays it. Added here (DECIDED): the lazy bot (fares and the debts only) pays every Friday's rent through Act II. Fail: as stated. Basis: his design and G8's figures. Rows: 2.7.4, RT-16, FIT-G8.
- **MP-05 Buyers have purses.** Every fence and buyer has a weekly purse, set by rung in the tuning table; an offer over what is left is refused or paid in part with the rest owed, said in words. In the greedy bot's last two weeks of Act II, at least one offer to a buyer is refused or part-paid (DECIDED). Fail: a buyer paying beyond his purse, or no refusal or part-payment in those weeks. Basis: KCD2's late merchants who cannot pay (the director's words third hand, RPG Codex; THIN), made a rule instead of an accident. Rows: 2.7.5, 2.7.6, 1.6.3.
- **MP-06 Money cannot buy the end.** From one saved game at Act III's start, run twice with only the money held changed (£0 and £5,000), every morning's grade is the same. Fail: any grade that differs. Basis: the design (G8's curve: in Act III money cannot buy the books clean); listed because the reference games let late money buy everything. Rows: 3.1.3, RT-01, 2.7.3.
- **MP-07 Growth is position, never numbers.** No purchase or event raises a fighting, driving or speaking value; every rung change the bots reach shows as three things a newcomer can point to (ER-RISE-03, in 2.12.5). Fail: any number that rises in Tom, or a rung change failing 2.12.5's test. Basis: KCD2's fast skill growth makes Henry "an unstoppable powerhouse" late (RPG Codex; two reviews say he becomes very strong, THIN); LEDGER has no skills by his ruling, so its sense of growth must come from position. Rows: OUT-06, 2.12.5, 2.7.8, 1.6.10.

## For the numbers run

The reference ratios above, for the run to compare against (all [I] from thin figures): RDR2 late cash a few times to about 20 times its dearest common purchase; GTA V's biggest single take about 33 times its first (Michael's $1.2 million to about $40 million); GTA V's late cash about 4 times its dearest property without the stocks and about 200 times with them; KCD2 the full price list reachable in a few hours of a mid-game trade. LEDGER's targets are MP-01 to MP-03: never everything affordable on a Friday, no take over the greater of £150 or one and a half weeks' income, Act II's last week 3 to 10 times Act I's, outgoings at least half of income. The run should also price the top rungs (MP-01's likely clash).
