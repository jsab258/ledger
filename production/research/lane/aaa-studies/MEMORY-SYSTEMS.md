# Memory systems: how a game makes the player feel that its world remembers him

23 November 2026, the lane, AAA studies set A, study 1. Opus 5.5 wrote this file from four helper reading passes (Sonnet) and its own check of their notes. The notes, with every source as read, are in notes/memory-mordor-kcd2.md, notes/memory-hitman-wdl.md, notes/steam-listings.md and notes/player-stories.md. Games are read only from the outside: critics, published interviews and talks as articles, wikis and guides, Steam reviews and forum posts. No video was downloaded and no game file touched.

Games: Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor (2014) and Shadow of War (2017), the Nemesis system; Hitman: World of Assassination (2016 to 2021, combined 2023); Watch Dogs: Legion (2020); Kingdom Come: Deliverance II (2025, KCD2).

## In plain words

1. Remembering is felt most when a person says it to the player's face, with the history in the words, and when it changes what he can do. An orc that taunts the player about last time is the most praised memory in any of these games; in KCD2, a woman the hero had just saved shouting "You're that criminal!" at a branded man is the plainest case read (K11). A memory that only tints a number is not felt.
2. The memory must belong to one person, and he must be worth remembering. Watch Dogs: Legion gave each citizen the player engages a routine and a memory, and critics and players still found them interchangeable (5 of 6 voices read). Nemesis gave a few orcs a name, a face, a scar and a voice, and players still ask for it in other games.
3. Players punish noticing that breaks its own rules more than they punish noticing that is harsh. In Hitman the complaints read were about guards who see through walls and trespass that seems random (5 of 9 players coded).
4. When the main story ignores what the world remembers, critics say the game is two games (Shadow of War). LEDGER's story must use Tom's own record.
5. What players retell are the memories that came back: in 82 player stories coded from forums and articles, the recurring enemy (Nemesis) and witnesses with recognition (mostly KCD2) carried most of the 25 stories that came back later (study 9, PLAYERS-STORIES.md).

How far to trust it: critics and developers are fairly well reached (12 press and developer sources for Nemesis and KCD2, 7 for Hitman and Watch Dogs: Legion). Players are thin: 22 Shadow of War reviews (only 2 read in full; 20 as one-line listing summaries), 11 different KCD2 reviewers (5 plus 9, 3 in both passes), 13 Hitman reviews plus 9 forum posters, 7 Watch Dogs: Legion reviews plus 6 critic or forum voices. Reddit, ResetEra and Steam's vote-filtered listings were not reachable from the cloud: Steam's old review endpoint was retired on 22 Oct 2026, and its replacement answered only from his PC (PLAYERS-KCD2-GTAV-RDR2-2026-11-20.md). A proper pull of these five games' Steam reviews the 20 Nov way is an open task for the PC (see the end). Every player count below is a direction, not a size.

## Shadow of Mordor and Shadow of War: the Nemesis system

How the orc shows it remembers (sources M1, M2, M5, M6):
- It speaks the history. Its lines refer to what the player did to it or it to him (de Plater, GameSpot, around 2017, M1). A captain who killed the player taunts him next time; one who escaped says the player will not be so lucky again (PC Gamer review, 5 Oct 2017, M6). A Kotaku writer, after two failed kills, was taunted by the orc and hoped for revenge later (29 May 2014, M5).
- It carries a mark. An orc "killed" may come back with a scar from how it died (M1).
- It acts on the memory: betrayal by a humiliated or abandoned orc, loyalty from a rescued one, a revenge mission whose reward reflects the feud (M1); rank fights between orcs that keep producing new showdowns (de Plater, PC Gamer, 19 Sep 2014, M2).
- How often a named orc recurs: no source reached gives a rate. De Plater says the team aimed at "five or six or seven" memorable orcs per game (M1): a design focus, not a measured rate.

What the makers said:
- The best bosses are those "totally unique to your game", made by the player's own history, not written into the story (de Plater, M2).
- A faction-wide morale and discipline layer was built and cut, and features such as dominating orcs grew from it (Game Informer on de Plater's post-mortem, 20 Jan 2015, M4). That the simpler person-by-person relationship was the better choice is this study's inference, not the makers' words.
- After launch the studio said buying orc followers "risked undermining the heart" of the system and reduced immersion, and removed the shop (GamesRadar, 3 Apr 2018, M8).

Critics: the feuds are "easily the best thing" in Shadow of War (PC Gamer, M6). The campaign "rarely leverages" the player's own Nemesis history, so it reads as two games; loot boxes and a grinding late game hurt (Tom's Guide, 19 Oct 2017, M7).

Players (Shadow of War Steam, read 23 Nov 2026; M15, M16): of 22 reviews seen, the Nemesis system is praised or valued in 19; loot boxes are mentioned in 1; repetition or grind in 3. Base and bias: only 2 read in full, 20 as summaries of one positive and one negative listing page, so the up-to-down ratio means nothing. The main complaint in the negative listing (8 of 10) is Warner Bros.' patent on the Nemesis system: players want the memory in other games.

## Kingdom Come: Deliverance II: reputation and witnesses

How the world shows it knows (K9 to K14):
- Opinion is kept per person, rolls up to groups (soldiers, villagers, traders) and to each village (KCD wiki, last edited 26 Jul 2024, may describe game 1, K9; Deltia's guide, undated, read 23 Nov 2026, K10).
- Witnesses report to a guard or confront the player; a crime nobody reported can still be found later; heavy crime in one place in a short time brings "Unrest": residents arm, guards are reinforced (K10). Low standing with soldiers brings more stops and searches (K9).
- Branding: a PC Gamer writer took a brand rather than jail; townsfolk shunned him, a woman he had just saved shouted at him, merchants refused trade, reputation gains were halved, and a second serious crime could hang him (Morgan Park, 4 Feb 2025, K11).
- Lines react to the exact act and even to what was stolen; about half the recorded lines are not story (Martin Klima, Warhorse, Wccftech, 13 Sep 2024, K13). A shopkeeper noticed the reviewer wearing the armour he had stolen (PC Gamer review, 3 Feb 2025, K12).
- Warhorse softened crime from history because the true punishment would be too harsh for the hero (Edge 420 interview reported by GamesRadar, 23 Jan 2026, about game 1, K14).
- Not confirmed by any source reached: washing blood off as a rule, recognition by the same clothes as a general rule, the stocks.

Players (KCD2 Steam, M17 and steam-listings 2): 11 different reviewers read across two passes. The world remembering is praised by 2 (Francisca: a guard recalling a trick from a week before, a noble disgusted at blood and dirt; Nightserg: stolen goods noticed), plus 1 known only from comments under a review (a killing seen and reported); 1 punishes dialogue that forgets the first game's deeds; 1 punishes cutscenes overriding the player's wins; the rest are silent on memory. Very thin.

## Hitman: World of Assassination: routines, disguises, witnesses

How it notices (Game Developer, Tommy Thompson, 7 Aug 2019, H1; PC Gamer, 23 Nov 2016 and 27 Jan 2021, H2, H3):
- Routines are set and predictable on purpose, because stealth players plan on them; the player's acts interrupt them at once or later (H1).
- Each person holds a private memory of what he saw (last known positions) on top of shared knowledge; sight and hearing only, local. Shared services refresh from time to time, so knowledge can go stale and be exploited (H1).
- Witnesses: civilians notice extreme acts and alert guards; guards react to bodies and drawn weapons; a body found tells those near it; a dead target starts a timed search from the last known spot; a crowd that panics "never" calms (H1).
- Disguises: only certain people (the same kind as the disguise) see through it; the critic found that clear and welcome (H3).
- Not reached: the on-screen instinct and suspicion signs, any IO talk on them.

The makers: scripted moments sit "on top of the systems", which must recover when the player breaks them; missions too big are tiring to replay (Engström, H2).

Players: on Steam 13 reviews (steam-listings 4): replay praised 5, the level as a puzzle 5, NPC reactions readable and consistent 2, routines named 0. A second pass coded 9 players (1 Steam review and 8 forum posters, notes sources 5 to 7; source 8 not coded): 5 punish noticing that feels unfair or unreadable (seen through walls or a crate floor, trespass flags that seem random, shot on sight), 1 a routine that stops (a bug), 1 praises the revamped people. Thin.

## Watch Dogs: Legion: the people of the city

How it remembers (Game Developer, Thompson, 9 Dec 2020, W9):
- Each citizen the game takes up gets a 24-hour routine, travels visibly and brings family and friends with him; the memory records what the player did and gives each a reason for his view. Harm changes his schedule (hospital) and spreads to friends and family; someone hurt by one operative may not know a different one; some come to hate the player too much to recruit. Lines were recorded about 20 times each, in different voices (W9).

What critics and players said: the traits are "drearily one-note" and swapping agents costs nothing, so "the magic evaporates" (PC Gamer, Fraser Brown, 5 Nov 2020, W10); the web of ties made London feel "populated with people" and the few he grew attached to mattered (PC Gamer review, 28 Oct 2020, W11); alive at scale, many duds, lines repeated word for word, permadeath adds weight (Inverse, 28 Oct 2020, W12). Of 6 critic and forum voices, 5 punish interchangeable people and 3 repeated or ill-fitting voices; 2 praise the scale or the ties. Steam, 7 reviews: recruit anyone praised 3, flat or shallow people 2.

## What makes remembering felt, across the four

Each line names its sources (letters above; notes for the URLs).

| What makes it felt | Seen in | What fails it |
|---|---|---|
| Said to the face by a named person, with the history in the words | Nemesis taunts (M1, M5, M6); KCD2 branding and the guard who recalled a trick (K11, steam B1) | A memory only in a number or a map icon |
| It changes what the player can do | KCD2 refused trade, halved gains, searches (K9, K11); WDL people who will not be recruited (W9) | Bought away (M8) |
| It is tied to the exact act or thing | KCD2 reacts to what was stolen and to stolen armour worn (K12, K13) | Generic lines (W12) |
| It comes back later, unannounced | KCD2 crimes found later (K10); orc returns scarred (M1) | Main story ignores it (M7); dialogue forgets (steam KCD2) |
| The person is worth remembering: a name, a face, a mark, a voice | Nemesis (M1, M2) | Interchangeable people (W10, W12; 5 of 6 voices) |
| Rules the player can read and predict | Hitman routines, same-kind disguise rule (H1, H3) | Seeing through walls, random flags (5 of 9 players coded) |
| Bearable: punishment that does not end play | KCD2 softened (K14) | A brand that felt "too realistic" to one writer (K11) |
| One person's memory, rolled up only by people | KCD2 person to group to village (K9); WDL personal memory (W9) | A faction-wide stat, which Mordor's makers built and abandoned in development (M4) |

## Standards for LEDGER

LEDGER's design already has the hard half (designed, not yet all passing in the prototype): per-person permanent memory (2.3.1), transmission by ties (2.4.1), showing what people know on a ladder (2.4.3) and it comes back (2.4.4). These standards make it felt. Each is written so a checker (the AI tester's log, the town code's chain print, or a reader of the line tables) can pass or fail it. Status for the coverage map: DECIDED unless marked.

- MEM-01, said to his face with the history. In week one, every deed a named person saw plainly is said to Tom's face by somebody he has met within the ER-EYES-08 window, and when the speaker saw it himself the line names at least one concrete detail he holds (the place, the thing taken, the coat, the hour), checked by the claim check against his facts. A speaker who only heard it may be vague ("I heard something about you"), as section 34 says. Rows: 2.4.4, 2.4.3, 3.6.2. Basis: Nemesis taunts and KCD2 (M1, M6, K11). DECIDED (the timing is already MEASURED in 2.4.4).
- MEM-02, the mark he is known by. A witness who caught a mark (the coat, the car, a bruise, a limp) but does not know Tom links him to the deed when he next sees Tom carrying that mark, at any time after (memory does not cool); seen in a changed coat, Tom is not linked by the coat. A person who knows Tom recognises him whatever he wears (canon's ladder; coverage-audit-hitman, 14 Sep). The checker plays both and compares the witness's rung and what he says. Rows: 2.2.2, 2.2.5, 1.4.4. Basis: KCD2 stolen armour noticed (K12) and three player stories of recognition by stolen gear or clothes (notes/player-stories.md rows 25, 29, 37); thin.
- MEM-03, it changes what he can do. In the first game week every person whose temper takes him to the refuse rung on the ladder refuses Tom something he could do before (a fare, service at the counter, a door, a word), and the tester sees that refusal at least once per such person within two game days. Rows: 2.4.3, 2.12.3. Basis: KCD2 refused trade (K11), WDL refused recruitment (W9).
- MEM-04, scars that speak. A man Tom beat carries a visible mark for at least a game day (as doors do, 2.19) and, at their next meeting, says something of it before anything else. Rows: 1.3.11, 2.19. Basis: the Nemesis scar and taunt (M1).
- MEM-05, the rules are readable. No person in the town reacts to a deed without a printable chain of sightings and tellings (the test hook of 2.4.4); a seeded week with every chain printed shows zero reactions without a chain; sight is symmetric (ER-EYES-02) and never through a wall, a floor or a closed door. Rows: 2.4.4, 2.2.3, 2.2.6. Basis: Hitman's most punished fault (5 of 9 players coded, thin). DECIDED (a guard).
- MEM-06, routines worth learning. A named person's routine is the same each ordinary day of its week in the same weather (the calendar's days and the weather's changes of who is out apart), so a player who watched it once can plan on it; a deed may change it, and the change is visible (staying in, a hospital day, a different door) and told to his ties. Rows: 2.1.1, 2.1.3, 2.1.4, 2.1.6. Basis: Hitman routines (H1); WDL harm changes schedules and spreads (W9).
- MEM-07, people worth remembering. Any witness whose telling reaches Tom or the police is meetable again by Tom (unless dead, in hospital or gone by a fate the town tells), with a face, a place and at least one own line (a regular) or a describable role and a place in a routine (a crowd extra raised by RT2-10). No crowd extra's report is decisive unless that extra can be found. Two witnesses never say the same line in one game day. Rows: RT2-10, 3.2.3, 3.2.4, 3.6.3, 3.6.4. Basis: WDL's interchangeable people (5 of 6 voices), KCD2 stories where unnamed witnesses decided the outcome (study 9).
- MEM-08, the story uses his record. In a full Act II run, every sit-down and at least 80% of Act II beats name at least one deed of Tom's own that the speaker holds through a chain, never only written backstory; every rival's pressure does so once that rival holds any chain. Rows: 3.1.2, 3.5.5, 2.10.3, 2.10.4, 7.4. Basis: Shadow of War's "two games" (M7); KCD2 dialogue that forgot (steam B5). The 80% is a design choice: DECIDED.
- MEM-09, never bought away. No payment, item or menu removes what a person saw; money, threats and friendship change what he does with it (as canon says). A checker searches the action list for any act that clears a memory: none. Rows: 2.3.1, 2.5.2. Basis: Shadow of War's shop "undermining the heart" (M8). MEASURED as his ruling and canon.
- MEM-10, felt in the first evening. Within the first 30 real minutes at least one person says to Tom's face something he did in front of a person (already ER-EYES-08); within the first two real hours at least three different named people do. Rows: 2.4.4, 7.1, 7.6. Basis: the earlier topic study's point that remembering is quiet unless something comes back (players-and-a-world-that-remembers, SUMMARY.md). The three in two hours is DECIDED.
- MEM-11, bearable. No memory, however bad, closes the game or a main thread: a refusal always leaves another road (another person, the dark yard, a bought man), and the tester finds one within one game day for every refusal it meets. Rows: 7.9, 2.10.3. Basis: KCD2 softened its punishment (K14). DECIDED.

## Built on, and where it differs

Read first (23 Nov run, second pass): the blueprint's PRIOR-RESEARCH-INDEX.md and the studies it points to on this subject.
- players-and-a-world-that-remembers (14 Sep): players retell; forgetting is loud, remembering quiet; something has to come back. This study agrees and gives it standards (MEM-01, MEM-10).
- emergent-story-legibility (14 Sep): Mordor's callback is the whole of it; enumerate, do not summarise, showing the player item by item why someone believes something. This study agrees on the callback (MEM-01 and MEM-04 make it testable) and takes the narrower road on enumerating: no list on screen, the item said in the person's words (MEM-01's detail) and the belief line (section 3), as his rulings allow. Its "1944 of 1944 below the comment level" is the old build (the index marks it partly superseded by 2.4.3).
- coverage-audit-hitman (14 Sep): the people who see through a disguise are the people who know Tom; a change of coat works on strangers and never on regulars; bodies found at a time by a person. Where this study's MEM-02 first said a changed coat stops the link, the earlier audit and canon's ladder win (recognition is gated by knowing him): MEM-02 now applies the coat only to witnesses who caught a mark and do not know Tom.
- rumour-propagation (14 Sep): the old Core's merge ignored independent corroboration; not re-checked here against the C# port (the index marks it partly). MEM-05's chain print is the place to test it.
- research-openworld-audit (July, written from memory, marked partly holding in the index): witnesses report crimes to authority, adopted early, which agrees; its reputation-meter row conflicts with the no-numbers ruling, and the ruling wins.

## Rulings to revisit
