## Kamurocho

### Developers
- Reuse is for speed: the map has been reused about 20 years, so effort goes to new assets where they matter (1).
- Density comes from "zakkyo" multi-tenant buildings: stacked shops of different trades, so one wall or staircase changes the scene. Western single-storey shop rows need more space to feel believable (1).
- Hamatsu: dense and confined yet "constant change over time"; new places can be added inside the existing layout (1).
- Reuse draws the complaint of being taken to the same places; the team answers with revisits that carry story meaning, and by spending saved time on one new thing each time: Gaiden added The Castle; Pirate Yakuza reused Hawaii and put effort into sea gameplay (2).
- Producer Sato: fixed landmarks (Serena, Stardust, Millennium Tower) constrain story; real city changes and real shop tie-ups are added; sometimes underground malls, rooftops or extra area are added; each new location is paired with new gameplay (6).
- Not found: interviews with Nagoshi or Yokoyama on this exact question, and any published block or street count (6 says it does not give one).

### Critics
- Changes across years are the praised core: Millennium Tower absent in Yakuza 0, nightclubs change owners, shops rebranded; "genuine, observable history" (3).
- Yakuza 0 review: "the scale and density of these spaces is remarkable"; substories "arguably the highlight"; "never far from a fight"; the reviewer over 100 hours and still enjoying it (5). Open world is "more restrained" with few civilian interactions (5).
- Smaller and denser is preferred: Ijincho crossable in under three minutes without random battles; writer 45 hours in and still "scratched the surface" (4). Caveat from 4: density rests on a decade of reused assets.
- Counter-case: the 3x larger Yokohama map is called "three times emptier", slower to cross, "open-world bloat" (7). After six Kamurocho games, a new city is "a much-needed breath of fresh air" (7).
- A recycled map still held a reviewer 15+ hours (Honolulu), kept fresh by a new hero, new movement (hook to rooftops), new combat mode, and returning stops testing memory (11).

## Sleeping Dogs
- Critics split on density. Amini: city "alive", streets busy and reactive (13). PC Gamer: "beautiful backdrop" rather than a bustling interactive city (12).
- Side activities: PC Gamer says police side-quests beat the triad missions, "better paced, more varied" (12). Amini found side missions "more distractions", short and "sort of dull", done mainly to earn Face for cars and clothes (13). GamesRadar: favours and drug busts plentiful, side missions can "easily tack on another 10" hours (14).
- Wore thin: missions "shuffled from a shallow deck", triad story content runs out (12); missions "samey", same handful of things done the same way (14); smashing and driving repeated (13).
- Numbers: campaign about 15 hours, about 25 with side missions (14); user review: 15 main, about 30 with side missions (15). HowLongToBeat figures not reached.
- Player evidence: one Giant Bomb user review, no Steam or Reddit reached. It says nothing on repetition (15).

## What keeps a small map fresh (source numbers)
- Change over time: shops change hands, buildings appear, nightlife thickens (3, 1).
- Stacked variety in a small footprint, many trades per street (1, 4).
- Each release or chapter adds one new place or mode on the old map (2, 6, 11).
- Revisits that carry story meaning, or return for a different reason (2, 9).
- Knowing the place: players stop using the map (9, 3).
- New traversal or hero on old ground (11).
- Substories as the main draw; silly, varied, found by walking (5).
- Time-of-day look (night praised) (10).

## What wears it out
- Open space without content: the larger Yokohama read as empty and slow (7).
- Side content that repeats a few verbs (Sleeping Dogs) (13, 14).
- Linear story missions that run out before the end (12).
- Street fights while exploring, "never far from a fight" (5); a complaint-level view is not confirmed by player data.
- Reuse across games draws "same places" complaints (2); after six games a new city felt like relief (7).
- Weak day look, missing quality-of-life such as substory search (10).
- A city with few civilian interactions feels like a backdrop (5, 12).
