# Talk as play: how talk becomes a verb

23 November 2026, the lane, AAA studies set A, study 3. Opus 5.5 wrote this file from two helper reading passes (Sonnet) and its own check of their notes: notes/talk.md (20 sources) and notes/steam-listings.md (Steam reviews hand-coded). Games are read only from the outside: critics, published interviews and talks as articles, guides and wikis, Steam reviews. No video was downloaded and no game file touched.

Games: Disco Elysium (2019, Final Cut 2021; the bar for dialogue, not AAA), L.A. Noire (2011, remaster 2017; interrogation), Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018, PC 2019) and Kingdom Come: Deliverance II (2025; talk in an open world); and the field of typed free talk with characters (Façade, 2005; demos of language-model characters, 2024).

## In plain words

1. Talk is play when a line costs something and the answer shows at once. Disco Elysium's failed checks are often the funniest result and players say so unprompted (3 of 25 Steam reviews read); to one designer writing on his own blog, L.A. Noire's interrogations were "much more intense than the checkpoint-rich action sequences" (T9).
2. A much-punished fault in talk is a choice that does not do what it says: L.A. Noire's "Doubt" often made the hero shout accusations and threats (Kotaku, 16 Nov 2017), a complaint in both long Steam reviews read (2 of 12); AV Club (21 Nov 2017) adds weak links between evidence and claim; KCD2 has a smaller version (Windows Central, 3 Feb 2025).
3. Who the hero visibly is changes the talk: in KCD2 clothes, cleanliness and standing move the odds, and past help can skip a test. LEDGER has no skill checks by design, so its version must come from what Tom wears, what he holds over a man, and what that man saw and was told.
4. Short and quick: Disco Elysium's replies are tweet-length, one click each, with a cue before a thought (Kotaku, 16 Jan 2020); RDR2 lets the player greet or provoke anyone with one button and no menu, and critics praise it.
5. Typed talk with a machine lives or dies on three things: it never shows an error (Façade deflects in character), it never funnels every idea to the one the writer wanted, and it answers fast. A 2024 hands-on punished lag, dodging and funnelling.
6. Reading is a cost: too much reading is the commonest caution about Disco Elysium (3 of 25 reviews read; a review-summary site's top two complaint themes are text as the core of play and very text-heavy, and it repeats a player estimate that about 90% of play is reading and listening). In the helper's reading it was not the main reason for either thumbs down, though one of them mentions lots of reading.

How far to trust it: Disco Elysium is sourced from its makers and critics; L.A. Noire, RDR2 and KCD2 from critics, a designer's blog, guides and wikis only, with no developer source reached. The two helper passes differ: the first coded 1 Steam review and found listing pages unreachable; the second reached recent-review listing pages and coded 25 Disco Elysium reviews. Players are thin: Disco Elysium 25 Steam reviews (mostly the 10 newest on two listing pages, many jokes), L.A. Noire 12 (positive only), KCD2 9 (none discuss its talk tests), RDR2 3 forum posters through one article. No source gives choices per minute or length of an exchange in seconds for any of the four, and the only share of play in talk found is a player estimate for Disco Elysium; these numbers are left for the footage counts on his PC (see the end).

## Disco Elysium

How talk works as a verb (sources T1 to T6):
- Skills speak: 4 attributes of 6 skills each, 24 inner voices that interject; the player can say the thought aloud or keep it. "Your skills talk to you" is the stated core idea (ZA/UM devblog, 19 Sep 2016, T1).
- Passive checks fire on their own when the number is high enough and give a line; active checks are options with odds, and some failed ones can be retried after a point is put into the skill (Kotaku review, 8 Nov 2019, T6). The "white" and "red" names for retryable and one-time checks are widely used but not confirmed by a source reached.
- Failure is content: a failed check can be the funniest result, and trying at bad odds is often worth it (Kotaku, 16 Jan 2020, T5).
- What a choice changes: thoughts carried forward (T1, T5); small later lines, of which the writers estimate "probably thousands" (Justin Keenan's GDC 2021 talk, reported by Game Developer, 21 Jul 2021, T3); a person insulted may close off (T6). Trials are settled by talk, not combat (T6).
- Length: short replies, one click each, the Continue button changing colour when a skill is about to speak (T5). Over a million words in all (developer figure repeated in a Steam review, T7; the original quote not opened). One four-way choice needed 428 new dialogue cards (T3). The writing tool froze under the volume: "we were writing too much" (Helen Hindpere, PC Gamer, 15 Jan 2026, T4).
- The makers: Robert Kurvitz wanted readers to forget they were reading, with no long blocks of exposition (GamesRadar, 9 Jan 2020, T2); talk skills should carry combat's tension (T1).

Players (Steam, 25 reviews, 23 up and 2 down, read 23 Nov 2026): writing and characters praised 9; skill voices or checks praised 5; failing is fun 3; choices matter 3; choice effects subtle 1; too much reading 3 (in the helper's reading not the main reason for either thumbs down); dislikes the checks or fears a dead end 2; little action 2; no talk theme 12. The helper judged both thumbs-down reviews to be mainly about other things (one does mention lots of reading). A review-summary site (vaporlens, undated, machine summary of an unstated number of reviews, T8) counts "text as core play" 33 and "very text-heavy" 28 as its top complaint themes: indicative only, and the page was not found again by the reviewer.

## L.A. Noire

How it works (T9 to T11): question a suspect, read the face against what is known, pick Truth, Doubt or Lie; Lie often needs evidence shown (a designer's blog, 21 May 2011, T9). The developers avoided extra meters on screen (T9). The 2017 remaster renamed the three Good Cop, Bad Cop and Accuse so the player could judge the hero's tone (Kotaku, 16 Nov 2017, T10).
- Punished: the labels did not say what the hero would do: Doubt often led to angry accusations (T10); evidence links weakly to the claim it breaks, so players collect "just in case" and guess; reloading to retry is tedious (AV Club, 21 Nov 2017, T11). The relabel did not cure it (T11).
- Praised: the faces and the tension of the questioning (T9, a designer's blog).
- Players (Steam, 12 reviews, all thumbs up, so only the praised side): faces praised 3, interrogation praised 2, labels not matching what is said 2 (both long reviews), trial and error 2, repetitive 1.

## Red Dead Redemption 2

How it works (T12 to T15): with the gun holstered, aim the attention at anyone and press greet or antagonise; defuse and surrender exist; provoking a lawman made him draw; repeated provoking leads to a fight or duel; striking first is a crime (Kotaku, 27 Oct 2018, T13; guide, undated, T14). One button, no menu, and the exchange ends in a reaction, not a branch. Honour rises and falls with acts and opens discounts and outfits (T13).
- Critics: built on the social play of Bully and "probably the most finely-tuned piece of world building" (GamesRadar, 4 Sep 2019, T12). Players: three Reddit posters quoted in one article enjoy it, mostly for picking duels (GamingBible, 30 Nov 2023, T15). No complaint was found; that is a gap in the sources, not proof there are none.

## Kingdom Come: Deliverance II

How it works (T16 to T18): six talk qualities (the review and a guide name them differently, so the exact set is not confirmed). Charisma comes from clothes and cleanliness and matters most with nobles and townsfolk; noble clothes do not help with bandits (Windows Central review, 3 Feb 2025, T16; Sportskeeda guide, undated, T17). Usually one or two such options show in a talk (T17). Overdone intimidation costs standing (T17). In the first game the hero's numbers were set against the other person's, seen only if Henry knew him (wiki, describing game 1, T18). Past help can skip a test entirely, and quests branch to different ends (T16).
- Critic: talk "far more engaging and impactful" than in the first game; at times Henry's words or tone do not match the option chosen (T16).
- Players (Steam, 9 reviews): none discuss the talk tests; 2 praise people reacting to Henry's look, past deeds or witnesses (one only in comments), which bears on TALK-02; 1 mentions trying to talk out of a crime; 1 punishes dialogue that forgets the first game's deeds; 1 punishes cutscenes overriding the player's wins. Not enough to judge KCD2's talk from players.
- Not confirmed by any source reached: the persuasion bars and timed choices.

## Typed free talk

- Façade (Mateas and Stern, paper about 2003, T19): typed text in real time, the characters can interrupt; about 40 kinds of speech act; a session of about 20 minutes, about 200 beats in the pool and about 18 used in a play, one beat roughly a minute; 6 or 7 replays expected. It never shows an error: a character deflects or changes the subject. Its known failure: the player can say millions of things and the characters answer in hundreds.
- Language-model characters, 2024 demos (PC Gamer, 12 Apr 2024, T20, one writer): praised: speaking felt more immersive than a menu. Punished: seconds of delay; characters dodged questions and felt empty; the talk was funnelled to a few preset outcomes, refusing ideas until the wanted one; freedom to speak is not freedom to act.

## What makes talk feel like play

| What works | Seen in | What fails |
|---|---|---|
| A line costs something; the result shows at once, failure included | Disco Elysium checks (T5, T6); L.A. Noire's tense questioning (T9) | Failure that only means reload (T11) |
| The choice says what the hero will do | RDR2's plain greet or provoke (T13) | L.A. Noire's Doubt (T10; 2 of 12 reviews); KCD2 at times (T16) |
| Who the hero visibly is moves the outcome | KCD2 clothes, cleanliness, standing (T16, T17) | |
| The person remembers past dealings | Disco's thousands of small reactions (T3); KCD2 past help skips a test (T16) | KCD2 dialogue forgetting the first game (1 of 9 reviews) |
| What the hero holds visibly breaks a lie | L.A. Noire at best (T9) | Evidence collected "just in case" and guessed (T11) |
| Short replies, quick turns, anyone addressable | Disco (T5); RDR2 (T12, T13) | Too much reading (T8; 3 of 25 reviews) |
| Typed talk deflects in character, never errors | Façade (T19) | Lag, dodging, funnelling (T20) |

## Standards for LEDGER's talk

LEDGER's talk is the consequence, not the main thing (section 34: five uses, under 30% of play, no skill checks, no prepared openings, decision lines are the game's own, live talk within 2 s). These standards take the lessons above into that frame. Status DECIDED unless marked.

- TALK-01, the line says what Tom does. Every suggested line and every decision line is the exact text Tom says (he is not voiced, so nothing is paraphrased), and each written decision line carries a stance tag in the data (yes, no, keep talking, as section 34 names them); the checker compares each tag with its line text and finds no yes-tagged line that refuses and no no-tagged line that agrees; in live talk the suggested line shown is the text sent. Rows: 3.6.5, 3.6.6, 3.6.8. Basis: L.A. Noire's Doubt (T10; 2 of 12 reviews), KCD2's smaller version (T16).
- TALK-02, who Tom is moves the talk, without numbers. For every decision talk (the deal, the witness, the sit-down) at least two outcomes depend on state the player can see or know: what this person saw and was told of Tom, Tom's clothes (the soft keys), his name rung, a hook he holds, the men at his side; and the person's reply names the reason in plain words ("not dressed like that, you're not"). The checker replays each decision talk with that state changed and confirms a different outcome or line. No hidden roll decides talk. Rows: 3.5.5, 3.5.7, 2.9.1, 2.12.3, 2.12.1, 2.5.2. Basis: KCD2 clothes, standing and past help (T16, T17) and 2 of 9 KCD2 reviews praising reactions to look and deeds; LEDGER keeps its rule of no skill checks.
- TALK-03, what he holds breaks a lie. When Tom puts a fact he holds to a person (a name, a sighting, a debt in the book), the reply changes, and the fact used is named in the line; the checker gives every witness and debtor talk once with and once without each relevant fact and finds a changed reply each time. No talk is lost by guessing: a wrong push ends in a state the town talks about (a witness who walks to the phone, a debtor who hides), never a dead end or a reload. Rows: 2.5.2, 2.8.2, 3.6.2, 7.9. Basis: L.A. Noire's guessing and reloads (T11); Disco's failure as content (T5).
- TALK-04, failure is a story. Every decision talk (the deal, the witness, the sit-down) can go wrong, and every way it goes wrong leaves a new state that comes back (said by somebody else, a refusal, a constable told), checked by the tester across every decision talk of Act I. Rows: 7.9, 2.4.4, 3.6.5. Basis: Disco Elysium (T5, T6; 3 of 25 reviews praise failure).
- TALK-05, they remember dealing with him. A person with an unsettled deal, promise or threat made with Tom cites it, from their own facts, in their first reply of the next talk Tom opens with them; the checker reads a week's talk log and finds no such talk that ignores it. This is apart from "it comes back", which stays once per deed (section 34). Rows: 2.3.1, 2.4.3, 3.6.2. Basis: Disco's small reactions (T3); KCD2 past help (T16); the KCD2 player who punished forgetting.
- TALK-06, anyone answers street manners at once. Every named person and every crowd extra answers each street manner (M) in body or voice within 1 s, with no menu; already standard 1.4.7, here confirmed by RDR2's praised greet and provoke (T12, T13). Rows: 1.4.7, 2.12.4. MEASURED (already in 1.4.7).
- TALK-07, short turns. Live replies have a median of about 12 words (studies/03's adopted target, on KCD2's measured conversation lines) and a cap of two sentences and 35 words, except a reply to a question tagged as asking for an account (what somebody saw, how Mickey died), capped at 70; a written street line about 7 words (3.6.3); no talk runs 60 s without a choice or control (6.7). The checker measures every live reply in a seeded talk bench. Rows: 3.6.1, 3.6.3, 6.7. Basis: Disco's short replies (T5) and its reading complaint (T8; 3 of 25). The 12 rests on KCD2's measured conversation lines; the 35 and 70 are design choices.
- TALK-08, typed talk never errors and never funnels. A typed line the simulation cannot act on gets an in-character answer, never an error or a system message (already the brush-off, 3.6.1). A typed line that matches a decision (an offer of money, a threat, a name asked, a request to keep quiet) never takes effect by itself: it brings up the matching written decision line for a plain yes, as section 34 requires (only a plain yes is taken; typing is for talk, never a command). The checker sends at least 20 phrasings of each such act to each decision talk, written by someone who has not seen the act table, and at least 90% bring up the right decision line; none is refused only because it is not the written wording. The 90% is a design choice. Rows: 3.6.1, 3.6.2, 3.6.5, 3.6.8. Basis: Façade (T19); the 2024 hands-on (T20).
- TALK-09, never dodges what he knows. A person asked something his facts hold answers it or refuses it in character for a reason of his temper or loyalty, never with an empty dodge; the claim check's log over a talk bench shows no reply that both lacks a fact and lacks a stated reason, apart from the 8 s brush-off and the offline written line, which are tagged as such and counted separately. Rows: 3.6.2, 2.14. Basis: T20's empty, dodging characters.
- TALK-10, the share of talk. Talk under 30% of play, aim about 19% (7.5), measured by the tester over Act I; at least half of Act I's decision talks are staged at the place of the matter (the doorstep, the cab, the quay, the yard) rather than called to the office, counted from the job and story tables. Rows: 7.5, 6.7, RT2-20. Basis: reading load is Disco Elysium's commonest caution (T8; 3 of 25 reviews); LEDGER is verbs first. The half is a design choice.
- TALK-11, the pace measured. Choices per real minute in talk, seconds per exchange and the share of play in talk are measured in the reference games' footage on his PC (RDR2 and KCD2 at least) and LEDGER's talk is held inside their band, while the 30% cap and the 19% aim hold whatever the band shows: PENDING that count (no source reached gives these numbers). Rows: 7.5, 6.7.

## Built on, and where it differs

Read first (23 Nov run, second pass): the blueprint's PRIOR-RESEARCH-INDEX.md and the studies it points to on this subject.
- coverage-audit-disco-elysium (14 Sep): show the working (itemised reasons) and failure gives content, not refusal. This study agrees on failure (TALK-03, TALK-04). On showing the working it takes the narrower road his rulings allow: no sum on screen, the reason said in the person's own words and the belief line (TALK-02; section 3).
- ai-npc-demos-hollow (about 14 Sep): five failures of language-model characters: talk without news, never pushing back, one wrong syllable, personality without consequence, players breaking them. The 2024 hands-on read here (T20) repeats three of them; TALK-08 and TALK-09 answer them. The earlier study's 77 to 83% negative player sentiment on generative AI stands beside this one as a reason to keep live talk short and checked.
- aaa-reference/studies/03-dialogue-barks (October, from KCD2's files under the earlier study rules): KCD2's conversation lines have a median of 12 words and the player's 8; all lines have a 90th percentile of 19 words (7.2 s), pulled down by short street lines; choices offered median 2, 90th percentile 4; about 400 timed choices, mostly 5 to 10 s; 1,280 skill checks. That study adopted replies of about 12 words, about 4 s. Its target wins over this study's first, looser cap because it rests on KCD2's measured conversation lines and was adopted for LEDGER; TALK-07 now aims at a median of about 12 words and keeps 35 as the cap and 70 for an account.
- research/holding-a-large-script (Sep): LEDGER's authored words are about 6.6% of Disco Elysium's million. No difference; it supports keeping talk the consequence, not the main thing.

## Rulings to revisit

None argued against. Checked and kept: no skill checks in talk (section 34; no stats or skills, section 3). Disco Elysium's checks and KCD2's speech tests are praised (5 of 25 Disco reviews praise the checks, 3 the fun of failing), but no source reached shows players punishing a game for lacking them, and the earlier Disco audit itself preferred consequences worked out to consequences rolled.
