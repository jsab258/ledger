## In plain words

1. GTA V and RDR2 build their main story from many hand-made missions, of at most about 22 to 26 minutes each (this study's arithmetic from story length over mission count; an upper bound), each a fixed script. GTA V forgives failure with checkpoints and offers to skip a part failed three times; RDR2's checkpoints were not found.
2. The complaint is not length but rigidity: a mission that fails the player for flanking, for going quiet or for any way the script did not plan. Of seven professional pieces, four say so (three read directly: PC Gamer on RDR2's PC release in 2019, Kotaku and Game Developer on GTA V in 2013; one second-hand: GameSpot's 2018 RDR2 review through a roundup, which calls it deliberate), and GTA V's heists, where the player picks the approach, are praised by name.
3. Loved side quests (The Witcher 3 above all) are whole stories: by their designers' own account, the item or the hunt leads to a conversation, a choice with no clean answer and a consequence that comes back later and can be seen. The most-loved ones in five "best of" lists read as stand-alone tales, not long chains [I, THIN].
4. KCD2's side quests are praised for freedom and many outcomes, and faulted where consequences pull their punches and where a quest is a fetch in disguise.
5. For LEDGER [I]: a job judges the result the town can notice, never the route or method; each planned job of the rise has at least two ways in; each side story has a turn, a hard choice and a consequence that returns, and most are short.

### Checkpoints, skips and failure

- GTA V: a failed mission restarts from its last checkpoint; after failing the same part three or more times, an option to skip it is offered (gta.wiki Missions page, edited 14 Sep 2026, an unchecked wiki: https://gta.wiki/w/Missions). Kotaku, Ben Lindbergh, 5 Oct 2013, confirms generous checkpoints and "the option to skip failed missions" (and wanted less forgiveness): https://kotaku.com/why-grand-theft-auto-v-had-me-begging-for-unforgiveness-1441560081. Two sources.
- RDR2: missions fail when the player strays from the script: flanking counted as leaving the gang, going quiet where the scene wants gunfire, breaking the law, tools taken away for the mission. Source: critic Mark Brown's post as quoted in a ResetEra thread (undated): https://www.resetera.com/threads/red-dead-redemption-ii-lacks-player-agency-during-its-story-missions.80582/page-1 (THIN: forum, one quoted critic). PC Gamer's review below says the same in its own words. RDR2's checkpoint behaviour was not found in the sources read.

### The "rigid mission" complaint

Professional pieces: seven (six read directly, one second-hand). Saying the missions are rigid or predictable, four:

1. PC Gamer, RDR2 (PC), James Davenport, 15 Nov 2019, 88/100: missions "tightly scripted" and "frustratingly rigid"; not following the script fails the mission. https://pcgamer.com/red-dead-redemption-2-review
2. GameSpot, RDR2, 25 Oct 2018, 9/10, read second-hand in GamesRadar's critics' roundup: missions predictable by halfway, little control over outcomes, stealth not always allowed; the roundup reports GameSpot treating this as deliberate (it "can drag, but for good reason"). https://gamesradar.com/red-dead-redemption-2-critical-roundup
3. Kotaku, GTA V opinion, Lindbergh, 5 Oct 2013: "Missions are rigid"; a fixed script with glowing markers. (URL above.)
4. Game Developer, GTA V opinion, Leigh Alexander, 20 Sep 2013: the game "gives you very little liberty to escape its structure". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/opinion-the-tragedy-of-i-grand-theft-auto-v-i-

Not saying it, three: Kotaku's RDR2 review (25 Oct 2018, about controls and pace), a GamesBeat piece (about scripted world events, not missions), and GameSpot's GTA V review (17 Sep 2013), which praises the heists: "your choice completely changes how the heist plays out". Polygon and Eurogamer were not read in full and are not counted. Two more voices, a GameSpot player review and the ResetEra thread, say rigid; they are players, not critics. Four of seven is a small sample (THIN for any ratio); it agrees with the players study (its theme "missions that waste time, give no direction or fail you for doing it your way": 7.4% mean share of complaints, rough; the fail-your-way part is not counted on its own) and the story-patterns study (missions on rails, 13 of 171).

What Rockstar has said: Rob Nelson, Rockstar North, in an IGN interview reported by Wccftech, 4 May 2018: they tried procedural companion missions and could not get their "connectedness and sophistication", so went hand-made. https://wccftech.com/red-dead-redemption-2-procedural-limits/ . No quote from Dan Houser or Imran Sarwar on mission design was reached.

[I] The pattern: hand-made missions are what make the stories land, and players do not punish their length; they punish being failed for a way of doing it that the world would have allowed. GTA V's heists are the counter-example praised inside the same games: a planned job with a choice of approach that changes how it plays.

## Side quests: The Witcher 3 and KCD2

### What The Witcher 3's quest designers say

- No fetch quests. Paweł Sasko and Mateusz Tomaszkiewicz, MCV/Develop, 5 May 2016: they set out to remove fetch quests; an item request is allowed only when it leads to a conversation, a choice or a secret; they "managed to get rid of most". https://mcvuk.com/the-witcher-3-quest-designers-talk-balancing-story-with-gameplay-and-declaring-war-on-fetch-quests/
- Hold information back, aim for feeling, give real dilemmas without a right answer, and bring consequences back later where they can be seen: delayed consequences returned at unexpected moments "leave the strongest impression". Sasko's GDC 2023 talk, reported by Game Developer, 22 Mar 2023 (most examples from Cyberpunk 2077). https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/10-key-takeaways-from-the-quest-design-of-cyberpunk-2077-and-the-witcher-3
- The best-known quest's first script ran to about 40 pages of plot before any dialogue (GamesRadar, 13 Feb 2025). https://www.gamesradar.com/games/the-witcher/the-first-script-for-the-witcher-3s-best-quest-was-40-pages-long-before-a-single-line-of-dialog-was-even-added
- Not all are good, and the studio knows it: "There are quests that are not that great" (Sasko, GamesRadar, 11 Jul 2024). https://www.gamesradar.com/games/the-witcher/the-first-quest-cd-projekt-made-for-the-witcher-3-led-to-the-best-one-in-the-rpg-there-are-quests-that-are-not-that-great-and-were-very-much-aware-of-that

## Standards for LEDGER (a checker can test each)

LEDGER's jobs follow the job template (3.5.6): offered by a person on a state (3.5.7), three keys to every lock and no job needing talk, a fight or a car (DEFINITIONS, from the description's section 14), places by landmark (4.1.3), nothing fails for leaving (4.1.4), no game-over and control back within 2 real minutes (7.9). Side stories: whole stories, not errands (3.3); about 25 (22 to 28) at a median of 10 to 14 minutes, about 5 hours in all (DEFINITIONS F15, which replaced "25 to 40"). Numbers marked DECIDED below are design decisions made here, not reference figures.

- **MS-01 A job judges the result, never the route.** For every job, the AI tester tries each of its three keys and at least one mixed way (by car and on foot, alone and with a man, openly and unseen where the verbs allow); every way reaches the result unless the town stops it, and ways the town notices add consequences (a witness, a telling, a statement), never a fail by rule. A job step fails only on something a person in the town perceived and acted on, or a state changed (the man gone, the goods taken, the window passed). Fail: any step failed by distance, route, method or a timer the world does not show. Basis: four of seven professional pieces fault rigid missions; RDR2's straying fails are the example. Rows: 3.5.6, GD-03, 4.1.4, 7.9.
- **MS-02 The planned jobs of the rise leave different traces by the way chosen.** Every job that Tom plans (a break-in, a squeeze, a front, the sit-down's lead-up) has its three keys, and at least two of them leave different traces in the town (who saw, what is said, who is owed). Fail: a planned job whose keys all leave the same trace. Basis: GTA V's heists, praised in the same reviews that fault its other missions (GameSpot, 2013); the three keys are the design's own rule. Rows: GD-08, RT-03, 3.5.6, 1.6.11. (Pairs with WQ-03 in WHERE-PLAYERS-QUIT.md, not a coverage row.)
- **MS-03 No dead legs.** No job carries a travel leg over 2 real minutes without a decision, a person, talk or a sighting; on foot this is met by the street itself (a person or happening on the way, GD-02's density), in a car by the radio and passengers. A job's whole length, offer to result, sits between 5 and 30 real minutes, except the few planned jobs of the rise, which may run to 45. Fail: a dead leg over 2 minutes, or a job outside its band. Basis: the players study's travel and boredom complaints. DECIDED: the 2-minute rule extends 1.2.7's rule for fares to every job leg; the bands are design choices (the reference missions' 22 to 26 minutes is only an upper bound and cannot set a band). Rows: 1.2.7, GD-01, GD-02, 3.5.6, 7.12, 7.5.
- **MS-04 Failing a job changes the town, never sends him back.** A lost or failed step leaves Tom in control within 2 real minutes with the cost said in the Ledger, and the job goes on as a state (another way, another day, or a person who now knows); no retry screen and no reload needed to go on. Fail: a failed step with no way forward but a reload. Basis: GTA V's skip after three fails shows players want to get past a failure; LEDGER has no game-over (OUT-12) and makes the failure the story instead. Rows: 7.9, 7.8, 3.5.6, OUT-12.
- **MS-05 Each side story has a turn, a hard choice and a consequence that returns.** Every side story: (a) after its start, something learned changes what it is about; (b) at least one choice where each answer costs somebody; (c) at least one consequence that reaches Tom at least one game day later (DECIDED), seen or said in the world (a person, a place, the paper), not only in the Ledger. Fail: a side story missing any of the three. Basis: The Witcher 3's designers (MCV 2016; GDC 2023); KCD2's soft consequences faulted (PC Gamer 2025). Rows: 3.3, RT-02, FIT-F15.
- **MS-06 No errand dressed as a story.** Any side story that asks Tom to fetch, deliver or find a thing leads, on the way or on arrival, to a conversation, a choice or a secret; a story whose only steps are go, get, return fails. Fail: a go-get-return side story. Basis: the no-fetch pledge (MCV 2016); TheGamer's "glorified fetch quests" (2025). Rows: 3.3, 7.14.
- **MS-07 Most side stories are short, inside F15's budget.** At least two thirds of the side stories finish within three steps and about 15 real minutes in the bot playthroughs; none runs over about 30 minutes; the library's median is 10 to 14 minutes and the whole set about 5 hours (F15). Fail: outside those counts. Basis: F15 sets the budget; the loved Witcher 3 quests read as stand-alone tales and the KCD2 rows that sound like long chains sit lowest (both THIN). DECIDED: the two thirds and the 15 and 30 minutes. Rows: 3.3, FIT-F15, GD-08.
- **MS-08 A threat in a side story is kept.** When a person in a side story threatens or promises something and Tom does nothing, it happens in the world within the time named (or within three game days if none is named, DECIDED), and the town can tell. Fail: a threat or promise that comes to nothing unexplained. Basis: KCD2's consequences that pull their punches (PC Gamer 2025, one review). Rows: 3.3, RT-06.
- **MS-09 Side stories never undercut the main story's urgency.** No side story is offered while a main beat's hurry is running (the hurry wrinkle as CLK-02 defines it, pending his answer on the clock), and none starts on Act III's mornings; side stories fill the slack Act I and Act II leave between beats. Fail: a side story offered during a hurry or on an Act III morning. Basis: PCWorld's pacing complaint about The Witcher 3 (2015; one review, THIN). Rows: 3.3, 3.1.3, 7.4, CLK-02.

## Rulings to revisit

- **23 Sep: jobs, no waypoints.** Evidence: the players study's theme "missions that waste time, give no direction or fail you for doing it your way" is 7.4% of reviews on average (rough, over-counted), with "no direction" inside it and not separated. The bar game, KCD2, points a marker at most objective starts and leaves the "where" to it (study 16, from its files, background only), and PLAYERS.md (12 Oct) named the tension as risk R6: players punish no direction, and LEDGER rules out waypoints. Recommendation: keep, with every go-to step named by a landmark and a person (4.1.3, 6.9) and the next main step always in the Ledger's Open page (WQ-01). A) keep, with landmarks, people who give the way and the Open page (recommended); B) add an optional marker as an assist, off by default; C) add markers. Until he answers, the ruling stands.
