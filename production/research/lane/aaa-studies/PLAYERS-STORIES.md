# Players' stories: what players tell each other happened to them

23 November 2026, the lane, AAA studies set A, study 9. Opus 5.5 wrote this file from one helper reading pass (Sonnet) that coded 82 player stories, and its own check of the notes: notes/player-stories.md (one row per story, with source and date). The rows were recoded once after review (four stories set to "did not come back") and every count below is recomputed by script from the rows. The memory and talk studies of today (MEMORY-SYSTEMS.md, TALK-AS-PLAY.md) are used where they touch. Games are read only from the outside: forum threads, Steam discussions, articles that quote players, staff memory pieces. No video was downloaded and no game file touched. GTA VI appears for its systems only; nothing of its story is written here or in the notes.

Games: Red Dead Redemption 2 (RDR2), Kingdom Come: Deliverance II (KCD2), GTA V (with a few GTA Online stories, marked in the notes), GTA VI (systems only), Hitman (World of Assassination, with three Hitman 2 clips), Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor.

## In plain words

1. The stories that come back later, the kind LEDGER is built for, come mostly from two systems: a recurring enemy who remembers (Shadow of Mordor's Nemesis: 21 stories, 10 came back) and witnesses with the law behind them (mostly KCD2: 21 stories, 7 came back). Stories coded as a person recognising a face, a coat or a talk all came back (5 of 5), but that holds by definition and is no evidence on its own.
2. KCD2's stories have LEDGER's shape: a small slip, a nobody sees a small thing (stolen clothes worn in front of their owner, a talk before a theft, a glimpse of someone fleeing), and the consequence lands later, at once or days after: a man arrested three days after a horse theft one stable hand saw; a widow who recognised stolen gear and reported it. 7 of KCD2's 13 stories came back.
3. Physics and car stories are funny and travel well as clips, but did not come back in this sample: physics 0 of 8, driving 0 of 5. GTA V's 13 stories: 10 funny, none came back.
4. The retellings rarely name the witness or enemy: 17 of 82 stories name a person, 9 of them Mordor's orcs (and 12 of the 21 Mordor stories still name nobody). In KCD2 the decisive witness is a widow or an unnamed stable worker in the retelling. Only 7 of 82 stories both name a person and came back.
5. Funny is the commonest tone (48 of 82). Sad stories are 13, and about half of RDR2's sad ones are scripted (5 of 10).

How far to trust it: 82 stories from 29 sources (of 33 listed, one went unused, one is Shadow of War and two are GTA VI reports not used), chosen by the people who posted them or the writers who quoted them, often in threads asking for the most memorable. They skew to extremes and say nothing of how often such moments happen in an ordinary evening. Reddit itself, ResetEra, GameFAQs and GTAForums could not be read from the cloud; most Reddit stories are read through articles quoting them (one through a public dataset copy of a Reddit post). Only RDR2 (24) and Mordor (21) reach 15 stories; KCD2 13, GTA V 13, Hitman 11, GTA VI 0. "Came back" was coded only when the source said so, so it is a floor. Three stories were checked against their original pages by the reviewer: two matched with corrections made (the briefcase did not come back; a Mordor captain's end reworded), one showed a KCD2 vendor reacted at once, not days later. Every share below is a direction, not a size.

## How the stories were coded

Each story read got one row: the game; a one-line summary in the helper's words; scripted, emergent, or scripted with the outcome changed by the player's choice; the system or systems that made it (from a fixed list of 15); whether a named person took part in the retelling; whether it came back later (the world reacted after the moment to something the player did); the tone; source and date. A story can count under two systems.

## Per game, in short

- RDR2 (24): random encounters and strangers' routines, retold once (a stranger stabbing another and walking off; a hanging seen in a town on first arrival). Only 2 came back, both scripted moments the player changed by choice (befriending a woman whose cabin John later visits; refusing a person who had turned him down earlier); the other 3 choice-changed moments did not.
- KCD2 (13): witnesses and recognition (6 and 4 as the main system). A vendor who had refused to sell tied a theft to the earlier talk at once; a man arrested three days after a horse theft that one stable hand saw; a player seen wearing clothes taken in a quest, after which the town treated him as a thief; a stolen shirt worn in front of its owner. Funny or tense, and the teller blames or admires the person, not the system.
- Hitman (11): funny physics (a thrown briefcase that homes in on a target) and staged discovery: a player ran a tap so staff would find a body, another fired a gun so a guard's body was found on cue. Routines and witnesses used as tools.
- GTA V (13): physics, driving and allies (online heist partners) make gags; none came back.
- Shadow of Mordor (21): every story is the Nemesis system: an orc that killed the player grew stronger and taunted him about it; one survived an arrow to the face, came back with a plate over it, and later in a bag; a cowardly captain who kept escaping rose to warchief before the player finally caught him. 10 of 21 came back; 9 named the orc.
- GTA VI: no player stories were read. Two secondary reports of its systems (gizchina, 28 Aug 2026; Engadget, undated) were seen and could not be checked; nothing from them is used. The lane's own GTA VI yardstick (production/research/lane/gtavi/) holds what is known of its systems.

## What the stories share

- A small human witness or enemy, a choice by the player that could have gone another way (steal or not, flee or fight, spare or finish), and a reaction later that the player did not schedule. Where the later reaction is missing (GTA V, Hitman physics), the story is a single gag.
- The teller remembers the person, not the rule: the widow recognised the gear, not "the reputation system fired".
- A mark often makes the comeback: stolen clothes, a scar or face plate, a talk before the deed. Four of the seven KCD2 stories that came back turn on a thing or a talk the witness could connect (rows 25, 26, 29, 37).
- Comedy comes from systems that are honest about their rules: a guard knocked out by a rake, a coward orc running on sight.

LEDGER's own rules already turn the references' weak kind into the strong kind: after a car hurts somebody in LEDGER, either answer comes back (2.18), so even a driving gag can return. That is the one-line design lesson of this study: tie every system that makes a gag to a person who saw it.

## Standards for LEDGER: emergent moments per evening

The game's description already sets the measure (section 2): a moment worth retelling is one where the player chose, it could have gone another way, somebody with a name was there, and it came back later; at least one in the first 30 minutes and two every hour after. The references show such moments are rare even among the stories players pick to tell: of the 82 coded, 21 came back, 17 named a person in the retelling, and only 7 did both. These standards make the measure countable and point it at the systems that produce it. Status DECIDED unless marked; the counts come from the tester's moment log.

- STORY-01, the moment log. The AI tester logs every moment that meets each of the four tests, with evidence for each: the choice (two or more options open, the one taken), the other way (a seeded replay with the other option gives a different outcome), the named person (by id), and the comeback (the chain print of 2.4.4 linking a later reaction to the moment). Each entry also records the systems involved (from the same list as this study), the mark connected if any (coat, car, thing taken, bruise, earlier talk) and the kind of moment. A moment missing any test is logged as a near miss. Rows: 7.6, 2.4.4.
- STORY-02, how many. In the tester's first evening (three real hours from New game): at least one full moment in the first 30 minutes and at least two in every later hour (the description's rule); measured over five seeded first evenings with different first choices, all five pass. Rows: 7.6, 7.1, 7.2.
- STORY-03, from people, not physics. Of all full moments in those five evenings, at least half come from witnesses, recognition or a returning person (rival's man, debtor, beaten man, crew), and none counts whose only systems are physics or driving unless a person saw it and it came back. Rows: 7.6, 2.5.1, 2.4.4, 2.10.2, 2.18. Basis: of the 21 stories that came back, Nemesis 10 and witnesses 7; physics 0 of 8, driving 0 of 5.
- STORY-04, a mark to connect. At least a third of the full moments turn on a mark the witness connected (from the log's mark field). Rows: 1.4.4, 2.2.2, 1.3.11. Basis: 4 of KCD2's 7 comebacks turn on a thing or a talk; the third is a judgement on that small base.
- STORY-05, the returning man. Once a rival has noticed Tom (the first rung of his ladder), at least one of that rival's men, not of Tom's own firm, meets Tom twice; and every man Tom beat or humiliated who is still on the street meets him again within three game days and refers to it. Rows: 2.10.1, 2.10.2, 1.3.11, MEMORY-SYSTEMS MEM-04. Basis: Mordor (10 of 21 came back).
- STORY-06, room for a laugh. At least one full moment in five is funny, tagged by the panel players (the tester's tags count only where the panel's spot-check of them agrees), within the tone of the game (dry wit, never satire). Rows: GD-05, 7.2. Basis: funny is 48 of 82 stories. The one in five is a design choice, below the references because LEDGER is noir.
- STORY-07, different next time. Two first evenings with different first choices share fewer than half their full moments (by kind and person, from the log). Rows: 7.6, GD-04. Basis, as a direction only: de Plater's point that the best Nemesis bosses are "totally unique to your game" (PC Gamer, 19 Sep 2014; MEMORY-SYSTEMS M2).
- STORY-08, the panel retells it. This extends the panel protocol (PANEL-BAR-2026-11-21.md asks only whether players recommend and would keep playing): after the evening the moderator asks one fixed neutral question ("What happened tonight?"), and at least 6 of 8 panel players retell a moment in a sentence that names a person and a later consequence. Rows: 7.2, 7.6. PENDING the panel.

None argued against. Checked and kept: no random encounters (section 3) and street happenings authored with a spec and a check (3.7): RDR2's random encounters made 9 stories and none came back, so the evidence supports the ruling; no random street fights or encounters (section 3): same.

## For the builder's PC measuring (KCD2, GTA V, RDR2 installed)

The rate players' posts cannot give: in one ordinary evening (three real hours) of each of KCD2, GTA V and RDR2 played without a guide, count the moments that meet the four tests of section 2 (chosen, could have gone another way, a named person, came back later), and the near misses, logged with STORY-01's fields. Without replays, "could have gone another way" is judged by the counter as met when at least two options were open to the player at the moment of choice and the game visibly offered or allowed each; a moment where only one was open is a near miss. This is the reference band for STORY-02 and STORY-03.
