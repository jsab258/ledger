# Players' first hours: KCD2, RDR2, GTA VI (waiting item 35, study 1; advisors 289 and 291)

Begun 21 Nov 2026 about 13:05 Swiss by the production session. About 6 PC hours estimated, not measured. Public video watched in the built-in browser, never downloaded. No GTA VI story reaches him: story beats are written here only as "story scene" with its length.

## Questions, written before any viewing (CLAUDE.md research method)
For each game, three or more players' first sessions, uncut, from the first control after the opening to at least the end of the first hour; players chosen across kinds (a streamer who speaks, a no-commentary player, a first-time player of the series where the title says so).
- P1 Boredom: minutes where the player says or shows boredom (sighs, "when does it start", menu idling, phone checks), each with its minute mark and the game's activity at that moment (walking, a cutscene, a tutorial, riding).
- P2 Skips: each skipped cutscene, dialogue or tutorial card, with its minute mark and how far in it was skipped.
- P3 Getting lost: each time the player says or shows they do not know where to go (turning on the spot, opening the map to look for the goal, asking chat), with its length until they find the way.
- P4 Laughs and delight: each laugh or delighted remark, with what caused it (a line, a physics moment, a bystander, a failure).
- P5 Quits or stops: where a session ends or the player leaves the game for a break, and what had just happened.
- P6 First tries: the first attempt at each verb (fight, ride or drive, talk, steal, shoot, the first chase), how it went (success, failure, a retry count), and the time from first control to it.
- P7 The first hour's shape: minutes in cutscenes, in tutorial cards, in free play, in travel; the time to the first free choice the game leaves open.
Counts per hour and per player; each number with its clip, its minute mark and the date read. What could not be read is listed below.

## Across the games (first hour; three players each, estimates from frames every 2.5 min, remarks from YouTube's transcripts)
- Control share: KCD2 about 39-45%, RDR2 about 46-67%, GTA VI about 57-76%. No player in nine said the first hour bored them; the one clear impatience is GTA VI's opening (7.5 min to control) and its home chores in the second hour.
- Getting lost: in all nine, almost entirely controls and interface (KCD2's crossbow feedback and dialogue options, RDR2's keyboard bindings and menus, GTA VI's unnamed buttons); the way is lost rarely and briefly (seconds), except KCD2's night escape (about 2.5 min) and RDR2's map at 3 h.
- The felt leash: said by both new RDR2 players and the KCD2 veteran ("can I just walk off the path?", "I can't stray too far"); not said in GTA VI.
- Delight: KCD2 and RDR2 mostly the look and sound (snow, light) and the first fight; GTA VI adds small domestic actions (about half its delight remarks after the home opens).
- Natural stops: the first place the game opens up (RDR2's camp, GTA VI's home; KCD2's parts ran past its opening with no stop).
- Skips: none seen in nine sessions (one RDR2 player had skipped the opening on earlier attempts).

## Not reached

## A reading for LEDGER (not a target; a target from it gets its own reviewer)
- Control early: two KCD2 players had control inside 2 min; GTA VI's 7.5 min drew the one impatience said at an opening.
- A leash needs a reason: movement limits in an opening were noticed and named by three of nine players.
- First verbs need plain feedback and named buttons: most getting-lost was "how do I" and "is this doing anything?", not the way.
- Small home actions delighted (GTA VI's home: pets, coffee, TV, phone); a room of his own with things to touch is cheap delight, but fifty minutes of chores tired a watcher.
- Players stop where the game first opens up; that moment is the first session's natural end.
