## Questions
1. How far does a period dipped beam light the road ahead (test points and any seeing distance)?
2. Main beam reach by the same sources.
3. Street lighting on British minor residential roads around 1990: BS 5489-3:1989 heights, spacing, lamp types; low-pressure sodium share.
4. The 1987/1988 Highway Code stopping-distance table against today's 23 m at 30 mph.

## Q1. Dipped beam

### Test points (not seeing distances)
- Directive 76/761/EEC (the EEC headlamp directive of 1976), read on EUR-Lex HTML, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:31976L0761, 25 Nov 2026. Annex I 6.2 to 6.3: the screen is 25 m in front of the lamp; the cut-off on the side away from the traffic is a horizontal line, on the other side horizontal or up to 15% rising; the horizontal part is set 25 cm below the horizontal plane through the lamp's focus; illumination is checked in Zones I to IV, points "75" and "50" appear in footnotes. The dipped-beam table of lux values is an image in the HTML and was not readable. The PDF (CELEX 31976L0761, OJ L 262, 27.9.1976, pp. 96-121) was fetched but is a scanned image; its table was not read. The dipped-beam lux values of the 1976 text are therefore NOT confirmed.
- The nearest readable photometric table is the later UN Regulation 112 (asymmetrical passing beam, filament lamps), the EUR-Lex consolidation CELEX 42014X0822(02), "Supplement 4 to the 01 series", in force 15 July 2013, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/112/oj (read 25 Nov 2026, text characters 0 to 200,000 of 487,000; the extract's Class A / Class B column pairing was marked by the reader as partly inferred). R112 is the 1990s successor to R20 and R1/R2 for these lamps, not the 1980s text; whether its values equal R20's is unverified. Section 6.1.2: intensity is measured at 25 m. Passing beam, right-hand traffic, in candela (Class A / Class B, as extracted):
  - 75R (0.57 deg down, 1.15 deg right): 5,100 / 10,100
  - 50R (0.86 deg down, 1.72 deg right): 5,100 / 10,100
  - 50V (0.86 deg down, 0): 5,100 / not shown
  - 75L (0.57 D, 3.43 L): 10,600 / 10,600 (the table text gave this as one figure; whether it is a maximum is not clear)
  - 50L (0.86 D, 3.43 L): 13,200 / 13,200
  - 25L and 25R (1.72 D, 9 deg): 1,250 / 1,700
  - B50L (0.57 U, 3.43 L): 350 / 350 (glare point)
  - Zone III minimum 625 cd; Zone I maximum 17,600 cd.
  - Section 5.7.2 and 5.8.2.1 mention a 25V minimum of 2,500 cd and "at least 2,500 cd" at 0.86 D to 1.72 L for the left-traffic version.
  - Min versus max for each line was not distinguishable in the extract: unverified which numbers are minima.
- Same family, modern: UN R149 as quoted in the Australian draft ADR 103, https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00929/asmade/2026-07-08/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html (read 25 Nov 2026, first 100,000 characters): Table 7, Class C, minimum cd: 75R 12,100; 50R 10,100; 50V 5,100; 25L and 25R 1,180. Modern, not period.
- Arithmetic by this note, not from a source: the 25 m screen reading in lux is candela divided by 625; the same candela at 50 m on the road is divided by 2,500 and at 75 m by 5,625 (normal incidence). The angles imply a point on the road about 0.75 m below the lamp at 50 m (0.86 deg) and 75 m (0.57 deg), so "50R" and "75R" are road points 50 m and 75 m ahead on the kerb side. This reading of the angles was not confirmed in a text.
- 2004 UN document (summarised from the search result only; the page itself returned 403 on fetch, https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/513462/files/%5BE_ECE_%5DTRANS_WP.29_GRE_2004_6-EN.pdf): points 75R and 50R (75L, 50L for left-hand traffic) are used to set the beam when there is no clear elbow. Unverified from the page itself.

## Q2. Main beam
- Directive 76/761/EEC, EUR-Lex HTML (read 25 Nov 2026): point H must lie within the isolux of 90% of the maximum; maximum "shall not be less than 32 lux" at the 25 m screen; at least 16 lux to 1,125 mm either side and at least 4 lux to 2,250 mm; values corrected proportionally if the standard lamp's flux differs from 700 lm. 32 lux at 25 m is 20,000 cd (by arithmetic, 32 x 625). Dipped-beam table not readable (see Q1).
- UN R112 (2014 consolidation, as above), driving beam, minimum cd, Class A / Class B: H-5L 3,400 / 5,100; H-2.5L and H-2.5R 13,500 / 20,300; H-5R 3,400 / 5,100; Imax 27,000 / 40,500. Imax must not exceed 215,000 cd; HV within the 80% isolux of Imax. By arithmetic, 27,000 cd gives 2.7 lux on a surface square on to the beam at 100 m, 0.27 lux at 316 m. That is intensity at distance, not a seeing distance.
- Wikipedia "Headlamp" (as above): European main-beam peak capped at 140,000 cd per side (this differs from the 215,000 cd in R112 for the pair, a possible per-side versus pair difference, unconfirmed) and 225,000 cd as "the international European limit" in the page's wording.
- Main-beam seeing distance for the period: not found.

## Q4. Highway Code 1987/1988
- No scan or text of the 1987 or 1988 edition reached.
- Current table (Rule 126 as quoted, Wikipedia "Braking distance", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braking_distance, read 25 Nov 2026): total 20 mph 40 ft (12 m); 30 mph 75 ft (23 m); 40 mph 118 ft (36 m); 50 mph 175 ft (53 m); 60 mph 240 ft (73 m); 70 mph 315 ft (96 m). The page says nothing about history. Note 118 ft at 40 mph here against 120 ft in the 24 Nov note (rounding difference).
- AA newsroom, https://www.theaa.com/about-us/newsroom/insurance-news/drivers-overestimate-stopping-distances (read 25 Nov 2026): typical stopping distances "haven't changed since 1960's". Does not give the table or the 1946, 1959, 1965 dates; those dates appeared only in a search summary of another AA page and are unverified.
- So the claim that the 1987/1988 table equalled today's 23 m at 30 mph has two secondary supports (the AA bullet and the search-summary dates) and no primary text: unverified, not denied.

## The session's own reading of the saved PDFs (25 Nov 2026 about 01:24, pdftotext on the copies the reader fetched)
- **Directive 76/761/EEC as made (OJ L 262, 27.9.1976, p. 96 on), Annex I 6.3** (the scan has a text layer): dipped beam on the 25 m screen, right-hand-traffic points with the left-hand-traffic twins: B50L (B50R) at most 0.3 lux (glare point); **B75R (B75L) at least 6 lux; B50R (B50L) at least 6 lux**; B25L and B25R at least 1.5 lux; Zones III, IV and I: the OCR puts the values one line out (it reads Zone III > 2.0, Zone IV < 20.0, Zone I blank); the usual ECE pattern, Zone III at most 0.7, Zone IV at least 2, Zone I at most 20, is unverified here. Main beam: maximum not less than 32 lux at 25 m (6.4). https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:31976L0761. These are type-approval minimums on a screen, not seeing distances: point 75 aims at the road 75 m ahead on the kerb side, so the law asks for usable light on the kerb side to about 75 m and very little on the far side at 50 m.
- **Seeing distance, a lead from American low beams (not ECE):** Olson and Sivak's 1984 experiment, as re-analysed (Engineering Sciences, 2004, "A Luminance-Based Analysis of Olson and Sivak's 1984 Research on Visibility Response Distance to Dark-Clad Pedestrians", the saved copy, p. 1): mean response distances to a dark-clad pedestrian **145 ft (about 44 m) on the kerb side and 75 ft (about 23 m) on the far side** (US right-hand traffic; mirrored for Britain). NHTSA DOT HS-806 563 (April 1984, Conspicuity final report, Table 14 discussion): a dark-clad pedestrian (jeans, navy sweatshirt) detected at a mean **70 ft (about 21 m)** by alerted drivers at about 30 mph, recognised at about 49 ft (15 m). US SAE beams of the period differ from European E-beams (sharper cut-off, less far-side light in Europe), so these are leads for the order of size only: a dark-clad person by dipped beam alone is seen at about 20 to 45 m.
- **British street lighting:** TRL PPR380 (Crabb, Beaumont and others, "Review of the class and quality of street lighting", CSS project SL1/2007, the saved copy; year of issue not read) refers to the "former British Standard (BSI, 1992)", BS 5489 as revised in 1992, and to replacing low-pressure sodium in residential streets with white light; it gives no 1989 column heights or spacing. BS 5489-3:1989's tables remain unreached.
