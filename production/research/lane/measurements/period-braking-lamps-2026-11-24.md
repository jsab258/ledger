## Q1. Highway Code stopping distances

Table below read at https://chalkdustmagazine.com/blog/stopping-distances-highway-code-wrong/ (24 Nov 2026; a modern article, metres only, not a 1987 scan):

| mph | thinking | braking | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 6 m (20 ft) | 6 m (20 ft) | 12 m (40 ft) |
| 30 | 9 m (30 ft) | 14 m (45 ft) | 23 m (75 ft) |
| 40 | 12 m (40 ft) | 24 m (80 ft) | 36 m (120 ft) |
| 50 | 15 m (50 ft) | 38 m (125 ft) | 53 m (175 ft) |
| 60 | 18 m (60 ft) | 55 m (180 ft) | 73 m (240 ft) |
| 70 | 21 m (70 ft) | 75 m (245 ft) | 96 m (315 ft) |

- Feet: the article gives them only in an image. The feet in the table are worked by this note from the article's formula (thinking ft = mph, braking ft = mph squared / 20); the totals 40, 75, 120, 175, 240, 315 ft match the figures usually quoted (a Wikipedia search summary also gave 75, 240 and 315 ft).
- The table implies braking of about 0.68 g (same article).
- Did it differ from today's? Unverified. No 1987 or 1988 text could be read. Secondary pages (AA, via search summary only) say the 70 mph row was added in 1965 and the typical figures have not changed since; the AA page itself was fetched and did not show the table or any history. The 1987 HMSO edition exists in the catalogue (https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/highway-code-0), text not seen.

## Q2. Braking of family saloons

Measured stopping distances from 30 and 60 mph for the Sierra, Cavalier or Montego: not found. Searches returned only 0-60 times and general impressions. Pages read with no braking figures: Motor Sport, Sierra 1.6L, Dec 1982 (https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/december-1982/92/the-ford-sierra-16l); Motor Sport, Sierra 4x4 RS, May 1991 (https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/may-1991/25/on-test-ford-sierra-4x4-rs-4-door/); Hagerty triple test, 2016 (https://hagerty.co.uk/articles/triple-test-rep-cars-ford-sierra-vs-vauxhall-cavalier-mk2-vs-austin-montego). Autocar's Montego test page returned HTTP 402 (paywall, not got round). A joint Autocar and Motor test of 5 Oct 1988 (Sierra 2.0i Ghia, Cavalier 2.0 SRi, Peugeot 405) is listed on Flickr, text not read.

Legal minimum (rule): Road Vehicles (Construction and Use) Regulations 1986, regulation 18, as originally made, Table I (https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1986/1078/regulation/18/made, read 24 Nov 2026). Car first used on or after 1 Jan 1968: service brake 50% total braking efficiency, secondary 25%. Other (older) cars: 30% and 25%. Later amendments between 1986 and 1990 were not checked. Braking efficiency is braking force as a percentage of weight, so 50% is 0.5 g. Parking brake in Table II: no car figure given on the page read. That the MOT of the time applied these same figures is unverified (the MOT regulations were not read). A 1960 and a 1975 secondary source (via search summary only) give the same 50% and 25%. Roller brake testers were to be required at test stations by 1979 (Hansard, 21 Jan 1976, search summary only).

Arithmetic only, not a measurement: at a steady 0.5 g, braking distance is about 18 m from 30 mph and about 73 m from 60 mph (excluding thinking time).

## Q3. Headlamps

Rules, Road Vehicles Lighting Regulations 1989 (read 24 Nov 2026):
- Dipped beam, Schedule 4 Part I (https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1989/1796/schedule/4/part/I/made?view=plain): two lamps for most vehicles; centre 500 to 1200 mm above ground; each within 400 mm of the vehicle side; colour white or yellow; no intensity requirement; no size of illuminated area. Aim: approved lamp, cut-off slopes down as marked, default 1.3% if lamp centre is 850 mm or lower, 2% if higher (over 25 mph vehicles); a lamp that doubles as main beam is aimed horizontal or slightly below. Minimum 30 W for four-wheelers first used before 1 April 1986, none after. Approval mark or BS AU40 required except cars first used before 1 April 1986.
- Main beam, Schedule 5 Part I (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1989/1796/schedule/5/part/I): two for most vehicles; each must be dippable at the driver's will or switched off with a dipped beam; paired lamps switch together; adjustable while stationary; white or yellow; no intensity requirement; same 30 W and marking exemptions.
- So the 1989 regulations set no reach or lux figure; those sit in the lamp approval regulation, not read.

Measured or published reach: not found. The E-approval photometric values (UN Regulation 20 for H4 lamps) could not be read: https://unece.org/fileadmin/DAM/trans/main/wp29/wp29regs/R020r3e.pdf returned 403. Wikipedia's Low beam page (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_beam, first 100,000 characters read) gives only a European main-beam cap of 140,000 candela per side and a French rule of lighting 100 m; it states no dipped-beam reach. The same page says headlamps cannot light the stopping distance above about 60 km/h (40 mph); that is a general claim, unverified for 1990. Search summaries mention a 1985 Cibie brochure listing 800 to 1,400 ft low-beam reach, which its forum poster called unreliable: unverified, not used.

## Q4. Street lighting

- Standard: BS 5489-3:1989, "Road lighting, Part 3: lighting for subsidiary roads and associated pedestrian areas", published 31 Aug 1989 (BSI listing via search summary), replaced by BS 5489-3:1992 (published 15 Aug 1992, withdrawn 11 Dec 2003; https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/road-lighting-code-of-practice-for-lighting-for-subsidiary-roads-and-associated-pedestrian-areas, read 24 Nov 2026). The 1977 edition preceded it. A BS 5489-1 fourth edition is dated 1987 (https://www.simoncornwell.com/lighting/timeline/index.htm, first 100,000 characters only).
- Column height and spacing for minor residential roads: not found. The standard's tables were not reached. Unverified.
- Low-pressure sodium: https://www.simoncornwell.com/lighting/collect/lanterns/gec-z9565-1/index.htm (read 24 Nov 2026), a collector's history page, says SOX was once judged unsuitable for residential streets, the 1970s energy crisis removed that objection, and by the end of the 1980s it was the dominant street lamp in the UK. A residential lantern (Vectra, 1980s) took a 70 W SON-T (high-pressure sodium) lamp (http://www.simoncornwell.com/lighting/collect/lanterns/industria-vectra-1/index.htm). So orange SOX was usual but not the only lamp; the share on side streets in 1990 is unverified.
