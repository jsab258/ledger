## Q2. Did the table change?
Only secondary evidence, unverified: the AA says stopping distances first appeared in the 1946 edition, 20-60 mph in 1959, 70 mph added 1965, "the same since" (reported via search summary of https://theaa.com/breakdown-cover/advice/stopping-distances; the page itself, fetched 25 Nov 2026, returned none of that text, so this is NOT confirmed from the page). gov.uk history page (read 25 Nov 2026) says stopping distances first appeared in the third edition and that the 1978 edition had 70 pages and the Green Cross Code; no distances or years given: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/history-of-road-safety-and-the-driving-test/history-of-road-safety-the-highway-code-and-the-driving-test
No primary source shows 1978/1987 figures equal to today's.

## Q3. Implied deceleration and reaction time (own arithmetic on the current metric table)
- Thinking: 30 mph = 13.41 m/s; 9 m / 13.41 = 0.67 s. 70 mph = 31.29 m/s; 21 / 31.29 = 0.67 s. In feet: thinking ft = speed in mph (30 mph = 30 ft = 44 ft/s x 0.68 s).
- Braking: a = v^2 / 2d. 30 mph: 179.8 / 28 = 6.4 m/s^2. 50 mph (22.35 m/s): 499.6 / 76 = 6.6. 70 mph: 979.2 / 150 = 6.5. Roughly 6.5 m/s^2 (about 21 ft/s^2, 0.65 g). Feet rule: braking ft = mph squared / 20 (30 mph: 45 ft; 70 mph: 245 ft; plus thinking 70 = 315 ft). Metric rounding makes 20 mph look 6.7.
- Chalkdust (read 25 Nov 2026) reaches the same: 0.68 s, braking term v^2/20 implying friction 0.68; it says the figures exist as an easy formula in feet. A critic's claim, no official source cited: https://chalkdustmagazine.com/blog/stopping-distances-highway-code-wrong/
- No official statement of the table's design assumption was found. The 45 ft braking split at 30 mph is derived, not seen printed.
