# A small district fresh for 50 hours (waiting item 37, study 2; advisor 291, his answer 21 Nov 12:12 "All 10")

Begun 21 Nov 2026 at 15:06 Swiss by the production session. About 4 PC hours estimated, not measured. Public video watched in the built-in browser, never downloaded; our words and numbers only. Games: Yakuza 0 (Kamurocho; Like a Dragon's Kamurocho as a second sample if time allows) and Sleeping Dogs (North Point, Central). The game's content rule holds for what becomes a target: bars, hostesses, drink, gambling halls and children are seen in footage but never counted as targets (written "excluded, content rule" where they fill a frame or a count).

## Why
LEDGER's town is a few streets that must stay interesting for many hours of play. Kamurocho is the best-known case of a district a few hundred metres across that players walk for 50 hours and more; Sleeping Dogs is a denser open city at street level. The numbers give our street's minimum of things per street minute, what must change by hour and by chapter, and how often a player crosses the same street.

## Questions, written before any viewing (CLAUDE.md research method)
Footage wanted: uncut free-roam walks with no commentary, filmed by the uploader in the game itself (PC or console), day and night, and at least one long uncut story playthrough part for crossings. Each clip with its game, platform where stated, chapter where shown, and its link.
- Q1 Things per street minute: walking a main street at walking pace, every distinct thing the player can notice or do in each minute: shop or door that can be entered, a street person who speaks or calls out, a staged incident (a fight starts, someone asks for help, a street event marker), a vendor, a sign or screen that moves, a sound source that changes. Counted per minute of walking, split "can be entered or acted on" and "seen or heard only".
- Q2 People in frame: counted on frames every 10 s on a main street and a back street, day and night; share standing (talking in pairs or groups, waiting, leaning, phoning) versus walking.
- Q3 Changes by time of day: what differs between day and night on the same street (people count, kinds of people, lit signs, shops open or shut, sound, staged incidents), and whether the game has fixed day/night (by chapter) or a running clock.
- Q4 Changes by chapter: what differs between chapters on the same street (people, closed areas, new events, police presence, decorations), from footage or guides with chapter shown.
- Q5 Crossings of the same street an hour: in an uncut story playthrough, how many times the player passes one named street in an hour, and the share of play time spent on streets versus inside or in cut scenes.
- Q6 Size: time to walk the district's longest street end to end at walking pace and at a run; the number of enterable places seen on that street.
- Q7 Street-level detail per frontage: signs, lit boards, posters, vending machines, bins, bicycles and other props per frontage or per 10 m, read on still frames.

## Findings (first pass, 21 Nov)
1. A small district carries many hours because the street is a stage, not a corridor. In one Yakuza 0 story hour the player is free on the street about a fifth of the time (clip 3: 5-8 of 30 frames), but about a third of the talk also plays out on the street (5 of 17 talk frames). Sleeping Dogs shows its streets for over half the hour, mostly from a car (clip 4: 10 driving, 7 on foot outdoors of 30).
2. Crowds are thin, not dense: Yakuza 0's day streets 0-3 people in view, once 9 (clip 1); Sleeping Dogs' main streets 4-10 (clip 5); night and rain empty the frame further (clips 2, 5). Density is not what holds the player; things that happen are.
3. Things that happen: at night in Yakuza 0, at least 2 street fights in 10 minutes of walking, each 40 s or more, the second drawing a ring of 10-12 onlookers (clip 2). Onlookers gathering round an incident is the cheap way the thin crowd reads as alive.
4. Each frontage is dense even when the street is empty: 2-4 signs and 1-3 loose props per frontage in Kamurocho (clip 1); Hong Kong's frames rely on hanging signs more than ground clutter (clip 5).
5. For LEDGER (our kind of game, a few streets, on foot): the numbers that fit are Yakuza 0's on-foot ones, not Sleeping Dogs' driving share. A target to check against our own prototype in study 2: a staged street incident every 5 minutes or so of walking at night (Yakuza's rate, cut to our quieter town and its content rule), onlookers gathering at any incident, 2-4 signs and 1-3 props per frontage, and talk played on the street, not only indoors. These are first-pass figures from small samples; they become targets only after study 2 measures ours by the same method.
