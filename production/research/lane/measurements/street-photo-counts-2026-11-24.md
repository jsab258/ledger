## Q3. Lamp spacing

No usable measurement. Photo 1 shows one column. Photo 2 shows replica columns in a redeveloped square whose spacing is a design choice, so it does not test the 34 m figure. Unverified.

## Q4. What makes counts unsure

- Only one town, one photographer, and slides taken on a single July Sunday (photo 1 shares its day with six other Hull pictures).
- Photo 1 is a main-road corner: the column belongs to the main road, not a side street.
- Photo 2 is a conservation scheme with replica lamps; the stretch length rests on one car length and heavy foreshortening.
- Frontage width of 5.0 m is an assumption (the brief's range 4.5 to 5.5 m would move the stretch by about 10 per cent).
- Pavement-level hidden items (bins behind walls, kerbside poles out of frame) cannot be ruled out in either picture.
- Dates in the Newcastle and South Tyneside records are loose ("c.1990", "Unknown").

### Method notes

- WebFetch returns text only for web pages. A photograph can be counted only where the page gives a direct image link and the image is fetched (the tool stores it and it is then looked at). Pages that give a caption but no reachable image could not be counted, and are listed as caption-only.

## After two tries: the method questioned (25 Nov 00:20 Swiss, the session)
Two tries (24 Nov 23:15 web readers; 25 Nov captioned archives) reached four dated Hull photos and no lamp spacing: dated 1988-1992 street photographs are too scarce online, and a text reader cannot look at a picture it has not fetched. The genuinely different method: large-scale Ordnance Survey plans, which draw street lamps (marked LP; symbol unverified, the OS legend for the period not yet read), counted along a terraced street's length. Free online copies: NLS's 1:2,500 National Grid plans of England, 1944-73 (1:1,250 only for London and the south-east; https://maps.nls.uk/os/, read 25 Nov). Tried at 00:19 in the browser pane: maps.nls.uk/geo/explore answers with a "confirm you are human" check (HTTP 405), which the session may not pass. Not tried further; the Quay Street spacing stays on the 1977 code's 34 m as his 24 Nov ruling says.
