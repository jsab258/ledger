# Street life in RDR2, GTA VI and KCD2 (waiting item 37, study 10; advisor 291, his answer 21 Nov 12:12 "All 10")

Begun 21 Nov 2026 at 15:21 Swiss by the production session. About 3 PC hours estimated, not measured. Public video watched in the built-in browser, never downloaded; our words and numbers only; no story spoilers carried into anything he reads. Content rule as in the other sheets: drinking, gambling and children seen in footage are never counted as targets (written "excluded, content rule").

## Why
LEDGER's few streets need people who look alive when the player stops and watches. The three games are the reference for that: what people do when nobody needs them, how they react when the player does something, and whether they talk to each other.

## Questions, written before any viewing (CLAUDE.md research method)
Footage wanted: uncut walks with no commentary in a busy town view (RDR2's Saint Denis or Valentine, GTA VI's busiest pedestrian streets, KCD2's Kuttenberg market), the player mostly standing or walking slowly; plus clips of players provoking reactions (bumping, drawing a weapon, running, shouting) where available.
- Q1 Idle actions a minute: in one busy view held for about a minute, the people in view and, for each, what they do (walk through, stand idle, a scripted action: sweeping, carrying, smoking, reading, leaning, sitting, eating, working at a stall); count of distinct actions per minute and share of people doing something other than walking.
- Q2 Talking to each other: pairs or groups talking in view, how long they hold, whether they walk while talking, whether a passer-by joins or a seller calls out.
- Q3 Reactions to the player: what follows a bump, a run, a weapon shown, a crime seen: the first reaction's delay by eye, its kind (look, word, step back, flee, call guards or police, crowd gathering), how far it spreads (one person, a ring, the whole street), and how long until the street settles.
- Q4 Changes by hour: the same street by day and at night, if footage allows: people count and kinds.
- Q5 Density: people in view on frames every 10 s in the busiest views.

## Findings (first pass, 21 Nov)
1. A busy street in all three games holds few people in view: 2-7 in KCD2's market by day (clip 4), 2-10 on GTA VI's seafront at night (clip 5); RDR2's Saint Denis away from its centre and out of hours 0-3 (clip 2). What reads as alive is what those few do, not their number.
2. Share not walking: about half on GTA VI's promenade (standing groups, seated at tables), about a quarter in KCD2's market (stall-keepers at their stalls); people stand where there is something to stand at: a stall, steps, a table, an awning.
3. Talking to each other shows as stance: groups of 2-3 facing inward and still (GTA VI 5:00; Saint Denis 6:00). Stills cannot show how long a talk holds or whether passers-by join.
4. Props carry the street between people: KCD2's dense stalls and parked carts with no one at them (clip 4) do the work a crowd would.
5. For LEDGER (a quiet British street, 1990, on foot): the fitting figures are the low counts and the places-to-stand rule. A target to test in study 2 on our prototype: 2-7 people in a busy view by day, about half of them standing or seated at a place made for it (a bus stop, a shop door, a kerb, a bench, a kiosk), groups of 2-3 facing inward, and props (parked vans, crates, a barrow) where people are few. Figures from small samples; not yet targets.
