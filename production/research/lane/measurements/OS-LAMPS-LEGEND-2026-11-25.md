# OS large-scale plans and lamp posts: notes, read 2026-11-25

## Questions (written before reading)
1. Did OS 1:1,250 and 1:2,500 National Grid plans (1970s to 1990) draw lamp posts; symbol or abbreviation; from or to which edition?
2. Does an official OS legend or guide (OS leaflet, Harley 1975, Oliver) state this? Short quote, page.
3. Where can 1:1,250 / 1:2,500 plans of northern English town side streets (c.1970-1992) be viewed free, and do the samples show lamp posts? How many along a terraced street?
4. If shown: spacing between consecutive LP marks in metres, on a free sample, with URL.

## Answers

Q1. Not confirmed for the period and scales asked.
- An OS abbreviations list gives "LP" as "Lamp Post", with no scale qualifier (other entries on the same page carry "small scales" or "large scales" notes; LP does not). Read 2026-11-25: https://rosdev.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/79RM/pages/76155645/P15.5+Abbreviations+used+on+Ordnance+Survey+Maps . The page is published 30 Jan 2015, archived, content Crown Copyright 1999; it is a later compilation, not a dated 1970s legend.
- A Margate local-history page glosses "L.P." as "Lamp Post" on an 1852 map. Read 2026-11-25: https://margatelocalhistory.co.uk/DocRead/OSMap%20Symbols.htm . This shows 19th-century use only.
- Digimap's historic overview says the 1:500 Town Plans (published 1848-1939) can show "lamp posts and man holes". The page names no lamp posts for the National Grid series, whose dates it gives as published 1943-1996 (paper sheets 1946 to about 1980, microfiche 1980-1999, digital from 1997). Read 2026-11-25: https://digimap.edina.ac.uk/help/our-maps-and-data/historic_overview/
- No source read gives a start or stop date or edition for lamp posts on 1:1,250 / 1:2,500 National Grid plans.

Q2. Not reached in full text.
- Geograph's abbreviations article is only partly readable (the "A" section is the only part returned). It is a work in progress. Per a search-result summary only (not read in full, so unverified here), it says Harley's "Ordnance Survey Maps: a descriptive manual" (OS, 1975) has an abbreviations table showing the scales on which each abbreviation appears. https://www.geograph.org.uk/article/descriptive-words-on-os-maps (read 2026-11-25).
- The OS Bench Mark abbreviations PDF (https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/resources/OSBM-list-abbreviations.pdf, read 2026-11-25) returned only metadata (Bench Mark Lists; created Feb 2023); its entries could not be read.
- No text of Harley or Oliver was read, so there is no page quotation.

Q3. Partly.
- Digimap states National Grid maps at 1:1,250, 1:2,500 and 1:10,000/10,560, published 1943-1996, are available to view or download (same Digimap URL as above). Digimap is an academic-login service; no sample image was viewed.
- A search-result summary (unverified, not read from the page) says NLS's map-images index lists 1:1,250/1:2,500 National Grid maps 1944-1972 for London and south-east England only, and that NLS's records search covers paper maps 1841-1991. NLS record help page: https://maps.nls.uk/geo/records/help.html (refused, see Not reached).
- Edinburgh University's index lists 1982 OS 1:1,250 plans as catalogue metadata only, with no image: https://www.geos.ed.ac.uk/~rsgs/cgi-bin/ifa_item1.pl?item=8606 (search result only; page not read).
- No public sample image of a northern English side street was viewed; no LP count.

Q4. Not answered: no free sample image was viewed, so no spacing could be measured.
