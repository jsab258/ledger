# Judging play from recordings: what the research says (lane task 4t, piece 6)

Written by the cloud lane, 20 Nov 2026, 04:20 to 05:00 Swiss time, before building the panel (tools/lane/prototype/reviewers/panel/). Questions: (1) how well do vision models judge game footage, and what do they get wrong; (2) how many frames, and which; (3) how to compare before and after fairly; (4) what to compare against when we cannot show KCD2 or GTA footage. Sources found by search and read on 20 Nov 2026 through a reading helper; two pages were cut short, as marked.

| Source | What it says | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| Taesiri, Ghildyal, Zadtootaghaj, Barman, Bezemer, "VideoGameQA-Bench" (NeurIPS 2025 datasets track; arXiv v2, 18 Dec 2025) https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15952v2 (page cut short; the false-positive figures below come from the helper's summary of the unread part, unverified) | The best models found glitches in up to 82.8% of still images and 78.1% of videos; weak on body poses, fine clipping and common-sense faults. Asked to find one glitch in a long video, GPT-4.1 noticed it in 72 of 100 but placed it within 5 seconds in only 19. About 17.8% false alarms for GPT-4o; at a 5% glitch rate, only about one alarm in five was real. Glitches stayed visible at one frame a second in 95% of a hand check. | A judge's eye is a weak fault finder and a frequent false alarm: the panel's faults are "to replay by machine", never counted as found. Faults are the machine checks' job (pieces 1, 3 and 5). |
| Lu, Senchenko, Sayle, Hindle, Bezemer, "How Far Can VLMs Go for Visual Bug Detection? 19,738 keyframes from 41 hours of gameplay" (FSE 2026 companion, June 2026) https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22706v1 (read in full) | Judging chosen keyframes one by one: precision 0.50, accuracy 0.72; an added judge model or retrieval gave little (accuracy 0.67 to 0.75) at about double the cost. Picking keyframes beats evenly spread frames, which dilute what matters. Nearly half of the detections were on-screen text that plain text reading would catch. | Give the judge both: an even strip for pace (dead minutes need even spacing) and clips chosen at the moments that matter (the player's best, worst, faults, quit, and the biggest bursts of talk). Put the on-screen words in the log as text, not only in the pictures. No second judging layer. |
| Shi, Ma, Liang, Diao, Ma, Vosoughi, "Judging the Judges: position bias in LLM-as-a-judge" (arXiv v9, 11 Nov 2025) https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v9 (page cut short) | 15 judges, over 150,000 judgements: the bias for the first or second answer is real and differs by judge and task, and is largest when the two are close in quality. Method: judge each pair in both orders; a winner that flips counts as a tie. | Before and after a loop is exactly the close case. Each pair is judged twice with A and B swapped; only a winner that holds both ways counts. |
| Our own earlier research, AI-PLAYTESTING-METHODS-2026-11-20.md (TITAN: 30% of an AI tester's fault reports false; MIMIC; Holmgard) | Fault claims need a machine check. | Same rule here. |

Answers. (1) Models find small faults in frames poorly, with many false alarms; nothing found measures how well they judge pace or taste, so that is tested against his own words in piece 8. (2) Our own choice, not from a source (VideoGameQA's 95% figure is at one frame a second): one frame every 15 seconds or more across the session, at most 48 frames, so up to about 12 minutes it is every 15 seconds, at 30 minutes every 38 seconds and at 60 minutes every 77 seconds; the clips carry the close looks. Plus up to six six-frame clips at chosen moments; the words on screen as text beside them. (3) Both orders, ties when they flip; the judge is not told the order, and each order is judged in a fresh session. (4) We never download other games' footage, so the comparison is written anchors drawn from players' and critics' words (PLAYERS.md, FEEL-REFERENCE-GAMES-2026-11-20.md) in ANCHORS.md; from 23 Nov the builder's own measurements of the reference games are added beside them, and frames of those games could join only if taken on his PC by the builder.

Found nothing published on judging taste (fun, tension, aliveness) from game frames by a model; the anchors' numbers are our judgement and are tested against his own words in piece 8.
