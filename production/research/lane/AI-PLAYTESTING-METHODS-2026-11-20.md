## In plain words

Nobody publishes a finished recipe for what he asked. The pieces exist separately: player types ("personas") that play with different aims, testers that hunt bugs and judge them, agents that rank difficulty like people do, and planted faults to test the testers. None of the sources measures boredom or quitting directly; a patience budget is our own addition and must be checked against his own play. The main lessons: give each type a clear aim, not just a mood; judge from what actually happened on screen, never from a summary; expect a share of false alarms and check each claimed fault by machine before it counts; and keep humans (him) as the final word.

## What this changes in our plan

1. Machine checks first and every build (cheap, no model), as the lane prompt says: they also confirm or reject every fault the AI players claim (TITAN found 30% of its AI tester's reports false).
2. Player types defined by aims and limits, not moods (Holmgard; MIMIC): each card says what the type wants, what it ignores, what bores or frustrates it, how much it remembers, and a patience budget in minutes of nothing it cares about. The types read only the screen (Xiao and Yang: human-like, not perfect).
3. Quitting is our own measure, not a researched one: no source measures it. It is calibrated against his own sessions (piece 8), and until then counted as a signal, never a gate.
4. Breakers use a stuck rule (no progress after about 20 actions: rethink, then report) and every crash, soft lock or nonsense they claim is replayed by machine from the log before it counts.
5. The panel judges recordings (frame strips with the log), never a summary, as he asked; the existing panel (tools/lane/prototype/reviewers/) reads a written packet and is replaced for the loops.
6. Testing the testers plants faults taken from our own earlier findings (muPRL's method), weekly, and reports the share caught by each piece.
