# Ideas from GTA VI for LEDGER, ranked (20 November 2026)

New mechanics and features worth taking, ranked by what they would add to LEDGER's promise (VISION: the town is watching; who can see him, how far did that carry, who is walking to the phone box). Each is checked against his rulings (a car hurts and never kills; Tom cannot die; guns rare and late; no alcohol or gambling shown or spoken of; no children anywhere; Britain 1990) and canon's moat, says what it would look like in the Hook, gives its cost in plain words, and says whether it fits the browser prototype or only Unreal. Costs are the lane's own judgement [I], not measured. Ideas the first read already adopted (S18, section d) are only repeated where GTA VI's release adds something new to them. Systems only; nothing of GTA VI's story.

## The ranking

1. Shed what they know, one piece at a time. GTA VI shows which details the police hold (face, clothes, car, weapon, partner, recordings), each cleared its own way (P, GTB-W; R, KOT, SHK on shedding by clothes and car). LEDGER's town already holds seven slots per witness (canon); the new part is making shedding a verb for each slot. In the Hook: the dark coat back on its hook in the office and the donkey jacket on; the cab swapped for the second car, or the roof sign taken off; a cap; walking apart from Ron. Each change answers one slot only, and only for witnesses who have not yet told anyone (gossip already carried is not undone). Shown as Tom's own guess in the Ledger, never as icons on screen. Rulings: fits. Cost: small in the browser (the slots exist; this adds actions and a check that a changed item no longer matches), small to medium in Unreal (clothes swap is the costly part). Browser and Unreal.

2. The search that stays where it happened. GTA VI leaves a marked search area round the last known place, and going back too soon re-identifies the player (P, GTB-W; R, S18 on the red search layer). The one-shot already has a constable walking to where he last saw Tom. The idea: the place stays warm for days, not minutes. Next morning a constable knocks along that street; the woman at the window is asked; the cab rank there is watched for two nights. No red circle on screen: the player learns it from who stands where. Rulings: fits; police corruptible as people (canon). Cost: small in the browser (a remembered place, a schedule change for named officers, decay by days). Browser and Unreal.

3. Crash damage that changes the drive. GTA VI's front-end damage pulls the car to one side, cuts acceleration and puts out the headlights so the night road goes dark (R, KOT, GT). In the Hook: a dented wing on the firm's cab pulls left until Sheila pays the garage; one headlamp out on the coast road at night; a dent is itself a witness slot (people remember a cab with a stove-in wing), so damage feeds being seen. Rulings: fits; a car hurts and never kills applies to people, not cars; 1990 cars had no electronic aids, so a pull is period-true. Cost: small in the browser (a steering bias and a lamp flag on the driving study's car), medium in Unreal (damage meshes). Browser and Unreal.

4. A passenger who helps in a chase. GTA VI lets a passenger give the driver directions in a pursuit, and two leads in a car talk with hints that do not feel like a tutorial (P, GTB-P; R, KOT, VGC). In the Hook: whoever rides with Tom says "left after the chapel, he cannot follow you through the yard", in words that carry where (LEDGER has no waypoint). The same voice in ordinary fares teaches the town's streets. Rulings: fits; no markers rule kept, the words carry the where. Cost: small to medium (lines per junction for the core streets, written once; a live voice later). Browser and Unreal.

5. The boot is seen. GTA VI's car carries long guns, clothes and stolen goods, and what is in the boot can draw attention if seen (R, KOT, GT). In the Hook: the envelope, a spare coat and later goods ride in the cab's boot; opening it on a lit quay is a witnessed act; a constable who stops the cab may look. Rulings: fits; guns stay rare and late (in early weeks the boot holds envelopes, coats and boxes). Cost: small in the browser (the boot as a container, opening as an act others can see). Browser and Unreal.

6. Taking a parked car is a method, with time and noise. GTA VI's ways in (window, lock and wire, a tool, a key cloner for dear cars) differ by the car (R, KOT, GT). His question on taking parked cars was decided by its recommended answer (parts tree, 20 Nov); this gives it shape for 1990: a brick through the quarter-light (fast, loud), a slim jim and screwdriver in the ignition (slow, quiet), a car with a steering lock that cannot be done. A tracked car has a 1990 form: a plate the constables have been told to watch for. Rulings: fits; never a game over if caught. Cost: small in the browser, medium in Unreal (animations). Browser and Unreal.

7. A buyer with a taste. GTA VI's fences take different goods and pay by demand; damage and trackers cut the price (R, S18; KOT, GT). In the Hook: the man behind the second-hand shop wants radios and video recorders, the scrap yard wants cars for parts and asks no questions, a man at the back of a café takes jewellery. Venues stay inside the content rule. Each buyer is also a listener, so selling spreads word (canon's gossip). Rulings: fits. Cost: small in the browser (a price table per buyer, a demand that falls as he sells). Browser and Unreal.

8. Blood on the coat. GTA VI notices bloodstains until the player showers at a safehouse (P, GTB-W). In the Hook: a fist fight marks Tom's shirt or coat; anyone who sees him remembers it under clothes; the sink in the office or a change of shirt clears it. Rulings: fits (violence shown as consequence, no children anywhere). Cost: very small in the browser (a flag on clothes), small in Unreal (a decal). Browser and Unreal.

9. Restraint is noticed. GTA VI's hidden record weighs how a player works, restrained or violent, and open-world acts weigh more than set jobs (R, VGC, KOT). LEDGER's rule stands (derived from people's beliefs, never a stored meter; S18). What is new: open-world acts weighing more than job acts, which matches LEDGER's no-rails design, and a worst level that cannot be undone, which matches canon's permanent memory. In the Hook: a man who saw Tom put a docker down with three blows and walk away tells it differently from one who saw him keep kicking. Rulings: fits; Tom cannot die is untouched. Cost: small (a weight on how the act is described in gossip). Browser and Unreal.

10. Local news that answers. GTA VI's radio, news and social feeds report on the player's conduct (R, KOT, VGC, TR). 1990 form: the local evening paper's front page and the local radio's news on the cab radio, a day late and a little wrong, carrying only what reached a reporter (a constable, a shopkeeper). It shows the gossip's reach without a meter. Rulings: fits; no alcohol or gambling adverts. Cost: small in the browser (headline lines from the belief network), medium in Unreal (voiced bulletins). Browser and Unreal.

11. Have-a-go witnesses. GTA VI's armed bystanders step in (R, S18). Already adopted as a courage trait (S18). New: GTA VI shows it in robbery crowds; for LEDGER's rare, late hold-ups (ruling 20 Nov) a shopkeeper or customer with a past who steps in, unarmed. Cost: small. Browser and Unreal.

12. Places stay open. Buildings used in jobs stay enterable afterwards (P). In the Hook: the warehouse, the yard and the landing remain places, so the town never closes behind a job. Cost: none extra if planned now, large to retrofit. Unreal mainly.

13. Settings an adult expects. GTA VI's list (aim modes, tap-and-hold assists, simplified mini-games, reduced car damage, colour-blind modes, text size, captions, narration, tinnitus reduction) is the bar (R, PD). Cost: medium in Unreal, spread over the build; captions and text size in the browser. Browser in part, Unreal fully.

14. Context prompts that never misfire. GTA VI's crowded prompts are criticised (R, KOT). Turn its fault into a check: two prompts in reach never fire the wrong one. Cost: small; a test in the AI tester's walk. Browser and Unreal.

## Ruled out or not for LEDGER

- Body stats, gym and calories: LEDGER has no stats (VISION), and critics call the chores fussy (S18).
- Two playable leads and switching: one player (S18).
- Shops open all night: breaks 1990 and the town's clock (S18).
- Stars on screen, police as icons, a red circle on the map: LEDGER shows knowledge through people and Tom's guess, never a HUD meter (VISION).
- A phone with apps: 1990. The office phone, phone boxes, the cab radio and later a pager or a rare car phone carry the same jobs.
- Focus slow-motion shooting and a large arsenal: guns rare and late (ruling 18 Nov).
- GTA VI's pastimes (hunting, golf and the rest): scope, and some fall outside the content rule.
- 30 fps as a target: LEDGER's frame target stands.
