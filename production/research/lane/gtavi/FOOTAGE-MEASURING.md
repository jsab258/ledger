No number becomes a target by copying (his rule of 17 Nov); GTA VI is one more game on the sheets.

## The spoiler rule while measuring

He will play GTA VI himself. So:
- Choose free-roam, police, driving and systems videos (titles naming free roam, police chase, driving test, fist fight, robbery, open world). Avoid any video named after a story job or a chapter.
- Skip every cut scene and every scripted story job; measure only free roam and side activities. If a video turns to story, move on.
- Write no dialogue, no names, no story place and nothing that happens in the story. Minute marks and numbers only.
- A fresh reviewer screens the filled sheet for spoilers before it is pushed.

## The footage problem peculiar to GTA VI

- It runs at 30 frames a second on every console (R, Digital Foundry via Pure Xbox). A frame is about 33 milliseconds, twice the driving method's 60 fps step, so short times (fist start-up, hit-stop, steering response) carry about twice the error per frame. Count across many frames (the driving method's lesson: one frame between marks can mean an error of about 76%, ten frames about 7%), and note 30 fps on each such number.
- Uploads at 60 fps repeat each game frame; the clip check finds the true game rate first (driving method section 3). PS5 Pro footage holds 30 best; base PS5 and Xbox drop in chases and explosions, so prefer PS5 Pro clips and log any dip.
- The game's speedometer, if shown, is in miles an hour (American setting); check it against dash timing once.

## What GTA VI adds (new rows on the sheet)

- G1 Crash damage pull: after a front-end hit, the steady correction needed (heading drift per second at a set speed against the road's lines), and the frames until the player compensates. Five crashes. About 20 minutes.
- G2 Headlamp loss: road visible ahead at night with both lamps and with one or none, in car lengths. Three cases. About 15 minutes.
- G3 Car entry by method: seconds from first touch to driving away, for window, lock and wire, the slim-jim game and the key cloner; whether bystanders react. Five each where found. About 30 minutes.
- G4 Report to response: seconds from a witnessed crime to the stars appearing (the call), and to the first police arriving; with and without a mask. Five each. About 30 minutes.
- G5 The search area: its size on the map against the street grid (in blocks, from the map screen), how long it stays, and whether returning re-identifies. Three cases. About 25 minutes.
- G6 Shedding a detail: seconds to change clothes from the boot, respray, swap car; which mark clears and when. Five each. About 25 minutes.
- G7 Crowd count: distinct people in one busy view, counted on three frames each from a tram stop, a beach and a quiet street, by day and by night. About 20 minutes.
- G8 Repeated lines: in one unbroken 30-minute free-roam stretch, count ambient lines heard twice (note only the count, never the words). About 30 minutes, kept separate from the split of an hour because counting lines needs its own attention.
