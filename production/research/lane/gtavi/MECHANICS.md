# GTA VI: every mechanic a player meets (20 November 2026)

The second read, a day after release, grouped as LEDGER's parts tree groups its parts. Systems and gameplay only, by his rule: no story, no characters' fates, no mission plots. The two leads appear only as a mechanic. Outside sources only (reviews, guides' systems pages, Rockstar's support pages), found by web search on 20 Nov 2026 and read through a fetch tool that returns a summary of each page, so every number is second-hand until someone reads the page itself. The first read (production/research/aaa-reference/studies/18-gta6-first-read.md, 19 Nov) is not repeated in full; this file adds to it.

Tags: R a post-release source (the GTABase police guide, GTB-W, was written before release and only updated on 19 Nov, so it counts as P); P pre-release (previews, guides written before release); U one source only, or sources that disagree. Source keys are at the end.

## 1 What the player does with his hands

Driving
- Heavier handling than GTA V; an overturned car cannot be rocked back by steering (P, GTB-P).
- Roads about 15% narrower than GTA V's and busier (P, GTB-P; R, KOT on narrow streets and more traffic).
- Crash damage cuts steering and acceleration, can pull the car to one side so the player must hold a correction, and a smashed front end puts out the headlights, so nights become dark (R, KOT, GT).
- Fuel: a gauge on cars and bikes, refuelling at petrol stations, electric cars charged; running dry can strand a chase (R, SHK; P, GTB-P). Engine damage said to waste fuel (U, SHK).
- Doors, boot and bonnet worked one by one from a vehicle menu (P, GTB-P). Nitrous from mod shops (P).
- A passenger can give the driver directions in a chase (P, GTB-P).
- Repair and respray shops; a respray clears the police's "vehicle known" mark; some tracked cars are refused (R, SHK; P, GTB-W).
- Several hundred distinct vehicles: cars, bikes, boats, aircraft, buses, lorries, a bulldozer (R, KOT, GT; counts differ, U).
- Radio: more than 20 stations plus on-demand podcasts, a switch to mute licensed music (U, SHK; R, PD for the settings).
- Camera settings separately for on foot, land or water vehicle, aircraft and underwater; cinematic crash and kill cameras from 1 to 5, default 3 (R, PD).
- Ride-hailing app for escapes and car swaps; an app that brings the player's own scooter or vehicle (R, GT; P, GTB-P).

Taking cars
- Anyone driving can be pulled out; a passenger may fight for the car (R, KOT; P for the passenger).
- Entry by method: elbow the window, pop the lock and hotwire, a slim-jim mini-game, a key cloner for expensive cars that cannot be hotwired (R, KOT, GT).
- A phone scanner reads a car's lock, value, alarm and tracker before the attempt (P, GTB-P, MOB).
- Tracked cars report themselves to police and are harder to sell; a fence removes trackers; damage lowers the fence's price (R, KOT, GT; P for the fence detail, U).
- Stolen cars are temporary until registered; unregistered ones are eventually lost; a three-car garage to start, extendable; abandoned personal cars go to the pound (P, GTB-P, U).

On foot
- Walk, fast walk, jog, run, with a hold or toggle sprint (R, PD).
- Body weight and fitness vary and change running, kicking doors in and swimming (R, GT). Stats named before release: health, cardio, fitness, stamina, strength, calories, body fat (P, GTB-P).
- Sweat shows after sprinting (P).
- Slide over a bonnet, vault and climb to escape (P, GTB-W). No climbing or stamina numbers found.
- Context prompts for looting, the boot and reloading sometimes fire the wrong action; many buttons do two jobs (R, KOT).

Fists and close fighting
- The two leads fight in different styles (P, GTB-P).
- Ground grapples broken by tapping a pulsing prompt; a short timed counter that can disarm; a standing subdue; silent takedowns on lone targets (P, ATH). Grapples, trips and repeated blows can knock a person out (U, SHK).
- No block, finisher or damage figures found in any post-release source. Melee is the thinnest-documented verb.

Guns
- No bottomless wheel: on the body two pistols, or a pistol and a sub-machine gun, plus one melee weapon; long guns live in the car's boot (R, GT; P, GTB-P).
- Open carrying of a long gun draws strong reactions from people and police (R, GT; first read).
- Focus: a slow-motion meter for aimed non-lethal shots (knees, arms, disarming) and for engines and tyres (R, GT, TR, GI). Crosshair colour shows stun or kill (P, U).
- A wounded police officer or gang member may fall back (R, KOT).
- Body armour, ammunition and armour from gun shops (R, SHK).
- Cover with blind fire; thin walls shot through; leaning out of a car to shoot with a rifle (P, ATH).
- Reviewers say cover plus Focus makes most fights easy (R, GI).
- Settings: lock-on, partial or free aim; aim assist 0 to 4 (another site says 0 to 5, U); quick draw, drive-by assist, holster in vehicle (R, PD).

Stealth, disguise and crime
- Robbery verbs: masks, back entries, alarms, wiping recordings, safes, sparing the clerk, a getaway car parked out of sight (R, KOT).
- Changing car, clothes, mask or hair sheds what the police know; spare clothes kept in the boot (R, SHK, KOT; a haircut, P, GTB-W).
- Blood on clothes is noticed; a shower at a safehouse clears it; exploded dye packs mark the player (P, GTB-W).
- Lock picks, CCTV destroyed on site (cloud recordings cannot be), back-room keys from a fleeing clerk; explosives lower a safe's payout, slow cracking raises the risk of police arriving (P, GTB-P, U).
- Robbery targets from small shops to banks, available nearly any time; payout scales with how cleanly it went, less the fence's cut (R, GT, KOT). Counts conflict: about 1,000 robbable businesses (GT) against more than 600 shops (KOT) (U).

Talking to people
- Contextual verbs on anyone: greet, taunt, defuse, provoke (P, MOB, U; R, first read on greet and taunt).
- Firing to scare makes people flee (P).

The phone
- A physical phone the camera moves in to, not an overlay (P; R setting "default or first person", PD).
- Calls, texts, photos, a car scanner, a social app the player cannot post to, banking (cash still paid in at cash machines), fitness, video, ride-hailing (R, KOT, TR; P for the full list).
- People answer after about three rings (R, TR).

Two leads
- Switch with the d-pad in the open world, and live during some jobs; invite the partner along to activities; splitting up clears the police's "partner known" mark (R, KOT; P, GTB-W for splitting up).

Life and pastimes
- Gym work raises health, stamina and Focus; eating and sleep add to them; safehouses restore them (R, TR, GT). Strength wants three days running at the gym (P, U).
- Fishing, diving, hunting with a payout per kill (one example, $450, R, KOT), kills kept in the boot, golf, basketball, a shooting range, zoos, drones, motocross and more (R, KOT, GT, GI).
- Dogs can be petted with the owner's leave, scolded and fed (P, U).
- Photo app named; full photo mode not confirmed (P, U).

## 2 The town under the hood

- People react in the moment to staring, following, crowding, outfits, eavesdropping (P, GTB-P); reviews confirm reactions to bad driving, taunts and open guns (R, first read).
- Three to four times the lines per person (the page names no baseline), and talk after an interaction (R, VICE, one review); 600,000 or more animations against about 55,000 in GTA V and 300,000 in RDR2, by Rockstar's figure (P, GTB-P, TS).
- Some people carry hidden guns and may fight back during a robbery (P; R, first read).
- A tram stop seen with more than 100 distinct people moving (R, KOT, U).
- The police system: a wanted level starts only when a crime is reported (a witness calls it in, an alarm, a camera, a tracked car); six stars; states named responding, suspect unknown (hollow stars), in pursuit, sight lost, and heat, a red area on the map round the last known place which stays, where going back can re-identify the player (P, GTB-W updated 19 Nov; R, KOT, TR on witnesses and cameras; R, S18 via TheSixthAxis and Vice on the red search layer).
- Seven marks under the stars show what police know: appearance, vehicle, outfit, visible weapon, partner, on-site CCTV, cloud CCTV; each cleared its own way (P, GTB-W; R, KOT, TR, SHK on clothes, car and mask shedding).
- Police share the description by radio; gunfire or a killing brings a faster response; dog units track hard; hiding in bushes, behind unlocked doors or crouched in a parked car works; police vanish from the map in a pursuit (R, KOT on the radio; P, GTB-W for the rest).
- Getting killed or arrested loses cash carried, unowned guns and stash bags; cash paid into a cash machine is safe (P, GTB-W, U).
- Criminal profile: a hidden record of how restrained or how violent the player is, with named tiers (three, four or five names across sources, U), the worst said to be permanent; it changes how people and police treat the player; acts in the open world weigh more than acts in jobs (R, KOT, VGC, TR).
- Economy: fences with different tastes paying by demand, pawn shops as fences, marked bills, cash banked at machines, some jobs needing a minimum stake (R, first read; P, GTB-P). No source found for laundering or owning businesses in play this time (the first read had one review on laundering, so U).
- Shop hours: Kotaku says every shop is open all night; another review says shops open and close (U, conflict stands from the first read).
- Weather is local and lasts, with wind, puddles, storms and hurricanes (P, GTB-P).
- More than 170 animal species in rarity tiers (P; 100 or more, U, SHK).
- Buses and trains on timetables; frequent random events (R, first read; KOT).
- In-world social media and news report on what the player did (R, KOT, VGC, TR).

## 3 Story and people (craft only)

See STORY-CRAFT.md. Mechanically: two leads, at-will and live switching, jobs started by phone calls and by meeting people, dialogue choices at key moments, branching endings, a hidden relationship value, the criminal profile (see section 2). No content.

## 4 The place

- Map about twice GTA V's and three times RDR2's; the main city about twice Los Santos (P, TS, GTB-P).
- A very large number of enterable interiors (shops, lobbies, roofs); job locations stay open afterwards (P, no count).
- Murals by more than 50 real street artists (P, TS).
- Swamp, mountain, military base and city (R, KOT).

## 5 How it looks and sounds

- Lighting, reflections, water and hair singled out by Digital Foundry and reviewers (R, PX, TR).
- Licensed radio, new DJs, adverts and podcasts; songs placed at charged moments (R, VGC for radio; KOT, TR, GI for songs).
- Faces, breath and sweat carry feeling in scenes (R, GI).
- Animation roughness beside the fidelity noted by one review (R, first read).

## 6 The interface

- A hideable HUD; the minimap, compass, turn-by-turn GPS and door markers each switchable (R, PD; P, GTB-P).
- Stars with the seven knowledge marks; police not shown as map dots (P, GTB-W).
- A satellite-style pause map with fog of war; destinations set without opening it (P).
- Missions with a suggested route that may be ignored, and very little signposting (R, VICE; GR).
- Criticised: crowded context prompts and the weapon wheel (R, KOT, TR).
- Accessibility: three control layouts, sensitivity 0 to 14, dead zones, aim curves, tap-and-hold assists, simplified mini-games, auto drive, reduced vehicle damage, an accessible combat setting, colour-blind modes, four text sizes, subtitles and captions, screen narration, audio description, tinnitus reduction, mono, 12 languages (R, PD, matching RS's list).

## 7 The game's shape

- Main story about 50 to 80 hours (R, VGC about 60, one roundup 50, VICE 80 or more; P, Rockstar about 80), about 100 to 150 hours with most side content (R, TR, KOT); figures differ (U).
- Failure is soft: one review found a single fail state; mishaps cut pay (R, first read, one review).
- Saving: 15 manual slots and one autosave; manual saves only from the pause menu outside jobs; cheats stop saving; no job replay and no medals (U, GB only).
- Difficulty not directly adjustable; damage and encounter settings stand in (R, BGR, PD).
- Story jobs, side jobs, heists, robberies and pastimes woven, with planning, stake-outs and loud or quiet approaches (R, TR, KOT).

## 8 Technical

- 30 frames a second on every console, no 60 mode; base PS5 and Series X a softer 1440p; Series S 1080p, mostly mid to high 20s, near 20 in car destruction; PS5 Pro best, near 4K look with ray-traced shadows; drops in crowded streets and big firefights (R, PX for Digital Foundry; KOT, TR; first read for base PS5 low near 23).
- About 212 GB installed on Xbox after updates (U, OZ). Two patches before launch with no published notes (U, OZ).
- Almost no bugs reported by critics (R, first read; TR).
- No PC version announced [I, from the study's brief; not checked today].

## 9 The machinery

- Take-Two's head says generative AI has "zero part" in the game's creative content, while the company uses AI for testing and productivity across about 200 projects (P, TNW 19 May 2026; Take-Two's words, not Rockstar's).
- About 600,000 animations and a dedicated team for ambient lines (P, GTB-P, GTB-GI).
- Nothing reached on Rockstar's tools or testing methods.

## Early player reception (one day in, marked early)

Critics: Metacritic 99 (144 to 153 reviews by the day's count), OpenCritic 98 (R, OZ, OL). Players so far, anecdotal: the 30 fps cap, drops in crowded streets, chores and pastimes felt as filler, a slow opening, fewer chaotic heists than GTA V (R, IBT, early). Nothing yet from Steam-style review counts: there is no PC version, so the method of the players study cannot be run on GTA VI.
