## Notes

1. Lamp columns. The enthusiast site streetlightonline.co.uk (Chris Pratt's collection, not a maker) is the only source reached for columns. It confirms the 1960s to 1980s residential type: a 15 ft concrete column (GEC, Stanton 1805, Concrete Utilities) with a cranked bracket and a 35 W SOX lantern, often a Thorn Beta 5 put up in the early 1980s to replace mercury lanterns. Concrete Utilities (Ware, Hertfordshire, now CU Phosco) were "amongst the biggest of the manufacturers of concrete street lighting columns" (thisbelongsinamuseum.com). Shaft section, bracket length and door position need either a scanned maker catalogue or measuring a surviving column from photographs. BS 1308 (concrete columns, 1970 edition) holds the official dimensions but is paywalled.
2. Beta 5 spigot. Side entry 34 mm, 80 mm long; Thorn also made a 27 mm option for old 3/4 in BSP brackets. A top-entry Beta 5 existed and was the type on cranked brackets (97IA: "top-entry Thorn Beta 5 35 Watt SOX lanterns"). If the game's column has a cranked bracket, the lantern should be the top-entry form.
3. Beta 5 bowl. 1980s lanterns had the deep bowl; the shallow bowl came with the slimmed-down version later in the 1980s. Either fits 1990.
4. Pillar boxes. Type A was cast only from 1991 (Hillsyde Foundry, per LBSG), so it does not belong in 1990. Type K was installed from 1980 to about 2000. In a 1990 street a Type B (older, narrower, about 385 mm) or a Type K (about 483 mm, no separate cap) are the right choices. The 489 mm target fits a Type K body, but then the box has no separate cap. Above-ground height is about 1,350 mm from two salvage listings ("all measurements approximate"); LBSG's 64 in is unexplained and may include part of the buried base.
5. Bollards. Only modern reproductions were reached. The target 1,082 mm is a little lower than both makers; a figure between 1,100 and 1,200 mm is safer.
6. Gully grates. Modern sizes are listed above. A 1980s grating would be grey iron to BS 497, probably 430 x 370 or 450 x 450 clear opening, but no BS 497 table was opened, so this is inferred.
