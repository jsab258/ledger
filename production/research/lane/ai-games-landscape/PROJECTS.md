# AI-built games, 2025 to November 2026: one line each

Researched 23 Nov 2026 and checked once by a fresh reviewer (15 fixes) by the landscape job (claude-opus-5-5 with two Sonnet helpers). Fields in order: who; when; models and tools; team; time; cost; scale; how made; story; evidence of quality and how sure; what went wrong; what they would change. SR means self-reported by the maker. Every source is named with its date; "nf" means not found. Unreached: the lappemic/awesome-ai-built-games repository itself (GitHub refused this session; its ecosyste.ms mirror of 23 Nov was read instead), gamestar.de's Codex Mortis review, Tripo's, Meshy's, Rodin's and ElevenLabs' own terms pages, every X post (only as quoted by articles), every video. The helpers' full notes are kept in notes/.

## Large or ambitious, mostly AI

- Age of Dragon: Mike Houser (says he cannot code; works for Tesana, a vendor in his own pipeline; reportedly quit a job two months before); X thread about 10 Nov 2026; Claude Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5, three.js in the browser, Blender driven by Claude for rigging, Tripo for models, ElevenLabs via fal for voice, effects and music; 1 person; about 4 days (SR); US$1,400 and one top-tier plan (SR), 21 billion+ tokens and 85,000+ tool calls (SR); 144 km2 island, 79 places, 34 quests, 20 bosses, 482 models, 694 animations, 544 voiced lines, about 80,000 lines (all SR); every landmark began as a painting, then a place; one shared creature skeleton with a retimed motion library; loop of write, run, read the console, fix; story nf; evidence weak: a 2-minute flyby video, no public build confirmed by the article (explainx.ai, 11 Nov 2026); the article warns of voice tone drift with no voice sheet, unchecked asset licences, a flyby that cannot show combat feel or bugs; would change nf (the article advises one region, five quests, one boss first).
- Caliber, first called GT-Caliber: Ziwen Xu (Hyperecho founder); from 10 Jun 2026; a Claude Max 20x plan, agents in a loop (coding tool not named), Godot, then Unreal 5 on day 7, Unreal 5.8's MCP on day 9; 1 person plus agents, public repo taking community requests; cost nf; city scale; day 2 the agents built Los Angeles instead of the asked-for Miami, day 8 every agent opened the game at once for screenshots and crashed the machine, people lost their walk animations; at full city scale the frame rate fell to about 1 a second; the GTA-based build was torn down in August and rebuilt as an original game (Wccftech, 19 to 25 Jun 2026; TweakTown, 18 Jun 2026, says he ended on Unity, unresolved; GTA BOOM, 3 Sep 2026); no playable build; his words: the 5.8 MCP "actually slows the build down... The loop runs better without it" (Wccftech).
- World of ClaudeCraft: Levy Street, New Zealand; built over a weekend, press 22 Jun 2026; Claude Fable 5, TypeScript, three.js, WebSockets, PostgreSQL; small team, size nf; cost and lines nf; 9 classes, 3 zones, about 90 quests, dungeons, a 10-player raid by v0.12; open source; free and bought asset packs plus procedural terrain, so not all AI (PC Games Hardware, 26 Jun 2026); over 30,000 active players (SR via PCGH); press covered it, Winfuture called the code partly unstructured (cited by Notebookcheck, 24 Jun 2026); the raid's loot failed for half the players because a party limit of 5 stayed hard-coded from dungeon code, with no error shown; lessons: scope, not speed, is the limit; diagnosing bugs is still slow; regular content drops beat one launch (dev.to, 23 Jun, year inferred as 2026).
- Codex Mortis: GROLAF, solo, publisher Crunchfest; first commit Jul 2025, demo 9 Dec 2025, Early Access 26 Mar 2026, 1.0 24 Sep 2026; Claude Code (mostly Opus 4.1 and 4.5 per TweakTown, undated, from the developer's Reddit post), GPT for art, TypeScript, PIXI.js, bitECS, Electron; its own text-readable engine because ready-made editors suited the agent badly; 14 months, about 3,450 commits, 350,000+ lines (SR, press release 24 Sep 2026); cost nf; 200 spells, 31 heroes, 4-player co-op; a bullet-hell roguelite, story nf; Steam 81% of 98 reviews, Very Positive (store page read 23 Nov 2026: sure, but a small count); early reaction divisive over inconsistent art and an AI-look promotional video (TweakTown, undated; Gigazine, 15 Dec 2025); 20+ patches in Early Access from player reports; lesson: "define the task, delegate it, review the result"; a blunt instruction file helped.
- Firefield: Naoki Fujinaga (Robe of Fire), solo, no coding background; Early Access 8 May 2026; tools nf; about 3 months (Der Standard, about 21 May 2026, read in part); cost nf; Diablo-like, 8 dungeon floors; Steam 70% of 24 reviews, Mostly Positive (store page read 23 Nov 2026, small count); Steam disclosure says most content is AI-made and every piece was reviewed by the developer; what went wrong nf.
- 100 Bodies in the Grass: a Reddit user, Nov 2026; Godot, Claude Code, Meshy for first models, Blender with Claude for small fixes, Steam networking; 1 person; time and cost nf; 4-player co-op in a three-act 1287 setting; Steam page, no release date; animation was Claude's weak point (done by hand or bought), complex Blender work unreliable, game feel tuned by hand; architecture stayed stable (Shazoo, 11 Nov 2026, SR).
- The fishing game: two maybe-same projects. Week 3 post by u/RUSuper (18 Aug 2026 via explainx.ai): Godot 4.7 with Godot MCP, Blender MCP, Claude Desktop then Claude Code, ChatGPT for concept art, Rodin tried and dropped for cleanup cost, US$200 plan that hit its limit; separate sessions for art direction, geometry, integration and a dispatcher; tests are screenshots from the game camera; critics with written criteria, ship at 8 of 10 with blind A/B; every decision logged as USER or AI; islands approved from above looked like pancakes from the player's camera; lessons: never ask the model whether an image is good, test from the real camera, write the critics' criteria first, build the interface last. Shazoo, 11 Sep 2026: a playable build at week 6 sent by file transfer, Tripo for characters, no store page; evidence weak.
- 100,000-line Unreal game: a solo developer on Apple's developer forums (thread 834043, date nf); about 2 months; Unreal C++, "various AI models", thousands of AI 3D and interface assets; a mobile match-3 puzzle in closed TestFlight, not an open world; the developer did the core maths, pacing and boss design; evidence none beyond the post.
- Battle Drones: Simon Willison; Jul to Oct 2026; Fable 5, then Opus 5.5 in Claude Code orchestrating subagents, OpenAI images for sprites; 1 person who read almost no code; 12 rounds, 321 commits; a 2 to 4 player RTS with replays and 7 maps; 1,000+ simulated playtests to rebalance, 1,200 AI-against-AI matches cut undecided games from 14% to 0.9%; cross-browser sine and cosine differences desynced replays; open source Apache 2.0 (simonwillison.net, 20 and 28 Oct 2026, SR but open). His other small games (a 15-minute story RPG, a farm game) took at most about 4 hours of his time each; lesson: playtesting still needed, story and state bugs (a person in the wrong place) persist.
- SpaceMolt: Ian Langworth; Feb 2026; Claude Code; about 59,000 lines of Go and 33,000 of YAML over a weekend (SR); a game played by AI agents; bug reports researched, fixed and deployed by the agent and he has not read the code (Decrypt, 10 Feb 2026).
- Era Online: Kyle Easterly; talk 7 May 2026; Claude Code explored a 1999 VB6 MMORPG, built its own tools, then ported it to a modern stack; lesson: build context up front (Code with Claude talk abstract, 7 May 2026; details nf).
- Grumbulus: Grigory Sapunov and his son; Mar 2026; Claude, vanilla JavaScript; 2 people, about 2 days; about 15,000 lines; parallel agents' files did not fit each other; bosses designed but never wired in, 15 behaviour flags never read, one sprite in the wrong coordinate space after six tries; lessons: play early, describe the problem not the fix, integration audits, analytics from day one (gonzoml.substack.com, 25 Mar 2026).
- The Librarian 2: a YouTuber; Aug 2026; Claude Code about 90 minutes from a spoken prompt, then GPT Codex for fixes and balance, a shared markdown design file; about 10,400 lines; reversed controls, a soft lock and untested balance found by the human (MindStudio, 13 Aug 2026).
- Project Aegis: Daniel Steegmuller; Mar 2026; Claude Code (Opus) with Unity MCP, decision records, a planner and challenger debate, a quality-gate skill; 1 person, 20 to 22 hours over 20 days, 309 sessions; 42,628 lines, 589 tests; a mobile tower-defence game; Claude silently dropped features and fixed symptoms; context rot on large work packages; save state missed because the spec missed it; lessons: diff the build against the plan, automated gates, one phase per session, Opus over Sonnet for scope discipline (devlog, 17 Mar 2026, SR).
- Robot Battle rebuild: author per the URL handle tgvashworth; Feb 2026; Opus 4.6 agent teams, 77 subagents, Docker sandbox, a browser-tester agent; about 11,000 lines and 647 tests; property tests found a bug hand tests missed, running the game found more; lessons: goals beat over-specification, interfaces before code, always-working milestones (substack, 11 Feb 2026).
- Spell Cascade: developer nf; Feb 2026 (post's year not shown); a 5-agent Claude Code team on Opus 4.6; 9 of 17 tasks done, 0 sales; idle blocked agents, duplicated context, cost, designer work undone by later builder changes (dev.to, 19 Feb 2026).
- Failed metroidvania: lpshred; Godot forum post-mortem, 23 Apr 2026; Gemini, Replit, Cursor, Cline, Godot and GitHub MCPs; about 40 hours; combat broke, fixes broke more, the player script grew too large, abandoned; lessons: early success hid a weak base; agents lost track beyond 2 or 3 scripts; adversarial AI review helped.
- RTS in Godot: Lucca Sanwald; Jun 2025; Claude Code on a US$20 plan; markdown design files as truth; conflicting context broke the controller; later scrapped after losing control of the code (dev.to, 22 Jun 2025, and a commenter).
- Shin KoiKoi: morinaga; May 2026; Claude with Godot 4.6 C#, about 45 Gemini and Midjourney images; 3 weeks of design, 2 days of building; 184 tests; export failures found late on two platforms; lesson: automate screenshots and smoke-test every platform early (dev.to; game released 2 May 2026).

## Tools and studies others used

- Claude Code Game Studios (Donchitos; MIT): 49 agents, 74 skills; its own test built one brief at four process levels: 1 document before code against 30; all ran, and two blind reviewers and a human playtest ranked the 30-document build last: the extra documents "bought traceability, not a better game" (Developers Digest, 31 Oct 2026, reporting the README; the author's own single test).
- GameCraft-Bench (CUHK Shenzhen, Tencent Hunyuan, NUS, SJTU; arXiv 2606.17861; AI Weekly, 17 Jun 2026): 140 Godot games from written specs, judged by replayed play; best, Claude Code with Opus 4.7, 41.46% score; core mechanics 55%, content depth 39%, art and presentation 37%.
- TITAN (arXiv 2509.22170, 26 Sep 2025): a GPT-4o playtest agent on two commercial MMORPGs found 15 bugs against human testers' 5, with 30% false alarms.
- Blender MCP (ahujasid; MIT; runs any Python, unauthenticated local socket); Unreal-MCP (IvanMurzak; Apache-2.0; Unreal 5.5+, beta); TRELLIS.2 (Microsoft; MIT; 24 GB card); Hunyuan3D (Tencent community licence excludes the UK, EU and South Korea); Aura Autopilot (Ramen VR; open-source game-playing test agent for Unreal, Unity, Godot; announced 30 Oct 2026; licence not stated); Mixamo, Meshy, Tripo, DeepMotion terms from a third-party guide only (Cinevva, Sep 2026).
