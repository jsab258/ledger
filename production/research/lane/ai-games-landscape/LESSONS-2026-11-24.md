# What other AI-built games teach LEDGER

23 Nov 2026, landscape job (claude-opus-5-5). Evidence: PROJECTS.md, about 25 projects, mostly self-reported.

## The answer first

Nobody has shown a large, story-first 3D game made mostly by AI that players rate highly. The well-rated AI-built games on Steam are small 2D action games (Codex Mortis, 81% of 98 reviews, after 14 months and 20+ patches; Firefield, 70% of 24). The big open-world claims have no playable proof: Age of Dragon is a flyby video; Caliber's agent-built city fell to about 1 frame a second and was torn down. LEDGER is ahead of the evidence; these lessons come from smaller games.

## (a) Is LEDGER's approach right?

Right in its parts; the timing and the story are the risks.
- Supported: one design document as the truth; hidden tests and a fresh reviewer each round (Aegis, Battle Drones); one builder on the shared files (parallel builders broke Grumbulus and Spell Cascade); art fixed before building (Age of Dragon painted every landmark first); the browser first; the one-shot then loops (his ruling, 18 to 19 Nov).
- Slow: the start waits on a complete blueprint (424 parts, 371 open gaps; plan 7 Dec). One weak signal says extra documents do not make a better game (Claude Code Game Studios' own single test ranked its 30-document build below its 1-document build). Against it: Shin KoiKoi designed for three weeks first. Measuring is not wrong; letting open gaps block the first build is.
- Weakest in all the evidence, and LEDGER's centre: story, talk and feel. Willison's story bugs persisted; Where Winds Meet's chatbot characters read hollow; Whispers from the Star works with one live character; 100 Bodies did animation and feel by hand; the best agent on a 140-game test scored 39% on content depth, 37% on art. Loops should spend most effort here.
- Unpriced: the late Unreal move. Caliber's engine switch cost animation and speed, and agents opening the engine together crashed the machine.

## (b) Copy now, best first

1. A feature census after every loop: every part's test re-run and the build checked against the plan, so dropped features show (Aegis, ClaudeCraft's raid loot, Grumbulus' bosses). Partly exists; cost unmeasured.
2. Each loop, a reviewer reads a played evening's transcript against the casting sheets, not only the tests. Cost unpriced.
3. Many scripted playthroughs per loop: they catch soft locks and broken state, not quality (TITAN: three times human testers' bugs, 30% false alarms). Cost unpriced.
4. A voice sheet per character before mass voice lines (Age of Dragon's drift).
5. Outside players on the first playable (Codex Mortis climbed by patching from them); the friends playtest plan exists.
None of these moves the dates; question 1 would.

## (c) Avoid

Midway engine switches; agents launching the engine together; giant scripts; many-agent hierarchies; AI-look trailers; trusting self-reported scale; Hunyuan3D (banned); Blender's agent link left open beside his files.

## (d) Use rather than make

Blender MCP (MIT) for agent-driven Blender work (no garment tool found, so Kiln stays); TRELLIS.2 (MIT) for props; Unreal-MCP (Apache-2.0) at the Unreal stage, tried against plain scripts; Aura Autopilot (licence unstated) as the Unreal tester; GameCraft-Bench's replayed-play scoring for loops.
