# Reference mechanics: five games against LEDGER's design

Dated 20 November 2026, written the night of 19 November.

What it is: the full list of gameplay mechanics of GTA V, Sleeping Dogs, Mafia: Definitive Edition, Kingdom Come: Deliverance II (KCD2) and Red Dead Redemption 2 (RDR2), single-player only, taken from their public wikis, guides, manuals and reviews, for LEDGER's parts tree. Each mechanic is marked against the whole-game design (production/lane/synthesis/DESIGN.md, 18 November).

How it was made: Sonnet helpers each extracted one game from pages they found by web search, one line per mechanic with its source, sorted into ten groups. An Opus pass then merged the five raw lists into one, joining the same mechanic across games into a single line, and marked each line against DESIGN.md, reading canon.md and RULINGS.md alongside. Where canon of 19 November is newer than the design (fares few and chosen, no taxi-sim parts) the mark follows canon. Nothing here was fetched afresh; every link is one a helper reached.

How to read a line: an id such as M1.5 (group 1, line 5), the name, what it does, then "Games:" with each game and its source links, then after a bar the mark. The mark is one of:
- covered: DESIGN names the mechanic or its LEDGER equivalent, with the section number.
- partly: DESIGN touches it or hints at it, and says what is missing.
- not covered: a gameplay gap DESIGN does not address.
- not for LEDGER: ruled out by canon's content rule, the setting or period, a cut in DESIGN 18 or one of his rulings, or held to canon's "smallest budget" for anything that feeds neither the verbs nor the town's memory (said so in the mark; that is not a ban).

Limits:
- Outside study only, as his rule of 17 Nov requires: no game files, no footage downloaded. Guides vary in quality and some lines are loose paraphrases of the source.
- Pages not reached, from each raw file's "Not reached" section:
  - GTA V: the Gamepressure control pages list categories only, so exact bindings were not read; no official manual page was reached; Fandom GTA V pages other than the iFruit phone page (which was fetched and is cited) and IGN and GameFAQs guides were not fetched in the time box.
  - Sleeping Dogs: three Prima guide pages (going above the law; street cred, the reputation system; welcome to Hong Kong) and four TrueAchievements walkthrough pages were not reached; Fandom never surfaced in search; Wikipedia was read only to its 100,000th character.
  - Mafia DE: Wikipedia was refused by the permission system; Fandom, IGN and GameFAQs did not appear in search, so editions, add-ons and save detail were not read; the platform line rests on a search result alone.
  - KCD2: Fandom and IGN pages were not returned or not fetched; the Fextralife crime page was cut off before stocks, recognition and disguise; the Gamepressure maps, a game8 add-on page and a PC Gamer schnapps article were found but not fetched.
  - RDR2: the Fandom Red Dead wiki and Fextralife were seen in search only; no Rockstar page appeared.
- The raw files are kept in production/research/lane/refmech-2026-11-20/ (GTA-V.md, SLEEPING-DOGS.md, MAFIA-DE.md, KCD2.md, RDR2.md). FOOTAGE.md in the same folder is a separate study and was not used here.
- Lines the raw files tag as alcohol or gambling are listed only so they can be marked out; none is a proposal.

- The gaps are re-ranked: hand verbs first, then pickpocketing, counter and parry; carrying and searching come before clocks and bells; the horn sits beside greet and needle; photo mode is added for his 23 Sep ruling.
- Limits: the iFruit page is the one Fandom GTA V page that was fetched. The KCD2 source for hunting (M1.55) is its horse wiki page, a weak source, as the line now says.

## Gaps the parts tree should take

These are the not-covered and partly-covered mechanics that matter most for LEDGER, ranked against his rulings: verbs first; KCD2 as the bar for immersion and finish; GTA's hands always busy; the town's memory as the engine under them; a car that hurts and never kills; Tom who cannot die; guns rare and late; fares few and chosen. The verbs Tom does with his hands come first. Block, dodge, stamina, strings, the warning tell, grapple, robbing a man who is down, healing and stepping into a fight are covered by the melee sheet and the combat design of 12 Oct (see the note under the header), so they have left this list.

1. M1.5 Sneak or crouch: break-ins, back ways and the landing at night all turn on noise. A quiet walk puts the sound rule of DESIGN 2 in the player's hands.
2. M1.110 Call out to stop someone: the witness's walk is the town's timer (DESIGN 2). Tom needs a verb that stops a walker before he can talk him round, pay him or threaten him.
3. M1.6 Climb fences, walls and crates: chases run through alleys and yards, and Mickey's yard has two escapes. A context climb over a wall keeps the no-jump ruling and gives the chase its shape.
4. M1.16 Search drawers, cabinets and wardrobes: searches are one of the hands jobs in the hour budget, and a break-in is felt in the hands. How a room is searched is unwritten, and KCD2 is the bar.
5. M1.80 Steal a parked car: the coat buys doubt about the face and none about the car (DESIGN 2). A car that is not the firm's is the obvious counter-move, and the design says nothing about it.
6. M2.69 Talk down or pay off the law: canon makes the police corruptible as individuals, yet a constable's challenge has only stand or run. A word, or a note offered, is the missing third answer.
7. M1.97 Pickpocketing: a hands verb done in full view of the town's eyes, where the head that turns (DESIGN 2) is the whole game.
8. M1.28 Counter: the melee sheet sets block and dodge but no counter. Sleeping Dogs, Mafia and KCD2 all build their melee skill on one, and the sheet itself names Sleeping Dogs' counters as something to take.
9. M1.27 Perfect block or parry: a timed answer inside the C5 window would reward reading the wind-up, which ER-FIGHT-07's "skill beats mashing" needs.
10. M2.55 Enemy fighting types: authored fights must differ (a boxer who blocks, a grappler, a big man, a man with a knife), or players punish the same loop. That is DESIGN 18's own reason for cutting random fights.
11. M1.74 Handbrake turn: getaways and tails need it, and fun-first driving as non-sim open-world games do it (ruling 18 Nov) expects it.
12. M1.98 Throw a stone to distract: the town's ears are the engine. A thrown noise lets the player use the sound rule against a watcher.
13. M1.46 Knock out from behind or while asleep: a night watchman on a break-in needs a quiet answer that does not kill, so killing stays rare.
14. M1.49 Disarm: guns come only through people, "taken" among them (DESIGN 7). Taking a knife or a bat off a man is the fists-first way.
15. M1.92 Hold up a shop or a person: robbery sits in the arrest table (twelve hours) with no verb behind it.
16. M2.56 Protection shakedowns: rounds open at rung 3, and the empire is run on the street through people. The shakedown itself is a hands verb not yet written.
17. M1.12 Carry and drop a body or a person: moving or hiding a body is an owed verb. Tom doing it himself is the hands version.
18. M1.109 Greet or needle a passer-by: this is GTA's hands-always-busy bar. Cheap social verbs fill the walk and let the rise be heard in greetings by regard.
19. M1.75 Horn: the cab's own greet and needle. It is a hello on the rank, a warning or a provocation, and a noise the town hears.
20. M2.28 Followed person reacts: tails are the town's eyes in reverse (DESIGN 4). Being made is the other side, and it is what makes a tail tense.
21. M2.8 Search after losing sight: a chase now ends at 7 s or 70 m unseen. What the constable does next keeps "the law near" a live tension source.
22. M1.76 Look back while driving: the tail wrinkle (a car in the mirror) needs a way to look.
23. M3.6 Planning board after scouting: tails and windows already gather what a plan needs, and the board exists. Laying out a big job before the night ties them together.
24. M4.21 Clocks show the time: windows, appointments and "wait until" all need the hour, and there is no timer on screen. Clocks in the street carry it.
25. M4.22 Bells near their buildings: the chapel's bell can tell the hour by ear. The school stays closed and silent (canon).
26. M5.1 and M5.2 Music radio and broadcasts: period sound is half of 1990 by ear, canon still owes the pirate radio station, and local news is one more way the town's talk surfaces.
27. M4.12 Readable letters and newspapers: the street's version of the fire as printed fact, Alison's paper, and a deed coming back in print.
28. M3.28 Spend time with a friend: tension source 9 (the two lives) needs a day life worth keeping beyond Ada's one tea.
29. M2.16 Town lockdown after a crime spree: the town should harden after a burst of deeds below the gun dial, not only at War.
30. M6.31 Haggling with a price slider: Tom meets the fence at minute 19 of the first hour, and how a haggle plays without a meter is open.
31. M7.31 Load slows you: the load is a tension source (DESIGN 3), and a holdall should be felt in the walk.
32. M2.83 Masks and face cover: the night coat is the only disguise. A scarf or a pulled-down hat is the period answer, and a give-away of its own.
33. M1.101 Carry a light at night: a hand torch on a break-in lights the way and shows Tom to every window, which puts the light rule in his hands.
34. M1.78 Ram another vehicle: car against car in a chase is undefined, while a car must never kill.
35. M2.41, M2.44 and M1.3 Bodies in water, cars in water, swimming: a port town with a quay edge needs rules for what goes into the harbour, Tom included.
36. M9.2 and M5.32 Photo mode and its camera tools: his ruling of 23 Sep puts "optional presentation (photo mode, credits, after the ending)" in the genre's floor, and DESIGN does not carry it.
37. M9.4 and M9.11 Key remapping, toggles and tap assist: basic PC access for a full release, beside DESIGN 17's keyboard-and-pad rule.
38. M8.13 Pop-in and draw distance: KCD2 is the finish bar, and reviews faulted both Sleeping Dogs and Mafia for pop-in. DESIGN 17 has a frame-rate rule but none for pop-in.
39. M1.25 Tackle: a foot chase that Tom wins has to end somehow, and a tackle is the hands answer.
40. M1.94 Safe cracking: a safe in a break-in should be one more lock with several keys (DESIGN 4), not a minigame.
