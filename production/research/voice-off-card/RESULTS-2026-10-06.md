# The voice off the card: results (6 October 2026)

Proof P2 (production/research/pre-production/3-PROOFS.md). The method and its sources are in NOTE.md beside this file. Everything here was measured on Jafar's PC (Ryzen 5 5600X, RX 6700) on 6 October by a bounded helper session.

## The answer

**Neither approach passes.** The voice's own share is far over 1.0 s even with nothing else running. On this processor, Nano's own work, at its approved settings, takes longer than the sentence it is making. Beside the game, A and B take 4.18 s and 3.36 s, against today's 3.66 s.

On the frame lines both processor approaches pass, and they spare the game more than today's voice does:

- They cost 0.4 to 0.5 ms of median frame while speaking, against today's 1.0 ms. Today's voice also adds 0.8 ms of the card's time.
- Their slowest 1% is 17 to 20 ms, against today's 21 ms.
- They put nothing on the card.

Only the delay fails. The PC was shared with the production session's Blender and Unreal work during the runs beside the game; what overlapped which run is named below.

## What was built (beside today's voice, which is unchanged)

- **tools/voice-live/off_card.py.** The whole voice on the processor through ONNX Runtime 1.24.4. It uses the same model, voices, sampler (in the same order) and seeds as today; only where the arithmetic is done, and at what precision, changes. It has four parts:
  - **The token loop:** Nano's one-token step (the 2 October graph) in 8 bits, after a prefill by the library's model. The voice's fixed prefix is kept once per voice (exact to 6e-6; it saves about 0.25 s a line).
  - **The decoder's flow:** the conformer encoder and the two meanflow steps, exported here as one graph (nano-flow.onnx, 482 MB). It matches the library to 0.0001% of the spectrogram's range. The 8-bit copy (nano-flow-int8.onnx, 433 MB) has 8-bit MatMuls, per channel with reduce_range; the convolutions stay at 32 bits.
  - **The vocoder (HiFT) and the watermark (Perth):** the library's own, on the processor.
  - **Settings:** 4 threads, spinning off, and the voice's own process kept to logical processors 8 to 11. The game is not touched.
  - The graphs are on F:/LedgerTools/voice-graphs/nano, a folder retention already protects.
- **tools/voice-live/voice-server-off-card.py.** Today's one-line protocol, so the game could be pointed at it with -VoicePython= and -VoiceScript= without a change to the game. Settings are environment variables (its header).
- **tools/voice-live/off_card_bench.py.** The measurement, off the game and beside it (its header says exactly how).
- **The two approaches:**
  - **A:** the 8-bit token step and the 8-bit flow, reading the voice's full 10 s reference.
  - **B:** the same 8-bit token step with the full-precision flow, reading only the last 4 s (100 tokens) of the reference.
  - A and B make the same sound tokens for each line (same graph, same seeds), so they differ only in the decoder.

## Off the game: the voice's share, 24 of the cast's lines

**How.** The 24 spoken lines of content/dialogue/crime-witness-v1.json were handed one at a time to each voice server, across Ron's, Lena's and Darren's voices (rocco, lena, sam), with nothing else running. The clock ran from handing the line to the server's line naming the first piece's sound file. The first piece is the first sentence: median 2.1 s of speech, 51 sound tokens. These are the cast's real lines, longer than the short bench lines of 1 to 2 October.

| | Median | 90% within | Slowest | Fastest | Work per second of first piece | Processor busy while speaking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today's voice (card) | 2.23 s | 3.21 | 3.58 | 1.32 | 0.97 s | 5.35 logical processors |
| A (pinned to 8–11) | 3.01 s | 3.66 | 4.14 | 2.20 | 1.34 s | 3.65 of its 4 |
| A, not pinned | 3.13 s | 4.07 | 4.35 | 2.36 | | |
| B (pinned to 8–11) | 2.42 s | 3.25 | 3.51 | 1.77 | 1.11 s | not recorded (see below) |

Medians per stage for the first piece:

| | Tokens | Flow | Vocoder |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.91 s | 1.49 s | 0.59 s |
| B | 0.97 s | 0.75 s | 0.65 s |

**Where the time goes on the processor:**

- **The flow works on the voice's whole reference every line.** The reference is 250 tokens, 500 frames, against a typical first sentence's 100 frames. With the reference at 10 s, about 87% of the decoder's work is the reference.
- **8 bits gains little on this processor:**
  - **The flow:** 1.06 s against 1.22 s at 32 bits and 1.53 s in the library, on short lines.
  - **The reason:** Zen 3 has no VNNI. ONNX Runtime's docs warn of exactly this (NOTE.md).
- **B's shorter reference halves the flow, but the token loop and the vocoder stay.** Tokens run at about 50 a second, against the 25 a second the speech needs. The vocoder takes 0.6 s for 2 s of speech.

**Streaming would not rescue them.** Every approach, even idle, does more than a second of work per second of first piece (today's voice on the card 0.97 s, A 1.34 s, B 1.11 s). A piece played early would run dry before the next was made, so a first sound with no gap after it comes no sooner.

**Processor-use reading.** The first A run and the B run read 0 for processor use. The voice environment's python.exe is a launcher, and the work runs in its child process. This was fixed before the pinned A run and the game runs, which count the child.

## Beside the game

**How.** The packaged game (F:/LedgerTools/played-game, built 6 October) stood in the street exactly as the build machine times it (tools/slice-perf.py's step):

- -LedgerSlice, 3440 by 1440 drawn at half, uncapped, off screen;
- the engine's own CSV profiler;
- its own voice off (-NoVoice).

The voice server under test was started first and readied. Then, inside one capture: 40 s to settle, 25 s quiet, six lines, 25 s quiet, six lines, quiet to the end.

- The lines were the first 12 cast lines, the same for every voice. Their first pieces run 2.4 s of speech at the median, a little longer than the 24 lines' 2.1 s.
- Each frame was placed in time by adding up frame times from the moment the profile file appeared. Frames within a second of a change were left out.
- "Worse" compares each speaking stretch's median frame with the quiet stretches' median frames in the same run.

**A clean window.** The PC was shared with the production session all morning.

- The script waited, checking every two minutes as the brief asks, and while each run went it noted every 5 s any other graphics work: Blender, an Unreal editor or commandlet, the build machine.
- The first full-length A run (24 lines, 26,000 frames) had the production session's editor rendering vignettes for 510 of its 704 s. Its slowest 1% reached 262 to 287 ms in quiet and speaking stretches alike. It is kept (F:/LedgerTools/tmp/voice-off-card/A-game) but not used, except its first 115 s: quiet 13.88 ms median, speaking 14.36 ms, slowest 1% 17.4 ms, nothing over 25 ms.
- The runs below are the shorter ones (12 lines, 14,000 frames, about 3.5 minutes).

| | Quiet: frame median | Speaking: frame median | Worse by | Slowest 1% speaking | Frames over 25 ms speaking | Voice's share, median (90%, slowest) | Voice's processor use | Other work seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 13.82, 13.85 ms | 14.35, 14.35 ms | **+0.5 ms** | 17.7, 20.1 ms | 0, 7 | **4.18 s** (5.60, 6.48) | 2.4 logical processors | a Blender background job during the second speaking stretch |

**Reading:**

- **Neither approach comes near 1.0 s beside the game.** In the first speaking stretch the first six lines took 3.99 s median for A (nothing else running) and 3.01 s for B (two short Blender jobs).
- **The token loop is the floor.** Beside the game it runs at 36 to 40 tokens a second, against 50 idle; a typical first sentence of 58 tokens takes 1.45 to 1.6 s on its own. The flow then took 2.05 s (A) or 0.97 s (B) more, and the vocoder 0.8 to 0.9 s.
- **Today's voice beside the game measured 3.66 s**, the same median as 2 October's (on longer lines, at 3440 by 1440 drawn at half). It is the only one of the three that costs the card: its card time rose by 0.8 ms while it spoke, and the frame median sat right on the 1 ms line.
- **The frame lines pass for both processor approaches.** Speaking costs the game 0.4 to 0.5 ms of median frame. The slowest 1% stays at 17 to 20 ms, under 25, with the voice kept to logical processors 8 to 11 and the game's own threads free to use them too.
- The game's card memory read 4.2 to 4.3 GB in every run, with nothing of the voice on the card.
- **A clean rerun of today's voice was tried from 12:17 and stopped at 12:39.** The production session's editor, Blender and then a new build-machine job kept the PC busy, and today's voice was holding the card while the build machine was about to time its own frames.
- **To repeat all three on a free PC:** `python tools/voice-live/off_card_bench.py --engine today|A|B --lines 12 --game --frames 14000` (system Python, which has psutil). Each run takes about five minutes, and the script waits for other graphics work itself.

## Quality

**Not judged by ear.** This session cannot hear. A listening set is kept for that, on F: and not in git, in F:/LedgerTools/tmp/voice-off-card/listen/ (seven-day scratch). It holds 18 files: six lines, each in today's voice, A and B, named line-engine.wav:

- Ron's lines 0 and 9;
- Lena's lines 4 and 10;
- Darren's lines 8 and 14.

Every first piece of every run is beside it, in the run folders.

**The project's voice screen** (tools/voice-live/take_gate.py: CommonAccent's accent check, Whisper's word check, likeness to the character's own clip) on all 72 first pieces:

| | Passed | Ron: likeness median, passed | Lena | Darren | Read as American |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today's voice | 17 of 24 | 0.57, 6/8 | 0.69, 7/8 | 0.51, 4/8 | 2 |
| A (8-bit decoder) | 15 of 24 | 0.55, 7/8 | 0.71, 6/8 | **0.44, 2/8** | 3 |
| B (lighter decoder) | 18 of 24 | 0.53, 7/8 | 0.70, 5/8 | 0.49, 6/8 | 3 |

- **A changes Darren's voice.** A and B make the same tokens and differ only in the decoder. Darren's likeness to his clip falls from 0.51 (today) to 0.44 with the 8-bit decoder, and four of his eight lines fail on likeness that pass in B.
  - Spectrograms on the same tokens and noise: the 8-bit flow differs from the exact one by 3.7 to 4.8% of the range on average. A fresh take with different noise differs by 5.6 to 6.0%.
  - It is consistently darker and quieter: the mean log level is down 0.36 to 0.48, where the fresh take moved +0.18 to +0.25.
  - The research found the same: Chatterbox's decoder is its most quantisation-sensitive part (NOTE.md, source 24).
- **B keeps likeness close to today's.** Its spectrograms differ by 3.3 to 5.7% and are also a little darker (−0.30 to −0.58). Whether the shorter reference changes how each voice sounds is for the ear.
- **The American drift is Nano's own.**
  - Ron's line 9 reads American in all three, today's included. Darren's line 20 reads American in all three.
  - The off-card runs add Darren's line 14, from the 8-bit token step's different tokens.
  - Two against three of 24 is not enough to say the 8-bit step drifts more, but it is not better.
  - FINDINGS already lists Nano's drift.

## What is left

- **The decision is a money ruling, as the proof says when it fails.** The paid streaming voice (about $0.28 an hour of play), or keep today's delay. Neither processor route reaches 1.0 s; the card route is today's.
- **Not tried, each changing the voice:**
  - one meanflow step instead of two (Resemble's card says the decoder was distilled to one step);
  - a reference shorter than 4 s;
  - a faster vocoder graph (HiFT in ONNX).
  - Together, by the stage times above, they might bring the processor's work to about real time idle. They cannot reach 1.0 s beside the game while the token loop alone takes about 1 s idle and 1.5 s beside the game for a typical first sentence.
