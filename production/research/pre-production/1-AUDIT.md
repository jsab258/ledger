## 1.1 Why it matters here

Pre-production ends at a gate. Before it, a team has a playable proof that the game is worth making, and it knows what each kind of asset costs to make. Cerny's "Method" ends pre-production in a "publishable first playable" and sets the idea aside if it does not excite players [W, Mark Cerny]. Clinton Keith's version is to make a few production-quality assets of each type, measure the effort, and scale up. Each asset type moves into production separately, once its pipeline is proven [SS, "Production Debt", 2008; the blog is UNREACHED]. When the gate is passed without a proven core or proven tools, the real pre-production happens inside production. *Mass Effect: Andromeda* built most of the game in its last 18 months, and *Anthem* in its last year [W, citing Kotaku; the Kotaku pages UNREACHED].

## 1.3 Reading

- **Has:** a narrative bible (item 18), an interface style guide, roles and an approval process.
- **Partly:** everything a studio uses to plan production: the gate, the design document, the art bible, the asset list and methods, the technical design, the performance budget, the pipeline, licensing, money, QA and release.
- **Not:** a schedule or a risk register.

The pattern is consistent. LEDGER settled *what it wants* early: canon, the bar, the rulings. It did not settle *what each thing costs to make and whether the method works*, which is the half of pre-production that prevents mid-production surprises. Sections 2 and 3 apply that missing half to every area.
