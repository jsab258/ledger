# P1 completed packaged (7 October 2026)

PLAN.md, phase 1, item 1.4: "P1 completed packaged: day, night, walking, Windows memory, sustained speech." The 3 October profile (P1-RESULTS-2026-10-03.md) ran in the editor's game mode; this is the played copy, the packaged game of build a32eeeb.

## How

- **The game.** The played copy (F:\LedgerTools\played-game), started through its own launcher, the real binary found by its path and measured (tools/p1-packaged.ps1). Its -PerfHook puts the view at the scene file's cam_hook and records 1800 frames with the engine's CSV profiler, each pass's GPU time included; "walk" carries the view up the street at 1.4 m/s.
- **The conditions.** 3440 by 1440 drawn at 55%, uncapped, off screen. RX 6700, Ryzen 5 5600X, 32 GB.
- **The voice, speaking throughout.** The cast voice on the card (voice-server.py through tools/voice-live/voice_load.py) fed line after line for all three captures: 176 lines in 381 pieces, 719 s of speech made in 661 s (0.92 of real time), so it never fell behind.
- **Windows' own memory counters,** read every two seconds while each capture ran: the card's dedicated memory in all and per process, and each process's private memory and working set.
- **The reading.** tools/perf-hook.py against the ruling's line (median frame 16.7 ms, slowest 1% 33.3 ms) and the planning line (GPU median 14.0 ms). Its first reading took 44 to 68 of the 1800 frames: the engine writes the full list of columns after the frames when a statistic first appears part-way, and only the first list was read. Fixed and tested; the numbers below are all 1800 frames.
- **The record.** runs.jsonl in p1-packaged/ (the corrected lines marked "reread"); the frames at F:\LedgerTools\tmp\p1-packaged.

## What it says

**Standing at the hook the game misses the line by day and at night, with the voice speaking.** 59 frames a second by day, 51 at night; walking up the street, 71. The 3 October profile had the same view at 78 by day and 71 at night with the budget's picture (63 by day at Highest), the voice on the card each time. Two causes, both found in the numbers:

1. **The picture.** The played copy draws at Highest: its settings were saved on its first run, before the 3 October budget, whose High keeps Epic's reflections and lighting and leaves Highest's upscaler and post-processing (3.3 ms here against 1.5). A new copy's first launch starts at High (TitleScreen.cpp); this one never came down. Fixed for the next build: a copy whose saved picture is Highest and predates the budget comes down to High once (PictureBudgetOnce).
2. **The shop rooms' lights.** Shadow work went from 0.8 ms (3 October) to 5.2 ms by day and from 3.7 to 7.7 at night. Standing, two lights besides the sun redraw their shadows every frame by day (eleven at night), 800 more shadow draws; walking away from the shops, none. The shop rooms' ceiling tubes (3 October, after the profile) are lit by day too, cast shadows, reach 7 m, and every mesh in that reach is drawn into each one's shadow map every frame: the street's walls, which are merged street-long by material, and the people on the pavement. The engine's own test (LightSceneInfo.cpp) lets a light cast shadows from "cinematic" objects only; the rooms' tubes and lamps now do, the cinematic objects being the room's own walls, fittings and display (VignetteShot.cpp, RoomShadowsOwnOnly; -RoomShadowsAll for the old way).
