# P1: the hook camera, profiled (3 October 2026)

Jafar's order of 3 October: "P1: profile the hook camera, standing and walking, with the voice speaking, with Nanite and virtual shadows on and off, and tell me plainly what the proof view can add within 60 frames a second on this card, and what would make room: the voice on the processor, the settings, or both."

## How it was measured

- **The view.** The game's new -PerfHook=stand|walk (CrimeProbe.cpp) puts the view at the scene file's cam_hook in the played street, with its people, light and fog, and Tom hidden as on his page. "walk" carries the view up the street at 1.4 m/s from there. Once the street has settled, the engine's own CSV profiler records 1800 frames, each pass's GPU time included (-csvGpuStats).
- **The reading.** tools/perf-hook.py reads the profile against the ruling's line (median frame 16.7 ms, slowest 1% 33.3 ms) and the review's planning line (GPU median 14.0 ms, 4-BUDGETS.md 4.1).
- **The conditions.** 3440 by 1440, his monitor's size, drawn at 55% (the rung the game settles on) and upscaled. RX 6700, Ryzen 5 5600X.
- **The voice.** The game's own cast voice (voice-server.py, the three cast voices, warmed first) was fed line after line for the whole capture by tools/voice-live/voice_load.py, on the card, or on the processor where marked.
- **Where it ran.** In the editor's game mode, so Nanite could be switched on for the street's 118 meshes and compared like with like (then switched back off: the audit reads 0 on). This build's packaged slice at 50% measured within about half a millisecond of the same here (build machine, 3 October: frame 13.32 ms, GPU 11.43 ms; here at the hook, frame 13.81 ms, GPU 11.91 ms).
- **The record.** Every run's numbers are in p1-runs/. The frames are kept in F:/LedgerTools/renders/p1-hook.

## What it means

1. **Today, at Epic with the voice on the card, the proof view has 0.7 ms.** In practice that is nothing: the frame is already at 16.0 ms against the 16.7 line.
2. **The settings are the big lever, but not all of High.** High everywhere frees 5 ms, but Mickey's windows go black: the room behind the glass is lost (frames: epic-vs-high-crop.png), which step 2.6 forbids. Keeping reflections and lighting at Epic brings the windows back almost to Epic's (epic-vs-high-epicrefl-gi-crop.png). Nearly all the saving that is left is in the upscaler and post-processing, 3.3 ms down to 1.5. With that mix and the voice on the card, the proof view has 3.0 ms standing and 2.5 ms walking by day, and 1.4 ms at night.
3. **The voice on the processor** frees about 0.7 ms of the card (1 ms at Epic) and 2 ms of the frame. But on the processor today it makes speech at 1.38 times its length, against 0.77 on the card (voice_load, 3 October). A sentence would then take longer to be heard, so this waits on item 3, the 8-bit decoder test, which is exactly about the voice's speed on the processor.
4. **Nanite and virtual shadows make no room on this street.**
   - Nanite alone costs 0.7 ms more: its meshes are drawn twice into the cascaded shadows.
   - With virtual shadows it saves 0.4 ms against today.
   - Either way it takes 1.1 to 1.5 GB more of the card's memory, which the voice needs.
   - Both stay off. The asset plan's Nanite assumption gives way to meshes with their levels of detail, as 4-BUDGETS.md's tables are written.
5. **Walking costs about what standing does.** The slowest 1% stays under 33 ms in every run but one (21 to 28 ms).
6. **Night is the tight case:** the lamps' shadows take 3.7 ms. Night's dressing has to come out of 1.4 ms, or lamps that cast no shadow.

## The budgets the proof view resumes within (decided 3 October, DECISIONS.md)

- **The picture:** High with Epic reflections and lighting, at 55%. The game's own picture ladder is set to it first, when the proof view resumes. The voice stays on the card until item 3 proves it quick on the processor.
- **What the proof view may add:** **2.5 ms by day** (the walk, the binding case) and **1.4 ms at night**.
- **How the shares fit inside those 2.5 ms,** taking the review's order of value: building detail about 1.0, the rooms behind the windows about 0.8, the props and food about 0.7.
- **What does not fit unless something else pays:** the hill's added geometry, the cars, passers-by and a reserve (the review's 0.4, 0.5, 1.7 and 1.2).
- **What would pay for them:**
  - the voice on the processor once it is quick enough, about 0.7 ms;
  - drawing at 50%, about 1.4 ms, with a softer picture;
  - nothing else measured here.

## Not measured here

- A stress test of triangles (the street four times over).
- Memory reports (memreport) and Windows' own counters: the card memory above is the engine's own.
- The packaged game for every run: one rung was calibrated against the build machine instead.
- Night with the rooms lit.
