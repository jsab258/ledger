# Mickey's first: how studios build a room before dressing it

Research, 30 September 2026, by a separate research helper. (The helper wrote the detail beside this; the session that asked for it saved this summary from the helper's text and corrected one fact: Tom's body width.)

## What professionals do, in order

1. Write what the room is for: what happens there, who stands where, what the player does.
2. Gather references from many places: photographs, TV, plans, objects, other games.
3. Draw it from above with measurements, checked against the character and the camera.
4. Build it in the engine from plain grey blocks, coloured only to mark what can be used and where people stand.
5. Play it: every route, the camera in every corner, every conversation, every object. Then someone fresh plays it.
6. Change it and play again. Grey blocks are cheap to move; finished art is expensive to throw away.
7. Light it plainly, judged through the game's own camera.
8. Lock the layout. Only then does art replace the blocks and the room get dressed.

## Why one exact photograph is not needed

No studio in these sources waits for one. The Gone Home team built a lived-in 1990s house from web searches, libraries, old floor plans and a 1990 mail-order catalogue.

Photographs decide what things looked like. That matters when the room is dressed, and our rule still holds there. Where the counter goes comes from what happens in the room and from the camera.

We already hold enough for a layout:
- a 1980 minicab office;
- 1987 office photographs;
- a period phone and radio microphone;
- TV dispatch offices of the time.

## The real problem: the camera

Third-person games make rooms roomier than life. The Max Payne team reportedly built rooms at one and a half to two times real size, with real-sized furniture and wider doors.

Mickey's built shell is 6 by 8 metres, generous for a British shop, though a stair may take nearly a metre of its width. Tom's street camera sits over three metres behind him, so indoors it will bump walls almost everywhere. Tom is 68 centimetres wide, so the real front door leaves him about 11 centimetres a side.

Studios handle this in three ways:
- they bring the camera closer and higher indoors;
- they widen doors;
- they keep space between walls and where people stand.

The first layout must test this before anything else.

## What the first layout must be tested for

- Tom walks in from the street, reaches every room and the yard, and leaves by both yard exits without snagging.
- In every corner and doorway, the camera never shows a wall through the picture and never jumps.
- Talking to Sheila and to Ron frames both faces.
- The book of fares, the radio, the phone and the doors are easy to reach and to spot.
- The radio is heard from the waiting bench and the counter.
- The rank is visible from the window.
- The room reads clearly under plain light, through the game's own camera and exposure.

## The steps for us

1. A one-page brief from canon: half a day.
2. A reference board from several sources: a few hours.
3. A measured plan from above: half a day.
4. A grey blockout in Unreal, built by script, with a colour key: a day.
5. An indoor camera setting, tried at three distances: half a day.
6. Walk it, fix it, walk it again; then a fresh reviewer and the AI tester walk it: a day.
7. Lock the layout. Dressing follows, judged against the photographs.

About three to four days in all. That is my estimate, not a measured figure.

One canon question came up: nobody in canon works the radio. My recommendation: Mickey did, and the chair is empty now.

## What could not be verified

- Most design websites were closed to this helper, so much of this rests on search summaries rather than pages read: the Level Design Book, the conference talks and the design articles.
- The Max Payne room sizes come from summaries that do not quite agree.
- No shared colour code for blockouts was found; each studio sets its own.
- Little first-hand evidence was found on how shipped games handle tight rooms.
- No dated survey of British shop sizes was found. The shell's measurements are the project's own drawing and were not checked against the built street in the engine.
