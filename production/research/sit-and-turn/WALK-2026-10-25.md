## How the sample's foot lock decides "planted" (read from its graph and the engine's source)

- The sample's character graph turns each clip's contact curve into a foot speed: `contact = (1 - contact) * 100` (its RemapCurves node, exported to text).
- Its Foot Placement node, in its manual speed mode, plants a foot only below its plant setting's SpeedThreshold of 1 (`AnimNode_FootPlacement.cpp`, the plant test). So a foot is held only where its curve is above 0.99.
- In the gliding spans the curves run between 0 and 1: partly marked. Motion matching also blends clips, so a blended mark often sits just under 0.99.

## Try 1: mark a low, still foot as planted

`runs/contact_fix.py` sets each walking clip's contact curve to 1 wherever the ball is within 2.5 cm of its lowest and moving under 60 cm/s in the clip's own frame. This is the field's contact test (Epic's own graph mode uses 60 cm/s). Elsewhere it is left as it was.
- 7,919 keys raised on 222 of the 223 clips.
- The originals are copied first to `F:/LedgerTools/game-inputs/gasp/originals-2026-10-25` (347 MB); restoring is copying them back.

## Try 2: the stricter lock

`-GaspPlantSpeed=50` (SliceCharacter.cpp, set on the mover's own graph each frame, Epic's asset untouched) makes the lock hold any foot marked at least half planted. The result line reports the value in force (`plantSpeed=50`).
