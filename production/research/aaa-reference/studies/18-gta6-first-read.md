# Study 18: GTA VI, a first read of its systems (19 November 2026)

Release day. Systems only, by the owner's rule: no story, no characters' fates, no mission plots, no endings. Where a source mixed story and systems, only the systems were taken, and missions appear here only by mechanics. Outside reading only: no game files, no video. The study's own words and numbers. Tags: R read in a post-release source, P pre-release (trailers, previews, Rockstar demonstrations), U unverified or single-source.

The short answer. GTA VI keeps a shared record of the player on the police side (what the player looks like, wears, drives, carries, who is with the player, whether a camera caught it). A pre-release guide says the record stays after the stars drop; no review read says so in play. People on the street react in the moment and speak more varied lines. None of the sources read describes a person who remembers the player for good, or two people telling each other what they saw. LEDGER's moat holds, and the thing to copy is the surface, not the engine.

## (a) The edge check

Do people remember the player?
- Police memory is persistent and global: a profile of face, clothes, vehicle, weapon, camera footage and companion that stays after the wanted level drops (P, GTABase guide, updated 4 Oct 2026). Post-release, Push Square says police reactions take in outfit, vehicle and weapons, and Kotaku says officers share details over the radio (R); neither, nor Vice, says the record outlasts the wanted level, so persistence is P only. One shared record, not one per person.
- A second record, the Criminal Profile, tracks how professionally or how violently the player works, with its own menu and no always-on bar, and changes how people and police treat the player (P, GTABase previews recap; R, Push Square, TheSixthAxis). TheSixthAxis names four tiers (Professional, Hardened, Violent, Irredeemable); VGC says the worst tier is permanent (R, one review; the previews recap says the same, P).
- Individual passers-by: Vice says Rockstar roughly tripled or quadrupled the lines per person (the page names no baseline) and added talk after an interaction so people do not seem to switch off (R, one review). Windows Central heard no repeated line in about 60 hours (R, one review). That is variety, not memory. No source says a person recalls an earlier meeting days later (U: absence in what was read).

Do people react to what they saw?
- Yes, in the moment. Open-carried long guns and masks make people and police suspicious (P; R, TheSixthAxis). People comment on outfits by style, and react to staring, following and crowding; people listen to talk and notice an eavesdropper (P, previews recap; no review read confirms these). Reviews confirm reactions to bad driving, mocking and taunting (R, Windows Central, Push Square, Kotaku).
- Some witnesses act: armed bystanders may shoot back during crimes (P; R, Windows Central saw an armed would-be hero in a robbery crowd). A carjacked passenger can fight for the car (P only).

Do people talk to each other about it?
- No source read describes it. The nearest thing is the police radio: Kotaku says police share the player's details over it, and TheSixthAxis says police know whether the player works solo or as a pair (R). That is the law side passing facts along, not the town's gossip. Treat gossip as not present until shown otherwise (U).

How witnesses, descriptions, wanted level and police work (P, GTABase guide and previews recap; R where named):
- A wanted level starts only when a crime is reported or detected: a witness calls it in, an alarm trips, a security system or camera detects it, or a tracked car reports itself. Unseen crimes make none. Windows Central says witnesses quickly lead to emergency calls (R).
- Six stars. Hollow means a crime is known and no description; solid white means identified with line of sight; flashing white means line broken, pursuit going on; red means the search of an area.
- Icons under the stars show which details police hold. Each is cleared a different way: a mask or hat or hair change for the face, an outfit change (up to five outfits in the trunk, R, Vice), abandoning or repainting the vehicle, getting rid of the weapon, destroying camera footage, splitting from the companion.
- Red areas stay on the map, and going back too soon can re-identify the player. At six stars the area can be the whole map (P, one guide, U).
- Preparation can keep the response at hollow stars: avoiding witnesses and cameras, a mask before the report.
- Reviewers split on the result: Kotaku says police are much harder to shake; TheSixthAxis says evasion is too easy and police a weak threat; Vice says some may find police presence overwhelming.

How close this is to LEDGER's town memory and gossip (canon.md, The moat)
- Seven slots: close in spirit. GTA's list is six (face or appearance, clothes, vehicle, weapon, camera, companion). LEDGER's is seven and read per witness; GTA's is read by one authority.
- Five-rung ladder, with recognition gated by relationship: absent. GTA's identification is a yes or no on the record, not a climb by who the witness is to the player.
- Permanent per-person memory: absent in what was read. GTA's is said to persist for police (P) and is short and local for people.
- Gossip through schedule intersections: absent. The world does keep a clock (shops open and close and traffic varies by time of day, R, TheSixthAxis; buses and trains run on set schedules, R, Kotaku), so the raw ingredients exist and nothing reported uses them to spread news.

What this means for what makes LEDGER new
- The August note called GTA's systems shallow global versions of the perception engine. The first read agrees: global, shared, and excellent at the surface. LEDGER's newness is the opposite shape: many small private memories that move only where lives cross, with recognition gated by relationship.
- The risk is narrower than feared. A reviewer will now say "the police know the outfit" is old. LEDGER must show on screen that the baker knows and the docker does not, and why. The proof is the player's own surprise, not a number.
- Caution: a post-release patch, a mod tool or a deeper system not reached here could change this. Re-check at the first month and after the first big patch.

## (b) The August triage, point by point

Source for the August note: Extended Look 2026-08-27 and the IGN 99-details transcript (ledger-v2/research/gta6-triage.md).

Held:
- The pattern: shallow global perception with a strong player-facing surface. Held (see (a)).
- What-they-know HUD, hollow versus solid versus red stars: held (P, GTABase guide); reviews describe outfit and vehicle changes breaking descriptions and the red search layer (R, TheSixthAxis, Vice).
- Pre-crime prep verbs: held; Push Square describes scouting and changing clothes and getaway car; Windows Central adds intel, disguises, fuelling the getaway car, lockpicks and safe codes (R).
- Civilians who act: held (armed bystanders, R, Windows Central; fighting passengers, P only).
- Eavesdropping noticed: held (P; no post-release confirmation read).
- Contextual verbs on anyone (greet, taunt, rob): held (R, TheSixthAxis, Kotaku).
- Criminal profile as stored meter: the August note put it under adapt, never a stored global meter. It does exist in GTA, with its own menu and no always-on bar (P). LEDGER's rule to derive it from beliefs stands.
- Non-lethal surfaced: held (leg shots, or several chest shots, end a fight, R, VGC; kneecap and arm shots, R, Kotaku).
- Car theft by method: held and richer (hotwire, slim jim and key cloner, R, Kotaku; keyless entry opened through an app, and tracked cars that must be re-registered, R, TheSixthAxis).
- Out-of-area scale and world that runs without the player: held (schedules, R; wildlife and random events, P, previews recap).
- Rockstar's no-generative-AI statement: not re-checked today (P, August note only; U for today).

Did not hold, or needs correction:
- "Shallow" is fair for memory and unfair for the police surface. Several reviewers praise the police and heat system (R, VGC, Windows Central, Push Square, Kotaku). Do not underrate it.
- Store hours: Kotaku says every store is open 24 hours, an unrealistic break; TheSixthAxis says shops open and close. Reports differ (U). The August bankers' hours idea survives either way.
- Destructibility skip: reviews describe crash damage that reduces steering and acceleration (R, Kotaku) and vehicle damage physics (R, Windows Central); for cars this matters to LEDGER only through the content rule (a car can knock a person down and hurt them badly, never kill).

New since August:
- The profile's tiers and their weight, with open-world acts counting more than missions (R, VGC, relaying Rockstar).
- Cars as storage for weapons, disguises and stolen goods (R, Windows Central; TheSixthAxis for the weapon stash); fences with different tastes who pay by demand (R, TheSixthAxis); laundering (R, Windows Central).
- Failure is soft: TheSixthAxis found one fail state only, and other mishaps cut pay (R, one review).
- Real-time switching between the two playable leads and a button to invite the partner along (R, Vice). August already skipped character switching; the real-time form is the new detail. Still skipped.
- Life chores (eating, exercise, fuel) and appearance changes through body shape and haircuts (P, previews recap; reviewers count chores among the fussy parts, R, Output Lag); a photo mode at launch (P only).
- Story missions with very little map signposting, often started by a call (R, Vice, one review): relevant to LEDGER's no-waypoint rule.

## (c) What critics praise and punish

Counts. The deep reads for this study cover six reviews: Video Games Chronicle, Windows Central, Push Square, Vice, TheSixthAxis, Kotaku. A roundup (Output Lag, 18 Nov 2026, scores checked that morning) read 18 reviews and gave Metacritic 99 from 153 critic reviews, OpenCritic 98 from 65. That page's own positive count (147, none mixed or negative) does not add up to 153, so the 99 is solid and the breakdown is U. Scores seen: VGC 5 of 5, Push Square 10, Vice 10, TheSixthAxis 9, Windows Central and Kotaku none. Scores near 99 on release day mean all counts below sit inside nearly unanimous praise.

Praised:
- A reactive, dense world: 18 of 18 in the roundup; all six deep reads praise the world.
- Police and heat: 4 of 6 deep reads praise it (VGC, Windows Central, Push Square, Kotaku); Vice is mixed; TheSixthAxis calls police a weak threat. Roundup: 7 praise, 4 find it fussy, mainly early.
- Freedom in approaching jobs: most deep reads (VGC, Windows Central, Push Square, Vice); Kotaku praises missions adapting to the player's choices instead.
- Weightier aiming and the slow focus mode: roundup 7; Push Square, VGC.
- Variety of NPC speech and animation: 2 of 6 (Windows Central, Vice).
- Visual fidelity: VGC, Windows Central; Digital Foundry on lighting and the hair system (via PureXbox).

Punished:
- Frame rate: 30 fps with no 60 option. The roundup's count of critics is inconsistent on its own page (9 of 18 in one section, 5 in another), against 4 praising performance (U). In the deep reads, 2 report dips (VGC rare, Kotaku on crowded streets and in big police firefights), 2 report it smooth (Windows Central, Push Square), 2 silent. Digital Foundry (18 Nov, via AltChar and PureXbox): base PS5 low of about 23 fps in heavy explosions, PS5 Pro near locked (a brief 24 in the same worst case), both 1440p internal with the Pro using PSSR; Xbox Series X drops in chases and explosions; Series S mostly 25 to 30 with drops near 20 and 1080p output (R; PS5 figures single-source via AltChar).
- Controls and prompts: 2 of 6 (Kotaku clunky, misfiring context prompts; Push Square stiff adaptive triggers).
- Pacing and length: roundup 8 of 18 find slow opening hours, downtime or a sag; Kotaku finds momentum fades for a stretch. Main content is reported at about 60 hours (50 to 80 range), completion at 150 hours or more (R, Output Lag).
- Learning curve and fussiness (car damage, repair costs, heat tracking, chores): roundup 6 warnings.
- Evasion too easy or police overwhelming: 1 each (TheSixthAxis; Vice).
- Animation roughness beside the fidelity: 1 (Windows Central).
- Armoured enemy types that soak damage: 1 (Push Square).
- Bugs: almost nobody. None of the 6 deep reads reports bugs beyond performance and controls; crashes rare per the roundup.

## (d) Adopt, adapt, ignore

Pillars: P1 seven slots, P2 five-rung ladder, P3 permanent per-person memory, P4 gossip through schedule intersections (the first four lines of canon.md, The moat). Some items below rest on other canon or studies instead, and say so.

Adopt
- A readable "what they know" display, kept to the player's own guess at what the town knows, not a full printout. Pillar P1. Already adopted in August; reviews confirm players read it.
- Prep verbs: scouting, concealment, clean vehicle, keeping the stash in the car. P1, and canon's verbs-first pillar.
- Clues that clear one detail at a time (change the coat, change the car), each tied to one slot. P1.
- Failure that costs and continues, not a restart (TheSixthAxis). Study 16's rule; loosely P3, since the town remembers the botch.
- Different buyers who want different goods and pay by demand. Loosely P4, if each buyer is also a listener.

Adapt
- Hollow versus solid stars: use it for the law only, as "reported but nobody named", while the town's belief stays in people. P2.
- Persistent police record: make it the local station's file, held by named officers, passed on by their shifts and by one corruptible friend (canon: police corruptible as individuals). P3 and P4.
- People who act on what they saw: a have-a-go witness is a courage trait of one person, with a past. P3.
- Profile tiers: replace with the derived reputation of the belief network, never a stored meter. P3.
- Eavesdropping: the minicab office's radio, which nobody can help overhearing, is the venue, per canon. P4.
- Missions started by a phone call with little guidance: matches the no-marker rule; only if the words carry "where" (Study 16). Not a P1 to P4 item.

Ignore
- Real-time switching between two playable leads: LEDGER has one player. Outside P1 to P4.
- Always-open shops and 24-hour life: breaks era and bankers' hours. P4 needs people at known places at known times.
- Whole-map heat at six stars: the town is one map and the law should not be omniscient. P2.
- Lethal-profile permanence as a switch: canon already makes killing rare, permanent and remembered by the town, not by a counter. P3.
- Chores as survival fussiness, 60 hour length and open-world checklists: scope, and the critics' own complaint on pace.
- 30 fps as a target: not LEDGER's to copy; its frame target is set elsewhere.
