**His purpose.** One small, dense district can carry LEDGER's 15 to 20 hours: Yakuza 0 fills about 30 hours of story with two districts each about 8 minutes' walk across, and Shenmue 20 to 40 hours with a few streets and a harbour. This study reads both from public sources only (no game files), for the design of phases 6 to 10.

## His questions

1. How big is the district, and how long is the game?
2. How dense is it: side stories, activities, named people?
3. How do the people live: routines, schedules, backstories?
4. How is the day structured, and what made it tedious?
5. How does street fighting use the street?
6. What carries hours besides the main story?
7. How is the district reused?

## Findings

| | Yakuza 0 (2015) | Shenmue (1999) |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | Tokyo and Osaka, Dec 1988 to Jan 1989 | Yokosuka, 1986: Dobuita Street and the harbour |
| District size | Kamurocho, modelled on Kabukicho (about 0.36 km2); about 8 min to walk end to end (I) | A few streets plus the harbour |
| Length | Story about 30 h (listed 20 to 40) | 20 to 40 h; Suzuki planned 45 h in 11 chapters |
| Side content | About 100 side stories, 50 per lead (U); two business games | Over 300 named characters, each with a routine and backstory |
| Fighting | Brawling with street objects and the street itself | Virtua Fighter-style brawls, quick-time events |

- **Density.** Yakuza 0's side stories are stumbled into on foot, with no markers early on; most are comic, a counterweight to the serious story, and often exploration or minigames rather than fights. Its two business games (Kiryu's real estate, and Majima's **[cut: content rule]**) were called the best part by reviewers.
- **People.** Shenmue's shop owners open in the morning and go home at night; a **[cut: content rule]** worker shops by day and works at night. A man leaves in a suit every morning although his firm went bust, and wanders town all day because he cannot tell his family. **[cut: content rule]**; a woman who looks like a housewife spies on the yakuza for the police. Repeated sightings build familiarity; a learnt secret changes how a player sees a background face. PC Gamer contrasts this with Yakuza's "faceless and machine-generated" crowds.
- **A schedule bug worth knowing.** Every warehouse worker went to one shop for breakfast at the same time and got stuck; the fix was an automatic door and a limit on how many could enter.
- **The day and its tedium.** Shenmue runs on its clock: 24-hour routines, home by 11:30 pm, appointments at set hours. Its most criticised side: waiting for set hours with no way to skip, nightly trips home, repetitive questioning ("frozen molasses pace"). Others called its mundane detail its poetry.
- **The harbour job.** A part-time job at the docks: crates between warehouses by forklift, and forklift races each morning.
- **Fighting with the street.** Objects: bicycles, sign posts, car doors, traffic cones, rubbish bags, **[cut: content rule]** crates. Finishers: a face into a brick wall or the pavement, through a shop window, off a bridge into the river; alleys with bins have their own moves. Yakuza's random thug fights are frequent (I), against LEDGER's rule that most confrontations end before a blow.
- **Reuse.** Sega has reused most of Kamurocho for about 20 years. RGG's design manager: a compact, dense district stays memorable and can change over time, while sprawling maps are dashed through and new additions go unnoticed. Density comes from going up (upper-floor **[cut: content rule]** shops), not out.
