## 1. Baraff, Witkin, Kass 2003, "Untangling Cloth" (ACM TOG 22(3):862-870)

Full paper text NOT read (Pixar PDF redirects to a landing page; ACM page refused with 403). Everything below is abstract-level or secondary.

- PRIMARY (abstract as relayed by the SIGGRAPH history archive page, which paraphrases it): history-free method that analyses intersections across the whole cloth mesh at each step; untangles pinched cloth once geometry allows and also fixes tangled starting conditions; also introduces "collision flypapering", a cloth-SOLID collision method that reduces flutter in pinches. Used in production. https://history.siggraph.org/?p=115940 (read 18 Nov 2026). Paper DOI per that page: https://doi.org/10.1145/882262.882357
- SECONDARY (student summaries, UW-Madison course, 2006): global intersection analysis finds intersection curves between pairs of meshes; flood fill colours both sides of each curve and keeps the smaller side; particles in the kept region are held (not allowed to move) while pinched; moving pinching surfaces blended by an alpha weight. https://research.cs.wisc.edu/graphics/Courses/777-s2006/TextWeb/Summaries/Week11/gregc-untangle.txt and https://research.cs.wisc.edu/graphics/Courses/777-s2006/TextWeb/Summaries/Week11/krill-untangle.txt (read 18 Nov 2026). Both say they give little or no detail on the cloth-solid method, forces or cost.
- SECONDARY (ContourCraft, arXiv 2405.09522v2): Baraff et al. "detect connected components separated from the rest of the mesh by a closed contour of penetrations"; the approach "does not address open-contour intersections such as penetrating pairs of mesh boundaries"; the method applies attractive forces between such component pairs. https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09522v2 (read 18 Nov 2026).
- Cloth-vs-rigid body: the abstract states a cloth-solid method (flypapering) exists. Whether the contour/flood-fill analysis itself is applied to cloth-vs-solid, and what move/force undoes a tangle through a solid, is UNVERIFIED (full text not read).
- Costs: UNVERIFIED (no timing found in anything read).
- Relevance flag only: ContourCraft's note that the contour analysis needs a CLOSED contour (open boundaries not addressed) is relevant to a sleeve end/cuff, which is an open boundary of the cloth mesh. Whether the sleeve-beside-the-forearm case yields a closed contour on the cloth is UNVERIFIED.

## 2. Volino and Magnenat-Thalmann 2006, "Resolving surface collisions through intersection contour minimization" (ACM TOG 25(3):1154-1159)

Full paper NOT read (not located as a readable open PDF).
- SECONDARY: method minimises the length of the intersection contour between intersecting surface regions by inducing relative displacements. Search-result description of abstract; bibliographic record https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tog/VolinoM06.html (reached only via search listing; not read in full).
- SECONDARY (ContourCraft): ICM "aims to resolve cloth intersections in the general case by minimizing the length of intersection contours"; for each intersecting triangle pair it computes the gradient of intersection length with respect to the involved mesh vertices, and applies local displacements. ContourCraft uses only the translational part of that gradient because the distortional part "induces compression in the fabric and provokes strong reaction forces". Failure case (their Sec. 7): a pocket popped out may be kept because pulling it back "would increase the length of the contour". https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09522v2 (read 18 Nov 2026).
- ContourCraft says its contour analysis is for cloth-cloth; it does not describe a cloth-body ICM. Whether Volino's original applies to cloth vs a body mesh: UNVERIFIED.
- Eurographics 2016 short paper (https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/egsh20161013/053-056.pdf) refused (403); only a search-result description seen: ICM "often exhibits unpredictable convergence behavior"; same listing says that paper assumes a watertight body with outward normals so cloth vertices inside it are identified and pushed out. Search-snippet level only: UNVERIFIED.

## 6. Detection measure: ring not enclosing a limb

- Linking number (Qu and James 2021, "Fast Linking Numbers for Topology Verification of Loopy Structures"), arXiv 2106.12655, read first 100,000 of 147,654 characters: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.12655 (read 18 Nov 2026). Linking number is an integer invariant of two oriented closed curves ("the number of times each curve winds around the other"); proposed as a checksum for topology (e.g. detecting yarn pull-through in cloth simulation). Applies to closed curves; open curves only with ends fixed to rigid blocks via virtual closing connections. The paper does not address a loop around a limb directly. Counting crossings and Barnes-Hut are the fast methods (under 2 s on their examples).
- Winding number for inside/outside: search-result descriptions only (UNVERIFIED): a 2018 SIGGRAPH talk "Making Space for Cloth Simulations Using Energy Minimization" (D. Minor) uses signed ray crossings of a closed mesh to flag cloth vertices inside; the PDF was fetched but not parseable (https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2018-Talks-Minor_Making-Space-for-Cloth-Simulations-Using-Energy-Minimization.pdf). Winding-number inside tests are point-based, i.e. they have the same blind spot as point-contact when all points are outside the skin.
- SENC (arXiv 2407.12479, search-result description only; the PDF exceeded the fetch size cap, not read): remeshes garment so intersections lie on edges, closes holes such as the cuff to get a closed mesh for volume computation, and notes the penetrating side can be topologically ambiguous. UNVERIFIED.
- No source found that uses a "tube ring encloses limb axis" winding test in practice. Not confirmed to exist.
