# Checklist sweep for the town lane, second pass, 28 September 2026

What this is: a second pass over the same 979-item checklist (production/archive/ROADMAP-checklist-to-2026-09-24.md), for the same thirty-minute build and ROADMAP stages 4 and 5, after NOTE.md, whose items became TOWN.md r to z; none of those comes back here. The same rules: town lane, plainly missing, not done, no Unreal needed. TOWN.md's list and NOW.md's Handovers were read again. Evidence is from grep and reading the code on branch town at 458ac10a. Most of what is left sits where a friend spends the half hour: typed talk.

## Candidates, most important first

1. **A01.03 "Startup that does not require unrelated windows or manual commands"** (line 766; with A01.12, line 774, and V9 "Build and release engineering", line 756). About 3 h.
   Why: on a friend's PC talk is the game, and as things stand the helper cannot talk there at all.
   Not done: TalkHelper.csproj targets net8.0 and is framework-dependent (no RuntimeIdentifier, no SelfContained), so a PC without .NET 8 cannot start it; tools/play/PLAY THE ENCOUNTER.bat builds it from the repo with dotnet. It finds its cards by walking up to production/cast/cards (Program.cs `CardsDir`) and the cast file at cards/../../specs (`LoadCast`); without them `LoadCards` returns quietly and every line answers "no-card". The game starts it with "--early" alone (CrimeProbe.cpp `LiveHelperStart`), so `--relay` never arrives, and with no ANTHROPIC_API_KEY every character is "offline" and brushes him off. That, and no connection at all (`catch (Exception)`, marked timedOut), gets no plain note: `AiNotice.TalkPaused` covers only the relay's refusals (t). Publish it self-contained, find cards and cast file beside the exe, read the relay and copy code from a file there, say so when it cannot talk; staging the folder goes to the builder.
2. **A47.11 "No exposed placeholder keys or internal labels"** (line 1214; with A31.18, line 572, which NOTE.md set aside only for the waiting brush-off). About 2 h.
   Why: a friend who asks "Got kids?" or "Fancy a pint?" is likely to hit it in the first half hour.
   Not done: when the content rule refuses a reply, `ResponseValidator.Deflect` puts in "(Ron Kirby looks at you a moment, seems to lose the thread, then changes the subject.)": a bracketed stage direction naming the speaker, the thing the prompt forbids the model to write. There is no second draft for it: `ConversationEngine.SayToAsync` keeps it as the reply and in the transcript (lines 833 to 843), where the model then sees narration as its own line. The game shows it and sends it to the voice unchanged (CrimeProbe.cpp `LiveHelperPump`, `LiveVoiceSay`; voice-server.py only splits sentences), so the cast voice reads it out. The helper marks it `generated` (Program.cs:578) though the game wrote it. "kid", "kids", "pint" and "drinking" are all in `ContentWords.Speech`; how often it fires is not measured.
3. **A15.10 "A chance to respond before an alert spreads, where promised by the design"** (line 696). About 4 h.
   Why: a suspicious person asks him straight out, and a friend's first move is to answer ("No, I was at the cafe"). Today the answer changes nothing, and they ask again in every reply.
   Not done: at Suspicious and above the prompt says "in this reply, whatever they said, ask them straight out" (ConversationEngine.cs:237-238), and the helper sets the level afresh from the game's evidence on every line (Program.cs:452-463), so nothing he says moves it. The alibi check that did ("the result, not the LLM, decides whether the lie lands": `Claims.Extract`, `Claims.Process`) is called only by the retired Unity layer (Game/LawHost.cs:238-252), and its places are the old map (`Claims.KnownPlaces` reads `HookMap`; CoreTests has "the Hook Street pub" as bar_door). Check his answer against what they know, with the cast file's places; ask once, then weigh the answer; return the claim and its result for the game's gossip to carry (the carrying goes to the builder).
4. **A46.07 "Optional navigation assistance where the world is difficult to parse"** (line 1033; A04.06 "A discoverable first destination or activity", line 826; A13.11 "Conversations with actual listeners", line 655). About 4 h.
   Why: there is no minimap and no marker, so asking a local is the way round; and whether friends mention a person is one of the runbook's three things to watch. Today nobody can name anybody.
   Not done: the prompt allows only names and places "already named in what you have been told here" (ConversationEngine.cs:168, 176), and the check holds them to it (`ClaimCheck.KnownItems`). Ron's and Darren's cards name nobody alive in the town; Sheila's names only Ron. The cast file has each person's role, friendships and hourly places, and plain words for every place (production/specs/hook-cast.json `ties`, `places.said`), but the helper uses it only for the speaker's own place (Program.cs:415, `CastDay.WhereWords`), never `Ties` or `Together`. So Ron cannot say that Sheila keeps the office, where Darren is at this hour, or who is standing beside him. Add who they know, who is with them now and the street's places to the prompt and to the check's known items.
5. **A31.01 "A clear way to start and leave a conversation"** (line 727). About 2.5 h.
   Why: on day 2, "Morning, Ron" arrives as the next line of last night's talk, and no conversation ever ends from the character's side.
   Not done: ConversationEngine keeps the last twelve messages for the whole game (`TrimTranscript`, `MaxTranscriptTurns`), with no times in them; only a load clears them (`RestoreTalk`), and the talk save keeps them. The helper holds one engine per person for good (Program.cs `_engines`). No reply field lets a character close a conversation (the fields at Program.cs:582). A conversation after a gap should start as a new one, the old exchange kept as memory; a character who is done, or insulted, can say so and end it.
6. **A31.07 "A distinction between asking for information and making a consequential commitment"** (line 565). About 2.5 h.
   Why: friends will try "keep an eye out for me tonight", "meet me at the quay at nine", "lend us a tenner". A character who agrees and then does nothing is the invented person again: a promise the world cannot keep.
   Not done: the prompt's rules cover invented people and places, specifics of past events, the content rule and narration (ConversationEngine.cs:131-228), not a character's own promises or typed actions; the claim check reads details of what happened (`ClaimCheck.RequestVerify`), so "I'll watch the gate for you" passes. The router that could turn such a line into a verb the game offers (`IntentRouter`) is not called by the helper. A rule, a check on promises, and bench lines to measure both.
7. **A47.02 "Subtitles and audio using the selected supported languages"** (line 1205, ruled by G12 "English only for now"; with AI08, line 1089). About 2 h.
   Why: his friends may type in German or French (the notice already answers to the Swiss FADP). A 1990 docker answering in German breaks the town, and the content rule would not read it.
   Not done: nothing tells a character to speak English or what to do with a line in another language ("English" appears nowhere in ConversationEngine, CharacterCard, ClaimCheck or the helper). All 87 `ContentWords` patterns and `SafetyRule`'s are English, so "Bier" or "Kinder" in a German reply passes `ResponseValidator`; the cast voices are English. Not measured: a few lines in German and French through the real engine come first.
8. **Stage 5's measure, "The four Meridian Test conditions read off one session"** (line 141). About 2 h.
   Why: condition 2 is the town reacting to something he did, and the likeliest place for it is talk; the session record can only log that as a "question" (production/specs/session-record.md, `known`).
   Not done: a reply gives "level" and "heard", the memories retrieved into its prompt, not the ones it spoke from. The claim check works out each detail's source ("source": "M1", `ClaimCheck.CheckAsync`), and ConversationEngine keeps only the invented ones (`LastInvented`). So a friendly "They're saying you were at Rita's window" at Wary cannot be told from small talk. Return the memories a reply drew on, so the game can write `known` with how "remark" or "question" (the writing goes to the builder).
9. **A47.09 "Sensible number, date and measurement formatting"** (line 1212). About 1 h.
   Why: "What day is it?" gets "That's all I know", though the routines run by weekday (Ron is at the allotments on Sundays).
   Not done: the prompt and the check's "now" item get only `GameTime.ToString()`, "D2 09:00" (GameTime.cs:51; ConversationEngine.cs:127; `ClaimCheck.KnownItems`), and the check counts a weekday as a claim that must agree with an item (ClaimCheck.cs:633). `CastDay.Weekday` knows the day; the helper never says it. A calendar date waits on canon, which names no month.
