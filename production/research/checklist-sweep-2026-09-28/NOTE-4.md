## Candidates, most important first

1. **When talk cannot reach the model, nobody is told** (the thirty-minute build's "every live AI call through a server of ours"; NOTE-2 item 1 planned "say so when it cannot talk", and town list aa shipped without it). About 3 h.
   Why: on a friend's PC the first real contact with the relay is the session itself. If the relay is down, a firewall blocks it, or it refuses the request, every character says "Not now, boss. Rank's busy." to every line for the whole half hour, and the friend, and Jafar watching, take the town for rude or the game for broken.
   Not done: the helper turns every failure except four relay refusals into the character's brush-off marked `timedOut`, with no note (TalkHelper Program.cs:705-709; `AiNotice.TalkPaused` knows only allowance_spent, relay_stopped, too_busy and unknown_copy, AiNotice.cs:46-56). The relay's own refusals `upstream_unreachable` (502), `not_a_game_request` (400) and `too_large` (413) (Relay.cs:227, 232, 313), a dropped connection and a bad key all end there. No key at all gives `offline` and a brush-off with no text either (Program.cs:632-633). The ready line's `online` is true whenever a client exists, reachable or not (Program.cs:869); the relay has `/health` (Relay.cs:431) and nothing calls it. And the relay lets through only three prompt shapes (Relay.cs:62-71), tested against hand-written stand-ins (Relay/Program.cs:113, 123), never against the prompts the Core really builds, so a changed opening line in `BuildSystemPrompt` or `ClaimCheck` would silence every friend's copy this same quiet way. The work: a check at start (the relay's `/health`), a plain note in AiNotice for "cannot reach the town's talk" said once and again when it comes back, returned as `paused` is; a test that the Core's real talk, check and reflection prompts pass the relay's shapes; the showing handed over.

2. **A talkative friend may spend the copy's allowance inside the half hour** (the thirty-minute build: "a spending stop ... each copy's allowance counted"). About 3 h, and about $1 if the estimate is confirmed with real calls.
   Why: the relay gives each copy $0.50 a day (Relay.cs:48). If it runs out at minute fifteen, every character brushes him off for the rest of the session and the note says talk comes back tomorrow: the playtest ends there.
   Not done: the only measured cost of a line is from 23 September, about half a cent (production/research/talk-helper/cost-of-an-hour-2026-09-23.md), before the claim check (items 3, 6a: a check on the first sentence and another on the reply, one more per flagged detail), the second drafts (about a third of lines), the people lists (6ad, 300 to 450 words a turn) and the rules added since. Estimated from the prompts' own sizes (the talk rules alone 5,464 characters, the card about 3,000, the check's instructions 4,724 plus what the person knows): a talk prompt of about 3,500 tokens and each check about 2,700, so roughly 2.3 cents a line to Sheila (Sonnet) and 1.3 cents to Ron or Darren (Haiku). $0.50 then buys about thirty lines; the 23 September note's "fair amount" is thirty in half an hour and "a lot" sixty. Nothing is cached: no cache_control anywhere in LlmClient.cs or the relay, though that note named caching the fixed part as "the obvious first saving"; the talk prompt puts its fixed rules after the memories and the scene (ConversationEngine.cs:198-411), so no fixed prefix could be cached as it stands, and the relay reads the system prompt as one text and counts no cached tokens. The work: one scripted half hour of about fifty lines through the real engine to replace the estimate; the fixed parts of the talk and check prompts put first and cached, the relay taught to pass and count cached tokens, the bench's invented and false-alarm rates checked unchanged; then the figure for friends' copies on his page, since the allowance is his (money). The builder's list item 9 measures an hour in the game; this is the relay's side of it.

3. **Walking off does not stop the reply, and the next person waits behind it** (the slice: "conversation fast enough to feel like talk"; town list v). About 3 h.
   Why: friends walk away from a slow answer and try somebody else. Today the abandoned reply runs on to its end, its second draft and checks are paid for, and Darren's answer cannot start until Ron's finished: up to about six seconds more on top of the voice's five (FINDINGS).
   Not done: the helper reads one line at a time and answers it before reading the next (Program.cs:871-877), so the `{"walkedAway"}` the game is told to send "while a character is still answering" (NOW.md, 6v) is only read after that answer is complete; `WalkedAway` then corrects the memory after the fact (ConversationEngine.cs:739-752). Nothing cancels a turn on request; the only cancellation is the eight-second patience (Program.cs:645-692). The selftests send `walkedAway` only after a turn has ended (Program.cs:1151, 1280). The work: read lines as they come, one turn at a time per person; a `walkedAway` for a person whose turn is running cancels it through the rollback the timeout already uses, keeping only what he heard; other people's lines go ahead; selftests with a slow stand-in model.

4. **At the clock rates on his page, a conversation starts over in the middle, and the person is told they have moved while they stand still** (the first hour's clock, town list x; town lists ae and u). About 2 h.

Over a three-minute talk six game hours go by, and Sheila, still facing him in the office, is told she is outside the fish market.
   Not done: the helper starts a conversation afresh itself after 120 game minutes between two lines or on a new day (ConversationEngine.cs:25, 1198-1199; `StartFresh` clears the talk and the count of straight asks, line 38), whatever the game says: 60 real seconds at two, 40 at three. Where each person is comes from their routine at each line's hour (Program.cs:452-453, `CastDay.WhereWords`), and the game is told to send only the weather (6u), so a person held in place to talk is moved by the routine: Sheila's day goes office, fish market at 12, office at 13, Rita's step at 17 (production/specs/hook-cast.json). Her card also says "You are in Mickey's, the minicab office" (production/cast/cards/lena.md, "What You Know About The World"), shown to the model and read by the check as a C item on every line, so at the fish market or on Rita's step she is told both; her brush-off is "Not now. I've the books open." The work: once the game has ever sent `fresh`, starting a conversation is the game's (as `KnowsHimFromGame` does for acquaintance); the place held from a conversation's first line until it starts fresh, or taken from the game when it sends it (a new optional field, on the protocol page and its check); Sheila's card says where she works, not where she is (its approval rechecked); tests at both rates.

5. **A friend's first questions, measured** (ROADMAP's slice and the first hour's step one, "he moves"; the runbook: "Don't explain the game"). About 3 h and about $1.
   Why: with no instructions, a friend's first lines are to the people in front of him: who are you, what is this place, what am I meant to do, how did Mickey die, sorry I missed the funeral, what's behind that door, where do I sleep. If Sheila, who kept Mickey's books for thirty-one years, answers "That's all I know. I don't deal in guesses.", he is lost in the first minute and the town feels fake.
   Not done: every bench so far asks about a night's events (ClaimBench's sets and lines: about, business, drift, looks, news, time, wearing and the rest), brands in small talk (ClaimBench `SmallTalk`, twelve questions) or the mood (ConvoProbe); none asks what a newcomer asks. The cards know Mickey died three weeks ago and nothing of how, the funeral, the letter, the one door Sheila does not open or where Tom sleeps (lena.md, rocco.md, sam.md); the funeral reaches them only as "June, Mickey's daughter, back for the funeral" in the people lists. The claim check refuses a time, place or happening no item gives, so a kind invention ("small do at the chapel") becomes the fallback line. The work: twenty such questions to each of the three through the real engine and check; count the fallbacks, brush-offs and wrong answers; fix in the Core what is the Core's (the check's reading of a card's own facts); and put the facts the answers need to Jafar beside his ruling on the story outline and the first hour, which settle the funeral, the door and the letter (canon).

## Faults found on the way (not missing features)

- Walking off mid-reply cannot interrupt anything: the helper reads the `walkedAway` line only after the reply it is about has finished (Program.cs:871-877), so town list v's timing never happens as the handover describes (candidate 3).
- The helper's own "fresh after two game hours" rule restarts a conversation after 40 to 60 real seconds of silence at the rates on his page, and Sheila's card places her in Mickey's wherever her routine has taken her (candidate 4).
- `spokeOf` cannot fire for a line said to his face or a question about his deed, by the claim check's own rules; its only test uses a third-person line and a scripted checker (candidate 6).
- Any failure but four relay refusals is a silent brush-off, and the relay's allowlist of prompt shapes is tested against stand-in prompts rather than the Core's own (candidate 1).
