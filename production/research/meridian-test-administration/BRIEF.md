# BRIEF: how the Meridian Test is administered, by one person, with friends

STATUS: BRIEF. Written 2026-09-19, before any research began, per the standing
instruction that a topic's brief is a file in the topic's folder, in Jafar's
words.

This is gap 5 of `production/research/brief-coverage/SUMMARY.md`.

## Why this topic exists

From the commission:

> It is this project's only definition of success, and three of its four
> conditions require someone to play for thirty minutes and describe the town as
> alive without being prompted. Nothing in forty-four deliveries mentions a
> playtest, a tester or recruiting, and production/stranger-test/ is a
> simulation sweep rather than a protocol for a human.

## The constraint that changes the shape of the question

> One constraint changes the shape of this from the usual advice: the testers
> available are friends who play games, not strangers. Friends want you to
> succeed and will say the town feels alive because they can see you want to
> hear it, and the hardest condition is precisely the one they would hand you
> unprompted out of kindness.

## The question, in his words

> So the question is how to run a first-impression test with people who know you
> and still get an answer worth having.

> - What practitioners do about acquiescence and demand characteristics when the
>   tester knows the maker.
> - What can be observed rather than asked, since behaviour lies less than
>   opinion.
> - How to phrase a question so it does not name the thing you are hoping for.
> - When a friend's session is worth something and when it is worth nothing.
> - What sample size means anything at this scale, and what the known ways of
>   fooling yourself are.

## The selection rule for methods

> Weigh methods by whether one person with no budget and no lab can run them,
> not by rigour alone.
