# Chimney smoke by day: why the local fog volumes show nothing, and what to use instead

Research note, 3 October 2026 (a helper's thirty minutes, read-only). The problem: 25 runtime-spawned
`ALocalFogVolume` puffs over five stacks on the hill (VignetteShot.cpp 2890-2920; positions in
production/specs/unreal-look.json 109-113) show nothing in the 2560x1440 hook frame. All engine
sources below are the UE 5.8 files on this PC, read 3 October 2026; web sources are dated where given.

## 1. What the engine needs before a local fog volume draws

Read from `Engine/Source/Runtime/Renderer/Private/LocalFogVolumeRendering.cpp` (LFVR below),
`Engine/Shaders/Private/LocalFogVolumes/*.ush|usf`, and the component files.

- **Switches.** `r.SupportLocalFogVolumes` (project setting, read-only, default 1; LFVR 19-23,
  RendererSettings.h 1050-1054) and `r.LocalFogVolume` (default 1; LFVR 13-16). Neither appears in
  Engine/Config/*.ini or ue-probe/Config (searched). The view must have the Fog show flag and no
  debug view mode, and the scene must hold at least one volume (LFVR 115-123).
- **No height-fog or volumetric-fog requirement in the code.** `ShouldRenderLocalFogVolume` checks
  nothing about them (LFVR 115-123). Epic's page says one of Sky Atmosphere or Exponential Height Fog
  should be present (both are here). [Epic, "Local Fog Volumes in Unreal Engine", 5.8 page, read 3 Oct 2026]
- **Mobility.** The component makes itself Movable in its constructor (LocalFogVolumeComponent.cpp 24);
  the actor un-hides itself (same file 217). The setters only take effect when dynamic changes are
  allowed, which Movable satisfies. Runtime spawning is fine.
- **Size.** Base volume is a sphere of radius 500 cm (LocalFogVolumeComponent.h 74-76). The proxy uses
  the largest axis scale times 500 as the radius (LocalFogVolumeSceneProxy.cpp 34-37): a non-uniform
  scale silently becomes a sphere of the largest axis. Here scale = R*100/500 gives radius R metres (correct).
- **Units of RadialFogExtinction.** Per metre at the centre, normalised so that a ray through the
  centre has optical depth = extinction x radius in metres: the shader integrates (1-d^2) across the
  unit sphere times 3/4, then multiplies by the radius in metres (LocalFogVolumeCommon.ush 175-204, 277).
  For this project's numbers: first puff R 1.25 m, 0.6 -> centre transmittance exp(-0.75) = 0.47
  (53% cover); top puff R 3.1 m, 0.18 -> 43% cover. These are clearly visible values, not faint ones.
- **Distance and count limits.** No far limit. Nothing draws nearer than `r.LocalFogVolume.GlobalStartDistance`
  (source default 1000 cm, LFVR 65-68; Epic's page says 2000, the source on this PC governs). At most
  `r.LocalFogVolume.TileMaxInstanceCount` = 32 volumes per view (LFVR 50-53, 533-536); 25 is within it.
  A volume with both extinctions at 0 is skipped (LFVR 421-424).
- **Culling.** Per 128-pixel screen tile, by the frustum planes and, if a current HZB exists, by the
  HZB (LFVR 337-400; LocalFogVolumeTiledCulling.usf). The draw then uses a depth-bounds test and a
  depth test starting at the nearest volume's distance (LFVR 802-827, 848-853, 894-908).
- **Compositing.** Height fog first, then the local fog volumes in their own pass, then volumetric fog
  (DeferredShadingRenderer.cpp 3787-3838; Epic's page lists the same order). If volumetric fog is on
  and `r.LocalFogVolume.RenderIntoVolumetricFog` is 1, they are composed inside the fog pass instead and
  their density inside the volumetric-fog distance is clamped to `r.LocalFogVolume.MaxDensityIntoVolumetricFog`
  0.01 (DeferredShadingRenderer.cpp 2276-2280; LFVR 30-38). Here volumetric fog is off (the fog component's
  `bEnableVolumetricFog` is never set and defaults off; VolumetricFog.cpp 1353-1362), so the separate pass applies.
  Consequence for this street: the volume's light is added on top of the already-fogged picture, so
  smoke at 120-215 m would not sit inside the day haze that greys the hill.
- **Lighting.** One directional light (the most intense, LightGridInjection.cpp 1494-1519) through a
  phase function, plus the sky light's spherical harmonics; no shadowing, no self-shadowing
  (LocalFogVolumeCommon.ush 300-327). A puff is a smooth, evenly lit grey ball.

## 2. What was measured (not guessed)

- The run that spawned the 25 puffs: ue-probe/Saved/Logs/vign-proof21.log line 1754 ("LedgerSmoke: 25
  puffs over 5 stacks"), hook frame taken at lines 1804-1806; frames copied to
  F:\LedgerTools\renders\proof-2.2\smoke2. Compared with the same frame before any smoke (…\v6).
- Positions are right: four of the five stack points lie within 0.54 m of a brick vertex in
  production/assets/street/quay-street.glb in the street's frame (the fifth is ambiguous only because its y is 0.2).
- Each puff was projected into the hook camera (cam_hook in production/specs/vignette-scene.json:
  x -3.0, y -1.6, eye 2.2 m, yaw 20.4, pitch -3.4 as placed by VignetteShot.cpp 3946-3954, 46-degree
  vertical field); the projection puts the top-row stack at a visible chimney pot. Brightness change
  inside each predicted puff against v6: 0.0 to 2.0 grey levels over the hill, 0.00 to 0.09 against
  the sky, the same as the ring around each puff (run-to-run noise). The expected change at 43-53%
  cover is tens of grey levels. **The volumes contribute nothing at all; this is not a contrast problem.**
- By this projection two plumes (stacks at 141.3,-67.3 and 190.1,-103.9) fall left of the hook frame;
  the other three are inside it.
- A PerfHook CSV of the first attempt (ue-probe/Saved/Profiling/CSV/Profile(20261003_190255).csv, the
  slice run that logged "5 plumes") shows the `LocalFogVolumeVolumes` GPU scope and the
  `RenderLocalFogVolume` CPU scope in every frame (GPU mean 0.006 ms). So in that run the scene held the
  volumes and the passes ran, but drew little or nothing. No such profile exists for the vignette run.

## 3. The most likely reason, honestly stated

The cause is **not established by reading**. Every condition the 5.8 source places on these volumes
is met: switches on by default and not overridden; Movable; visible by day (VignetteShot.cpp 3597-3598);
25 under the 32 cap; extinction non-zero; puffs beyond the 10 m start; volumetric fog off, so the
separate pass runs. Since the pass runs but nothing reaches the picture, the fault sits between the
tile culling and the draw. In order of likelihood:

1. The tile culling rejects every tile (frustum or HZB test). It is the only stage that depends on
   scene data, and the near-zero GPU time fits a pass that drew no tiles (weak evidence).
2. The culling runs on async compute by default (LFVR 55-58, 89-92); an ordering fault on this card
   would give the same result. Cheap to rule out.
3. The draw's depth test or depth-bounds test rejects the pixels. The arithmetic was checked for this
   camera and passes; least likely.

**The one run that settles it** (console variables on the command line, no file changed):
`-ExecCmds="r.LocalFogVolume.TileDebug 1"` tints every tile that holds a volume (LFVR 60-63, 143-152;
non-shipping builds). No tint at the puffs means culling (then repeat with `r.LocalFogVolume.UseHZB 0`,
then `r.LocalFogVolume.TileCullingUseAsync 0`); a tint with no smoke means the draw. As a cross-check,
`r.LocalFogVolume.RenderDuringHeightFogPass 1` (LFVR 25-28) draws them inside the height-fog pass and
skips the tiled path entirely.

## 4. The fix to try first

Do not spend more on local fog volumes for this. Even working, they are the wrong tool for distant
chimney plumes: smooth uniform spheres with no wisps, lit without shadow, at most 32 a view, and
composited over the height fog, so they would stand out unfogged against a hazed hill (section 1).
Magnopus's fog survey (15 January 2025) likewise treats fog volumes as close-range fog "in one local
area" and fog cards as the tool for shaped, composed detail.
Run the TileDebug check once only if the cause matters for later ground mist.

## 5. How it is usually done, and the cheapest believable version

- **The professional method is sprite particles.** Epic's own 5.8 how-to builds smoke from a Niagara
  sprite emitter: spawn rate, random lifetime, upward velocity and acceleration, size and rotation per
  particle, and an 8x8 flipbook played by a SubUV module with sub-UV blending, using Starter Content's
  smoke material. [Epic, "How to Create a Smoke Effect Using Sprite Particles in Niagara", 5.8 page, read 3 Oct 2026]
- **For a chimney at 120-215 m:** per plume about 15-30 particles alive, lifetime 10-20 s, rising
  0.5-1 m/s with a sideways wind drift, size growing from about 1 m to 5-6 m, opacity fading out; a
  translucent material with a soft depth fade; light it as volumetric non-directional translucency, or
  unlit with a pale grey a little darker than the overcast sky. Translucent materials take the height
  fog by default ("Apply Fogging", Material.h 1080-1082; per-pixel option at 1094), so the smoke sits in
  the same haze as the hill, which local fog volumes cannot do.
- **Cost:** each puff covers 10-50 pixels across at that range; five plumes of a few dozen sprites are a
  few hundred thousand shaded pixels at most, an estimate of well under 0.1 ms on the RX 6700 (not
  measured; the PerfHook CSV's translucency column would show it).
- **Two ways in from this project's C++:** a Niagara system asset spawned with
  `UNiagaraFunctionLibrary::SpawnSystemAtLocation` (NiagaraFunctionLibrary.h 93), the studio-standard
  route; or, all in code with no Niagara asset, a few `UMaterialBillboardComponent` sprites per plume
  (MaterialBillboardComponent.h) with the drift and fade done in the material. The first is the method;
  the second is the quicker proof.
- **The texture, under the allowlist:** Starter Content's smoke flipbook is Epic content, which the
  allowlist does not name (it names Epic animation content and MetaHumans only), so it would need his
  ruling. Clean options: (a) self-made in Blender: a short smoke simulation rendered as an 8x8 flipbook,
  or a soft noise puff rendered once; (b) Kenney "Smoke Particles", CC0, 70 sprites, released 2014
  [kenney.nl/assets/smoke-particles, read 3 Oct 2026], stylised, so for a quick proof rather than the
  final look; (c) no texture: a radial falloff times a self-made tiling noise, scrolled in the material.
  At 10-50 pixels across, (c) or a single self-made puff is likely enough.

## Sources not reached or not used as evidence

- Epic forum thread "Can't see Local Fog / Volumetrics" (November 2023, UE 5.3): its fix was enabling
  volumetric fog for material-based volumetrics, not local fog volumes; not evidence here.
- No Epic issue-tracker entry was found for runtime-spawned local fog volumes failing to draw (one search,
  3 Oct 2026). UE-215351 (offset when far from origin and rotated) does not apply: these are near the origin and unrotated.
