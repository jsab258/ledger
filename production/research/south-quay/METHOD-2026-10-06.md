# The south quay: method and sources (6 October 2026)

Why: item 1.1's first gate review (production/audits/phase1-exit/GATE-1.1-REVIEW-1.md, item 1) found
the built world ending past Quay Street's south end, seen from Mickey's pavement (the walk-in,
production/playtest/ai-tester/2026-10-06-1522/step-025.jpg). What closes it is what the adopted atlas
(production/art/atlas-01/data/atlas.json) has there: the junction at atlas (400, 320), Quay Street
turning west for the jetty root, the Harbour Board approach leaving east, the open apron, the Old
Basin's north quay at atlas north 280, the jetty strip at north 220 to 240, the sea, and the Hook's
dockside on the east land. This note is how it was built and from what.

Every source below was opened on 6 October 2026 unless it is listed under "not reached". Nothing
from any source (pictures, text) was copied into the repository; only figures are cited.

## 1. The method

The professional route for a closure like this is a modular environment kit laid out on the
adopted plan: block out from the map, give every piece its real dimensions from records and
standards, keep the pieces to the street's own surfaces so look-development stays in the engine,
and judge it from the viewpoint it has to serve (here Mickey's door, and the walk-in's frame).
The street's own recipe (tools/art-recipes/terrace-front.py) already works this way: Blender for
shape and layout, Unreal for look, one reflection of y at the crossing into Unreal. The kit does
the same and reads every level, material and map position from its home rather than retyping it:
the street's levels and material table from terrace-front.py, the junction, roads and coast from
the atlas.

## 2. Quay walls, copes and paving

- Bristol Floating Harbour quay wall, list entry 1202530, via British Listed Buildings
  (https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101202530-floating-harbour-quay-wall-and-bollards-extending-for-approximately-900-metres-central-ward):
  1893; granite and Pennant rubble with cast iron; granite curbs to the quay on a Pennant
  harbour wall; cast-iron bollards.
- Charlestown Harbour, list entry 1327290, via British Listed Buildings
  (https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101327290-charlestown-harbour-comprising-the-inner-dock-and-quays-outer-basin-and-piers-eastern-breakwater-and-the-eastern-wall-st-austell-bay):
  quays paved mainly in squared granite blocks; dressed-granite kerb stones along the tops of the
  quays, and large granite-block kerb stones; mooring posts round the quay edge and several
  cast-iron bollards along it, one an upturned cannon barrel; flights of granite steps; begun 1790.
- Setts, pavingexpert (https://www.pavingexpert.com/setts01): setts run about 100 x 100 to
  200 x 250 mm on plan, 150 to 200 mm deep; "coursed" (courses across the traffic) is the
  traditional 18th and 19th century pattern.

Taken: a granite cope along every quay edge, flush with a granite-sett apron (quay_stone), the
wall below in squared stone. The cope's size, 0.60 m across and 0.30 m deep, standing 50 mm proud
of the wall, and the wall's batter of 1 in 20, are CHOSEN: no reached source gives a harbour cope's
section. The apron is laid level with the road's kerb top: no reached source gave a quay's fall,
and at 35 m a drainage fall would not be seen. The setts are a surface for the engine, not geometry
(no setts map is in the project's CC0 library today; one would need a catalogue line).

## 3. The water's level

- NTSLF, chart datum and ordnance datum (https://ntslf.org/tides/datum): chart datum below
  ordnance datum by 3.05 m at Newlyn, 3.00 m at Whitby, 2.60 m at North Shields, 1.50 m at
  Lowestoft.
- Visit My Harbour, Newlyn (https://www.visitmyharbour.com/harbour/newlyn/): MHWS 5.6 m,
  MHWN 4.4 m, MLWN 2.0 m, MLWS 0.8 m above chart datum.
- RICS isurv, berth design parameters (https://www.isurv.com/info/1403/ports_and_harbours/11058/berths/3,
  the free part only): the top of the cope "the higher of either one metre above the highest
  astronomic tide or 1.5 metres above MHWS"; a low cope may suit berths used only by small craft.

So at a Newlyn-like port half-tide (about mean sea level) is 3.2 m above chart datum, 2.4 m under
MHWS. An old quay's cope set 0.8 to 1.0 m over MHWS stands 3.2 to 3.4 m over half-tide; today's rule
(1.5 m over MHWS) would make it 3.9. The kit puts the water 3.20 m under the cope (z = -3.15 with the
road crown at 0). The atlas's proposed height datum (street = +4.0) would put that half-tide at
+0.85; if the atlas means its 0 as mean sea level the two differ by 0.85 m, inside the range above.

## 4. Bollards, rings and ladders

- Mooring bollard, Dimensions.com (https://www.dimensions.com/element/bollard-mooring): one
  example 762 mm high, 533 mm wide, 457 mm deep; bollards commonly 762 to 1220 mm high.
- The street's own clutter set already has a cast-iron cannon-and-ball bollard from a Historic
  England list text (production/art/clutter-2026-09-29/README.md).

Taken: a cast-iron bell bollard 0.72 m high, 0.50 m across its base flange, a 0.34 m barrel and a
0.44 m head (iron_black), set 0.75 m back from the cope's nose; ten of them. Mooring rings: iron
rings 0.25 m across hanging under the cope, CHOSEN (rings set in quay copes are named only in the
search summaries for Portsmouth and Dover, section 9). The ladder (0.45 m between stiles, rungs every 0.30 m, 0.15 m off the wall, its stiles
carried up over the cope as handholds) is CHOSEN to the common fixed-ladder figures: the port-ladder
figures seen (EN 14329, 400 mm rungs, 150 mm from the wall) were only in search summaries, not read.

## 5. The harbour light

- Watchet Harbour Lighthouse, Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchet_Harbour_Lighthouse):
  1862, a tapered hexagonal cast-iron tower with a lantern, about 6.5 m, at the end of the west pier;
  fixed green light.

Taken: the same type at the jetty's end: a hexagonal tapered tower 4.6 m on a stone plinth, a
gallery with a rail, an eight-sided lantern and a domed roof, about 7.0 m in all; tower paint_white,
ironwork iron_black. The lantern is red (lamp_red) because the jetty is on the port hand of a boat
entering the basin, which is red in IALA region A: that rule is from memory, not from a source read
today, and should be checked before the light is lit in the game.

## 6. The junction

- Traffic Signs Manual chapter 5 (2009), section 3, on Wikisource
  (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Traffic_Signs_Manual/Chapter_5/2009/3): give-way marking,
  diagram 1003: two broken lines, 600 mm marks, 300 mm gaps, 200 mm wide, 300 mm apart, at the minor
  road's mouth, normally to the centre line.
- The street's own markings (terrace-front.py): double yellows 2.5 and 2.7 m from the centre,
  the diagram 1008 centre line, 2 m marks and 4 m gaps from x = -2.

Taken: the street's lines carried to the junction, the give-way line across the Harbour Board
approach's entry half. The 1990 figures are ASSUMED the same as 2009's, as the street recipe already
assumes for 1008. The kerb radii (8 m inside Quay Street's turn, 6 m at the approach, 12 m on the
outside of the turn) are CHOSEN: no design source was reached.

## 7. Boats and quay objects

- FAO, fish box standard requirements (https://www.fao.org/4/r1263e/R1263E06.HTM): wooden fish
  boxes type A 510 x 420 x 191 mm to type E 1115 x 638 x 448 mm; type C 840 x 510 x 248 mm.

Taken: type C wooden fish boxes (prop_timber), in stacks; coils of manila rope and rope fenders
(rope); a galvanised dustbin of the street's 90-litre size; the atlas's Hook objects are "rope
fender, wooden fish box, galvanised dustbin". Three inshore boats lie alongside (10 to 13 m, a
wheelhouse aft, a mast and derrick forward, generic and unnamed): their sizes are CHOSEN, no source
for them was reached. They are there because at half-tide a boat alongside shows only its mast and
wheelhouse top over the quay edge, which is what says "harbour" from Mickey's door.

## 8. Dockside warehouses

- John Deheers Warehouse, Weymouth, list entry 1272117, via British Listed Buildings
  (https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101272117-john-deheers-warehouse-weymouth-and-portland-melcombe-regis-ward):
  formerly a fish warehouse; mid to late 19th century; dark brick in Flemish bond; hipped slate roof;
  four storeys, 3 x 6 bays; three-light wood casements under flat segmental heads with stone sills;
  central glazed doors with railings to former hauling ways; a wide central ground-floor opening;
  plank loading doors; broad brick pilasters with a stone capping band; a stepped brick eaves cornice.
- Warehouse on Wylam Wharf, Sunderland, list entry 1208788, via British Listed Buildings
  (https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101208788-warehouse-on-wylam-wharf-premises-of-rose-line-limited-at-nz-4025-5736-hendon-ward):
  18th century and about 1830; rubble and brick; Welsh slate roof; four storeys; small
  segment-headed openings with projecting sills; loading doors at each floor; a gabled, boarded
  hoist opening with a projecting pulley; hipped at one end, a coped gable at the other.

Taken: brick ranges of two to four storeys (red and grey, from the street's palette), slate roofs
gabled or hipped, stone sills and lintels, sash windows with dark glass, one hauling way per long
front (a cart door, loading doors above, a boarded hoist hood with its beam), one blank painted
name band (render_cream, no lettering). Storey heights 3.4 m and 3.0 m, the 35-degree pitch of the
street and a lower 22 to 25 degrees on the wide sheds are CHOSEN.

Placed by the atlas: the quay row's fronts stand back at y = +58 so the atlas's quayside walk
(the swing-footbridge proposal's line along atlas east 455) runs on open quay in front of them; two
ranges stand in the atlas's H05 massing block behind them, one in H04 behind Mickey's yard lane,
the Harbour Board's office at the approach's end (atlas landmark H3), clear of the Sea Road line,
and the Old Basin Cold Stores (atlas place WT_COLD) where the west road turns for the jetty.

## What the atlas itself gets wrong here (for the session)

- Quay Street's atlas line runs south along atlas east 310 from north 300 to 240, which is 10 m
  inside the basin's west edge (east 300): the road would be in the water. The kit ends the west
  road at the atlas corner (310, 300) and does not follow it south.
- The atlas's swing footbridge across the basin mouth is a proposal ("engineering and gameplay
  unverified"); the kit leaves the mouth open.
