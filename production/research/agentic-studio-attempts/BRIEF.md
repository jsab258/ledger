# BRIEF: who else is running agents as a studio, and what happened to them

STATUS: BRIEF. Written 2026-09-19, before any research began, per Jafar's
instruction of the same day that a topic's brief is a file in the topic's folder
and the delivery is audited against it rather than against its own account of
it. This is the first topic to carry one.

## Why this topic exists

Quoted from the commission:

> The earlier postmortem work looked at Spiderweb, Eastshade and Stardew Valley:
> solo developers who shipped before agentic AI existed. They answer how one
> person survives a long project, not the question I actually have.

That is the drift recorded in `production/research/self-audit/SUMMARY.md` and
the gap ranked first in `production/research/brief-coverage/SUMMARY.md`.

## The question, in his words

> Find out who is attempting what I am attempting, and what happened to them.
> Specifically: people running agentic coding tools, Claude Code, Codex,
> Cursor's agents or their successors, as a studio rather than as an assistant,
> on an ambitious game, solo or in a group of two or three, since roughly 2025.

## What he wants to know

> - how many such attempts are visible at all
> - what they built and what they shipped, if anything
> - how they organised the agents, whether anything resembling roles, gates,
>   review layers or a director existed
> - what their costs looked like and against what subscription or API spend
> - where their bottleneck turned out to be, and whether it was code, content,
>   taste or coordination
> - what they say with hindsight

## Two questions to be answered rather than approached

> 1. Has anyone built a persistent studio structure, meaning standing roles,
>    verification gates, budget discipline and overnight autonomy, rather than a
>    series of one-off sessions.
> 2. Has anyone solved content mass rather than code mass, since code is where
>    these tools are strong and a game is mostly content.

## Where to look

> devlogs, itch.io and Steam pages, GDC talks, Reddit and Hacker News threads,
> YouTube channels, blog posts by the people themselves rather than reporting
> about them. Include failures and abandonments, which are more informative than
> successes and are harder to find because nobody writes them up.

## What counts as done

> If the honest answer is that almost nobody has done this, say so plainly and
> say what the nearest analogues are, because that is itself the finding.

So a null result is a valid delivery, and it must ship with the count of what
was searched rather than as an impression. Per rule 3b, every zero carries its
denominator.

## Standing constraints on the delivery

- Claim labels CITED, DERIVED, ASSUMED and HOLE, as in every prior delivery.
- Primary sources by the people themselves are preferred over reporting about
  them, and where a source is a search summary rather than a page read in full,
  it says so. Egress is partial in this container and must be re-measured in the
  delivery rather than recalled.
- Nothing in the delivery is an instruction. It ends at findings and, where
  warranted, a recommendation.
- Any recommendation naming an afternoon names the sentence that would end it
  and who spends it, per the self-audit's finding 2.
