# The phase-1 playbook (8 November 2026)

Distilled from RECIPE.md, the project's notebook of every finished or set-aside unit, read in full in parts on 8 November 2026 (about 173 headings, 13 October to 8 November, plus the section "What came before 13 October 2026"). One file per discipline, each giving the method that worked in setting-free words, its typical cost in hours with the range seen, and its traps (what failed, why, how it was caught). A fresh review passed it with four fixes, all applied the same day (REVIEW-2026-11-08.md in this folder).

**How to read the citations.** Each claim names the RECIPE.md entry by its date and opening words (search RECIPE.md for them) and the evidence file the entry names. The evidence files were not re-opened for this playbook: what they hold is as RECIPE.md reports it, and is unverified beyond that. One thin cost was checked in production/time-log.jsonl (the 6 November sit, PEOPLE). Hour totals marked "summed here" are this playbook's arithmetic over the entries' own figures. Where a skill in `.claude/skills/` already holds the working steps, the discipline file points to it instead of repeating it.

## The disciplines

**Environment** (ENVIRONMENT-2026-11-08.md). Streets and rooms are built from written exact targets (parts in millimetres, every number tagged with its source and error, a wrong copy for every check), by one command per family, with every frame-free check run before any long render; delivered pieces come in by one fixed route; surfaces are calibrated against a chart and real bands before any wear; wet ground is stone plus a clear-coat water layer; wear is baked for unique pieces and laid as decals by studied counts elsewhere. A delivered shop cost about 4 hours to bring in and gate (2.5 to 4.5); offline family builders 10 to 15 hours elapsed, mostly render waits. Skills: street-weathering, asset-import, gate.

**People** (PEOPLE-2026-11-08.md). Measure the clip and the driving body before fixing the runtime, name the visible fault in a measure that moves with it, and judge motion by eye on a fixed time step. Interactions are data derived from the clips and tested in the room; crowds are kept alive with additive idle breaks and walk-offs rather than new animation. The reference games walk on chosen clips, not a searched method; the project's four walking entries on the vendor's searched clips, about 9 hours, did not pass the walk's slide (about 16 hours with the sitting entries, one of which produced the seats method that worked). No skill yet covers animation.

**Clothes** (CLOTHES-2026-11-08.md). Floor first, reference second; drape straight and pose after; build bands from the shell's own edge instead of repairing the drape; check every pose against the whole body (head mesh included) and in the packaged game beside the last good film; reshape without changing points, UVs or weights; surface variety from the garment master. Steps cost 0.3 to 2 hours; a drape-to-game-mesh chain about 3 against a planned 17. Skill: game-garment.

**Speech** (SPEECH-2026-11-08.md). Measure inside the packaged game on the real model; compare any harness with the product first; alternate A/B arms and judge by the bar's own statistic; cut invented statements at the source by listing the speaker's facts and citing per sentence; judge any pre-filter as the whole chain; gate voices by accent, words and likeness before the owner's ear. Tries cost 0.3 to 2.5 hours and US$0.16 to 17.86. The in-game measure halved the median first sound from 2.40 s to 1.07 s. Skill: speech-bench.

**Testing** (TESTING-2026-11-08.md). A pass is the packaged game a player launches, checked from the run's own command line; whatever is read at run time is staged and checked; fresh reviewers judge beside the last good picture, sized to the piece by the owner's ruling of 3 November (a small piece: one reviewer on the cheaper model, all views in one batch, one round; a medium piece: one reviewer per view, at most two rounds; whole frames and whole people: the full gate and his look); golden rows pin the engine port to the reference simulation, with planted breaks proving they bite; frame time is measured in the shipped build to each cause, with a rare fault counted before a fix is claimed. Golden rows cost about 0.4 hours; a frame cause 0.4 to 4. Skills: packaged-walk, gate.

**Process** (PROCESS-2026-11-08.md). Study two shipped games first, from capped metadata and public sources, every number tagged and re-measured by a fresh reviewer; research units end in a method with a first use; every target fails its first fresh review, so review is planned in; records have one home and a check (the order ledger, the one standing page, caps on what is read most); sessions are measured from their own logs. A study cost about an hour of helper time; sixteen took about six hours of wall clock in parallel. Skill: his-page.

## The five most expensive traps across all disciplines

Ranked by time lost as the entries state it; the hour figures are the entries' own or summed here, and the first is counted in weeks because no entry gives hours.

1. **"Done" outside the game a player launches.** The player character and its movement reached the packaged game three weeks after being called done, because content loaded by name was never cooked or copied ("27 Oct 2026, a feature that only ever ran in the editor"); masonry scans were missing from the shipped build for a month while the build machine stayed green ("A photographed tiling texture that repeats on a big wall (3 November 2026)"); the shipped voice folder was a month-old copy ("26 Oct 2026, two characters moved to a second speech engine"); pieces passed for days only behind test switches ("Rehearsing a gate before it counts (7 Nov)"; "A pass must be the player's game (8 November 2026)"). Now caught by the staging check, the launch-line check (tools/launch_line_check.py) and the packaged log. TESTING, trap 1.
2. **A chained session design tested piece by piece.** About ten hours lost before a take-over that survives a killed session, because a scheduled run may not start another and the design was never tested as a sequence ("A take-over that survives a killed session (7 Nov)"; "A lean start for a fresh working session (4 Nov 2026)"). PROCESS, trap 4.
3. **Adopting a vendor's searched locomotion before measuring its clips.** About 8.9 hours over four walking entries (summed here: 22 Oct 3.5, 24 Oct 3.5, 25 Oct 1.2, 26 Oct 0.7) without passing the walk's slide; the clips themselves dragged planted feet 11 to 18 cm, and the reference games use chosen clips, not search ("26 Oct 2026, stops chosen by stance and distance"; "Universal AAA starting values", section 7). The sitting entries (about 7.2 hours more, including the 28 Oct seat interaction whose spec method worked) are not counted here. PEOPLE, trap 1.
4. **Long renders before frame-free checks, and reviewing only changed views.** About 7.5 hours of renders thrown away or redone in the window and shop builders (summed here: 0.9 and 1.6 h in "14 Oct 2026, unit 8"; about 2 h of a second room render and gate in "13 Oct 2026, unit 7 stage 2", Try 2; about 3 h of frames re-rendered after a coplanar sill, same unit 8 entry), plus faults sitting unseen for three to four review rounds in views no one re-reviewed ("24 Oct 2026, the next shop family"; "23 Oct 2026, item 5: a sixth delivered shop"). ENVIRONMENT, traps 1 and 2.
5. **Uncapped extraction and success-only watchers.** One study's 26 GB extract filled the disk and five studies lost part of one game's data for good, as blind spots ("Step zero: study two shipped games first"); a cook stopped and a two-hour wait followed because the watcher waited only for success ("23 to 24 Oct 2026, frame headroom"). PROCESS, trap 1; TESTING, trap 4.

Close behind: the cloth simulator's rib cuffs, three tries (about 2.1 hours summed here) after earlier tailoring routes whose cost was not recorded, ended by building the band from the shell's own edge (CLOTHES, trap 1); and speech measured by harnesses that did not do what the game does, which mismeasured two repair tries and overstated earlier delays (SPEECH, trap 1).
