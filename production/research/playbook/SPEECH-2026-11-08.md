# Playbook: speech (live talk, the claim check, voices, written lines)

Distilled on 8 November 2026 from RECIPE.md (read in full that day). Citations give the RECIPE.md entry's date and opening words and the evidence file it names; the evidence files were not re-opened. The working steps for measuring talk live in `.claude/skills/speech-bench/SKILL.md` (the Long Pause bar, invented claims, fall-backs, cost); this note records what the notebook shows worked, its cost and its traps.

## 1. The method that worked

**Measure where the player is.** The delay from a player's line to the first sound is measured inside the packaged game, on the real model, by a scripted run that asks a fixed set of lines, each once the last answer is heard, under a hard spend cap; the talk program keeps a transcript of each reply's stages. That run found an always-present but empty evidence record promoting every line to the slower model; routing by what the record carries plus escalating a failed fast draft to the better model cut the median from 2.40 s to 1.07 s ("Measuring a talking character's delay inside the shipped game, and halving it (2 November 2026)"; production/research/voice-latency/IN-GAME-2026-11-02.md). Earlier: "Delay measured on the real path, not beside it" ("What came before 13 October", PROVEN, Voice and talk).

**Compare the harness with the product before trusting it.** Split the path into stages from per-line marks (draft, check passed, sound made, heard), rank levers by their saving on the measured split ("25 Oct 2026, speech re-timed the game's way"; production/research/voice-latency/CAST-VOICE-2026-10-25.md). A bench that stands in for a production component calls that component's whole entry point ("26 Oct 2026, a refused spoken sentence rewritten alone", the correction).

**A/B by alternation, judged by the right statistic.** Alternate arms in one sitting beside the same running game; run each condition at least twice (two identical 60-turn runs differed 17 to 9 in slow turns); when a timing splits into two groups, judge by the share under the bar and the 75th and 90th percentiles over at least 40 lines an arm, and read the exit's own definition of the bar first ("1 Nov 2026, a latency fix judged by alternating runs"; production/research/voice-latency/REMEASURE-2026-11-01.md; "26 Oct 2026, a refused spoken sentence"). Before building a lever, count which group the target cases fall in (1 Nov entry).

**Cut invented statements at the source.** List the speaker's facts beside the rule and ask for a citation per sentence (the published method: allow "I don't know", restrict to sources, cite each claim): invented statements written fell by three quarters. Sort leaks by harm with a blind reader; test with the real reply model, the same rows in every arm, a pilot to price rows, resumable runs ("27 Oct 2026, invented statements cut at the source"; production/research/invented-claims/SOURCE-TEST-2026-10-27.md). Then strip the citation markup at every point text flows, including half-written marks in the stream (same entry). Earlier: a second model reading each reply against what the character knows took invented details from 30% to 7%; facts chosen by code before writing reduced empty answers ("What came before 13 October", PROVEN).

**Hedge only what is not shared.** Before sending a slow call twice, measure whether the two copies' slowness is shared (rank correlation); send both at once; choose on a signal that exists before content (the service beginning the request), never on first words ("27 Oct 2026, a slow, uneven model call sent twice at once"; production/research/voice-latency/CHECK-TIME-2026-10-27.md). Test the owner's condition on a switch on fresh items where the named harm can fall, paired and alternated, with a sign test ("28 Oct 2026, the owner's condition on a speed switch tested on fresh data").

**A fast pre-filter is judged as the whole chain.** Fix the design and the cut-off on tuning data before the test exists, run the held test once per cut-off, count leaks of the whole chain against the slow check alone, time it beside the shipped game keeping only answers given while the game ran ("26 Oct 2026, the fast checker's third try"; "27 Oct 2026, a small local fact checker scaled up"; production/research/invented-claims/LOCAL-CHECKERS-2026-10-26.md). Ship what was measured, behind a switch, off by default, settings in one spec file; order the gates hard rule, fast checker, slow check ("27 Oct 2026, a small fast checker put in front of a slow model check").

**Cost levers.** Run each option on the same held lines through the real engine and its check, alternated with the shipped way, repeated; judge first speech, fall-backs and cost together, the voice by a reader blind to the arm. A prompt cache only reads an unchanged start: move what holds to the front and check it passes the model's minimum, by reads in each answer ("A cost lever on a talking model's calls (6 Nov)"; production/research/llm-inference-economics/TALK-LEVERS-2026-11-06.md). Confirm the saving inside the shipped program, old and new in one hour on one build ("Confirming a measured saving inside the shipped program (7 Nov)").

**Voices: gate before the owner hears.** Same lines, same reference clip, same loudness so only the voice differs; gate by accent over the whole line and in windows, words heard back, likeness to the approved voice; the owner's ear decides identity ("26 Oct 2026, a listening test of two voices for the same people"; production/approvals/2026-10-26/page.json). Recasting from an open corpus: read only the needed members of a large remote zip by byte range; gate each joined take, not only each line; check every scripted word (a dropped word passed a word-error bar) ("26 Oct 2026, a character's voice recast from an open speech corpus"; production/casting/darren-milner/voice-2026-10-26/REVIEW.md). Switching engines: use the exact settings the owner heard, remake every made-ahead line too, match loudness by measure, and make the build copy the voice program in ("26 Oct 2026, two characters moved to a second speech engine"; production/research/voice-latency/POCKET-SWITCH-2026-10-26.md). Accent gate on 2.5 s windows, calibrated on real speech ("What came before 13 October", PROVEN).

**Sharing the machine.** A helper sharing the processor with the game runs below its priority before its thread count is cut ("22 Oct 2026, item 8, part: a speech model on the processor"; production/research/voice-latency/POCKET-CPU-2026-10-21.md). See TESTING for the card-sharing trace.

**Written lines.** Count the reference games' dialogue and bark data by speaker type and situation, never copy it, and compare with the project's own counts first ("28 Oct 2026, how much a believable town says"; production/research/aaa-reference/studies/03-dialogue-barks.md). Ambient lines as data with rules and a walk simulation; content written from design documents read by a fresh reviewer for what each person could know ("What came before 13 October", PROVEN, The simulation; production/cloud-week/lines/NOTES.md). Text one program writes and another reads is a byte-for-byte contract pinned by golden rows ("28 Oct 2026, the text a game sends to its talk program pinned byte for byte"; see TESTING).

## 3. Traps: what failed, why, and how it was caught

1. **A harness that does not do what the game does.** The timing tool voiced after the check where the game voiced before it, so every earlier figure overstated the delay ("25 Oct 2026, speech re-timed the game's way"). A fix measured outside the game used the wrong model ("Measuring a talking character's delay ... (2 November 2026)"). The repair bench judged by the check's first step alone, far stricter than the whole check, so both repair tries were mismeasured ("26 Oct 2026, a refused spoken sentence"). The timing tool ran a different voice launcher than the game and failed for a different reason ("1 Nov 2026, a game's voice silently lost"). Caught by fresh review and by measuring in the shipped game.
2. **Timing an idle machine.** A run longer than the game timed mostly an idle machine; keep only answers time-stamped while the game ran ("26 Oct 2026, a small local fact checker trained"). Caught by fresh review.
3. **A pre-filter judged on its own.** A filter that clears what the slow check would have caught adds to the chain's leaks (1 of 42 became 2) ("26 Oct 2026, a small local fact checker trained"). A bigger model was slower and crowded its scores so a safe cut-off cleared nothing ("27 Oct 2026, ... scaled up").
4. **Silent model loss.** A cleanup that removes unregistered large files removed a voice model the game needed, and the program fell back quietly; register every model the day it is adopted and make the game log what came up ("1 Nov 2026, a game's voice silently lost to its own disk cleanup"; production/retention.json).
5. **The shipped copy is stale.** The game's voice folder was a month-old copy, so no voice change had reached play ("26 Oct 2026, two characters moved to a second speech engine").
6. **Racing on first words** biases toward short answers; caught by review ("27 Oct 2026, a slow, uneven model call"). The first cost figure was the counter's, not the bill (same entry); hand-written subtotals did not match the run record ("A cost lever ... (6 Nov)").
7. **Caches that do not cache.** A start just under the model's minimum silently saved little ("A cost lever ... (6 Nov)"); a cache that was read saved no time because a short prompt's wait is the provider's before its first word ("The live-talk reply's wait: the fact check's third try, set aside (4 November 2026)").
8. **Default caps stopping runs.** The first full source test stopped at the bench's default $1 a run ("27 Oct 2026, invented statements cut at the source"); an unattended run starts no talk program unless one is named and waits forever ("21 Oct 2026, item 8, part: a small processor speech model").
9. **Accent drift.** A faster voice drifted American on every line and was kept off the owner's page ("26 Oct 2026, a listening test"); the accent checker cannot tell some regional recordings from drift ("What came before 13 October", FAILED, Voice and talk).
10. **Repair models re-invent.** Given the facts and a refused sentence, a repair model re-invents from the speaker's identity; told to add nothing, it answers "don't know" ("26 Oct 2026, a refused spoken sentence").
11. **A tool overwriting shared files.** A conditioning tool's single-voice mode overwrote the shared manifest and vocabulary; read the diff of any tool's run ("A background voice swapped to a chosen speaker (6 Nov)").
12. **Judging against the wrong bar.** A study's main fault was a conclusion judged against the wrong bar, not a wrong number; judge against the project's own recorded bars, cited by path ("1 Nov 2026, a reference study of a shipped game's on-device language model"; production/research/study-a-inzoi/STUDY-A-2026-11-01.md).
