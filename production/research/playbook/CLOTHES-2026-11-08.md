The working steps live in `.claude/skills/game-garment/SKILL.md` and in RECIPE.md's own section "Clothing: one game garment, the professional way" (28 October); this note does not repeat them step by step. ## 1. The method that worked

**Floor first, then reference, then pattern.** Load any fetched garment pack in a throwaway project of the right engine version and judge it against the written period floor before any fitting; stop if nothing passes ("22 Oct 2026, item 7, the clothes"; DECISIONS.md 22 Oct 10:35). Read one shipped game's comparable garment from its files (pieces, edges, weights, triangles, texel density) and copy the method, never the asset ("Clothing: one game garment", step 1; production/research/game-clothing-pipeline/PIPELINE-2026-10-28.md, MAFIA-JACKET-2026-10-28.md). In the reference games cloth simulation is only on named characters' loose parts and hems ("Universal AAA starting values", section 5).

**Drape straight, pose after.** When the cloth tool wraps a piece round one straight volume per limb, drape on a copy of the body with the joints straightened and bend the result back with the body's own skin weights; prove the bend-back by bending the body itself and comparing (0.0 mm median, 5 mm at most) ("28 Oct 2026, a garment's sleeve end that never closed round the forearm"; production/research/game-clothing-pipeline/SLEEVE-END-2026-10-28.md).

**Build bands from the shell's own edge, not from the drape.** When a draped band will not wrap, delete it and loft a new one from the shell's open edge ring (always closed) to a far ring sized from the body by textbook ratios (a welt toward 0.90, a neckband 0.92 of its neckline); check the head still passes the neck opening at the knit's stretch ("30 Oct 2026, a knitted top's rib bands built from its own shell edges"; F:/LedgerTools/garments/tom/jumper/bands-j3-try2). This replaced three failed cloth-simulator routes (traps 1 and 2).

**Simulated drape to skinned game mesh by script.** Put each piece back on its true flat outline, grid each piece through a no-fold map, hold narrow strips still in smoothing, make the winding one before turning outward, bake each piece only from its own source piece and count normals leaning past 60 degrees, skin from the body ("27 Oct 2026, a simulated garment of many pattern pieces made into a skinned, baked game mesh"; production/art/clothing/tom-outfit/BAKE-PLAN-2026-10-27.md). Cost about 3 hours against a planned 17.

**The body under the clothes.** Skin through clothes is not fixed garment by garment: the character system's own hidden-face map, painted from the actual garments with a 2 to 3 cm margin, set on a project copy of the base wardrobe item, built first into a side folder and compared with a control build ("30 Oct 2026, a character's body cut under its clothes"; production/research/character-pipeline/HIDDEN-FACES-2026-10-30.md). A body brought into the cloth tool by its own importer is measured against the current avatar by height band before adoption ("A body brought into the cloth tool by its own character-file importer (2 November 2026)").

**Check every pose, against the whole body, in the packaged game.**
- Per-pose check (rest, arms raised and forward, stride, sitting) on the new and the last good version together; a fault only one shows is named by the bone that moves its points ("30 Oct 2026, a garment that passed every offline mark and broke in the game"; F:/LedgerTools/tmp/pose-check-2026-10-30).
- A garment is not handed on until seen in the packaged game at the closest distance the player meets it; keep the last good film of the same walk and roll back at once on failure ("29 Oct 2026, a garment's rebuilt cuffs ... failing in the game"; F:/LedgerTools/tmp/ai-tester/film/2026-10-29-1828).
- Before a garment fix, pose body and garment together offline and find which skin crosses the cloth and where it sits at rest; skin visible at rest cannot be hidden ("A pose-driven fault that a hidden-geometry map cannot fix (2 November 2026)").
- The head mesh can run down the chest and be absent from the exported body: read it from the rig's data file, draw cloth and head into a 1 mm grid from the side the fault is seen, and move only the cloth there ("A carried garment cleared of the head mesh (7 November 2026)"; "A garment cleared of the head mesh from behind (8 November 2026)"; F:/LedgerTools/gate/new/dress-cast-2026-11-07/slits/diagnosis.md).

**Reshape without touching downstream.** Footwear changed toward a reference by measured silhouettes (heights at 1, 3, 5 cm behind the tip, spring, width), only the toe's points moved with falloff, the sole thinned from below; points, faces, UVs and weights identical to the last version; UV stretch under 1.25; the character's raise lowered to match ("A shoe's toe reshaped toward a reference by measured silhouettes (2 November 2026)"; "A shoe's sole thinned and its welt trimmed (2 November 2026)"; F:/LedgerTools/garments/tom/boots/maps/).

**Surface from the master, not new meshes.** Knit: the garment master's tiling detail-normal layer, a whole resampled CC0 scan as the body tile, a rib tile drawn only where the part ID says rib, switched per instance, repeat size from the garment's measured UV scale against the reference game's repeat ("Knitwear surface: a tiling knit, a turned rib, a colour each, a lower neckband (8 Nov 2026)"; F:/LedgerTools/gate/new/knit-2026-11-08). A painted-in garment on a background body is recoloured by flood-fill from several seeds at tight tolerance, light ratio kept ("A painted-in garment recoloured (6 Nov)").

**Tooling for the cloth program, no clicks.** A plug-in started once runs job files from a folder on the program's own thread, with a heartbeat file and no network port; every job checks its files exist first, since a wrong path opens a box only a person can close ("26 Oct 2026, a desktop design program driven by script through a one-click plug-in"; tools/md-bridge/). Its menu item is started through the operating system's accessibility interface by one hard-coded path, guarded by heartbeat and busy marker ("30 Oct 2026, a desktop program's one menu item started by the operating system's accessibility interface"; tools/md-bridge/start_bridge.ps1). Both are under the owner's rulings on computer use and UI Automation (RULINGS.md, 29 and 30 Oct, as cited in CLAUDE.md; not re-read here).

## 3. Traps: what failed, why, and how it was caught

1. **Repairing a drape that cannot close.** Rib cuffs in the cloth simulator failed three times: a separate cuff never encircles the wrist because the sleeve's end it is sewn to is itself a flap ("27 Oct 2026, a garment's rib cuffs scripted round a wrist ... a third, researched try"); placed close the cuff's points start inside the collision shell, placed far its seam will not close ("28 Oct 2026, a garment's sleeve end"); the tool's own elastic left a single-layer band bulging or rolling ("29 Oct 2026, a garment's knitted cuffs gathered by the cloth tool's own elastic"). Measure the base piece alone before the part sewn to it; build the band from the shell edge.
2. **Every offline mark passing while the game draws holes.** Rebuilt cuffs passed every check and the game drew holes over back and chest; the per-pose check had not been run and the game's reduced levels of detail were never looked at ("29 Oct 2026, a garment's rebuilt cuffs"). The next day: a rule meant for buttons averaged a whole sleeve's weights because a recipe option was retyped, and a flat-pattern UV layer exported first moved every map ("30 Oct 2026, a garment that passed every offline mark and broke in the game"). Caught by the packaged film beside the last good. Lesson recorded: read a delivered asset's options from its recorded recipe, never retyped; put the maps' UV layer first in the export step itself.
3. **A measuring body missing a part.** A body cut at the neck made the collar follow the chest and put every shoulder line flat and armhole high in two garments ("30 Oct 2026, a skinned stand collar re-weighted"; "30 Oct 2026, a knitted top's rib bands"). The head mesh, absent from the body file, defeated two pushes ("A carried garment cleared of the head mesh"). Measure the pattern's and the test's source body first.
4. **One set of weights cannot serve two masters.** A skinned stand collar can follow the trunk or the neck, not both; set aside after two tries, with a corrective shape or a joint driven by neck pitch named next ("30 Oct 2026, a skinned stand collar"; "A pose-driven fault that a hidden-geometry map cannot fix").
5. **Hem cut before the shoe was chosen.** Trousers drafted for a bare foot either stretched round the boot or rode 6 cm up; a coarse mesh eased point by point let laces tear through. Three tries at one fault, counted per fault, not per method ("30 Oct 2026, trousers made for a bare foot put over boots").
6. **Signed distance against an open mesh** is noise away from its edge; the first height limit came from a false reading and moved nothing ("A garment cleared of the head mesh from behind").
7. **Mirrored cuts and seam direction.** A piece cut unmirrored beside its unmirrored neighbour is sewn inside out (a cuff 80% wrong in the bake) ("27 Oct 2026, a simulated garment of many pattern pieces"); a seam sewn against the wrap direction twists a band through itself ("27 Oct 2026, a garment's rib cuffs").
8. **Script calls that report success and do nothing.** A placement setting read back its value but moved nothing until a second setting followed; read back every setting and export unsimulated pieces to measure where they really are ("27 Oct 2026, a garment's rib cuffs"; "29 Oct 2026, a garment's knitted cuffs"). Run the same multithreaded job twice before trusting one result (same 29 Oct entry).
9. **Turning a tile without turning its normals.** The first rib ran round the bands because band UVs run the other way; turning a normal tile means turning its vectors too ("Knitwear surface ... (8 Nov 2026)").
10. **A shape change cannot add construction detail.** After toe and sole reshapes the boot still lacked a toe-cap seam and lacing ("A shoe's sole thinned and its welt trimmed").
11. **Wrong-project imports.** The garment presence-and-freshness check now reads the one shipped project, after a past import into a side project changed nothing ("Asset checks widened to every folder that ships (8 Nov 2026)"; production/asset-checks/street-sweep-summary.json).
12. **Modal boxes stopping scripted jobs.** Two cloth-program jobs stopped on boxes the owner had to close (a wrong file name, an old scale; bodies in centimetres read as millimetres, imported at scale 10) ("26 Oct 2026, a desktop design program driven by script").
13. **A visible part that cannot be seen is a shortfall, not a block.** A sleeve end always under an outer skinned garment that never rides up was listed, not chased ("30 Oct 2026, a knitted top's rib bands").
