# Jeans: a full seat, loops that lie flat, a heavy hem (research note, 30 September 2026)

After Darren's jeans failed two blind reviews; a separate helper, read only, about twenty-five minutes. **D** = documented, with source; **I** = inference.

## In short

1. **Drape the seat on a closed collider, not the bare body.** Once per body, from waist to gluteal fold, replace each horizontal slice of the back by its convex hull (where a taut tape lies); blend back to the real body over 3–5 cm below the fold and at the side seams. Blender 5.x could close the hollow with SDF nodes instead (out about 2 cm, then back); 4.5 lacks them. The real body serves only a final no-poke check (I, from [3][4][5][9]).
2. **Give the solver a bending term** (dihedral or isometric), and only push cloth out of the body, never in. Without bending, cloth follows every hollow and crumples at every free edge: the cleft, the frilly hems and the kneecaps alike (I; constraints D [10][11]).
3. **Size against the hull girth:** seat about 8–10% over it, knee about 20% (I). A modern size-32 501 measures 42 in round the seat and 17.5 in at the knee (D [12][13]).
4. **Map details after the drape, onto the smoothed seat;** make the centre-back seam and yoke stiffer strips (felled, several layers) so they span the hollow (I).
5. **Belt loops:** a closed band about 12 mm wide, three layers (about 2.5–3 mm), 5–7 cm long, lying flat on the waistband, or on the belt at the belt's thickness; ends tucked or bartacked flat; edges rounded; no gap, no outward bow (D for size [14][15]; the rest I).
6. **Back pockets:** one layer, a step of about 1 mm, the rim rounded into the leg, stitches in the normal map; never a slab with vertical walls (I).
7. **Hem:** a 2–3 cm band several times stiffer in bending than the leg; simulate on 15–20 mm edges, then subdivide; one soft break on the shoe (I; D for resolution [6][7] and the break [16]).

## (a) The seat

- Body scanners take girth as the convex hull of a horizontal slice, because a tape bridges the hollows at the buttocks and crotch (D, search summary [9]).
- Marvelous Designer's collision thickness (1.5 mm a side) and skin offset (3 mm) are lowered for tight fits (D [1]); being uniform, neither closes a hollow (I). Pressure is for padding (D, search summary [2]); it would inflate the whole seat (I).
- On CLO's forum, a garment showing too much body "can't be fixed in CLO"; change the avatar (D [3], about 2021). A Chaos cloth tutorial prefers a low-poly static body as collider (D [5], 2025); such a body has no deep cleft (I).
- Blender 5.0 added SDF Grid Offset and Fillet, the latter rounding only concave regions (D [4]); Houdini closes with VDB dilate then erode (D, search summary [8]).
- Too long a back rise bags under the seat (D [17], 2016).

## (b) Loops, pockets, seams

- Loops: finished ½ in or less, the strip folded twice to a third (D [14], 2020); 5–7 cm long (D, search summary [15]). Usually five: centre back, two at the back 3–5 cm from the side seams, two outside the front pockets (D, search summary, page not identified); check the 501 photograph.
- Simulated loops "non stop twist and jitter" (D [18], 2018); model them (I).
- Games: loops and pockets as geometry, stitches and floating detail baked into the normal map (D, weak, search summaries [19][20]).

## (c) Hem and knee

- Smaller particle distance, finer wrinkles: about 20 mm while working, under 5 mm for final (D, search summary [6]); one production artist uses 7–8 mm for wrinkly clothes, more for stiffer (D [7], 2018). Denim: buckling ratio near 0% (D [22]).
- A jeans artist smoothed his knee folds, which "made the jeans look more like latex" (D [23], 2018).
- Rigid denim folds with more definition; stretch slumps (D, weak, retail [24]). One break at the shoe is usual (D, search summary [16]).
- A 501's 16 in leg opening dwarfs the ankle: the hem hangs as a tube resting on the instep (I).

**Not found:** a studio's own fix for a sunken seat; seat ease from a menswear pattern book; 1990 501 measurements; loop or pocket thickness in a game asset; denim thickness in mm (only 12–14 oz ≈ 400–475 g/m² [21]).
