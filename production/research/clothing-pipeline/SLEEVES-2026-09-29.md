# Scripted sleeve draping in Blender 4.5: drape with the arms held out, then pose down (research note, 29 September 2026)

**The problem.** The donkey jacket cut from FreeSewing's Brian (production/art/clothing/donkey-jacket-pattern) drapes and closes in the body, but its sleeves fail: with the arms in the rest pose (about 45 degrees down) they collapse into strips along the top of the arms and the body's side seams are pushed 19 cm open; with the arms raised about 20 degrees, each flat sleeve starts about 18 cm from its armhole and the sewing springs drag it off the arm into crumpled strips before anything drapes. Jafar's ruling of 29 September: Blender stays; the known fix is to drape with the arms held out from the body and pose afterwards; research it first, then one more attempt, capped at one day. About thirty minutes of research by a separate helper given the problem; saved here by the builder (the helper cannot write files). "Mine" marks the helper's own inference. All sources accessed 29 September 2026.

## 1. Placing sleeves before sewing

- **Marvelous Designer.** Each body part has a bounding volume with arrangement points; a pattern placed on a point is wrapped along the volume's curve, so a sleeve starts as a tube round the arm (a pattern longer than the volume wraps in a spiral). support.marvelousdesigner.com, "Arrange Pattern with Arrangement Points", "Add Bounding Volume" (undated).
- **Fuhrmann et al., "Interaction-free dressing of virtual humans"**, Computers & Graphics 27(1), 2003: pieces are placed automatically on cylinders and cones round the body, so the sewing starts converged. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0097849302002455
- **GarmentCodeData** (Korosteleva et al., arXiv 2405.17609 v3, September 2024): sleeves rotated to the arm angle of an A-pose body (plus or minus 15 degrees); the first frames without gravity to help the stitching; collisions between chosen body and garment parts switched off; cloth found inside the body pushed out; about 72% of samples pass. https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17609
- **Garment Tool** (Blender add-on, v2.0 docs, undated): a bend modifier wraps sleeve patterns round the arms; "Initialize Simulation" ramps gravity from 0 and raises sewing force and shrink by a warm-up factor; suggests quality above 15 and 2 mm collision distance. https://joseconseco.github.io/GarmentToolDocs/quick_guide/
- **Simply Cloth Pro** (docs, undated): "Bend Selection" for arms; "Simply Sewing" merges seams during the simulation within a distance. https://simplydocs.org/simply-cloth-pro/
- Mine, for this script: wrap each flat sleeve on a cylinder along the straight arm (shoulder joint to wrist), radius the sleeve's width at the bicep line over 2 pi, plus the collision distance and about 1 cm; the underarm seam under the arm, the cap's crown at the shoulder point; before simulating, check every paired seam point is within 2 to 3 cm. The 18 cm starting gap is the root cause of the strips.

## 2. Order and settings (Blender 4.5 Python)

- Blender manual (current source): sewing_force_max at 0 (unbounded) is unstable while seams are far apart; key shrink in gradually from 0; Dynamic Mesh lets modifiers above the cloth change its rest shape; the object-collision vertex group excludes faces from colliding.
- Properties: cloth use_sewing_springs, sewing_force_max, shrink_min, shrink_max, vertex_group_shrink, vertex_group_mass (pins), pin_stiffness, use_dynamic_mesh, quality; collision_settings.distance_min, self_distance_min, collision_quality, impulse_clamp, vertex_group_object_collisions.
- Mine: ramp effector_weights.gravity 0 to 1 and sewing force 0 to 5-15 over 15 to 20 frames; shrink 0; freeze the body panels (drape the body alone, apply, then pin at weight 1 except a band of 5 to 8 cm round each armhole fading to 0); quality 10 to 15, collision quality 4 to 5, distance 3 to 5 mm, self-collision on at about 3 mm, impulse clamp about 1; once seam gaps are under 1 cm, welding the seam points is an alternative to springs. Blender cannot sew between separate objects, so draping in the held-out pose avoids needing per-part collision switches.

## 3. From the held-out pose to the rest pose, and export

- Blender manual, "Cloth with Armature": start from the bind pose and move to the target pose over several frames, with the Armature modifier above the Cloth modifier.
- Marvelous Designer's "Change Pose by OBJ Morphing" (undated): morph the avatar over many frames while simulating; through in-between poses for large changes.
- Mine: Surface Deform moves the garment inside Blender but does not export as skinning; only armature weights and shape keys reach Unreal (FBX or glTF); Corrective Smooth, Surface Deform and Data Transfer survive only baked.
- Skin weights: nearest-surface transfer fails at armpits and crotch. "Robust Skin Weights Transfer via Weight Inpainting" (Abdrashitov et al., Epic, SIGGRAPH Asia 2023): reference code MIT (https://github.com/rin-23/RobustSkinWeightsTransferCode); a Blender add-on GPL-3.0 (https://github.com/sentfromspacevr/robust-weight-transfer); both to check against the licence allowlist. Unreal 5.8 has it built in: the Dataflow TransferSkinWeights node (inpainting by default), also usable from an Action Utility. https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/node-reference/Dataflow/TransferSkinWeights
- Mine: lower the arms by abduction only, no roll; transfer the weights again at the rest pose, twist bones included, since the skeletal mesh must be in MetaHuman's bind pose; Unreal skins linearly, so Blender's preserve-volume does not carry over.

## 4. Marvelous Designer by script (the owner's fallback, only if this attempt fails)

Its Python API (docs v0.1; changelog from MD 2024.2.189 to 2025.1.201) covers import (ImportFile, ImportAvatar, ImportPose), arrangement (SetArrangement, SetArrangementPosition), sewing (AddSeamlinePairGroup), simulation (SimulationStart, SimulationStop, SimulationFrameForward) and export (ExportFBX, ExportOBJ, ExportAlembic). Limits: it runs only inside the open application (its Python editor, or a registered .py plug-in from 2025.1.107); no command-line or headless mode is documented; DXF import through ImportFile is unconfirmed; it is paid (a money decision). https://developer.marvelousdesigner.com/list.html

## The recipe for the one-day attempt (the helper's)

1. Pose a copy of the body with the upper arms about 10 to 20 degrees below horizontal, elbows straight, no roll; collision on, outer thickness 5 to 10 mm.
2. Resample each sleeve and armhole seam pair to equal point counts.
3. Drape the body panels in this pose, as now, then apply.
4. Wrap each sleeve on its arm's cylinder with the cap on the armhole; stop if any seam gap is over 3 cm.
5. Sleeve drape: the body pinned except the armhole band; sewing force and gravity ramped over 15 to 20 frames; shrink 0; quality about 12, collision quality 5, self-collision on; about 100 frames. Pass: every seam under 5 mm, no penetration.
6. Unpin all but a light collar pin, settle about 30 frames, apply.
7. Transfer weights from the body (inpainting); Armature above Cloth with Dynamic Mesh on; bring the body down to the rest pose over 40 to 60 frames (in-between poses if it breaks), simulate, hold 30 frames, apply.
8. Transfer weights again at the rest pose, twist bones included; export on the MetaHuman skeleton; in Unreal check the armpits with the arms raised.
