# Darren's jeans belt and pager (research note, 30 September 2026)

Reviews failed the belt: hip through it, the loose end lifting "like a stiff board", a dress-belt look, and a bezel-less pager. Sources read 30 September 2026. D = documented; I = inference.

## In short

1. **Weight each layer from the one beneath, not the body (D [11][14][15]; I).** Order: jeans waistband (a stiff ring, per [17]), then the strap, then tail, keeper, buckle and pager. Each piece is copied from the strap only, with Data Transfer's "Projected" or "Nearest Face Interpolated" [16]. Overlapping pieces then share weights and cannot part (I).
2. **Two bones at most (D [11][12]; I).** Pelvis, with spine_01 at the back; no thigh. Same weights across the width, smoothed only along the length. Buckle, keeper and pager: one constant set each.
3. **The tail follows the strap's curve (I).** Offset it from the strap surface (strap thickness plus 0.5 mm); do not model it straight. End it at the first jeans loop, 10-12 cm past the keeper.
4. **The hip (D [17]; I).** Test with the body hidden under the jeans, as the game does. Keep the belt 1-2 mm off the band. A Blender Shrinkwrap [11] will not reach Unreal.
5. **Look (D/I, see (a)):** 38 mm wide, 3.5 mm thick, brown or black; a brass or nickel single-prong buckle with a 4.5-5 mm bar; a rounded or pointed tip; five 4 mm holes 25 mm apart; one keeper 15-20 mm wide.
6. **Pager (D [18][19], photo reading):** a clear window set into the case, with a gloss-black border round a pale grey-green LCD.

## (a) The belt

- **The one measured British man's belt found (D [1]):** V&A, High and Mighty, about 1990. It is 30 mm wide, with five holes and a leather keeper. So 30 mm was not wrong in 1990 (I). Two more V&A men's belts, of 1990 and the 1980s, are black leather with metal buckles [2][3] (D).
- **Jeans belts sold today (D, search summaries [7][8][21]):** 38-40 mm. Use 38 mm; it reads as a jeans belt (I).
- **Belt-makers (D [4][5][6][9][10]):**
  - Leather: 3-3.5 mm for jeans, 2-2.5 mm for dress belts.
  - Holes: five, 25 mm apart, 4 mm across.
  - Keeper: 12-20 mm wide.
  - Tip: English point or rounded.
  - Buckle: 38.1 mm inside; bar 4.5 mm (search summary [9]).
- **Not found:** a dated British catalogue page (paid archives [22]); Levi's archive measurements.

## (b) Skinning

- **One group, no bending (D [11]):** points in one vertex group "will never deform". A sliding buckle has "more than 1 vertex group influencing it".
- **Same weights, same move (D [12]):** once all the belt's points share weights, "the belt doesn't deform at all".
- **Bottom up (D [14][15]):** weights are painted on the bottom layer and transferred up a layer at a time ([15], search summary). Clips and badges take them from one clean mesh.
- **The stiff board (I):** the tail's weights differed from the strap's beneath it.
- **Not found:** Epic advice on belts. Check the resizing graph does not copy body weights again [17] (D/I).

## (c) The pager screen

- **BT Message Master 1200, Science Museum (D [18]):** black plastic, rectangular screen, two silver buttons, a clip; 75 x 85 x 30 mm.
- **Its photograph (helper's reading):**
  - one clear lens, about 78% of the face wide and a third high;
  - a gloss-black border of 3-4 mm round a pale grey-green, unlit LCD;
  - a small step down from the pebbled case.
- **A late-1990s Motorola Bravo (D [19]):** the same, with the logo printed on the lens border.
- **To model (I):** the lens 1 mm below the face; the LCD 0.5 mm below the lens.
- **Not found:** measured depths.
