# A skinned knitted jumper that holds at the armpit, cuff, hem and neck (research note, 30 September 2026)

The clothing session asked for this after Ron's jumper failed two blind reviews. A separate helper was given the problem, not a theory, and worked read only for about twenty-five minutes. Every source was read on 30 September 2026. **D** means documented, with its source; **I** means the helper's inference. "Search summary" means only a search engine's summary was seen. It does not repeat the 29 September notes (CARDIGAN-BUILD, SKINNING-TROUSERS, SEATED-SKIRT).

## In short

- **Third and last attempt (I).** Stop copying weights from the nearest point. Write zone weights by script, blended over the surface, then normalise:
  - **Side panel** (from 6 cm below the armpit): spine_03 and spine_04, with clavicle 0.1–0.2 at the top. No upperarm.
  - **Under the sleeve:** upperarm rises from 0 at the side seam to 1 within 8–10 cm.
  - **Shoulder:** clavicle blends into upperarm over 6–8 cm.
  - **Cuff:** lowerarm_twist_01 0.6 and lowerarm 0.4, the same all round and through its depth. No hand or wrist joints.
  - **Welt:** the shell's own last 5–6 cm, drawn in 8–10% and turned 1.5 cm inside. The same weights up each column. Round the ring, the cardigan note's seated mix, blended into the knit over at least 10 cm.
  - **Neckband:** lofted from the knit's top row, 25–30 mm, doubled inside. Top edge spine_05 0.5–0.6 and neck_01 0.4–0.5 (spine_05 0.7 at the front).
  - **Hiding:** only faces at least 10 mm inside the jumper in every pose, and 8 cm from the welt, 6 cm from the cuff and 4 cm from the band. Test with the trousers on.
- **The fins are the known fault of nearest-point copying on a sweater** (D [J1]).
- **Real knit is one fabric:** the welt is the first rows; the band is picked up and folded (D [K3][K4]). Glued rings step and gap (I).
- **The Body Hidden Face Map is binary** and removes faces "always hidden" (D [E1]). No published margin rule was found.
- **No studio account of a skinned jumper was found.** Epic's armpit tutorial is a video, and it was not seen [J6].

## A. Armpit and cuffs

- **The fins (D [J1], 2022).** A sweater given its weights by transfer:
  - "At one loop, the closest part of the body mesh was the arm. At the next loop, the closest part of the body mesh was the body."
  - The weights jump from 50–75% to 0% "with no transition at all".
- **Side and underarm (search summary [J7]).** Mostly spine, partly clavicle. Set it rigid first, blend only where needed, and test at the extremes.
- **Blender (D [J2]).** Projected Face Interpolated casts along each point's normal; Max Distance rejects far matches. **I:** if you transfer at all, use it, so the side panel finds the ribs, not the arm.
- **The heavy body (D [J3]):**
  - lowerarm_twist_01 sits two thirds down the forearm, 8.6 cm above the hand joint.
  - The upperarm twists sit at a third and two thirds.
  - wrist_inner and wrist_outer hang off hand.
  - Fold the correctives (upperarm_in/out/fwd/bck, spine_04_latissimus, clavicle_pec/scap) into their parents.
- **Forearm (D [J4][J5]).** Weight elbow, twist, twist, wrist "1 to 1, with slight fading". The forearm loses volume if weighted to the wrist.
- **Cuffs (I).** His wrist skin rides the same twist joints, so the cuff stays with it; with no hand weight it cannot swing off. Make the inner face 2–3 mm off the skin, rolled 1.5–2 cm inside. The cuff shows "cuff and wrist watch" (D [K5]).
- **Notch and ripples (I).** Smooth along the sleeve only, never across the armhole ring.

## B. The welt when he sits

- **The real thing (D).** Vintage men's patterns knit the welt as each piece's first rows: K2 P2, 4 in; cuffs 3 in [K4]. A sweater sits "just below the belt line" and "will move and lift" [K5].
- **Why it became a shelf (I):**
  - Each welt point copied one knit point, so its weights come in patches.
  - Where the knit changes bone (the thigh-to-pelvis move ending at the waist, and side against back), neighbouring points jump and the ring steps.
  - The ring shares no points with the shell, so they part and a slit opens.
  - The move's hard edge tears the knit into notches.
- **Fix (I).** One mesh, with ribs from a normal map or 1–2 mm displacement. The draw-in answers "no draw-in".

## C. The crew neck

- **The real thing (D):**
  - The band is 0.75–1 in (5–7 rows of 2×2 rib), worked on smaller needles.
  - The front neckline is 3–4 in deep and 7–8.5 in wide [K1].
  - The band is at least 1 in "to draw in the knitting". A man's head is about 22 in, so the opening must be at least 14.5 in at 50% stretch [K2].
  - A folded band is "twice the width of the plain band, folded in half and stitched down to the inside" [K3].
  - The neck should "lie neat and flat" without hugging uncomfortably [K5].
- **Build (I):**
  - Loft 4–5 rows from the knit's top row, near vertical and leaning in 10–15°, the inner edge 3–5 mm off his neck.
  - Roll the top at a 3–4 mm radius and return down the inside to the base. That gives 6–8 mm of thickness and no slit.
  - The opening is his neck's girth plus 2–3 cm. The same weights inside and out; no neck_02 or head.
- **Neck weights matter (D [J8], 2023).** Without neck weights, a MetaHuman suit "disappears in the neck" when the head moves.

## D. Hiding the body

- **MetaHuman (D [E1]).** Paint on the body's UVs, white shown and black hidden. It removes the faces "always hidden by the clothing" at assembly. Head faces cannot be hidden.
- **Mutable (D [E2]).** A closed volume clips only faces wholly inside it. Plane-and-Morph instead tucks the inner layer under an edge, since "garments do not need to perfectly match, especially with animation".
- **I:**
  - Whole quads go, so the blocky edge must never show. The margin must beat the edge's worst slide across rest, walk, sit, arms up and reach; the hem rose on arms up and on sitting.
  - Delete faces; never shrink the body. Where skin pokes through inside the margin, add 3–5 mm of ease.
  - In the game the trousers' map covers the body below the welt.
