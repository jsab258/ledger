# Ron's outfit, the third try: the method for each fault (7 October 2026)

After two failed fresh reviews of Ron's plain outfit (F:/LedgerTools/garments/ron_plain_outfit: holes at the back of the neck band with the arms raised; a ragged welt; the trousers through the welt seated; a chest contour; seated trousers as leggings). Research by a fresh agent, read-only. D = source read; S = search summary only; I = inference. No studio talk on rigging a knitted neck band or welt was found; Polycount, Fab, ArtStation and Epic's issue tracker refused the reader (403), so those are summaries.

1. **The neck band.** Epic's weight transfer copies the body's weights only where the garment lies close to the skin and faces its way, filling the rest smoothly [1, D]; collars clip when they ride trunk bones while the skin under them follows the clavicle [2, S]. Likely cause (I): the band is on spine_05 and neck_01 while the knit under it carries clavicle. Recipe: each band column takes the weights of the body point under its base, the same up the column; the same for 5 cm of knit below; corrective joints kept there; blended into the loose knit over 6 to 8 cm. Accept: the band 2 to 8 mm off the neck in all five poses.
2. **A loose knit that reads loose.** Loose is 10 to 15 cm over the chest, oversized 15 or more [3, D]; about 16 cm round the hull, a straight tube from chest to welt. Drape on a smoothed stand-in body (each horizontal slice of the torso replaced by its convex hull), the tube as the rest shape [4, D], bending about five times higher, the yoke pinned, at most 25 frames; then smooth front and back with the edges held and push the knit out to the hull plus 1.5 cm. Accept: every slice below the chest convex. Weights from the stand-in body, smoothed about twenty passes, so the transfer does not press the body's shape back in.
3. **A clean welt.** Edge loops follow the hem (RETOPOLOGY-AND-SKINNING-2026-09-30.md); the welt is the knit's first rows in rib (JUMPER-2026-09-30.md). Cut the drape along a smoothed ring, resample it to 96 to 128 even points, build the welt by script (five rows, 6 cm, to a level ring drawn in 8%, a 2 to 3 mm rolled inside return), smooth the lowest 10 cm of knit with the edge held.
4. **The jumper over the trousers.** The outer garment's inner shell stays outside the one beneath with a minimal gap [5, D]; Epic's Mutable deletes what is always covered and tucks the inner layer under the edge (JUMPER note, D); in Maya the outer layer's weights are copied onto the inner where they overlap [6, S]; MetaHuman's hidden-face map cuts only the body (UE 5.8 source, D). Recipe: each welt point takes the weights of the trousers' point inside it, smoothed round the ring only; the trousers' waist in about 3 mm (a gap of 8 mm or more); trouser faces more than 5 cm above the welt's bottom edge deleted; the jumper draped against a closed copy of the trousers (the open waistband crumpled the knit in the second try).
5. **Seated trousers.** Games skin close garments and blend wrinkle normal maps by joint angle [8, S]; Unreal's ML Deformer needs training per garment [9, D]; Epic advises no cloth simulation on assembled outfits (library note). In UE 5.8 a Pose Driver set to drive curves reads thigh and calf [10, D]; a curve named like a morph target or material parameter drives it [11, D]; followers of the leader pose receive the body's curves (SkeletalMeshComponent.cpp about 2488 to 2513, bPropagateCurvesToFollowers, D). Blender: pose him seated, run 20 frames of cloth from the skinned trousers (waist pinned, the body colliding), bring the result to the rest pose by inverting each point's blended bone matrix (I), split per leg into shape keys sit_L and sit_R exported as morph targets, and model a 1 to 2 cm knee bag.

**Left to Unreal:** the Pose Driver and curves, the wrinkle-map blend in the twill material, the body's hidden-face map, the corrective joints, the packaged test. No Chaos cloth on this outfit.
