# Making a 1990 flat cap that reads as one (research note, 29 September 2026)

The clothing session asked for this after two blind reviews failed the Florent cap sewn round Ron. A separate helper had the problem, not a theory, for about thirty minutes; it builds on TROUSERS-AND-CAP-2026-09-29.md without repeating it. Every source was read on 29 September 2026. **D** means documented, with its source. **I** means the helper's inference. "Search summary" means only a search engine's summary was seen, not the page.

## In short

- **Third attempt:**
  - **Lay the band forward, don't stand it up.** Start the front band lying forward over the peak at about 25 to 30°.
  - **Weld the front.** Weld its seam to the top (the crown's front edge) to the peak's outer edge, 3 to 8 mm in, and fix that front.
  - **Project the rest.** Project only the top and the band's sides and back, with a bending term and no downward pull. Keep Florent for sizes and UVs (I).
- **Why it failed (I, from the pattern's numbers).** The band's upper edge is 1.72 times its lower (422 against 245 mm). Smooth, it can only be a wide cone reaching about 7 cm forward, over the peak, and rising about 3.5 cm. Standing on the brow it is 7.7 cm tall, which matches "4 to 5 cm too high". Its spare length gathers into the drawstring puckers and back-corner lumps.
- **Real caps pull the top forward onto the peak** and stitch or snap it there. Florent's last step does the same (D [2][7][8]).
- **Proportions.** The crown stands 4 to 6 cm above the front sweatband. The peak is 3 to 5.5 cm deep, and the cap about 26 cm front to back at size 59 (D [13][15], retailers, weak). Florent's peak is about 57 mm at the centre (D [3]; arithmetic I).
- **Florent is a flat cap** by its authors' account. No flat-cap pattern under MIT, CC0, CC-BY or public domain turned up (D [1][7][10][11]; I).
- **No bending stiffness, no flat crown.** Florent says "thicker fabrics shape better". Blender's cross-edge bending is weak at small angles; use a dihedral or isometric term (D [1][22]; I).
- **Weld the joint** rather than pulling two meshes together. Finish it as a rolled edge with topstitching 2 to 4 mm in (D [14], weak; I).
- **Games shape stiff hats** (stiff cloth, then sculpt) and attach them rigidly. No flat-cap-specific breakdown was found (D, weak [18][19][20][23]; I).

## A. How a real flat cap is built

- **Pieces.** A top, a side band and a peak (D [7][10][11]). Hobby patterns stitch the crown to the brim's edge (D [9]). A retailer calls the crown "single-piece or two-piece moulded", firm enough to stay flat when flexed (D [13], weak). The Kangol 504 is knitted in one piece and blocked, so its shape is moulded, not cut (search summary [16]).
- **Top to peak.** It is sewn down, snapped, or simply laid over the peak. Sewing or snapping keeps the cap on in wind; unsnapped, it looks like a baker boy (D [8], posts of 2 June 2021). MadCap uses a 1 cm snap (D [7]). The peak is buckram or canvas, topstitched near its edge (D [13], weak).
- **Cheese cutter.** Its peak "starts right at the top of the hat", making a wedge seen from the side (D [12]), as at Smith's Dock (I).
- **How far the top reaches over the peak.** No source gives a figure. The reviewers read the photographs as nearly to the edge (I).
- **Sizes.** The crown is measured "from the sweatband at the front centre to the highest point": 4 to 6 cm; the peak 3 to 4 cm (D [13], weak). Lock & Co.'s Gill has a 5.5 cm peak and "diameter 26 cm" at size 59 (D [15]). So the peak shows about 5 cm ahead of the brow (I).
- **Museums.** No museum record found gives a flat cap's measurements. The V&A's Dunn & Co. cap has none online (D [17]).

## B. How game and film artists do it

- A Peaky Blinders piece used Marvelous Designer's Adhesive Lining on the cap for "that realistic, structured look" (D [18]). Another made its clothes in Marvelous Designer, then detailed them in ZBrush (D [19]).
- A paid course covers a flat cap: rigid fabrics, fold angles on seams, and "flattening hat tops with internal lines" (search summary [20]). Fold strength sets how firmly a seam holds its angle (search summary [21]).
- Rigid pieces go on a head socket (search summary [23]).
- **For us (I).** Crisp edges and a flat top come from stiffness, fixed fold angles, or sculpting, never from cloth with no bending resistance. Once shaped, skin the cap to the head bone, with no Chaos cloth.

## C. Florent

- FreeSewing's pieces are a top, a side, a brim top and bottom, and a plastic brim under 1 mm. It recommends thick cloth, or interfacing for light cloth (D [1]). The cap "extends lower at the back of the head" for "better hold" (D [4]).
- **The code (D [3]).**
  - The side is cut on a fold at the centre front, 0.126 × base deep (base is about 592 mm for a 58 cm head, so about 75 mm).
  - The brim is (0.226 − 0.13) × base deep, about 57 mm.
  - The brim top is offset 3 mm past the brim's edge.
- **Instructions (D [2]).** Step 7 sews the brim to the head opening. Step 12 pins the brim top to the front of the body and stitches it from the lining side. So the band is meant to fold forward onto the peak; ours stood on the forehead (I).
- **Makers.** The showcases (2018 to 2021) and the issue tracker contain no complaints about its shape (D [5][6]). The photographs themselves could not be viewed.
- **Changing it (I).** Probably unnecessary: laid forward, the band's flare already brings the crown's front near the peak's edge. If the crown still overshoots, cut the band's centre front from 77 to about 60 mm, taken off the head edge and tapered to nothing at the ear. Then recheck the opening.
- **Alternatives.** MadCap allows small-business and family use only (D [7]). Sew Very Crafty and sew-ing.com state no licence (D [10][11]).

## D. What would most likely fix the four faults (all I)

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| Start the band lying forward over the peak, its centre front pinned to the peak's top (Florent's step 12) | Removes the height, the step behind the peak and the puckers at their cause |
| Weld the band-to-top seam to the peak's edge as shared, evenly spaced vertices | Ends the ragged joint, where a sewing pull fights the edge lengths |
| Bending term, with flat rest angles from the pattern; the peak locked | Keeps the top flat and the band a smooth cone; no bell, no back-corner knots |
| Drop the downward pull; hold the top taut from the front seam to the nape | Stops the top sagging onto the skull's dome |
| Sew to the peak's very edge while the band still stands | Not enough on its own: it tears again |
| A lining as a second simulated layer | No: it adds collisions. Add thickness afterwards with Solidify |
| Model the crown directly; the pattern gives only its size and UVs | The fallback if this attempt fails |
