# Shoe toe and lacing: numbers for Darren's trainers and Sheila's derbies (30 September 2026)

D = documented (source number in brackets). I = my inference. L = shoe or last length. Hb = height of the top of the last at the ball line, above the insole, about 42 mm on an average adult foot [3].

## In short

- **Build a last, not a swollen foot.** A last's toe is only about as tall as the big toe itself. The top line falls steadily from the instep to the tip and never rises (D 2,3,4). The current script rises to the tip, and that is the dome.
- **Toe top, side view:** Hb at the ball line (28% of L from the toe); about 0.75 Hb halfway to the tip (I); 0.55 to 0.65 Hb at the big toe, 10% of L from the toe, which is 24 mm for a woman and 26.5 mm for a man (D 2,4); and a nose only 10 to 15 mm tall at the tip (I). Add 2 to 3 mm of leather on top (I).
- **Plan view.** The widest part is the slanting ball line, at 63.5% of L from the heel on the little-toe side and 72.5% on the big-toe side (D 2). The outside edge curves in from there. The inside edge stays nearly straight to 90%, then rounds off. At 10% of L from the toe the width is about 0.7 of the ball width, and the tip lines up with the second toe (I).
- **Length and spring.** The last extends 16.5 mm (women) or 20 mm (men) beyond the foot (D 2), so the present 12 to 16 mm is about right. Toe spring: 10 mm on the trainer (D 5), 10 to 12 mm on her derby (I).
- **Trainer:**
  - 7 eyelet pairs (D 7), 12 to 14 mm apart, so the lace row is 75 to 85 mm long (I);
  - the throat 30 to 35% of L from the toe, just behind the ball (I);
  - eyelets about 50 mm apart across the laced shoe (D 6), leaving a 15 to 20 mm gap with the tongue showing (I);
  - eyestays 15 to 20 mm wide; the tongue 45 to 55 mm wide and standing 15 to 25 mm above the top eyelet; the collar a padded roll 8 to 12 mm thick (I).
- **Derby:**
  - the throat 38 to 44% of L from the toe (D 2), about 90 to 105 mm on a 240 mm shoe;
  - 4 eyelet pairs at least 10 mm apart (D 8), in practice 12 to 14 mm (I), so a 40 to 50 mm lace row;
  - eyelets about 25 mm apart across the laced shoe (D 6), so the facings nearly meet (I);
  - the tongue about 5 mm above the facings (D 8).

## (a) Toe of the last

- **Foot scans** (D 3; 20 adults, mean foot 250 mm): big toe 25.9 mm tall, ball 42.0 mm.
- **Last toe thickness**, insole included (D 4, search summary only): 26.5 mm for men, 24.0 mm for women. So the last's toe barely clears the toes.
- **Chinese last design** (D 2): landmarks along L as above, little toe at 78%; the forepart's bottom tilted up 8 to 10 degrees; women's lasts lose 1 mm of toe spring per 10 mm of heel.
- **Springline** (D 5): a round-toe formal last has an 18 mm heel and 19 mm of spring.
- **Kaye (1994)** (D 9, summary only): shoes about 44 mm deep at 5 cm from the tip, inside or outside unstated.
- **Not found:** a published table of toe heights at set points, or a women's derby toe spring.

## (b) Lacing

- **British pattern cutting** (D 10, 11): the vamp point (the front of the throat) is 3/4 of standard last length from the counter point, measured across the side of the last.
- **Chinese last design** (D 2): women's court shoes put the throat at 26 to 30% against the derby's 38 to 44%, which shows the figure counts from the toe (I).
- **Eyelets** (D 8): 1.5 cm in from the edge; the inner facing sits 2 to 3 mm higher.
- **Rayne brogue** (D 12): 255 × 75 × 78 mm and unfinished; its eyelets are not recorded.
- **Not found:** documented eyestay widths or trainer tongue heights.

## (c) Game-resolution modelling

- **Pieces** (I): eyestays, facings, tongue, collar and heel counter as separate shells 1 to 2 mm thick over one smooth upper, each edge given a 2-segment rounded bevel of 0.5 to 1 mm so it catches light instead of reading as a slit. Laces are real tubes crossing a padded, wedge-shaped tongue; the collar is a rolled tube; the heel counter is one shell running cleanly into the sole. Stitching, perforations and eyelet rims go in the normal map.
- **Polycount** (D 13, summary only; the page sits behind a bot check, not bypassed): shrinkwrap onto a toe-less, last-like foot form, with 2 supporting loops at the toe and the ball.
- **Budget:** game shoes run about 4,000 to 10,000 triangles (D 14).
- **Against the dome** (I): take the toe's outline from the sole's plan and the last's falling top line, never from the toe mesh. Hide or flatten the body's toes under the shoe. A crisp turn where the upper meets the sole reads as a low toe from a distance.

## (d) Free bases and licences

- **Sketchfab** (D 15): every downloadable shoe last found is CC Attribution, for example "Foot Form / Shoe Last for Footwear Design" (8,950 faces, 2024). CC BY is not on the allowlist for 3D, so using one needs Jafar's decision. The CC0 filter found no lasts, only museum scans such as "Priest Karol Wojtyła's sports shoes", which ties to a real person and is not advised.
- **Poly Haven:** no footwear (D 16).
- **Meshy's free "CC0" shoes:** not allowed; the allowlist admits Meshy on paid tiers only (D 17).
- **Last generators:** no free parametric generator found.
- **MetaHuman:** the in-engine creator ships "an outfit" (D 19). Whether it includes shoes could not be established; the builder can check the engine's MetaHuman content. MetaHuman assets are allowlisted, but the licence page refused the fetch and was not read. The legacy web creator offered shoes (D 20, summary only). Fab's MetaHuman shoe packs cost money, so they are Jafar's decision.
