# Work trousers and a flat cap, 1990 (research note, 29 September 2026)

The clothing session asked for this before cutting Ron's dark grey work trousers and remaking the flat cap Jafar rejected ("not like this": too round, crown standing up, plain wool). A separate helper was given the problem, not a theory, and worked for about thirty minutes. Every source was read on 29 September 2026. **D** means documented, with its source. **I** means the helper's inference. "Search summary" means only a search engine's summary was seen, not the page itself.

## In short

- **The cap.** A true flat cap has a low crown pulled forward and snapped or sewn down onto a short, stiff peak. An eight-panel cap has a domed crown. Our rejected cap looked like the second (D [S3][S4][S5]).
- **Florent has one option** (head ease, 0 to 5%) and one measurement (head). Its crown is a fixed drawing scaled to the head, so no setting makes it lie forward. Extend the top piece's front ourselves and fix it to the brim (D [F1][F2]; I).
- **No newsboy or eight-panel cap exists in FreeSewing**; its only other hat is Holmes, a deerstalker (D [F3]).
- **Trousers: use Charlie** (built on Titan) for belt loops and a fly. The leg runs "straight down from the knee"; there is no hem-width, taper or pleat option (D [F4][F5][F6]).
- **Ron's waistband sits under his belly**: lower the front rise with waist height and waistband angle, and measure the waist where the band sits (D [T1]; I).
- **Drape once, then skin.** Pin the waist, drape in the pose with the boots as colliders, then skin to the body. Games skin close trousers and keep cloth simulation for loose parts (D [G1][G2][B1][B2]; I).
- **Weights:** work twill (65% polyester, 35% cotton) about 245 to 290 gsm; cap tweed about 300 to 450 gsm (D [W1][W2][W3]; I).
- **No dated photograph found shows how an ordinary 1990 British work trouser was cut.** The best dated sets are Peter Fryer's Smith's Dock (1990 to 1991) and Steve Thornton's Grimsby (1990). Someone must look at them before cutting (I).

**Cap.**

- A flat cap sits "low and flush", "skims the skull", and has a short, firm peak. A bakerboy "blouse[s] over" its peak, with eight panels meeting at a button [S3] (D; a retailer's blog, August 2026, so weak).
- The crown is "pulled forward and fastened to the brim with a snap or seam". The peak is about 5 to 6.5 cm deep, stiffened with buckram, plastic or interfacing [S4] (search summary).
- MadCap's Classic Flat Cap pattern has three pieces (top, side band, visor), with the top fixed to the visor by a 1 cm snap [S5] (D).
- The cloth is tweed or wool, and the cap is "associated with… working-class men" [S6] (D).
- Colours: browns, greys, green-flecked tweed and herringbone (I, from [P8][W3]).

**Trousers.**

- Terylene and other easy-care synthetics came into British menswear in the 1960s [T3] (D).
- The Dickies 874 (US, 1967) is a rigid twill, "creased at the center"; 8.5 oz, 65/35 polyester-cotton, high rise, straight leg [W1] (D for the crease; the rest is a search summary).
- 1980s fashion trousers were often pleated polyester [T4] (D, fashion rather than work).
- For Ron (I):
  - dark grey polyester-blend twill, belt loops and a belt;
  - a flat front or one shallow pleat;
  - a straight leg, no turn-ups, and a pressed crease gone soft;
  - the waistband under the belly;
  - the hem on the boot with a half break [T2].

## B. Patterns (FreeSewing, MIT)

**Florent** [F1][F2]:

- One option, headEase (2%, range 0 to 5%), and one measurement, head.
- Pieces: a top (two fabric, two lining, with a back dart), a side, a brim top and bottom, and a plastic brim insert.
- The code scales a hand-drawn shape until its seam matches the head (D).
- The instructions attach "the brim top to the front of the body of the cap" (D): the crown meets the brim at its back edge rather than lying over it (I).
- **The fix (I):** extend the top's front edge by about 3 to 5 cm, make it a little wider than the brim, and sew or pin it to the brim's upper face 1 to 2 cm behind the brim's edge, as a snap does.
- Our earlier lumps came from starting the pieces already bent round the head. Keep the flat pattern as the rest shape [L1] (D).

**Trousers:**

| Design | Verdict | What matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Titan** [F4][F6] | A block, not a garment | The measurements below. kneeEase 6% (1 to 25), seatEase 2% (0 to 10), waistHeight 100% (0 to 100), waistbandAngle −20° to 20°, crotchDrop, fitKnee. Unless fitKnee is on, the knee is at least the front seat width. |
| **Charlie** [F5][F7] | **Use** (I) | Titan's measurements. kneeEase 15% (10 to 30), seatEase 5% (0 to 10), waistHeight −4% (−15 to 40), waistbandAngle, crotchDrop, beltLoops 6 to 12, fly. No pleat option; slanted pockets set into the side seam. |
| Paco, Theo [F3] | No | Paco is a summer trouser; Theo is deprecated (an Aldrich draft with 5 options). |

- **Measurements** for both: waist, waistBack, waistToSeat, waistToHips, waistToUpperLeg, waistToKnee, waistToFloor, seat, seatBack, crossSeam, crossSeamFront, knee (D).
- **Settings for Ron (I):**
  - kneeEase about 22 to 25%;
  - seatEase 6 to 8%;
  - crotchDrop 3 to 5%;
  - a low waistHeight with a positive front waistbandAngle.
- Take the waist measurement at the band's level; FreeSewing has no belly measurement (I).
- Twig & Tale took 11 cm (4.4 in) off the front rise for a size-56 wearer who wears the waistband under the belly [T1] (D).

## C. Simulation

- **Waist.** Use a pin group, or a shrinking waist group inside a contracting belt collider, or the trousers fall [B1][B2] (D).
- **Crotch and legs.** Drape once, in the pose, and apply; do not simulate walking [B1] (D). Crotch trouble in motion is why studios skin trousers (I).
- **Belly.** Without the lower front rise, drag lines point at the belly and the front is pulled down [T1] (D, search summary). It needs a close collider (I).
- **Knee and hem.** Folds over the boot are the "break"; a half break rests the hem on the shoe [T2] (D). Make the boots colliders. Bunching comes from extra length, not width (I).
- **Cap.** The peak is stiff: buckram, card, plastic or wire [S3][S4] (D). Give the brim maximum bending stiffness, or leave it out of the simulation. Pin the sweatband line to the head, and sew the crown's front to the brim (I).
- **Weights** (1 oz/yd² = 33.9 gsm):
  - Russell J001M work twill, 65/35: 245 gsm [W2] (D).
  - Dickies 874 at 8.5 oz: about 288 gsm [W1] (D).
  - Jacket tweed at 9 to 14 oz: about 305 to 475 gsm [W3] (D).
  - Harris tweed: 400 to 480 gsm [W4] (search summary).

## D. Unreal

- Games "usually" simulate only big cloth such as capes. Close garments are skinned, with blended normal-map wrinkles, as in Uncharted 4 [G1] (D).
- Epic's 5.8 Clothing Tool: pick the sections that become cloth, paint max distance and backstop, collide with a physics asset [G2] (D).
- Tight parts such as trousers are "fully rigged"; Chaos goes on coat tails [G3] (search summary).
- **For us (I):** skin the trousers through MetaHuman's resizing graph without Chaos; at most, a small max distance at the hem, and none under the jacket.
