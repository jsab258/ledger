# Boots, trainers and flat shoes for skinned MetaHumans, 1990 (research note, 30 September 2026)

The clothing session asked for this before its first footwear. A separate helper was given the problem, not a theory, and worked read only for about twenty-five minutes. Every source was read on 30 September 2026. **D** means documented, with its source; **I** means the helper's inference. "Search summary" means only a search engine's summary was seen. It builds on SKINNING-TROUSERS and JUMPER and does not repeat them.

## In short

- **Recipe (I, from the D numbers below).**
  - Offset the body's own foot outward: 4–5 mm ease [L1], then 2–3 mm of leather.
  - Toe: 15–20 mm beyond the longest toe, with 8–12 mm of toe spring (5 mm for Sheila).
  - Sole bottom: one flat plane, parallel to the bare sole.
  - Heel stack: Ron 28 mm, Darren 25 mm, Sheila 20 mm. The visible forepart sole is thinner: 15, 15 and 8 mm. The upper comes down over the difference.
  - Weights: foot_l 1.0 behind the ball line and ball_l 1.0 in front. Blend over 30–40 mm, along a line slanted like the real joints (inner side further forward). No toe bones.
  - Ron's shaft: blend foot into the lowest calf joint over 50 mm at the ankle.
- **MetaHumans stand barefoot on the floor since 5.6.** A sole sits below the ground unless the builder adds a Component Based Offset of the heel stack (D [E1]).
- **Keep the foot flat.** Heels need a changed foot pose (D [F1]). At these low heels, build the heel into a flat-bottomed sole instead (I).
- **Unreal must not re-copy weights or resize the shoes.** Copying from the bare foot would hand the toe box to the toe bones (I, from SKINNING-TROUSERS).
- **Budget:** a shipped MetaHuman shoe is 2,104 polygons (D [F3]); aim for 2,000–4,000 triangles.
- **Hide the foot** with the binary Body Hidden Face Map (D [E2]). Keep a 20–30 mm margin below each collar (I).
- **Ron: a plain black steel-toe derby work boot,** ankle high. The 8-eye Dr Martens read as youth wear then (D [R6]); riggers were usually tan (D [R7]).

## A. What they looked like

**Ron.**
- NCB-issued heavy leather steel-toe boots, 1981, 299 × 110 × 174 mm (D [R1]).
- The V&A's Dr Martens, about 1982: black, "eight pairs of metal-bound eyelet holes", 18.5 cm high at the back (D [R2]). Their sole is about 1.5 cm (D [R10]).
- **I:** a derby-cut boot, 15–16 cm at the back, with 5–6 pairs of eyelets or eyelets and hooks. A broad, domed steel toe, a padded collar, a heavy rubber sole with a separate heel, and no yellow stitching.
- No dated photograph of dockers' feet was found; the 1989 strike archive at Report Digital refused access.

**Darren.**
- The V&A holds "worn white leather trainers", 1986 (D [R3]).
- Reebok's Classic Leather dates from 1983 (D [R9]); Hi-Tec's Silver Shadow (1981) was "Britain's top-selling running shoe" (D [R8]).
- A flat trainer last has 10–12 mm of heel pitch (D [L2]).
- **I:** cut below the ankle bones, with a padded collar, a heel tab and 6–7 pairs of eyelets. A rounded, fairly broad toe. No stripes or logos.

**Sheila.**
- Women wore lace-up brogues as much as men in the 1980s (D [R6]).
- Two Rayne women's brogues at the V&A (D [R4][R5]): March 1989, 75 × 255 × 78 mm (height, length, width); late 1980s, chestnut suede, 80 × 260 × 82 mm.
- **I:** a plain brown derby or Oxford: 3–4 pairs of eyelets, a round toe, a 20–25 mm stacked heel, a 6–8 mm sole.

**Toe shape (D).** A medium round toe leaves about 20 mm beyond the toes, a blunt last 10 mm [L3]. Toe boxes are widest at the ball; the loaded foot widens by up to 1.3 cm [L4].

## B. How game artists make shoes for skinned characters

- **A modelled solid, not cloth.** A shipped MetaHuman shoe is "Rigged to the MetaHuman base skeleton", resizes itself, and comes with an occlusion mask for the feet (D [F3]). Upper and sole are separate pieces with their own materials (I).
- **Heel height (D [F1]).** MetaHuman feet are posed flat. High heels need "different feet pose": fixed by a key pose or Control Rig in the Animation Blueprint, or by reshaping the shoe.
- **Sole thickness (D [E1][F2]).** Since 5.6, "all MetaHuman animation content is now standardized as barefoot". Epic's fix is a Component Based Offset "to account for the sole height". A 2026 forum answer adds bone or IK offsets [F2].
- **Skinning (I).**
  - Heel, quarters and the sole under the arch: foot only.
  - Toe box: ball only, kept rigid; the toes move unseen inside.
  - Copy the upper's weights straight down into the sole, column by column, so the two never part.
  - Keep ankle_fwd and ankle_bck out of shoes. Ron's shaft may take a little if its front collapses.
- **Hiding (D [E2]).** The map is binary. **I:** hide the foot to 20 mm below the shoe collar, 30 mm below the boot top.

## C. The simplest scripted build (Blender 4.5, Python), and what reviewers see first

**Build (I):**
1. Copy the body's foot, cut at the ankle (shoes) or at 16 cm (boot). Close the toes into one mitten.
2. Take sections every 10–15 mm. Scale each outward by the ease and leather, bottom edge held on the sole plane.
3. Extend 3–4 sections into the toe allowance, rounded in plan and lifted into toe spring. The toe box is 25–35 mm high at the tip for the boot, 22–28 mm for the others.
4. Loft the upper as one quad surface. Solidify it 2–3 mm, with a turned-in collar and a lining, so the opening never shows backfaces.
5. Sole: extend the upper's outline 3–5 mm for the welt edge (1–2 mm on trainers), then down to the flat plane. Cut a heel block with a breast, and lift the waist 3–5 mm.
6. Eyelets as low rings, 4–5 mm across; laces as flat bars over a separate tongue, with a bow and ends. Stitching goes in the normal map.

**What reviewers see first (I):**
- A toe too pointed, short or bulbous; a flat toe box; no toe spring.
- A sole too thin, or with no welt or heel step, so the shoe reads as a slipper.
- A sole off the floor or sunk into it.
- Bending under the arch or at each toe, not at the ball.
- Ankle skin through the collar when walking or sitting.
- The trouser hem crossing the boot.
- Laces too thick or even; "scuffed" shoes left pristine.
