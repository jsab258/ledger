# How clothing for photoreal characters is made, and what MetaHuman expects (30 September 2026)

Thirty minutes of reading. D = documented, with its source number; I = my inference.

## The pipeline in short

"Free" means Blender plus Unreal and nothing paid.

1. **Reference and design:** photographs of the real garment, its pattern and its fabric. *Free: yes.*
2. **Pattern or blockout:** big garments are sewn from pattern pieces in Marvelous Designer or CLO; small, stiff pieces are sculpted. *Free: partly. Blender's cloth has no real pattern tool.*
3. **Detail:** the drape is simulated, then folds, seams, stitching and wear are sculpted on top. *Free: yes.*
4. **Retopology:** an automatic first pass, then quads fixed by hand, with loops at the joints. *Free: yes, but it is hand work.*
5. **UVs:** seams go where the real garment's seams are. *Free: yes.*
6. **Bake** normal and ambient-occlusion maps from the high mesh to the low one. *Free: yes (Blender Cycles).*
7. **Texture:** studios use Substance Painter. *Free: partly (Blender painting; InstaMAT, free under a revenue threshold).*
8. **Skin:** copy weights from the body, then correct them. *Free: yes (Blender Data Transfer, or Unreal's TransferSkinWeights).*
9. **Stop the body poking through:** hide covered body faces and settle the layers. *Free: yes.*
10. **Simulate loose parts only:** hems, coat tails, skirts. *Free: yes (Chaos Cloth).*
11. **LODs:** MetaHuman expects four. *Free: yes (Unreal's Remesh node).*
12. **Deliver as a MetaHuman Outfit Asset:** one source size per body, resized to the rest. *Free: yes, in Unreal only.*

## 1. Stages of a professional garment

- Big garments are made in Marvelous Designer: trousers, jacket, hood and collar. Small ones are made in ZBrush: pads, belts, shoes and gloves. ZBrush then adds folds, seams, stitches and damage. After that come Quad Draw retopology in Maya, UVs, a bake in Marmoset and texturing in Substance. D [15][16]
- Some artists skip simulation and sculpt every fold. D [17]
- Another route is to sculpt a flat jacket with no folds, retopologise it, and cut UVs where the seams are. D [16]
- Retopology: auto-remesh the flat pattern pieces, then fix by hand. Every face must be a quad, and sewn edges need matching vertex counts. D [18] Auto-remeshing is a start, never the finish. I
- Budgets: about 100,000 triangles for a whole character without hair or weapon "meets the requirements of modern PC games" D [16]. Ryse's hero was 85,000 D [25, summary only], and Resident Evil 2's jacket about 43,000 D [26, summary only, unverified].
- **I found no documented per-garment budgets.** My estimate for a hero at LOD0: jacket 15–30k triangles, shirt and trousers 8–15k each, long coat 20–40k. I
- Blender bakes "selected to active" with a cage D [23]. InstaMAT is free under US$100k a year in revenue, with attribution D [24, summary only].
- MetaHuman expects four LODs (0–3), made by hand or by the Remesh node at 50%, 30% and 10%. D [3]

## 2. Skinning, clipping and layers

- **Weights**
  - Resizable (outfit) clothing "does a simple skin weight transfer from the body." This overwrites any custom skinning, Control Rig or RBAN, so parametric clothing needs no skinning beforehand. D [1] (5.7 pages, still describing 5.6)
  - The TransferSkinWeights node offers ClosestPointOnSurface or InpaintWeights. InpaintWeights is the default in 5.8 and has a LayeredMeshSupport option for inner and outer layers that sit close together. D [7]
  - Inpainting comes from Epic's 2023 paper. The paper says closest-point transfer struggles with loose garments and needs hand painting. D [12] Epic's tutorial still uses closest-point. D [3]
  - For fixed skeletal clothing: transfer, then fix shoulders, armpits, elbows, hips and knees by hand. No dated studio source found. I
- **Hiding the body**
  - MetaHuman's Body Hidden Face Map is painted on the body's UVs, white for shown and black for hidden. Hidden faces are removed at assembly. Head faces cannot be hidden. D [4]
  - Mutable deletes unseen body parts and tucks trousers into boots. D [22, summary only]
  - Star Citizen hides body zones in the shader. D [21]
- **The source body** needs clearance at the armpits and thigh gap, or resizing fails. D [2]
- **Layers**
  - Kingdom Come: Deliverance adapts outer layers over inner ones automatically. D [19, abstract only]
  - Cyberpunk 2077 squashes lower layers with a shape key. D [20, summary only]
  - At Naughty Dog, the joint count limited layered clothing. D [13]

## 3. Simulation

- Capes and long hanging garments are simulated. Tight clothing stays skinned. Uncharted 4 blended wrinkle normal maps instead of simulating. D [14]
- The Last of Us Part II uses a hidden low-resolution sim mesh that drives the visible one, blended with the animation. It moves Ellie's shirttail as she runs. Cinematics use offline simulation. D [13]
- **Legacy Chaos:** paint Max Distance (0 means skinned; 100 means free up to 1 m), backstop and anim drive, with the Physics Asset for collision. D [9]
- **Cloth assets:** an optional, simplified single-sided sim mesh drives the render mesh. D [1]
- **Unreal 5.8:**
  - The Cloth Asset is Production-Ready, the Outfit Asset is **Beta**, and the Panel Cloth Editor is deprecated. D [8]
  - A new VertexColorToAttribute node imports weight maps painted as vertex colours in another program, so they can be painted in Blender. D [8]
- **Tight garments:** I found no shipped game that simulates jeans, a shirt or a fitted jacket whole. At most a hem, an open front or coat tails. I

## 4. What MetaHuman expects (Unreal 5.6–5.8)

- **Two kinds of clothing:**
  - a Chaos **Outfit Asset**, which is parametric and resizes with the body;
  - a **skeletal mesh**, which is fixed to one body. D [1][5]
- **How an outfit resizes:** it warps its cloth assets from the source body to the target body. Realistic clothing warps badly when the bodies differ a lot, so make several source sizes; the system picks the nearest D [1]. 5.7 made resizing faster D [11, summary only].
- **The source body:** make a MetaHuman Character in Unreal, use *Export Combined Skel Mesh* (the name ends `_CombinedSkelMesh`), keep one shared skeleton in the working folder, and export the body as FBX to the modelling program. To convert old clothing, Epic recommends 4 or 6 of its 18 compatibility bodies. D [2]
- **No pose is stated** in the pages I read. I assume the garment is built on the exported mesh as it comes out. I
- **Bringing the garment in:**
  - The garment comes in as FBX imported as a static mesh; a skinned FBX is "not recommended". It can also be USD from Marvelous Designer or CLO with sim and render meshes, or a sim mesh made by hand.
  - Build a Cloth Asset: StaticMeshImport → TransferSkinWeights (from the body) → ClothAssetTerminal, plus LODs.
  - Build an Outfit Asset from the Resizable Outfit template. Its name must match its folder.
  - Under Sized Outfit Source, give one index per size, each paired with its body.
  - Resizing uses 1,500 interpolation points by default; 1,000 to 2,500 is recommended. D [3]
- **Testing:** wear the outfit in MetaHuman Creator's Outfit Clothing slot and change the body. A Wardrobe Item (WI_) carries the colour parameters and the Body Hidden Face Map. D [4]
- **Rules:** skeletal clothing must use the MetaHuman base skeleton with no bones re-parented, and every outfit size needs its body D [6]. Since 5.6 the +2 cm offset is gone, so shoe soles sit below the ground D [5].
- **Marvelous Designer's route** follows the same steps; MD 2025.2 needs Unreal 5.6 or later D [10]. **Epic has no Blender guide**: its pages cover only the Unreal side D [1].

## 5. What a small team with free tools can do

- **Everything above is possible except true pattern drafting.** Blender's cloth with sewing springs only approximates Marvelous Designer or CLO. CC0 base garments can be kitbashed, within our allowlist. I
- **Done by hand, never procedurally:** the final retopology of garments that bend [18], UV seam placement, joint weight fixes (inpainting reduces them [12]), and judging the look. I
- **Left to procedure:** LODs, weight transfer, resizing and baking. D [3][7], I
- **Cheapest sound route for plain 1990 clothes:** model on each exported body; skin rather than simulate; paint the hidden-face map; make one Outfit Asset size per body; simulate only loose coat tails or skirts. I

**Not found:** a GDC or SIGGRAPH talk giving the full pipeline with numbers (GDC Vault is paywalled; Polycount showed a bot check, which I did not pass); per-garment triangle budgets; Epic's required pose; 5.8 versions of Epic's parametric clothing guide (those links redirect, so I read 5.7); a shipped game that simulates tight garments.
