## Piece 1: the empty unit

Questions: (a) What did a shut shop look like in northern Britain 1988 to 1992: boards, whitewash, shutters, to-let boards? (b) Sizes and materials of boards and sheets. (c) Colours and signage left on the fascia. (d) What was in the window. (e) Wear: fly-posting, graffiti, dirt, broken glass. (f) How long units stood empty.

Answers:
- Peter Marshall's Shoreditch, Dalston and Stoke Newington photographs, 1986 to 1990: "innumerable empty shops many of them faced with graffitied corrugated iron" (text, not a caption). Source: https://flashbak.com/photographs-shoreditch-hackney-london-1980s-1990s-437283/ . Answers (a) and (e) for east London only; the page's captions give place and date, not condition, so no picture detail was read. Two captions bear on the piece: "Window, Shoreditch High St, 1990" and "Posters, Swanfield St, 1990" (images not examined: unverified).
- Alan Dein, East End, 1988: "some premises had been empty for more than five years"; "the diminishing, decaying local shops". Source: https://designyoutrust.com/2022/03/fascinating-photographs-of-old-shopfronts-taken-in-the-east-end-of-london-in-1988/ . Answers (f) for London. A secondary page; it describes no signage, boarding or paint.
- Not answered by any source reached: board sizes, sheet materials (chipboard or ply: LEDGER's own earlier note says "no dated 1990 source found", broken-window-look/SUMMARY-2026-09-29.md line 13), whitewash, to-let board look, fascia colours, window contents (a), (b), (c), (d).
- Northern evidence: none found. Unverified that the London pattern holds for a northern port town.

Dismissed as out of period (read, not evidence): Geograph 7359172 (Hull, 8 Dec 2022) https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/7359172 ; Geograph 4088729 (Bourne, 26 Jul 2014) https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/4088729 . Both give a "To Let" fact in the text only, none of the visible detail.

Unreached: Historic England archive record MBC01/04/092 (empty former Burton store, St Albans, "1970s to 1980s": a search summary only; page returned 403, so not used as evidence) https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/photos/item/MBC01/04/092 .

## Piece 2: the upper windows

Questions: (a) Which window types sat above shops in a Victorian northern terrace in 1990: timber sash (2/2, 1/1, horns), casement, replacement aluminium or plastic? (b) Proportions and sizes. (c) Paint and colours. (d) Glazing, curtains, nets, blinds, blinds in the dark. (e) Share replaced by 1990. (f) What sat behind: lit or dark, stockroom or flat. (g) Wear.

Answers:
- (e) A search summary of Historic England's Conservation Bulletin 14, June 1991 reports total UK sales of replacement windows and doors at about £2.06 bn a year, 6,350 suppliers, and "several companies ... doubling their sales every year since the early 1980s". The page itself returned 403; the figures are from the search result only: unverified. https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/conservation-bulletin-14/conservationbulletin14supplement
- (a) uPVC: dates from trade or marketing sites only (search summaries, not read in full, so unverified): a manufacturer says it launched uPVC windows in 1984 (https://www.everest.co.uk/about/history/ ); another trade source says double glazing took off in the mid-1980s ("U-volution of an industry", https://glasstimes.co.uk/featured-articles/u-volution-of-an-industry/ ). Design Buildings' history of glass, read in full: double glazing "did not take off in the UK until the 1970s"; Victorian windows had "fewer glazing bars" once larger panes arrived after the 1850s; float glass by 1960. https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/The_history_of_glass_in_the_UK_and_Ireland . It does not mention uPVC or the 1980s. This supports a mixed street of old sashes and replacements by 1990 only in general terms; no street count.
- Council and Historic England guidance (Historic England, Traditional Windows, 2014/2017) is of the 2010s and, from search summaries, says uPVC replacements often break original proportions and detailing; read in full: none (Chichester copy returned 403; Lancaster PAN14 and sash-case PDFs refused the connection; Peak District page 403). Unverified.
- (b) to (d), (f), (g): not answered by any source reached. LEDGER's own plan lists sash 2/2 and 1/1 with horns, 1970s to 1980s replacement aluminium, 1930s steel, casement (asset-plan/1-BUILDINGS line 112) and net curtains on every third window as the generator's rule (line 267); both are internal claims, not dated-photograph findings (the plan itself marks the bond and similar claims as needing checks against dated photographs, line 382).
