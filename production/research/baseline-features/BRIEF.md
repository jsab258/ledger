# BRIEF: the baseline features every game has had since the PS3 era

STATUS: BRIEF. Written 2026-09-23, before the work, per the standing instruction
that a topic's brief is a file in the topic's folder, in Jafar's words.

## The problem

> This project has studied the unusual systems in depth, perception, memory,
> gossip, the crime layer, and missed ordinary things every game has had since
> the PS3 era. Two were found by accident while playing a 2009 game: people
> turning their head to look at you when you move near them or talk to them, and
> sound that is positional, coming from where its source is and changing as you
> move. Neither exists in our Unreal build. There will be more, and they are
> invisible precisely because every game has them. We cannot afford to keep
> finding them by accident.

## The method

> Work backwards from real games across the years: at least one from around 2009
> to 2011, such as GTA IV, Red Dead Redemption, Uncharted 2 or Assassin's Creed
> II, and then GTA V, Sleeping Dogs, The Last of Us, Red Dead Redemption 2 and
> Kingdom Come Deliverance 2. Enumerate the baseline features a player takes for
> granted, the things only noticed when missing.

At minimum, in his words:

> - how characters move and look, including eye contact, feet that sit on stairs
>   and slopes, turning on the spot, idle variety and reacting when bumped
> - sound, including positional sound, falling off with distance, muffling
>   through walls, echo that depends on the space, footsteps that change with the
>   surface, background ambience and dialogue that lowers other sound
> - the camera, including not passing through walls, smoothing and field of view
> - how people behave around you, including avoiding you, reacting to noise and
>   to you coming close, talking among themselves and using doors
> - the world, including time of day, weather changing what people do, objects
>   that move when pushed and detail that appears without visible popping
> - what the player is told, including prompts, subtitles and feedback when
>   something happens

> Add whatever the list is missing.

## What to establish per feature

> For each feature: whether our Unreal build has it, partly or not, checked
> against the repository rather than assumed; whether Unreal provides it largely
> out of the box, which decides its cost; and which stage of ROADMAP.md it
> belongs to, presentable, the playable slice, or later. Expect nearly
> everything to be in, since these are the floor rather than choices. Rank the
> missing ones by how quickly a player would notice, within the first ten
> minutes first.

## Deliverables

> SUMMARY.md for me, one page in plain words: the missing features a player
> would notice in the first ten minutes, ranked, and which are cheap because
> Unreal provides them. And DELIVERY.md for the studio: the full checklist,
> feature by feature, in a form that can become the per-stage checklist in
> ROADMAP.md.
