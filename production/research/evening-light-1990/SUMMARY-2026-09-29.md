# Evening on a 1990 northern English street, in Unreal 5.8 (research note, 29 September 2026)

**The problem.** The story moves on to "later that week, evening" and the player then talks to people in a yard, but the street stays in bright daylight (the AI tester's run of 29 September flagged it as plainly wrong). The street needs a convincing evening (about 18:30 to 21:00 in autumn: dusk into night) that the game can switch to at run time, at about 60 frames a second at 3440 by 1440 with upscaling, under low-pressure sodium (SOX) lamps. About thirty minutes of research by a separate helper given the problem, saved here by the builder (the helper could not write files). "Mine" marks the helper's own inference, not a source.

## 1. The look

- **Sky.** At 53–54°N, 18:30–19:15 in autumn is overcast dusk: a blue-grey sky fading to slate; by about 20:00 it is fully dark. A cloudy town sky at night is not black: clouds throw the sodium light back down as a dull orange-brown glow, near cities up to about ten times brighter (Kyba and others, via Live Science 2015). (Summer time ended on 28 October 1990: from memory, not verified.)
- **The lamps set the picture.** SOX light is one colour, around 589 nm, so everything it lights is a shade of the same orange: a bright red car and a black car both look black (Wikipedia). Pools of orange under each column, dark gaps between.
- **Size and brightness.** A 35 W SOX lamp gives about 4,550 lm (Philips data); makers quote a nominal 1,700–1,800 K.
- **Warm-up.** A freshly lit lamp glows dim red or pink for a few minutes, then turns yellow-orange (Wikipedia): a detail for 18:30–19:00, as the lamps come on.
- **Windows (mine).** The only real colour comes from windows: whitish fluorescent from shops, warm tungsten behind house curtains.
- **Wet road.** After the lamps, the brightest surface: long vertical streaks under each lamp and lit window.

## 2. The Unreal method

1. **One switch, two or three presets.** The story cuts to evening, so no running day cycle is needed: an overcast day, a dusk (about 19:00) and a night (about 20:30), switched or blended behind the fade. Epic's Day Sequence plugin is marked Experimental in the 5.8 docs; a small blend in code over the same parameters is cheaper and safer.
2. **Sun.** The directional light a few degrees below the horizon for dusk; Sky Atmosphere works out the twilight colours ("Transmittance Min Light Elevation Angle", "Sky Luminance Factor"). At night, the sun off, or at least its shadows.
3. **Sky light.** Real Time Capture needs a Sky Atmosphere; it is time-sliced (about 0.2 ms a frame on PS4 at 128 px) and does not capture volumetric fog. RecaptureSky() costs at least 120 ms: once, during the fade.
4. **Night sky glow.** An unlit sky-dome material tagged IsSky, or an orange-brown height-fog inscattering colour; the sky light's capture then gives a faint orange ambient light.
5. **Exposure (the cause of a grey night).** Histogram auto exposure pulls every scene towards mid-grey. Turn on "Extend default luminance range", then either set the night preset's Min EV100 equal to Max EV100, or add an exposure compensation curve that drops at low average scene EV100 (Epic's 4.25 notes: a moonlit night about 0.8 EV). Starting points (mine): day EV100 11–12; lamp-lit night fixed near 2–3, under-exposed on purpose. Epic: always set up Local Exposure with Lumen; keep its shadow lift modest so the gaps between lamps stay dark.
6. **Sodium lamps.** A point or spot light in each lantern, in lumens (35 W about 4,500 lm), inverse-square falloff, a tight attenuation radius; only the two to four lamps near the player and the yard cast shadows. Lumen picks a few important lights per surface-cache tile, so many lights cost little there. MegaLights (production-ready in 5.8) costs roughly the same whatever the number of lights but needs hardware ray tracing and can blur or ghost.
7. **Windows.** Emissive plus the InteriorCubemap node for cheap rooms; a few real rect or spot lights only where a shop window spills onto the pavement (small bright emissive surfaces make Lumen noisy).
8. **Budget.** Lumen "High" targets 60 fps on current consoles; software ray tracing is the fastest Lumen path; 3440 by 1440 upscaled is near console 1080p internally.

## 3. Low-pressure sodium

- **Colour.** 589 nm has chromaticity (0.569, 0.430): about sRGB (255, 140, 0), linear about (1.0, 0.25, 0.0) (wavelab.tools and the helper's calculation). Not the Temperature slider at 1,700 K, which is broad-spectrum and keeps every colour.
- **Colour loss.** An RGB renderer cannot produce it: it treats the light as red plus green, so surface colours survive (Peters 2025).
- **Workaround (mine).** A global parameter in the master materials that at night blends base colour towards a grey equal to its reflectance near 589 nm (about 0.6 R + 0.4 G), masked off near lit shop windows; a plain post-process desaturation is cheaper but would grey the windows too.

## 4. Pitfalls

- Auto exposure brightening night back to a grey day (step 5).
- Local exposure flattening the pools of light.
- Lumen noise in dark areas lit only by bounce, and from small emissive surfaces.
- Shadow cost from many shadow-casting local lights.
- The RecaptureSky hitch; real-time capture needing Sky Atmosphere.
- An old sun or sky actor left active beside a new one: two suns, broken exposure.
