# Asset packs: what they cost and what buying actually saves

Research topic 16. The short answer is that for the street we are building,
buying saves almost nothing, and the reasons are in our own files rather than on
the internet.

## The one real price I could get

The British City Pack: **$169.99, or $118.99 on a 30 percent discount**, for 646
meshes and about four and a half gigabytes.

Against your spend plan of 50 to 150 a month and 300 to 700 one-off in the first
year, that is one to three months of the monthly average, or a quarter to a half
of the whole year's one-off allowance. So the question is not whether we can
afford a pack. It is whether one pack is worth a quarter of the year's art money.

## Two reasons I think it is not, and both come from our own documents

**The packs are the right country and the wrong decade.** Good British packs
exist. The best of them is photogrammetry, scanned on location in London, which
means it is by construction a 2020s street. And our own scene file already names
what that breaks, with a precision nobody would guess: nothing on our street "may
imply a mobile phone, the internet, **a wheeled refuse bin**, or blister tactile
paving."

A modern British pack is full of wheeled refuse bins. And tactile paving, and
uPVC, and satellite dishes, and modern signage. So the real cost of a pack is not
its price, it is auditing 646 meshes against a period rule and throwing away the
ones that fail.

I could not find out what share of a modern pack would survive that audit. It is
the number I most wanted and somebody with the pack open could get it in an
afternoon.

**And canon forbids the first thing the money was earmarked for.** Your spend
plan names vehicle packs first. Canon says every vehicle is fictional, no real
car models. A period British vehicle pack is, necessarily, a pack of recognisable
period British cars, because that is what makes it one.

Our own bill of materials had already spotted this. Its line for the parked car
reads: "Largest quality risk on the list if included... canon also forbids real
car models, so a recognisable real shape is a canon violation."

## The number that actually answers the question

Our street's bill of materials has 78 items, each tagged with how it gets made.

- 33 are already in the repository, licensed and attributed.
- 33 are generated from primitives by our own scene generator, which the file
  itself describes as "zero cost".
- 6 are not assets at all, just renderer features.
- 5 are candidates for free libraries.
- **1 needs a purchase decision.**

One item in seventy-eight. And that one is period clothing, whose own note says
the decision "is Jafar's about where the character pipeline goes, not about
money".

So on the street that exists, buying saves essentially nothing, because the whole
pipeline was built so the street composes itself.

## And there is a structural reason a pack fits us badly

Our scene file has a law in it: "THIS FILE IS THE SCENE. Every object in either
engine arrives via that engine's generator reading THIS file." A hand-placed
object disqualifies the picture as evidence.

An asset pack is two things: a set of meshes, and a way of using them, which is
placing them by hand. Our law forbids the second half. So a pack can sell us
meshes that our generator places to our dimensions. It cannot sell us a scene, a
kit-bash or a demo level, which is most of what you are paying for.

There is also a proportions problem. Our street has published measurements down
to a 915 millimetre kerb block and a 125 millimetre upstand, taken from British
trade standards. The buying literature's sharpest warning is that "an
incompatible silhouette or proportion system affects the asset from every angle",
which is exactly what a pack building dropped next to our generated terrace would
be.

## Where I do think buying is right

I should not leave this all negative.

**Clothing.** It is the one purchase decision on the list, the one thing the
generator cannot compose, and the one thing phone photography cannot supply
either.

**Faces**, which you have already separated out as its own decision.

**Interiors, later.** When authored rooms start, a room's contents are hundreds
of small distinct objects, which is precisely where a pack's economics are best
and our generator's are worst. That is the purchase I would expect to be worth
it, and it is not due yet.

**And tools rather than art.** The trade's own view is that studios buy
middleware constantly. A tool purchase has no period problem, no canon problem
and no proportions problem. Worth remembering that the only industry-standard
tool for writing a branching script froze under Disco Elysium's, which is a
purchase-shaped gap we have not looked at.

## The honest limitation

For a topic titled "what they cost", I got exactly one price. The marketplace is
not fetchable from where I work, and search summaries return feature lists rather
than prices. So section one is thin, and the conclusions rest on our own specs
rather than on a price comparison.
