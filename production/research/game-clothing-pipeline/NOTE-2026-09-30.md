# Game clothing, start to finish: how studios and Epic do it

Research note. All sources read 30 September 2026. OPENED: I read the page. SNIPPET: search summary only. (I): my inference.

**The question.** Everyday clothes on MetaHumans in UE 5.8. A jacket draped and judged as simulated cloth in Blender failed at the armpits and back. The owner's view: game clothes are skinned meshes, weighted from the body and fixed at shoulders, elbows and hips; only loose parts are simulated.

## In brief

The owner's view matches common game practice [I1][E8][E9]. Three things qualify it.

1. **Epic's MetaHuman outfit route copies the weights itself, inside Unreal, and does it again at every resize.** Epic's 5.6 page says custom skinning is overwritten by "a simple skin weight transfer from the body" [E1]. Wardrobe items also reset hand-made weights, still reported in 5.8 [F1]. So shoulder fixes made in Blender do not survive that route. Fixes must go where they last: painted maps inside Unreal's graph [E3], or a copy-back after assembly [F1].
2. **Epic's showcase route does simulate whole garments.** A Marvelous Designer simulation mesh drives the visible mesh [E1][G1]. Epic's own showcase jacket ran at about 30 fps [G1]. That is a hero or film route, not a route for a street of people (I).
3. **The MetaHuman skeleton is fixed, so no joints can be added.** It already has helper joints that Epic's solvers move. By default they only move at the closest level of detail, LOD 0 [E11][E12]. So garment fixes are weight fixes (I).

Epic names the jacket's failure: copying weights from the body can cause "tearing in areas like armpits and upper thigh regions" [E3].

## 1. Concept, modelling, retopology

- The usual chain: Marvelous Designer for drape and big folds, ZBrush for detail, then a clean light mesh drawn over it (retopology) in Maya or similar [I4][I7].
- From Marvelous, artists export the flat pattern as well. They draw clean quads over it, then carry the UVs and shape back to 3D [I5].
- Edges that will be welded must have the same number of vertices [I5].
- Epic pairs every garment with the body it was made on, the "source body" [E1][E4].

## 2. UVs, baking, texturing

- UVs, then bake normal, occlusion and height maps from the detailed mesh to the light one; texture in Substance Painter [I4][I7].
- Small wrinkles live in the normal map [I1].

## 3. Rigging: skinning to the body's skeleton

**Copying weights from the body (all common tools):**
- Maya's Copy Skin Weights; "closest point on surface" is the default [M1].
- Blender's Data Transfer modifier. "Nearest Face Interpolated" blends the corners of the nearest body face; Max Distance ignores far matches [B1].
- Houdini captures weights by biharmonic, proximity or painting; no page on body-to-garment transfer found [H1].
- Unreal: the TransferSkinWeights graph node [E7], and a Blueprint version [E8]. Epic: for garments "you typically model the clothing in an external DCC" as a static mesh, then "transfer the skin weights ... instead of manually painting weights" [E8].

**Why plain copying fails, and the better method:**
- Nearest-point copying goes wrong where cloth stands off the body or folds, such as armpits and upper thighs [E3][P1].
- Epic's Inpaint Weights (SIGGRAPH Asia 2023) copies only where the body is near and faces the same way, then fills the rest smoothly [P1][E8]. It is "much better at handling non-skin-tight clothing" [E8].
- Node defaults in 5.8: search radius 5% of the bounds, 30 degrees, 10 smoothing passes, at most 8 bones per vertex [E7].

**The usual fixes:**
- Shoulders, armpits, elbows and hips are the hard areas [I2]. Hips and crotch are "a miserable area to weight" [I3].
- In Unreal's graph: paint a map over the problem area. Feed it to SetSkinningSelection. Choose Relax, Prune, Hammer, Clamp or Normalize [E3].
- Riggers add helper joints (twist, corrective) driven by the main joints; games prefer them to blend shapes as cheap and prunable [I3]. MetaHuman already has them [E11].

**MetaHuman specifics:**
- Body correctives keep volume at the joints. They run at LOD 0 only by default; -1 runs them at all LODs [E11]. Since 5.6 they can be unpacked into editable pose drivers (experimental) [E12].
- A garment weighted to the same helper joints as the body bulges with it. One that drops them may let the body poke through at LOD 0 (I).

**Levels of detail:** MetaHuman expects four body LODs, 0 to 3 [E2]. Epic's example cuts the garment to 50, 30 and 10% [E2].

**Hiding the body:** a Body Hidden Face Map removes body faces the garment always covers [E5].

## 4. Where cloth simulation is used, and how

- Games simulate only big loose pieces, such as capes. Smaller garments stay skinned, for cost and control [I1].
- In Chaos Cloth you paint Max Distance. At 0 "the skinned vertex could not move"; at 100 it may move a metre [E9]. Black follows the body; white becomes cloth [I10].
- Collision uses physics-asset capsules or, since 5.4, a "kinematic collider" made from the body mesh [G1][I9].
- The simulated garment sits on a Chaos Cloth component under the body [I10][F2]. Leader-pose followers cannot simulate physics on their own [E10].

## 5. Epic's MetaHuman route, UE 5.6 to 5.8

- Two kinds of garment: fixed-size skeletal meshes, and resizable Chaos Outfit Assets [E1][E6].
- An outfit always has a render mesh (what is seen). An optional single-sided sim mesh drives it and also serves resizing and skinning [E1].
- Path A: an FBX render mesh only. Path B: a Marvelous or CLO USD with both meshes. Path C: a render mesh plus a hand-made sim mesh [E2].
- Import as a static mesh; rigged FBX imports are "not recommended" [E2]. "You do not have to skin your parametric clothing" [E1].
- Weights come from the source body's combined skeletal mesh (bodyShapeG_CombinedSkelMesh in Epic's example) [E2][E3].
- The outfit pairs each garment with its body. Resizing samples 1,000 to 2,500 body points (1,500 default) [E4]. For realistic clothes Epic advises several source bodies, to limit warping [E1].
- Strip Sim Mesh: the resizing graph strips the sim mesh unless it is set false (Epic staff, 5 August 2026, UE 5.8) [F2].
- Known faults in 5.8: cloth simulation is lost when an outfit becomes a wardrobe item [F3]. Wardrobe items reset custom weights; the 15 September 2026 workaround copies them back from the original after assembly [F1]. Resizing cannot run while the game runs [F4].
- The garment follows the body as a child of the Body component [A1], which is Unreal's leader pose [E10].

## 6. From Blender to Unreal

- FBX: turn Add Leaf Bones off, or every chain grows an extra end bone. Export only deforming bones. Rename the armature away from "Armature" [B3].
- Scale: Blender works in metres, FBX assumes centimetres; a factor of 100 [B3].
- Transfer from the same MetaHuman body the garment was made on, in its rest pose [E2][S1].
- If the bind pose does not match, import with "Use T0 as Ref Pose" or fix the pose in Blender [U1].

## 7. Testing

- Riggers scrub a range-of-motion clip while weighting: arms raised, spine bends, legs stretched; within real limits [I2].
- Epic advises "synthetic animation and poses that have a clear meaning" when checking weights [E14].
- MetaHuman Creator's website showed a body range-of-motion clip, but users found no way to export it (2021, 2023) [F5].
- A pass (I): no tearing; no body through the cloth; shoulders and back keep their shape with arms raised; hems stay round; no stretched texture. Seen from front, side and back, at LOD 0 and a lower LOD.

## What this means for a jacket on a MetaHuman in UE 5.8

1. Model the jacket on the exported body of the character it is for, in its rest pose. Leave room at the armpit and back. Clean quads, with loops at shoulder and elbow (I).
2. UVs, bake, texture.
3. Decide what moves. A heavy donkey jacket may need no simulation. At most, the bottom band gets a small Max Distance; the rest stays at 0 (I).
4. Export a static-mesh FBX. No weights are needed for the outfit route [E1][E2].
5. In a cloth asset: StaticMeshImport, then TransferSkinWeights from that body's combined skeletal mesh, with Inpaint Weights [E7][E8]. Paint maps over the armpits, side seams and back; correct with Relax or Hammer [E3]. Add LODs [E2].
6. Build the outfit asset paired with that body. Resize to the other builds with Strip Sim Mesh false [E4][F2].
7. Paint the Body Hidden Face Map [E5].
8. Put it on the character. Check the weights in the game are the fixed ones, because of the reset fault [F1].
9. Run the range-of-motion test in Unreal, on the assembled character, with body correctives on. Check LOD 0 and a lower LOD.
10. If it passes, the gate, then Jafar's page. Only then the other garments.

The alternative is a fixed-size skeletal mesh per build, skinned in Blender. It keeps Blender's fixes but risks the wardrobe reset. Attaching it by leader pose in our own blueprint may avoid that; unproven (I).

## Holes

- No 2023 to 2026 studio talk on skinned garments could be read (GDC Vault needs a login). The industry sources opened date from 2020 or are undated.
- Strip Sim Mesh is in no Epic page; only the forum answer [F2].
- No 5.8 Epic page says whether resizing still overwrites custom weights. The 5.8 copies of the 5.6 pages redirect.
- Whether morph targets on a garment survive the outfit route: nothing found.
- No Epic range-of-motion clip for clothing, and no published pass standard with numbers.
