## The answer

**CLO's CONNECT store gives away a professional pattern for every one of the ten silhouettes.** CONNECT Official's men's
(MV2) and women's (FV2) patterns, most made with KOFOTI (the Korea Federation of Textile Industries): .zprj or .zpac
project files with FBX and glTF, drafted by pattern-makers, auto-grading. Each page reads "provided under a free
commercial license by the Korea Federation of Textile Industries (KOFOTI)" (or carries CONNECT's Free License alone)
and asks for the hashtags #KOFOTI #Connectofficial. CONNECT's terms (updated 7 August 2025, read in full) allow them
inside a sold game ("an open-world game" is named) so long as players cannot extract them as files; the licence is
revocable for misuse. Allowed by Jafar's ruling, the revocable clause to be re-checked before release. Downloading
needs a free CLO-SET account (his).

Against the bar every one is a plain white block on CLO's avatar: the cut, the lapel and the pockets are a
professional's (what our own patterns failed on); the cloth, weight, interfacing, wear and the 1990 details are ours,
the step that decides KCD2 or not. Grades: A as patterns for the tailored ones, B for the rest as seen.

Nothing elsewhere beats them. Sketchfab and Fab hold mostly modern cuts, dense simulation meshes without patterns, or
crude game meshes (best: Style3D CG's CC-BY "Man Black Business Suit", 663,736 triangles, a fallback for drape only).
Every free CGTrader garment opened carries "Royalty Free License (no AI)": not allowed by his ruling. ArtStation's free
items carry its own Standard licence ("one commercial project, up to 2,000 sales"), a ruling. Gumroad's free blocks carry
no licence text. Two CONNECT items forbid commercial use (Marvelous Designer's Victorian suit, "Educational use only";
ksk's "Business Lane", personal use only).

## Stopped, 2 October late evening, and kept as input

An outside review of the whole clothing problem (production/audits/2026-10-02-clothing-plan.md) found that the method
failed, not the tools, and that the bar for clothes was never defined. Jafar stopped the draping of patterns in
Marvelous (these free CLO patterns included), and every new research programme. What this search found is kept as
the first input to his new order (CLOTHES.md): a fixed rubric for clothes, then ready-made, rigged suits screened
against it. The CLO patterns above are patterns, not ready-rigged garments; the ready-rigged items the search found
(Sketchfab's and Fab's meshes in the notes) are screened first. The last two passes (thirteen CONNECT items, the last
ArtStation and CGTrader pictures) were stopped unfinished; what they had not opened stays listed as not opened.
