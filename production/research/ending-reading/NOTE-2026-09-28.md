# Tom's reading of what the ending will cost: first attempt, set aside

Town list 6i, 28 September 2026. D58's second condition asks that every cost
deciding the ending be readable before it lands, "as Tom's reading with its
reasons, never as a number from another mind and never as a meter"; D33 says
the player sees his own position, never other minds.

## What was tried

A Core reading (attempt-1-EndingReading.cs.txt, tests in attempt-1-tests.diff.txt)
that took the ending's own state (the books, the police's stage, Ellis's case,
the successor, the best friend's loyalty, the witnesses) and put each cost into
words as clear, coming or landed, with reasons. It passed its own tests.

## Why it failed the gate (the second check, a reviewer who had not seen it made)

- Costs still landed unseen: the street's talk (Both needs it quiet) had no
  reading at all; Both's raw-books condition was read through the inspection's
  eased view; one body with one sure witness jumps the police from nothing
  straight to a manhunt, so the quiet exit shut without ever reading "coming";
  the life's "cooling" band sat wholly below the line where it is lost.
- Some reasons were false: Ellis's case does decide more than Both; "keeping
  anything means the books have to hold" is untrue for the Straight Life and
  the Quiet ending.
- Most seriously, it put hidden numbers into words: the best friend's loyalty
  threshold, the successor's hidden verdict ("could carry it", which the game
  itself refuses to tell: "Nobody will tell you whether they are right"), and
  Ellis's case, which comes from other people's rumours. That is a meter by
  another name, against D33 and D12.

The attempt, its tests and its page section are kept here; none of it is in
the game or on Jafar's page.

## What a second attempt needs, and why it waits

The faults share one cause: the reading was built from the true state, which
Tom cannot see. A reading that D33 allows has to be built from what Tom has
seen and heard (a detective on the street, Ellis using his name, who still
comes round, who called him by his first name, who watched him and has not
met his eye since, what the street says as he passes), and so it can be
wrong, as a man's reading of his own position is. Those signals are events
the game has to collect, which is the builder's side, and the choice between
the two is a reading of D33 and D58 that is Jafar's:

(A) Tom's reading comes only from what he has seen and heard, and can be wrong
    (recommended: it is what D33 says, and D58's "Tom's reading" reads the
    same way); the game logs those signals and the Core turns them into words.
(B) The true state, put into words: simpler, and certain, but a meter by
    another name.

## Second attempt, 28 September evening: also set aside

Jafar ruled (A) on 28 September: built from what Tom has seen and heard. The
second attempt (attempt-2-TomsReading.cs.txt, its tests, its helper request
and its page text beside this note) took only that: a view with no field for
anybody's loyalty, heat, case strength or verdict, and none of its words a
number. That half held under review.

It failed the second check all the same, and for one cause. The game's
visible signs do not change where the hidden lines are, so a reading built
honestly from the signs says "quiet" while a door shuts, and "shut" while
one is still open:

- a friend calls him Tom from loyalty 0.45 (PlayerIdentity.AddressBy), but
  the ending counts a friend only from 0.55;
- one killing with one sure witness is a manhunt at once, and nothing he has
  seen says so until the police come; a detective using his name comes at
  the investigation stage, before any manhunt;
- Sheila's word measures the raw books once, when she speaks, while Kingdom
  turns on what the inspection sees after his mornings with the inspector;
- one brush-off, or one flat remark, read as shut, though the last day's
  call or a quieter week can still reopen them.

Its stakes were also untrue in places (the straight life needs no books
either; Ellis's case can decide Kingdom through the books). The reviewer's
full list is in the town session's record of 28 September; the probe it ran
against the real Eligible() is in the review's scratch folder.

Also found: the old Unity layer's ActThreeHost.ActThreeLedgerLine prints the
live books phrase every evening, a meter by another name; it must not be
ported as it stands.

## What a third attempt needs

A sign at every line: for each thing that decides the ending, one thing Tom
can see that changes exactly when it is crossed (a friend who would stand by
him says so; the inspector's manner turns on the morning the books stop
standing; the police at the door when it is a manhunt; the street saying it
as fact only once the day circle holds it), then a reading built on those,
tested against Eligible() across the balance lab's range so that no ending
shuts while its reading says quiet and nothing reads shut while a move he has
left could reopen it. That is design across the game and some of it the
builder's, so it goes to Jafar as a decision (30 September page).
