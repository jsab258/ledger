# Tom's reading of what the ending will cost

Town list 6i, second attempt, 28 September. Your ruling of 28 September: Tom's reading is built from what he has seen and heard, so it can be wrong, as a man's reading of his own position is.

## What it is

In the Ledger, Tom keeps his own reading of each thing that decides how it ends for him: his books, the people from before, what the street says, Ellis, the police, who could take it over, and anyone who watched him. Each has a line, the reasons he has for thinking so, and what rides on it. Each is quiet, coming or shut.

It is built only from what he did, saw, heard and was told: his own books and his dealings with the inspector; what Sheila said when she took him through the books, if she did; who from before still comes round and what they call him; what he overhears; hearing that Ellis has been asking; the police over a body; what his crew did with what he gave them; and who has met his eye since watching him. How loyal anybody really is, how much the street really believes, how strong Ellis's case really is and whether anybody could really carry it are never in it. There is no place for them.

So it can be wrong. A friend who has not come round in five days reads as drifting, whatever she feels. Once Sheila has taken him through the books, her words govern, and her third answer ("the shape of it is wrong") reads as not sure, because the line the inspection draws falls inside it.

## Three evenings, as the game would show them

### Day four: nothing has happened yet

- **The books look like a cab office's books, to you** (quiet)
  - nobody has taken you through the books; you have only your own sense of them
- **Somebody who knew you before all this still counts you a friend, you think** (quiet)

  - Ada still comes round, and calls you Nowak
- **The street is not talking about the other work, that you have heard** (quiet)
  - you have not heard the other work talked of this week

### Day twelve: the other work has started to show

- **The people from before are drifting, and you cannot say who still counts you a friend** (coming)
  - June has not come round in five days
  - Ada still comes round, and calls you Nowak
- **The other work is talked about; it is not fact yet, to your ear** (coming)
  - you have heard it talked of, now and then
- **Ellis is asking, and somebody could give her what she needs** (coming)

  - the lad at the chip shop saw something she could use, and you have done nothing about it
- **Somebody who watched you is frightened, and frightened people end up talking to detectives** (coming)
  - Mrs Kelly watched you do it and has not met your eye since
- **The books look like a cab office's books, to you** (quiet)
  - Sheila took you through them: there are one or two months a careful reader would ask about
  - there is night money you never ran through them

  - Ron ran the rank for a week alone, and it went clean; Ron came when you called

### The inspector's fourth morning: most of it is closing

- **Nobody from before will have you now, as far as you can tell** (shut)
  - June has finished with you, and said so
  - Ada would not stop for you, the last time
- **The street holds the other work as fact now, as far as you can hear** (shut)
  - you heard somebody say it flatly, as a thing everybody knows
  - it is on the street every time you pass
- **They are hunting you; handing it all over and walking away is no longer a way out, because nobody can take a killing off your hands** (shut)
  - there is a body, and the police do not let a body go
  - they have questioned you about it
  - they are asking for you by name
- **You are not sure the books will stand being read** (coming)
  - Sheila took you through them: the shape of it is wrong, and a careful reader will see the shape
  - there is night money you never ran through them
  - you ran a lot through them for what the cars take honestly
  - you have given the inspector what he asked for, most days
- **Ellis is asking, but everybody you know of who saw anything has been answered** (quiet)
  - Ellis has used your name to your face
  - the lad at the chip shop now says he was mistaken
- **Somebody has run something of yours alone; whether they could hold it all, nobody will tell you** (quiet)
  - Ron ran the rank for a week alone, and it went clean; Ron came when you called
- **Those who watched you have held steady, as far as you have seen** (quiet)
  - Mrs Kelly watched you do it and has held your eye since

## What rides on each

- Keeping the business needs the books to stand being read, and keeping the people as well needs them to make sense, not only to be handled well; selling up or handing it over needs neither.
- Keeping the people needs somebody from before who still counts you a friend, whatever you do with the business.
- Keeping both the business and the people needs the street not to hold the other work as fact.
- Keeping both the business and the people needs her to have nothing that stands up on its own.
- Handing it over is the one way out that does not need the books; it needs somebody who can carry it, and nobody will tell you who.
- A witness who talks to Ellis gives her something that could stand up.
- Handing it over and walking away is shut while they are hunting you.
