# Asset plan, family 2: cars and other vehicles (3 October 2026)

Research helper, about thirty minutes, read only. It builds on production/research/aaa-street/4-VEHICLES-AND-FURNITURE.md and production/research/period-vehicles-and-props/NOTE.md (both 1 October) and does not repeat them. Marks: **[READ]** read at the source; **[SS]** search summary only; **[I]** my inference; **UNREACHED** the page refused me. Not legal advice.

**Scope.** Parked and moving cars; minicabs (saloons and estates, not black cabs); vans, lorries and buses; police, ambulance and a postal van; motorbikes, mopeds and bicycles; a milk float; the harbour's boats. There is no ice-cream van (canon: no children). There is no brewery dray (canon: no alcohol). No bus or lorry advertisement shows drink or betting.

## The answer in brief

- **Nothing free fits.** No free, allowlisted, period, fictional car, van, bus or boat exists. The cars stay ours, built as a kit by script. This is what both earlier notes found, and it holds.
- **A correction to the earlier notes.** Both recommended Epic's free **Automotive Materials** and **Automotive Substrate Materials**. Search summaries now say both listings show **"Allows usage with AI: No"** [SS, 16, 17]. Fab itself was UNREACHED. If that is true, both packs are NoAI and forbidden. The builder must look at the two listings on Jafar's PC before using either. Until then, plan our own car-paint master material; it is a small job (section 3).
- **How professionals do it.** GTA, Sleeping Dogs, Mafia and Watch Dogs: Legion invent a maker and borrow cues from real cars of one class. They change the badges, lamps and grille, and drive variety from data (colour sets, parts, dirt). Canon is stricter than this: every vehicle is fictional and no real model may be recognisable. So our rule is to **blend three or more cars of one class, take only class dimensions, and design the face, tail and window line ourselves.** A blind test checks it (section 3.6).
- **The one proof.** One small family hatchback is built the whole way and stands at the rank in the hook camera. That is item 10 of the proof frame (aaa-street/5-PROOF-FRAME.md). A second variant is then made from the same kit by swapping parts only, to prove the kit. About 4–6 days. Nothing is multiplied before it passes.

### 1.2 The design rules, as reported and as I read them

1. **Invent the maker and the badge.** No real name, emblem, hubcap logo or tyre lettering appears anywhere. That includes tyre brands, dealer names and plates [I, from 1 and 4].
2. **Blend a class, never copy a car.** Take the class's typical dimensions from three or more cars. Then design the signature parts yourself: the grille, the headlamp graphic, the tail-lamp graphic, the window outline and the rear pillar [SS, 1; earlier note].
3. **Give each maker an ethos.** For example: "the cheap British family maker", "the solid Swedish-style box maker", "the French soft-riders" [SS, 5; I].
4. **Drive variety with data:** colour sets, parts, extras, dirt and liveries per model [SS, 4].
5. **Keep cars off the key art, the store capsule and the title** (earlier note).

### 1.3 What the law cares about, as reported (not legal advice)

The earlier note covered UK design right, which has expired for any car sold before about 2001. It also covered shape trade marks (*London Taxi*, *JLR v Ineos*), US trade dress and the Rogers test. Two additions:

- **Copyright in designs.** The CJEU's *Cofemel* (12 September 2019) made originality the only test for copyright in a design in the EU. UK courts have **not** followed it. Section 51 of the CDPA 1988 still requires artistic quality, and a 2024 UK case acknowledged the gap [SS, 9]. So in EU markets a distinctive car design could arguably be a copyright work [I]. Steam sells into the EU.
- **Badges and names are the clearest risk.** Shapes are protected only where distinctive and recognised [I, from the earlier note's cases].

Canon's rule ("no real car models") is stricter than any of this. Meeting canon also meets the law as reported.

## 2. What a 1990 British street actually had

### 2.1 The market and the parked stock

- **New-car market by class.** The 1994 SMMT shares are the nearest I found: mini 1%, supermini 26%, lower medium 33.6%, upper medium 26.5%, executive 6.3%, the rest sports and 4x4 [SS, 10]. In 1990, Ford, Vauxhall and Rover had 55% of sales. The top sellers were the Fiesta, Escort, Cavalier and Sierra, with the Metro sixth (earlier note).
- **Age of the parked stock.** In 1994 there were 21.1 million cars, with an average age of 6.7 years [SS, 11]. That means 1990's parked cars ran from new to about fifteen years old. A poor port street skews older [I].
- **Plates as a result** [I, arithmetic from the above]. Prefix plates (A to G or H) were issued from August 1983. In 1990 they were on roughly 60–65% of cars. Suffix plates (for example "ABC 123X", up to Y in 1982–83) were on the other 35–40%. On Quay Street, read about half and half.
- **Japanese cars.** Their share was held to about 11% by a voluntary restraint [I, from memory; not checked].
- **Colours.** For cars first registered in 1990: red 25.5%, blue 22.5%, white 18.9%, grey 8.8%, black 7.1% [SS, 12]. Red led British sales from 1973 to 1997 [SS, 12]. The source's method is not seen; it may count only cars that survive today. Older cars on the street add beige, brown, orange, mustard, dark green, maroon and gold metallic [I]. **Faded, chalky red paint on older cars is a strong 1990 tell** [I].

**Proportions for the agent** [I, from the ranges above]:
- Height over length is about 0.38 for a supermini, 0.34 for a small family car, 0.31 for a family saloon, 0.30 for an executive saloon and 0.44 for a panel van.
- The wheelbase is 0.60–0.66 of the length.
- The belt line sits at about 0.62 of the height. The glass is about 0.35–0.40 of the height: taller than in 2000, when the glass shrank.
- Our carriageway is 6.0 m, two lanes of 3.0 m (vignette-scene.json). A double-decker fills a lane.

### 2.3 Plates (1990)

- **Format and year letter.** From 1983 the plate is the year letter, one to three digits, then three letters, such as "G123 ABC". The letter changes on 1 August:
  - E: August 1987 to July 1988
  - F: August 1988 to July 1989
  - G: August 1989 to July 1990
  - H: August 1990 to July 1991
- **Letters not used:** I, O, Q, U and Z as year letters.
- **Colours:** retroreflective, white at the front and yellow at the rear, compulsory for new registrations from 1 January 1973. Black-and-silver plates belong only to older cars.
- **Size and lettering:** characters 79 mm high on a 520 × 111 mm plate, in the Charles Wright lettering, to BS AU 145 [READ, 16a].
- **What the game does** [I]:
  - New plates follow the game's month. Before August the newest cars are G; from 1 August, H.
  - About half the cars carry suffix plates (2.1).
  - Draw the characters as geometry from the standard's dimensions. That avoids needing a font licence.
  - Plates are invented; the dealer strip under the number is blank or illegible.

### 2.4 Special vehicles in 1990

- **Police.** "Jam sandwich": white or grey with a fluorescent amber or red side stripe, sometimes edged in blue. The livery was used from 1965, and in London from 1978; Battenburg came only in the mid-1990s [READ, 17, 18]. **No Battenburg in 1990.** Small hatchbacks served as panda cars, with a single blue beacon on a "POLICE" roof box. Large saloons and estates were area and traffic cars, with light bars. Vans carried officers [SS, 19; I]. The force's name and crest are not minted (canon question).
- **Ambulance.** A white van conversion (box body or high roof), blue beacons, "AMBULANCE" across the front [I, check photographs]. No Battenburg in 1990 [READ, 18].
- **Postal van.** Red. Small vans were car-derived; larger ones were panel vans [I]. Canon still owes the postal cypher [READ, canon.md]. Until it exists the van carries no mark, as the pillar box does.
- **Milk float.** Battery-electric, three- or four-wheeled, 10–16 mph, quiet in the early hours [READ, 20]. Crates of pint bottles, and a dairy name that must be fictional. It is an early-morning vehicle.
- **Motorbikes and mopeds** [I]. Japanese commuters dominate: step-through 50–90 cc machines and 125s, plus courier bikes. Riders are adults (canon: no children).
- **Bicycles** [I]. Upright roadsters with baskets, drop-bar "racers", and new mountain bikes, which boomed around 1988–90. No BMX, which reads as a child's bike.
- **Minicabs.** Ordinary saloons and estates. Licensing required the car to be "not like a hackney" and to show its plate [READ, cab-office-1990 research]. Our markers [I]:
  - a council licence plate on the rear;
  - a whip aerial for the firm's radio (the office holds a radio licence, same research);
  - a small door sticker with the office number;
  - higher mileage, so more wear.
  - Jafar's ruling of 22 September: the rank holds **one or two** cars.

## 3. Making fictional ones: rules, kit, scripting, materials, canon test

### 3.1 A written design rule per body type [I]

Every vehicle starts from a one-paragraph brief. The brief names:
- the class and the invented maker's ethos;
- three or more real cars of the class used for **dimensions only** (the provenance line);
- the dimensions picked from the class range;
- choices for the signature parts, all drawn fresh: the grille (slot, bars, mesh or none), the headlamps (single rectangle, twin rectangles, wraparound with indicator, round on survivors), the tail lamps (horizontal band, vertical stack, ribbed or smooth), the window outline (four-light or six-light, the rear-pillar shape) and the side crease (one shoulder line or none).
- the bumper era: chrome, black-capped or black wraparound;
- what is forbidden: every listed "named-at-a-glance" shape (3.6).

**Per type:**
- **Supermini:** tall, upright, short.
- **Small family car:** wedge nose, hatch.
- **Large family car:** the newest look; flush glass on one variant, square three-box on another.
- **Estate:** roof extended, tailgate upright.
- **Executive saloon:** long boot, upright grille.
- **Survivor:** chrome and round lamps.
- **Vans:** a car-derived van from the small-family platform; panel vans with the cab and box separate.
- **Buses:** flat fronts, split screens; the operator invented (canon).
- **Boats:** working steel hulls, gantries and rust streaks.
- **Old side trawlers** were mostly gone by 1990. The museum ship *Ross Tiger* was converted in 1992 [SS, 21]. Stern trawlers carry the superstructure forward and the working deck aft, with a gantry [READ, 22].

### 3.2 The kit

| **Body surface** | Bonnet angle, screen rake, roof length, rear-window angle, boot deck, inward lean of the side glass, plan taper, shoulder line. CarGen's parameter list is a good checklist; its code is GPL-3.0 and not allowed [READ, 23]. | – |

**What the kit yields.** One platform × 3 noses × 3 tails × 3 bumper eras × 14 colours × wear seeds gives far more cars than a street needs. Three or four platforms give the street; GTA-style data variety does the rest [SS, 4].

### 3.3 How an agent builds it, by script [I]

**Blender 4.5, headless, as tools/art-recipes/ already works:**

1. **Loft, not bevelled boxes.** car-model.py today bevels boxes. The platform script should instead define about 12 stations along the length: nose tip, bumper face, headlamp, front axle, screen base, B-pillar, C-pillar, rear axle, tail. Each station is a half cross-section with parameters (shoulder, inward lean of the glass, sill tuck).
   - Bridge the stations with `bmesh.ops.bridge_loops` and mirror.
   - Add one level of subdivision, then weighted normals. This is the mid-poly method in the earlier note.
   - Cut the arches with a boolean.
   - Split the openings into a glass mesh.
   - Cut panel gaps as 3 mm grooves.
   - Budget: 15–25k triangles plus wheels.
2. **Shape keys** carry the platform's dimension range. A variant is a JSON line: platform, key values, modules, paint, extras, wear seed.
3. **Modules** are separate meshes snapped to named empties (sockets).
4. **Two UV sets:** UV0 tiles; UV1 is a unique layout for the wear masks.
5. **Material slots** named for the Unreal masters: paint, glass, chrome, black plastic, rubber, lens, plate, interior.
6. **Renders for the gate:** orthographic side, front and rear silhouettes, a three-quarter view, and the game-camera view.

**Unreal 5.8, by script:**
- Import the glb and assign material instances by slot name.
- **Use per-instance Custom Primitive Data** for paint colour, fade, dirt, rust and wetness, so one mesh gives many cars.
- Run the car body through Nanite. Glass stays a separate translucent or thin-translucent mesh, because Nanite does not take translucency.
- Tie the paint's wetness to the scene's day wetness (0.85, DECISIONS 2 October), so roofs and bonnets shine like the street.
- **Moving traffic comes after the proof:** a few kinematic cars on splines at walking-town speeds. City Sample's Mass traffic is a method to read only; its content is UE-only and outside the allowlist.

**Where a human eye must judge:**
- **Stance and proportion.** Ride height, how the wheels fill the arches, how big the glass is. Numbers do not settle whether it "reads 1990".
- **The canon test's borderline cases.**
- **The whole frame,** which is Jafar's.

The agent can measure everything else.

### 3.4 Materials

- **Epic's automotive packs: probably NoAI, so not usable until checked** (see the brief answer) [SS, 16, 17].
- **Our own masters, built by script:**
  - **Car paint:** Unreal's clear-coat model, or a Substrate slab with a coat. Inputs: base colour; a metallic flake normal (procedural); clear-coat roughness about 0.05–0.1. Then fade (raises roughness, lowers saturation, chalks reds), dirt, rust and wetness masks from UV1.
  - **Glass:** tint, grime, rain streaks, over a dark interior.
  - **Rubber and black plastic:** grey with age.
  - **Chrome:** pitted on survivors.
  - **Lamp lenses:** an inner normal pattern, emissive for brake and indicator.
  - **Plates:** generated.
- **CC0 inputs for wear:**
  - ambientCG painted metal and rust, for vans, lorries, the trawlers and the skip [SS, 24].
  - ambientCG's rubbers are floor rubbers, not tyre rubber [SS, 24]. Make tyres procedurally.
  - The project's Megascans grunge masks and oil stains (fab-free-megascans.md), if they pass the same NoAI check.

### 3.5 Wear by age [I]

The data gives each car an **age** (from its plate year) and a **care** level. Together they drive:
- fade;
- rust at the arch lips and sills;
- dirt height;
- dents;
- a replaced panel;
- missing wheel trims;
- a cracked lens.

Minicabs carry high wear. A G-plate car carries almost none. This is the grime-as-strategy rule of D53 applied to cars.

### 3.6 The canon check: "does it resemble a real model?" [I]

Every variant must pass all four steps before it reaches the street.

1. **Provenance line.** Three or more real source cars of the class, used for dimensions only, and what was designed fresh. It is kept beside the asset.
2. **The named-at-a-glance list.** No variant may echo the signature shapes a British enthusiast names instantly:
   - the Mini, Metro, Maestro, Montego and Rover SD1;
   - the Cortina, Capri, Escort, Sierra, Granada and Transit;
   - the Cavalier and Astra;
   - the Volvo 240 and 740;
   - the Golf (its rear pillar), the Beetle and the 2CV;
   - the Mercedes W123 and W124, and BMW's rear-window kink;
   - the Land Rover, Range Rover and black cab;
   - the Routemaster, the Leyland National and the Morris Minor.

   The test: if the outline, lamp graphic or grille would make him say a name, change it (earlier note, rule 4).
3. **Blind identification.** A fresh reviewer that has never seen the provenance gets four renders: side, front, rear and three-quarter. It runs through Claude Code on the subscription, with no API calls. Its questions: "What make and model is this? How sure? What country and years does it look like?"
   - **Pass:** no single model named with confidence, **and** placed in Britain or Europe, 1985–1991.
   - **Fail:** a confident model name. Change that feature and run again.
   - This one test checks both halves: reads as its era, resembles no model.
4. **Silhouette check, optional.** If blueprints of the source cars are held locally for dimensions, compare the side elevations. A near-identical outline to any single car fails. Blueprint images are never shipped or committed.

## 5. Variety, and the one proof

### 5.1 How many [I]

**Parked density.**
- Quay Street's vignette is 48 m long, with the parade from 3 to 39 m (vignette-scene.json).
- British parallel parking takes about 5.5–6 m a car. Both kerbs could take about 14 cars. Yellow lines, the yard entrance, the bus stop and the rank cut that down.
- **Six to nine vehicles in the hook view** is right for a weekday in 1990. That matches the sheet's density. Of these:
  - 1–2 on the rank;
  - 4–6 private cars;
  - 1 delivery van outside the fishmonger or grocer.
  - Later, one moving vehicle every minute or so.

**Repetition.**
- In one frame, the same body in the same colour twice reads as set dressing (earlier note).
- So the proof frame needs **at least three platforms**, or **two platforms with different noses and tails**, and **no colour repeated on the same body**.

**Town-wide, staged:**

| Stage | What | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Proof frame (item 10) | Small family hatch: the proof car plus one swapped variant; the rank's estate | 1 platform, 3 cars |
| Street-wide pass and the friends' build | Add the supermini, the large family saloon and estate, the panel van. Minicab extras | 4 platforms, about 12 looks from 3 noses and 3 tails each, colours and wear on top |
| Later, the town | Executive saloon, survivor, car-derived van (police panda and postal van come from it), Luton, 7.5 t box lorry, minibus, single- and double-deck bus, police area car and van, ambulance, milk float, 3 two-wheelers, 3 bicycles | about 20 more |
| The harbour, when it is in frame | Stern trawler (2 variants), seine netter or inshore boat, tug, 3 small open boats, the Meridian Ferry as a distant silhouette | about 8 |

### 5.2 The one proof

**What.** One small family hatchback from the kit, the class car-model.py already sizes (4.12 m). It is built the whole way:
- lofted body, nose, tail and black bumpers;
- wheels with invented tyres;
- glass with an interior behind it;
- clear-coat paint with fade and wetness;
- UV1 wear: dirt, arch rust, one dent;
- a G-prefix plate, white at the front and yellow at the rear, plus a tax disc.

Then **variant 2 is made by swapping parts only**: nose, tail, colour and wear. That proves the kit. Both stand at the rank and kerb by Mickey's.

**How it is judged:**
1. **Precheck.** All triangles draw (the Nanite stand-in fault of 1 October).
2. **The game's own camera and exposure,** at 5–20 m, with the day's wetness (CLAUDE.md: lighting is judged through the game's camera).
3. **Against the Hook sheet,** side by side: placement, density, how the cars sit in the wet frame, sky in roofs and glass, dark wheel wells, contact shadows.
4. **Against the KCD2 frames:** the same material richness and grime as their carts and walls.
5. **Against Marshall R09 and R07** (photographs.md) for period: black bumpers, rectangular lamps, size of the glass.
6. **The rubric:** the nine points of the period note, in its order: stance and wheels, reflections, shading, lines, lamps, interior, wear, period markers, variety.
7. **The canon test** (3.6).
8. **A fresh reviewer** against the bar. Only then does it go into a whole frame for Jafar, since he ruled the cars off.
9. **The two-tries rule applies.** After a third failure the cars stay off, as the period note already says.

**Effort** [I; consistent with both earlier notes]:
- loft generator, materials and the first car through the gate: 3–5 days;
- the swapped variant: half a day to a day;
- the canon test and its set-up: half a day.
- **About 4–6 days in all.**
