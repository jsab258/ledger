# Casting the Hook: who really lived there, and who the characters should be

One page. The sources and the full plan are in DELIVERY.md beside this file.
Written 24 September. Everything here is a proposal: you decide.

**Who lived in Hull and Grimsby in 1990.** From the 1991 census:
- **Age and sex:** among adults, about 29% were in their late teens and
  twenties, and 36% were 45 or over. Among people over 75, two in three were
  women.
- **Background:** 98.8% white. The largest minorities were Chinese families
  from Hong Kong in Hull and Indian families in Grimsby, each a few hundred
  people. Fewer than 1 in 40 were born abroad.
- **Home and work:** in Hull, over a third of households rented from the
  council and half had no car. Unemployment was about 12%, and worse for men.
  Fishing was under 1% of jobs by then; the fleets had gone after the Cod
  Wars.

**How they looked.**
- **Measured:** about 30% smoked, and half of those over 55 had no natural
  teeth. About one adult in seven was obese, against nearly one in three now,
  and people were slightly shorter.
- **From photographs and fashion history:** perms and sets on older women,
  moustaches on many middle-aged men, big glasses, faded tattoos only on
  seafarers and hard men, ears the only piercings, and nothing groomed. People
  looked older for their age.

**Why Lena's voice is wrong.** Every one of our 23 cast voices comes from one
research recording set whose speakers are 21 to 38, with no Hull accents. Lena
is voiced by a 22-year-old. Older local voices need a new source, and each
route needs your ruling: free older volunteers from Mozilla, training on
Google's northern English set, or hiring a few older local speakers under a
signed consent.

**Building faces.** Unreal has no photo-to-MetaHuman tool. The cheapest route
starts from the nearest preset and adjusts its face shape by script against an
approved portrait. A paid Blender add-on is the fallback, and a free trial
would test it first.

**The crowd.** Epic made 35,000 people from 12 faces and 6 bodies; people spot
repeated clothes before repeated faces. So: 16 faces, older than Epic's, 6
bodies, and the variety spent on jackets, hats, glasses, bags and cigarettes.
Each kind of person is placed on the street as often as the census says.

**The plan in three tiers:**
- **14 principals:** each with their own face and voice.
- **About 30 street regulars:** matched to the census by age, sex and trade,
  including a Hong Kong Chinese family running the takeaway, rather than one
  lone token.
- **The crowd:** 200 to 500 on screen.

**Names, fitted to the town.** First names are from the official lists for
each person's birth decade. Surnames are from Hull's 1990 phone book and old
East Riding and Lincolnshire names.

Tom stays Tom, with one letter changed: **Nowak**. That makes him the son of a
Polish post-war settler who married Mickey's sister, the typical case the
evidence found. His last rung of standing would become "Tommy" rather than
"Toma".

**What renames touch.** Lena, Rocco and Sam's renames reach the three
MetaHumans made today, their voices and about 50 files each. Their faces and
voices are being recast anyway. Every rename changes canon, which is yours.

**The rules, kept.**
- **No children:** every face must read clearly adult. Anyone a test group
  guesses under 21 is recast. Lena being taken for a child because she was
  short is exactly the case this test catches.
- **No real people:** a reverse image search and a "who does this remind you
  of?" check on every face and voice.
- **No stereotypes:** minorities are placed as real families, and older women
  are among the leads.
