## 1. Recognisable real people (face and voice)

### UK
- CASE. Fenty v Arcadia (Topshop) [2015] EWCA Civ 3, Court of Appeal, 22 Jan 2015. Selling a T-shirt with a real photo of Rihanna was passing off, because shoppers were likely to think she endorsed it. The court said there is no free-standing general right in England for anyone to control reproduction of their image. Three elements: goodwill, a misrepresentation of endorsement, damage. https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/judgments/details/1489 ; https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2015/01/court-of-appeal-confirms-that-sale-of-t.html (snippet)
- CASE. Irvine v Talksport [2002] EWHC 367 (Ch); CA 2003. A doctored photo of an F1 driver implied he endorsed a radio station, which was passing off. Damages were raised on appeal from GBP 2,000 to GBP 25,000, the notional endorsement fee. https://www.5rb.com/case/irvine-v-talksport-ltd/ (snippet)
- Takeaway (our reading): in the UK the risk is **false endorsement of a famous person with goodwill**. An unknown lookalike, or a character nobody reads as endorsement, is low risk under passing off. Defamation and data protection are separate routes, e.g. a recognisable real private person shown as a criminal.
- GUIDANCE/POLICY. UK Government Report on Copyright and AI, 18 Mar 2026. It says a "new personality right may be appropriate" and promises a digital-replica consultation for summer 2026. I could not confirm in this search whether that consultation has launched. https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/services/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-blog/uk-government-maintains-status-quo-on-ai-and-copyr ; https://www.vwv.co.uk/insights/articles/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-the-uks-pivot-towards-a-new-digital-replica-right/ (snippet). **Watch item: UK law may change before release.**

### Switzerland (studio's home)
- LAW. Art. 28 ZGB/CC: anyone unlawfully injured in their personality can seek court protection. The injury is unlawful unless justified by consent, an overriding private or public interest, or law. Courts treat the right to one's own image and voice as part of personality. https://www.ekr.admin.ch/rechtsgrundlagen/d507.html ; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recht_am_eigenen_Bild_(Schweiz) (snippet). Unlike the UK, this covers **ordinary people, not only celebrities**. That matters if a generated face happens to match a real private person, or if staff or friends are scanned or used as face references without written consent.
- LAW. Revised Swiss FADP (in force 1 Sep 2023), art. 5(c): biometric data that unambiguously identifies a person is sensitive personal data. Processing it needs express consent in many cases. https://www.pwc.ch/en/insights/fs/data-protection-switzerland.html ; https://www.swissstaffing.ch/docs/en/Legal-Counsel/Factsheets/20230308-merkblatt-datenschutzgesetz-revdsg-en.pdf (snippet)

### United States (Steam sells there)
- CASE. Lohan v Take-Two, NY Court of Appeals, 29 Mar 2018. A video-game avatar *can* be a "portrait" under NY Civil Rights Law ss. 50-51. The GTA V "Lacey Jonas" character and the Gravano-based character were **not recognisable** as the plaintiffs, so the claims failed. https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/court-of-appeals/2018/24.html (snippet). Takeaway: recognisability is the test.
- CASE. Noriega v Activision, LA Superior Court, 27 Oct 2014. Noriega's appearance in Call of Duty: Black Ops II was held transformative and protected speech. The claim was struck under California's anti-SLAPP law. https://www.cnbc.com/2014/10/28/judge-dismisses-manuel-noriegas-call-of-duty-lawsuit.html ; https://cdas.com/rightofpublicitydecision/ (snippet)
- CASE. In re NCAA Student-Athlete Name & Likeness (Keller v EA), 724 F.3d 1268 (9th Cir. 2013). Avatars with no names but real jersey numbers, height, skin tone and home state were **not** transformative, so no First Amendment defence. EA and CLC then settled for about USD 40m in 2014. https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/1640527.html ; https://www.espn.com/espn/otl/story/_/id/11010455/college-athletes-reach-40-million-settlement-ea-sports-ncaa-licensing-arm (snippet). Takeaway: **removing the name does not help if the rest of the profile identifies the person.**
- CASE. Midler v Ford, 849 F.2d 460 (9th Cir. 1988). Deliberately imitating a distinctive, widely known singer's voice to sell a product is a tort in California, even though her name and picture were not used. https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/849/460/37485/ (snippet). Takeaway: **a sound-alike voice is a likeness.**
- LAW. Tennessee ELVIS Act, signed 21 Mar 2024, in force 1 Jul 2024. It makes voice, including a *simulation* of a person's voice, a protected property right. Civil action and Class A misdemeanour. https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/04/first-of-its-kind-ai-law-addresses-deep-fakes-and-voice-clones ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELVIS_Act (snippet)
- LAW (pending). NO FAKES Act of 2025 (S.1367 / H.R.2794) would create a federal right over digital replicas of voice and visual likeness. The Senate Judiciary Committee advanced it on 18 Jun 2026 (CLAIM, law-firm note). Not law as of this search. https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1367 ; https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-legislation-to-protect-name (snippet)
- GUIDANCE (union contract). SAG-AFTRA 2025 Interactive Media Agreement, ratified July 2025 with 95% in favour. A digital replica needs separate, written, clear and conspicuous, reasonably specific consent. The trigger is whether the output is "objectively identifiable" as a specific performer, and that includes replicas made with generative AI prompted with the performer's name. https://www.sagaftra.org/sag-aftra-members-approve-2025-video-game-agreement ; https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mewu/inside-the-new-sag-aftra-interactive-media-agreement-new-standards-for-ai-and-di (snippet). It binds signatory producers only, but it is the industry benchmark for what "consent" means.

### EU (Steam sells there; AI Act reaches output used in the EU)
- LAW. EU AI Act Art. 50(4), applies from **2 Aug 2026**. Deployers of AI that generates a "deep fake" image, audio or video must disclose that it is artificially generated. For an "evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work", disclosure is limited to an appropriate manner that does not hamper enjoyment of the work, e.g. in the credits. Art. 50(2): providers of generators must machine-mark output. Grace period to 2 Dec 2026 for systems already on the market. https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/ (opened)
- GUIDANCE. European Commission FAQ on Art. 50, updated 24 Jul 2026. A deep fake needs (1) resemblance to (2) something that exists or could plausibly exist, and (3) a false appearance of authenticity, judged by audience expectations. On its reading, **purely fictitious persons would not meet the criterion**. The transparency Code of Practice was assessed "adequate". https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act (opened). CONFLICT: one commentary site (snippet) says fictitious but natural-looking people must also be labelled. The Commission's reading is the more authoritative, but it is unsettled. Cheap mitigation: a credits line saying AI tools were used to create character faces and voices.
- CLAIM. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Council adopted 29 Jun 2026) postponed high-risk deadlines but **left Art. 50 on 2 Aug 2026**. https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/eu-ai-act-omnibus-deal-reached-postponed-deadlines-watermarking-compromise-and-the-nudificiation-prohibition/ ; https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/article-50-transparency-deadline-2-august-2026 (snippet)

### Platform and tool terms
- GUIDANCE. Steam onboarding, prohibited content (opened, undated). It includes "Nude or sexually explicit images of real people", "Libelous or defamatory statements", "Content you don't own or have adequate rights to", "Content that exploits children in any way", and, since July 2025, content that breaches payment-processor standards. https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding
- GUIDANCE. Steam Content Survey, AI section (opened, undated). "Pre-Generated" AI content that ships and is consumed by players must be disclosed. The developer affirms there is no illegal or infringing content. The page says Valve does not want to ship "Live-Generated AI Adult Only Sexual Content". https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey. CLAIM: the Jan 2026 revision limited disclosure to player-facing content and marketing, not dev tools. https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/ (snippet). **Generated portraits and synthetic voices that ship are disclosable.**
- GUIDANCE. MetaHuman / Epic EULA. The user warrants that they hold all consents for any real person's likeness in input data, such as Mesh to MetaHuman scans, and that user content does not infringe privacy or publicity rights. https://www.unrealengine.com/eula/mhc ; https://www.metahuman.com/license (snippet)

### Generated faces can reproduce real people
- CLAIM (peer-reviewed). Carlini et al., "Extracting Training Data from Diffusion Models", USENIX Security, Aug 2023. More than a thousand training images were extracted from Stable Diffusion, Imagen and others, "ranging from photographs of individual people to trademarked company logos". Diffusion models are less private than GANs. https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/carlini (opened)
- CLAIM (peer-reviewed). Somepalli et al., "Diffusion Art or Digital Forgery?", CVPR 2023. Found cases where Stable Diffusion copies training data. Replication is more likely with small or duplicated training sets, including CelebA, a celebrity-face set. https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03860 (snippet)
- Takeaway (our reading): memorisation is rare but real, and most likely for heavily duplicated images, i.e. famous people. Prompts that name or describe a real person, or a celebrity style, raise the risk sharply.

### Face-matching checks: privacy side
- GUIDANCE. ICO biometric recognition guidance, 5 Mar 2024. Biometric data processed to uniquely identify a person is special-category data, and explicit consent is the likely condition. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/biometric-data-guidance-biometric-recognition/biometric-recognition/ (snippet)
- CLAIM. Clearview AI was fined EUR 30.5m by the Dutch DPA (Sep 2024) and EUR 20m each in Italy, Greece and France for a scraped face database. PimEyes, a similar public face search, is registered offshore and contested. https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/national-news/2022/facial-recognition-italian-sa-fines-clearview-ai-eur-20-million_en ; https://www.cpomagazine.com/data-protection/dutch-regulator-hits-clearview-ai-with-large-gdpr-fine-for-illegal-facial-recognition-database/ (snippet)
- Takeaway (our reading): uploading **our own generated or rendered faces** to an ordinary reverse-image search (Google Lens, TinEye, Bing) processes no real person's data unless there is a match, so it is low concern. **Avoid Clearview/PimEyes-type services**, whose databases are themselves found unlawful in Europe. Never run face recognition on photos of real staff or friends without explicit written consent. An internal comparison against a celebrity dataset you download yourself is itself biometric processing of those celebrities. Prefer the "look at it" check plus ordinary reverse-image search.

## 2. Avoiding stereotypes: minority characters, class, older women

- GUIDANCE. Xbox "Gaming for Everyone" Product Inclusion Framework, released at GDC, Mar 2024. It has four "doorways", one of them Representation, plus 10 Product Inclusion Actions. https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/games/articles/2024/03/xbox-releases-gaming-for-everyone-product-inclusion-framework-for-game-developers/ ; representation page https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/games/resources/productinclusion/representation/ (snippet)
- GUIDANCE. Riz Test (Choudry & Habib, 2018), five failure criteria for Muslim characters. The character is talking about, or is a victim or perpetrator of, terrorism; is irrationally angry; is superstitious, backward or anti-modern; is a threat to the western way of life; the men are misogynist or the women oppressed. 87% of 865 films failed. https://www.riztest.com/ (snippet). Directly usable as a checklist for any Yemeni/Arab or South Asian Muslim characters.
- GUIDANCE. GLAAD Gaming Report, Feb 2024. Recommends consulting LGBTQ media experts and hiring LGBTQ staff into authority. GLAAD consults studios. https://glaad.org/glaad-gaming/2024/ (snippet)
- GUIDANCE. Channel 4 Commissioning Diversity Guidelines, mandatory since Aug 2022. Portrayal should be authentic and challenge stereotypes. The page was opened but the detail is in a PDF I did not read. https://www.channel4.com/4producers/creative-equity/commissioning-diversity-guidelines
- CLAIM (independent review for BBC). Anne Morrison and Chris Banatvala, reported 29 Jan 2026, with 4,500+ public surveyed. Forced diversity in **period dramas** reads as "clunky", "inauthentic" and "preachy", and viewers of colour said so as strongly as white viewers. It recommends an organic, story-driven approach over a "smattering" in every programme. https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/bbc-told-stop-tick-box-195536783.html (opened). This is the erasure/tokenism tension in practice. For us the answer is **history**: cast who was actually there.
- CLAIM (history, relevant to erasure). Northern English ports had old, settled minority communities. South Shields' Yemeni seamen settled from the 1890s, numbered about 3,000 by the end of WWI, and are one of Britain's oldest Muslim communities. The 1930 "Yemeni riot" is documented by Tyne & Wear Museums. https://southshieldslocalhistorygroup.co.uk/index.php/people/migration/yemeni/ ; https://blog.twmuseums.org.uk/the-yemeni-riot-in-south-shields/ (snippet). Liverpool's long-established Black and Chinese communities are also well known (not checked here). A port town with *no* seafaring-diaspora families in 1990 would itself be the inaccuracy. Check canon.md.
- CLAIM. Tokenism = a single character standing in for a whole group, usually shallow. Commentators recommend more than one member of a group, with individual wants unrelated to their identity. https://mswordsmith.nl/tokenism/ (snippet)
- CLAIM (studio statement). Mafia III (Hangar 13/2K, 2016) opened with a developer note. The note says racist language and behaviour are abhorrent but are depicted because leaving them out would misrepresent 1968 and the people who suffered it. The racism is part of the world rather than the subject. https://www.gamespot.com/articles/mafia-3-begins-with-statement-about-racism/1100-6444303/ ; https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2017/01/03/506762046/ (snippets; the 2K page returned 403 and the NPR page timed out). Principle: racism belongs to characters, is visible in consequences, is never the player's reward, and the minority character is not only a victim.
- GUIDANCE/CLAIM. Disability: avoid the disabled villain, disability as a "cure" reward, and worth defined only by "overcoming" disability. Do not make the villain the only disabled character. https://ablegamers.org/video-games-disability-representation/ ; https://thenoraproject.ngo/nora-notes-blog/the-problem-with-the-disabled-villain-trope (snippet)
- CLAIM (research). Working class on UK screen. "Screened Out" (PEC/ScreenSkills, Apr 2021): working-class people are under-represented in the workforce, and portrayal leans on stigmatising "benefits TV" tropes. https://pec.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PEC-and-ScreenSkills-report-Screened-Out-FINAL-April-2021.pdf (snippet). No source was found giving a formal "flat caps and whippets" checklist. Our reading: give working people competence, humour, interior lives and varied politics, not poverty as spectacle or comic accent.
- CLAIM (research). "Aging in Play", AARP with Geena Davis Institute, 11 Mar 2025, covering the 49 best-selling games of 2023. Of characters aged 50+, 94% are male and 67% white. Only 7% of playable presets are 50+. Stock tropes are the wise mentor, elder in distress, silver fox and vain sorceress. https://geenadavisinstitute.org/leveling-up-the-role-of-age-in-video-games/ (opened). Older women are the most missing group, and they are an easy strength for a town-and-gossip game.
- GUIDANCE/CLAIM. Sensitivity readers and cultural consultants are an established paid service in games, e.g. James Mendez Hodes and Jennifer Lufau (Afrogameuses). https://jamesmendezhodes.com/sensitivity (snippet). For a small studio, one paid read of scripts and portraits for each minority community depicted is the practical form. Ideally use someone from that community in the North East or North West, or from a local heritage group.

## 3. Children: "no children anywhere"

- GUIDANCE. Steam prohibits "Content that exploits children in any way" and requires adult content to be labelled and age-gated. https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding (opened). Steam publishes no apparent-age rule for non-sexual content. With no sexual content in the game, apparent age is a canon and quality matter, not a store-compliance one. Still, a youthful-looking figure beside violence or crime can draw reports and ratings attention.
- GUIDANCE. Australian Classification: games are refused classification if they depict a person "who is, or appears to be, a child under 18" in a way likely to offend a reasonable adult. Games have been refused for sexual content involving characters who appeared under 18. https://www.classification.gov.au/classification-ratings/what-are-ratings ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_banned_video_games_in_Australia (snippet). The standard is "**appears to be**", not stated age.
- GUIDANCE. OpenAI usage policies: never "exploit, endanger, or sexualize anyone under 18". https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/ (snippet). Generator safety filters are tuned to *apparent* minors. A generator may also refuse or alter prompts that read as young, which is another reason to prompt for explicit adult ages.
- CLAIM. MetaHuman Creator has no child presets and its database is adult or late-teen only. Child looks need workarounds or third-party packs. https://yelzkizi.org/create-metahuman-child-characters/ ; https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-can-i-make-a-metahuman-child/2282600 (snippet, low-authority source). Implication: the **youngest MetaHuman presets are the risk band**. Blending "young" presets and smoothing skin is exactly how people fake teens.
- CLAIM (research). What makes a face or figure read young: a higher head-to-body ratio (bigger head relative to body) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10813407/); higher facial contrast, which reads younger (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0057985); craniofacial shape and absence of wrinkles (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3806007/); and the neck and jawline, the strongest region for perceived youth (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42322213/). All snippets.
- Practical rules (our reading, from the above):
  - Cast every figure with a written age of 25 or over. Keep a floor of about 30 for anyone *generated*, because generators and presets drift younger.
  - For adults at the young end: defined jaw and neck, adult nose and brow, some skin texture and lines, adult head-to-body ratio (no oversized heads), adult height with no scaled-down bodies, and period adult clothing and hair (work clothes, suits, perms, moustaches).
  - Crowds: no one below a height threshold, no school uniforms, satchels, prams or pushchairs, or playground props. Check signage and posters for children too.
  - Voices: no childlike or teen-pitched synthetic voices. Test each voice by asking a listener "how old is this person?"
  - "Apparent age" test: show each portrait and crowd frame, without names, to a few people and ask their age guess. Anyone guessed under 21 is recast.
