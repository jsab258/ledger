# Cast looks: working people in a northern English port town, c. 1988-1992

Research notes for MetaHuman casting. Compiled 2026-09-24. No images downloaded.

Labels on every line:
- **DATA** = a survey figure. **OBS** = observation from photographs, fashion history or journalism.
- **opened** = I read the page or PDF text myself. **snippet** = seen only in a search-engine summary, not verified on the page. **recalled** = from background knowledge, NOT found in a source this session; treat as unverified.

## 1. Hair

**Women**
- OBS (fashion history, snippet/opened): through the 1980s "big hair" was usually permed to get volume; the permed bouffant was "highly fashionable among women of all walks of life"; blunt cuts dominated late in the decade; burgundy, plum-red and auburn dyes were popular. Wikipedia "Hairstyles in the 1980s", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hairstyles_in_the_1980s, opened 2026-09-24 (US-leaning, not UK-specific).
- OBS: shampoo and set (a weekly salon appointment) had faded by the 2010s "except with members of the older generation". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shampoo_and_set, snippet. So in 1990 women born before about 1935 (over 55) are the set-and-perm generation.
- OBS (Tom Wood, viewed): younger women wear bleached or highlighted hair tied back with a fringe; permed short curls in their 30s-40s; headscarves over set hair in their 60s and older.
- Estimate for casting (OBS, my estimate, no data): women 45+, about half with a visible perm or set; grey left grey common from the 60s up, with a blue or silver rinse on some. Women 18-35: highlights or bleach, permed or crimped volume, big fringes. **No survey of hair dyeing in 1990 was found.** (Mintel 2017: 53% of UK women use home colourant, snippet, https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/brits-prefer-blondes-blonde-is-the-nations-number-one-dyed-hair-colour-of-choice/, for comparison with today only.)

**Men**
- OBS: mullets were a working-class and suburban style; businessmen kept short, neat hair and very short sideburns; fully shaved heads spread. Wikipedia "Hairstyles in the 1980s" (opened).
- OBS: male perms were in fashion from about 1982, especially among footballers, and declined later in the decade (https://hankeringforhistory.com/the-history-of-perms/ and Wikipedia, snippet).
- OBS: by 1990 skinhead crops had passed into football casual and far-right wear; sovereign rings were linked with that scene (see §6).
- Estimate (OBS): men 50+ wear short back and sides, side partings, some Brylcreem or oiled hair, and receding hair left as it is (no transplants; a comb-over is plausible). Men 25-45 wear short back and sides with more on top, the occasional mullet or grown-out perm, and flat tops among the young. Curtains (centre parting) are 1992 onward and too early for 1990.

## 2. Facial hair

- OBS: moustaches and full beards carried over from the late 1970s into the early 1980s and then waned; clean-shaven became the norm by the mid-to-late 1980s (Wikipedia 1980s hairstyles, opened). Debrett's: clean upper lips were "the order of the day" in the 1990s (https://debretts.com/a-brief-history-of-the-moustache/, snippet).
- OBS: moustaches stayed common among working men and footballers ("4 moustaches on show in the 1986 FA Cup Final", snippet, via Bleacher Report/Balls.ie results). Anecdotally they were common in Liverpool in the 80s (snippet, low quality).
- **DATA, for today only**: YouGov, 28 Sep 2023, https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/47434-most-british-men-now-have-facial-hair (opened). 54% of British men have some facial hair (37% in 2011, 42% in 2016); 34% have a beard and moustache, 13% stubble, 4% beard only, **2% moustache only**. Among 18-39s, 69% have facial hair. In 2011, 66% of women preferred clean-shaven men.
- **No survey of facial hair in 1990 exists that I could find.** Estimate (OBS, mine): men 35-60 in 1990, about 25-35% with a moustache, fewer than 5% with a full beard, the rest clean-shaven. Men under 30 mostly clean-shaven, with the odd bumfluff moustache. Designer stubble among the young is rare. Fishermen sometimes bearded after trips. Moustaches were full "chevron" or "walrus" shapes, not shaped or waxed.

## 3. Glasses

- DATA/history (opened): NHS frames ended in 1985-86. The optical voucher scheme was introduced in July 1986. Before then the NHS offered a limited range of frames: metal Windsor-type pattern 211/221, and plastic pattern 524 in Pale Flesh, Brown Mottle, Demi Blonde, Crystal, Ice Blue and Black. The first new ladies' design, the 924, came in June 1983. NHS glasses carried stigma. Science Museum Group Journal, "Rather unspectacular", https://journal.sciencemuseum.ac.uk/article/rather-unspectacular/ (opened); People's History of the NHS (snippet).
- OBS: in 1990 older people often still wore old NHS-pattern frames (flesh-pink or brown-mottle plastic, thin metal), while people who had bought frames since 1986 wore the fashion of the day: large lenses (plastic lenses made big frames light), oversized plastic or thin gold metal aviator shapes, tinted lenses. Fashion-era.com / College of Optometrists (snippet).
- No figure for the share of adults wearing glasses in 1990 was found. Contact-lens prescribing data only start in 1996 (Manchester 1996-2020 study, snippet). Estimate: glasses on about 30-40% of adults, mostly over 45 for reading; contact lenses rare, young and middle-class.

## 4. Skin, teeth, tattoos

**Smoking**
- DATA (opened, ONS General Lifestyle Survey 2011 report, ch.1, https://www.ons.gov.uk/.../chapter1smokinggenerallifestylesurveyoverviewareportonthe2011generallifestylesurvey/pdf): **30% of GB adults smoked cigarettes in 1990** (20% in 2011; 45% in 1974). **In 1990, 18% of male smokers and 2% of female smokers mainly smoked hand-rolled.** The average male smoker smoked 17 a day in 1982.
- DATA (snippet, several results citing the GHS): 1990, men 31%, women 29% (1972: 52% and 41%).
- DATA (opened, same ONS report): in 2011 routine occupations 31% vs higher professional 10%. ASH (opened): 2019, routine/manual 23.4% vs managerial/professional 9.3%. The manual/non-manual gap has held.
- **Recalled, NOT verified**: GHS 1990 by socio-economic group, professional men about 16%, unskilled manual men about 48%; women about 16% vs 36%. Check against the GHS 1990 report (OPCS/HMSO 1992) before quoting.
- Estimate (my figure): working-class men 30-60 in a northern port in 1990, about 40-50% smokers; women about 35-40%. Casting tells: nicotine-yellow fingertips on the index and middle finger, brown-stained teeth, deep lines around the lips on women smokers over 40, sallow skin.
- Outdoor work (OBS, general knowledge, no source opened): trawlermen and dockers have weathered, ruddy skin, broken veins on the cheeks and nose, squint lines, and chapped hands with scars and swollen knuckles.

**Teeth**
- DATA (opened, Adult Dental Health Survey 2009, Theme 1, Table 1.5.1, England, https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/6884/mrdoc/pdf/6884theme1_oral_health_and_function.pdf; my extraction of the table text was partly garbled, so the 65+ figure is marked). **Edentate (no natural teeth) in 1988:**
  - All adults: 20% (28% in 1978, 12% in 1998, **6% in 2009**)
  - 25-34: 1%
  - 35-44: 3%
  - 45-54: 15%
  - **55-64: 35%** (stated in the report text)
  - 65 and over: about 66% (garbled table, verify)
  - Men 16%, women 25%
- DATA (same report, Table 1.5.3, 1988): among dentate adults the mean number of teeth was 22.7 at 45-54 and 16.9 at 55+. Only 38% of dentate adults aged 55+ had 21 or more teeth.
- DATA (opened, Downer 1991, BDJ, abstract via Europe PMC, PMID 2007088): the 1988 survey found **"Northern Ireland, Scotland and the north of England remained the parts of the country with the poorest dental health."** So a northern port sits above those national figures. I did not find the regional table (Todd & Lader, *Adult Dental Health 1988*, HMSO 1991).
- DATA (opened, PMC4126783): UK edentulousness 20% in 1988, 12% in 1998, 6% in 2009.
- Casting implication: about a third of people 55-64 and about two-thirds of those 65+ wear full dentures. Dentures of the period are too even, too white and flat, with pink acrylic; without them the face has a collapsed mouth and a short lower face. Dentate middle-aged people have gaps, missing molars, grey amalgam fillings, and nothing whitened or straightened. Orthodontics are rare in adults over 25.

**Tattoos**
- DATA (snippet, 2015 survey reported in several places): about 20% of British adults tattooed in 2015, but only **7% of people born before 1950**. YouGov 2024: 24% (snippet).
- OBS: in 1990 tattoos meant sailors, trawlermen, ex-servicemen, dockers and prisoners, and some young working-class men. Typical work: blue-black, blurred and faded, on the forearms, the backs of hands and the fingers (dots or letters); swallows, anchors, names, hearts and "Mum". Women with tattoos were rare and the tattoos small and hidden. No 1990 prevalence data found. Estimate: 15-25% of working men 30-60 in a fishing port; under 2% of women.
- Acne scarring: no data; ordinary.

## 5. Build and height

- DATA (snippet, NHS Digital HSE trend pages): 1993 mean height **men 174.4 cm, women 161.1 cm**. Men have gained a little under 2 cm since, women a little over 1 cm. 1993 mean BMI 25.8.
- DATA (snippet, HSE): obesity in **1993: men 13%, women 16%** (all adults 15%). **2022: 29% of adults**; 2024: 30%. Overweight including obese: 53% in 1993, 64% in 2022, 66% in 2024.
- DATA (snippet): earlier, 1980 obesity was men 6%, women 8-9%; 1986-87 (Dietary and Nutritional Survey of British Adults) men 7%, women 12%.
- Casting implication: in a 1990 crowd about one adult in eight is obese, against one in three or four today. Severe obesity is rare. The typical middle-aged working man is lean in the limbs with a "beer belly" rather than big all over. Older women are stout rather than obese. Young people are thin. Shorter than today by 1-2 cm on average; older people (born 1920s-30s) visibly shorter again.

## 6. Grooming, make-up, jewellery

- OBS (fashion-history pages, snippet, low quality): frosted and blue eyeshadow, frosted lipstick and pencilled, thin, highly arched brows were late-80s/90s looks (the thin brow belongs more to 1993-99; in 1990 the brows are fuller late-80s brows). Dark lip liner with lighter lipstick belongs to the 1990s.
- OBS (Tom Wood, Looking for Love, snippet): nights out meant dangly earrings, plastic jewellery and heavy make-up.
- Estimate for casting: women 18-35 wear visible foundation (often a shade off, with a tidemark at the jaw), heavy mascara, blue, pink or brown shadow, and pink or coral frosted or glossy lipstick; blusher in stripes. Women 45+: powder, a bit of lipstick, often none on weekdays. **No lash extensions, fake tan, contouring, lip filler, microbladed or laminated brows, gel nails or whitened teeth.**
- Jewellery (OBS, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_ring and auction-house history, snippet): sovereign rings and pendants revived in the 70s-80s; football casuals wore gold chains and sovereigns. Working women: gold hoops, gold chains and rope chains, several rings, a wedding band and an engagement ring. Men: wedding ring, sovereign or signet ring, a gold chain on some younger men, a cheap watch.
- Ear piercing: common in women. One ear-stud or sleeper in young men (some). **No nose, eyebrow, lip or tongue piercings, and no ear stretchers.**

## 7. 1990 at a glance vs today

1. Faces shaved: moustaches on some men over 35, almost no full or groomed beards (a beard and moustache is 34% of men today).
2. Far fewer obese people: about 13-16% in 1993 against about 29-30% now.
3. Bad teeth everywhere past 45: dentures on about 35% of 55-64s and about two-thirds of 65+ (England, 1988); the north is worse. Nothing whitened, no veneers, no braces on adults.
4. Smokers: 30% of adults nationally, more among manual workers. Stained fingers and teeth, lined lips.
5. Perms and sets on women over 40; bleached, highlighted, big permed hair on the young; headscarves over set hair on the old.
6. Glasses: big lenses, old NHS pink or brown plastic on the elderly, gold metal aviators; contact lenses rare.
7. Tattoos only on seafarers and hard men, blue-black and blurred, on the hands and forearms; almost none on women; no sleeves or neck work.
8. Piercings: ears only.
9. Make-up: coloured shadow, frosted lipstick, unshaped natural or plucked brows; no lashes, fake tan, contour or filler.
10. Skin: weathered, sun-damaged in outdoor men, sallow in smokers; no cosmetic work of any kind.
11. People are slightly shorter, and the elderly look older at a given age (smoking, teeth, outdoor work).
