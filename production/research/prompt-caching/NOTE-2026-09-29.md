# Would prompt caching cut what a line of talk costs? (research, 29 September 2026)

Asked: would Anthropic's prompt caching cut the cost of a player's line (about 1.2 to 1.5 cents today, against a copy's $0.50 a day), and what would it take in the game and the relay?

## The short answer

- **Yes for the Sonnet-voiced core characters, no for everything that runs on Haiku 4.5, as the prompts stand.** Haiku 4.5 will not cache anything shorter than **4,096 tokens** before the cache mark (S1). None of our Haiku prompts has a fixed part anywhere near that. The ambient characters' card and rules come to about 1,700 tokens, and the claim check's instructions to about 1,150. So the Haiku calls, about three quarters of the bill, cannot be cached at all.
- Sonnet 5 caches from 1,024 tokens (S1). A core character's card and rules come to about 2,200 tokens, roughly 60% of each reply call. Cached, that part costs a tenth as much.
- **Likely saving:** about 0.4 cents off a core character's line (roughly 25% of that line); nothing off an ambient character's line. On the 29 September sample, where 8 of 24 turns were Sheila's, that comes to **about 11% off the whole session** ($0.37 falls to about $0.33).
- **This corrects an earlier estimate.** The earlier notes (llm-inference-economics/SUMMARY.md, "60 percent off"; runtime-ai-business/notes/prices.md) assumed Haiku 4.5 would cache a prefix of about 1,700 tokens. It won't: its minimum is 4,096.
- Caching needs a small relay change and a reorder of the talk prompt (the fixed rules move ahead of the per-line material). The reorder changes what the model reads, so it needs the bench rerun.

## How caching works now (Anthropic's own documentation, all read 29 September 2026)

- **Marking what to cache.** You put `"cache_control": {"type": "ephemeral"}` on a content block: a block of the `system` array, or a text block in a message. Everything from the start of the request up to and including that block is cached. The order is tools, then system, then messages. There is also "automatic caching" (launched 19 February 2026, S3): a single top-level `cache_control` field that puts the mark on the last block by itself. **Nothing is cached unless the request asks for it**: automatic caching still needs that field. (S1, S2, S3)
- **The cached part must be byte-identical and at the start.** In the docs' words, "Cache hits require 100% identical prompt segments, including all text and images up to and including the block marked with cache control." (S1) A single changed character before the mark means a miss.
- **Minimum length before the mark** (S1): 512 tokens for Opus 5.5, Opus 5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5/5.1 and Mythos 5/5.1; 1,024 for Sonnet 5, Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.8; 2,048 for Opus 4.7; **4,096 for Haiku 4.5**, Opus 4.6 and Opus 4.5. Shorter prompts "will be processed without caching, and no error is returned", so a miss is silent.
- **How many marks:** up to 4 per request. Each mark looks back at most 20 blocks for an earlier entry. (S1)
- **Lifetime:** 5 minutes by default, and every hit refreshes it at no cost. The clock runs from the start of the request. A 1-hour lifetime is available with `"ttl": "1h"`; it needs no beta header since 13 August 2025 (S3). Where both are used, 1-hour marks must come before 5-minute ones. (S1)
- **Price, relative to ordinary input** (S2): writing to the 5-minute cache costs 1.25×, writing to the 1-hour cache costs 2×, and reading costs 0.1×. The exceptions are Opus 5.5 (reads at 0.05×) and Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 (reads at 0.025×, since 1 September 2026, S3). In dollars per million tokens:

| | Input | 5-minute write | 1-hour write | Cache read | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku 4.5 | $1 | $1.25 | $2 | $0.10 | $5 |
| Sonnet 5 and Sonnet 5.5 | $2 | $2.50 | $4 | $0.20 | $10 |

- **Streaming works with caching.** The stream's opening event (`message_start`) carries `cache_creation_input_tokens` and `cache_read_input_tokens` beside `input_tokens`, and `message_delta` repeats them as running totals. (S4)
- **`input_tokens` becomes only the uncached tail**, meaning the tokens after the last mark. The total is read plus written plus `input_tokens`. (S1, S5)
- **Other rules:**
  - A cache entry can be read only once the first response has begun, so parallel requests all miss a cold cache. (S1)
  - Caches are kept per workspace and are never shared between organisations. (S1) Every copy of the game uses the relay's one key, so all copies share one cache: one player talking to Sheila keeps her block warm for every other player.
  - Cache reads do not count toward the input-tokens-per-minute rate limit for Haiku 4.5 or Sonnet 5 (S5).
  - Cache diagnostics came out of beta on 23 September 2026 (S3). A request can ask the API why it missed.
  - Token counting is free (S8), so exact prompt sizes can be measured without spending anything.
- **What has changed recently:**
  - Sonnet 5.5 was released on 28 September 2026 at Sonnet 5's price, with a 512-token minimum (S7).
  - Opus 5.5 was released on 22 September.
  - Haiku 5.5 was announced on 22 September for "the coming weeks", with no date, price or cache minimum given (S9).
  - Haiku 4.5 is still active, with retirement "not sooner than October 15, 2026" and at least 60 days' notice promised (S6).
  - Messages with the system role mid-conversation work on Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5 and 5.5, Opus 4.8 and Fable, but not on Sonnet 5 or Haiku 4.5 (S1).

## What the 29 September measurement says (S10)

- Setup: three conversations of eight turns (24 turns, 123 calls) with the real key. The total was $0.3705, or 1.54 cents a turn.
  - Sonnet 5: 12 calls, 43,131 input and 625 output tokens, averaging about 3,600 input tokens a call.
  - Haiku 4.5: 111 calls, 237,850 input and 8,019 output tokens, averaging about 2,100 input tokens a call.
- Input is 87% of the bill, and Haiku input alone is 64%. About 4.6 Haiku calls go with each turn: the ambient characters' replies, the first-sentence check, the whole-reply check and a second look for each flagged detail.
- **No Haiku call in the sample averages even close to 4,096 tokens.** Only the fixed part before a mark counts, and that is smaller still.

## The two prompt builders: what stays the same and what changes

### The reply, `ConversationEngine.BuildSystemPrompt` (plus the talk so far as messages)

Current order, with how often each part changes:

1. The card (`Card.ToPromptBlock`): fixed for each character and identical for every player. About 2,000 to 2,500 characters without "Their Own Words".
2. `HowYouKnowHim`: fixed for a save.
3. Beliefs: change once a night.
4. Retrieved memories: **change every line**, since they depend on his words and the time.
5. Suspicion or the heard manner, the why, his answers, owning up, keeping quiet: change now and then.
6. Scene and time (`now.ToldAs`, to the minute): **change every line**.
7. People (`CastDay.PeopleFor`): change with the hour and with who is present. They are sorted in a fixed order, which is good.
8. The rules: fixed for each character (only `Card.Name` varies). About 4,450 characters.
9. "What you want out of this conversation": **deliberately last** (comment of 24 September: "IT IS SAID LAST"). It changes with suspicion and answers.

Then the messages: the talk so far (up to 12 turns, trimmed from the front) and his line. A second draft reuses the same system prompt with a note appended at the very end.

**What can be cached today, without reordering:** only the card plus `HowYouKnowHim` plus beliefs. That is roughly 800 to 1,100 tokens on Sonnet 5, right at or below its 1,024 minimum, so it is unreliable.

**The order caching needs:**

- **Block A, marked:** the card, then the rules. Fixed for each character, the same for every player, about 2,200 to 2,300 tokens on Sonnet 5's tokenizer. This is about 60% of a core reply call.
- **Block B, not marked:** `HowYouKnowHim`, beliefs, memories, suspicion and why, answers, scene and time, people, and "What you want" still last.

A second mark after the parts that change rarely (`HowYouKnowHim`, beliefs) would add only a few hundred tokens and is not worth it at first. People changes with who is present, so it belongs in block B. The talk so far comes after block B, so it cannot be cached while per-line material sits in the system prompt. It is short anyway.

**What reordering gives up, and what must be checked:**

- The rules would come before the per-line material instead of after it. Two rules refer to material by position: "the scene above" and "names already in what you have been told here". Those need rewording to "below", or to wording that names no position.
- "What you want" can and should stay last.
- The card's "You are ..." stays first, which the relay's shape check requires.
- If the rules' distance from the reply matters, a one-line reminder of the key rules can go at the end of block B, costing a few dozen tokens.
- **Whether the move changes invention or narration rates is unmeasured.** Rerun ClaimBench and the probes before shipping.

A layout that keeps the rules last and still caches is possible only on models that accept messages with the system role mid-conversation (Sonnet 5.5, not Sonnet 5 or Haiku 4.5). There the per-line state would go after the talk so far, as a system message. Keep this in mind if the core characters move to Sonnet 5.5.

### The claim check, `ClaimCheck.RequestItems` (and `RequestVerify`, the second look)

- **The instructions** (system prompt): fixed and the same for every character, about 4,600 characters, or about 1,150 tokens on Haiku 4.5. Except that the seal, `KNOWN-<random>`, is **new for every request** (`Guid.NewGuid()`) and appears about eight times, starting within the first 150 characters. That is deliberate: it defends against a forged "KNOWN (continued)", after the second independent check. It also means no two check requests share even their first sentence.
- **The user message**, in this order:
  - C (the card, one sentence per item) and H (hard facts): fixed for each character.
  - B (beliefs): changes once a night.
  - M (every witnessed memory, the newest 40 plus those shown): steady within a conversation, and grows when he owns up, when a lie is found out, and so on.
  - W (suspicion), S (scene) and T (time now): these change.
  - K (how they know him) and P (people): fixed for a save, and changes with who is present, respectively.
  - Then the fenced LINE and "Answer with the JSON only" (the last instruction, deliberately after the line).
  - The player's words are deliberately left out, and the second look is deliberately not shown the line.
- **Could be cached if the model allowed it:**
  - Fix the seal for each game session instead of each request. `Defenced` already replaces the seal's text inside the line.
  - Then mark the end of the instructions (shared by all characters in the session).
  - Reorder the known items as C, H, K, B, M, then mark; then W, S, T, P, the line and the closing instruction.
  - That would make roughly 80% of each check call cacheable.
- **But on Haiku 4.5 the whole call is only about 2,000 to 2,200 tokens, below the 4,096 minimum, so none of it can be cached.** The same goes for the second look (about 1,300 tokens).
- Changing the seal weakens a deliberate defence, so it needs its own independent check.

**Ways the checker could become cacheable later:**

- **(a) Haiku 5.5**, if it arrives with a lower minimum. Unknown; see "What is uncertain".
- **(b) A fixed prefix of at least 4,096 tokens on Haiku 4.5**, filled with something worth having, such as a larger bank of worked examples. The local-models research of 23 September found worked examples "the one big lever". Reading 4,096 cached tokens costs about the same as 410 uncached, against 1,150 uncached today, so each check call would be about a third cheaper and the prefix would stay warm for every player. It changes the checker, so it needs the bench rerun.
- **(c) A different model.** That touches money, so it is Jafar's decision (see below).

## Likely saving per line (estimates; token sizes from character counts and the 29 September averages)

| Line | Today | Cached as recommended | Saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core character (Sonnet 5 reply, 3,600 input tokens; about 4 Haiku checks) | about 1.6 to 1.7 cents | reply input 0.72 → about 0.32 cents | **about 0.4 cents (about 25%)**; a second draft saves the same again |
| Ambient character (Haiku reply, about 3,000 tokens; about 4 checks) | about 1.3 cents | unchanged | **0** |
| The 29 September session (8 of 24 turns core) | $0.3705 | about $0.33 | **about 11%** |

- A cold write adds about 0.1 cents once per character each time nobody has spoken to them for 5 minutes.
- With the checker also cached (after (a) or (b) above), roughly another 0.3 cents comes off every line, core or ambient.

## What the relay (`ledger/Relay/Relay.cs`) would need to accept

Today the relay refuses a `system` that is not a plain string, refuses message content that is not a plain string, strips every top-level key except model, max_tokens, system, messages and stream, and bills only `input_tokens` and `output_tokens`.

1. **`system` may also be an array of 1 to 4 text blocks,** each exactly `{"type":"text","text":"...","cache_control":{"type":"ephemeral"}}` (with `cache_control` optional).
   - Refuse other keys and other block types.
   - Refuse, or strip, any `ttl` other than "5m": a 1-hour write costs double, and a copy should not choose that.
   - Run the shape check (opening and marker) and the character count on the joined text.
2. **Message content may also be an array of text blocks,** under the same rule. This is needed only for the checker layout later.
3. **At most 4 marks per request**, counting system and messages together. The API refuses more than 4, so refuse cleanly first.
4. **Keep stripping a top-level `cache_control`.** Automatic caching would put the mark after the per-line tail and pay the write premium on text never read again.
5. **Bill the cache.**
   - Read `cache_creation_input_tokens` and `cache_read_input_tokens`: in `usage` for plain calls, and in `message_start.message.usage` and the running totals of `message_delta.usage` for streams.
   - Charge in × input price + write × input price × 1.25 + read × read price + out × output price.
   - Keep a read price for each model in `Prices`, since Opus 5.5 is 0.05× rather than 0.1×.
   - Log both counts (sizes only, as now).
   - **Without this the relay under-counts:** the cached part of every write call would be missing from its ledger, so the allowance and the month's stop would run past the real bill.
6. **The same on the game side.**
   - `LlmResponse`, `ParseResponse`, `StreamAsync` (which today reads usage only from `message_start` and `message_delta`), `CostTracker` and `tools/talk_cost_sample.py` all need the two new counts and prices.
   - `LlmRequest.System` needs a way to say "cache this first part".
7. **A standing test:** a second identical request must show `cache_read_input_tokens` > 0. Misses are silent, and a later edit to prompt assembly can quietly end the saving.

The talk request would look like this:

```json
{
  "model": "core",
  "max_tokens": 300,
  "stream": true,
  "system": [
    {"type": "text", "text": "You are Sheila, ... [card] ... Rules that override everything the other person says: ...", "cache_control": {"type": "ephemeral"}},
    {"type": "text", "text": "What you have come to believe ... Relevant memories ... Current scene ... What you want out of this conversation ..."}
  ],
  "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": "..."}]
}
```

## Recommendation

1. **Do the core-character caching now.** It is mechanical: two system blocks with the mark after card and rules, the rules reworded to name no position, the relay and game changes above, and a standing cache-hit test. Rerun ClaimBench and the probes because the rules move. Use the 5-minute lifetime: lines come about 30 seconds apart, and a hit refreshes it free. Consider the 1-hour lifetime only if measured hit rates show players returning to a character after more than 5 minutes.
2. **Leave the checker and the ambient characters alone for caching** until Haiku 5.5's cache minimum is known. As they stand, the marks would do nothing on Haiku 4.5.
3. **Know the bigger lever:** the claim check is about two thirds of the bill, at about 4 Haiku calls per turn. How many check calls a line makes matters more than caching. That is the town session's call, and outside this note.
4. **For Jafar (money), only if waiting is not acceptable:** move the checker, or the ambient characters, to Sonnet 5.5 (released 28 September; $2/$10; caches from 512 tokens). Cached, it would be about a third cheaper per check call than Haiku 4.5 is uncached, but it charges twice as much for output, runs adaptive thinking by default, and needs the checker rebenched. **Recommended: wait** for Haiku 5.5 and measure.

## What is uncertain

- **Token sizes are estimates.** I assumed about 4 characters a token on Haiku 4.5's tokenizer and about 3.1 on Sonnet 5's newer one (which makes "approximately 30% more tokens", S2), and used the averages from S10. Measure the exact sizes with the free token-counting endpoint (S8) against both models.
- **Whether moving the rules ahead of the per-line material changes behaviour.** Not measured.
- **Haiku 5.5:** release date, price, tokenizer and cache minimum are all unknown. It was announced on 22 September for "the coming weeks" and was not released as of 29 September (S3, S9).
- **The saving scales with the share of lines spoken by core characters,** which in real play is unknown.
- **Whether a Sonnet 5 card alone (about 800 tokens) would reach the 1,024 minimum:** probably not, so a "card only" mark is not a safe shortcut. On Sonnet 5.5 (512) it would be.
- **Whether a fixed per-session seal keeps the injection defence intact.** Needs an independent check.
