## Research (a helper, read 25 October; pages fetched through a summarising tool, so read in summary, not word for word; notes in the session's scratch folder)

**Method, cheapest first:**
1. Wear in the master material with a few controls (wear, roughness, hue, brightness); walls in clean, aged and torn versions painted on to break repetition (a detective game's art team in Unreal 5.5 with Lumen: 80.lv, 8 January 2026).
2. Baked masks: occlusion and curvature drive dirt in corners and wear on edges (Adobe's dirt generator; Epic's vertex-colour material for mesh painting; Epic's material layers).
3. Decals for the particular marks, kept modest in number (Epic's decal documentation: cost grows with screen coverage and material complexity; mesh decals for wrapping marks).
4. Roughness tells most: dust for age, handprints for recent touch; wear follows where a thing sits, how long, and what it is for (80.lv, an environment artist's tips, 2018).
5. Set dressing that shows use: notices, small personal things.
The most for the least: roughness variation, one baked occlusion and edge dirt mask in the master material, and 10 to 20 decals on the hot spots.

**The period (where wear gathers):**
- Tobacco smoke leaves a sticky brown deposit on walls and ceilings, strongest where smokers sat (InspectAPedia, building stains from occupants); pale unstained patches where pictures hung (a 2025 news piece, secondary).
- Carpet traffic lanes: the pile flattens and reads shaded or soiled even when clean (CleanLink, 2013).
- Vinyl seats crack at the front edge, corners and seams (a seller's page, snippet only: unverified).
- Unverified, no source found: scuffs at shoulder height behind a bench; finger grime round switches, push plates and handles; curled, yellowed notices and tape; chipped door frames; dust on high surfaces; worn laminate.
- No dated 1985 to 1995 photograph of a British minicab office, office or waiting room could be reached (a 1990 café interior by Peter Marshall is catalogued at the London Museum, the picture not seen).

## The wear target, ranked (each with its place and the cheapest method)

1. **Smoke:** the ceiling and upper walls a warm yellow-brown, darkest above the bench, the radio desk and the light fittings, fading lower down. A height gradient and tint in the room's material. (Canon allows tobacco.)
2. **Pale patches** on that stain behind the calendar and the notices, a darker rim round them. One decal each.
3. **The carpet:** a darker, flattened band from the door to the counter and to the radio desk, the strip along the walls untouched (the path exists; extend it to the radio desk and keep the edges clean).
4. **The bench:** shine and cracks along the seat's front edge, worn arm ends. Roughness and a normal decal.
5. **The counter:** pale laminate where forearms rest (exists, where the book lies), grime on the customer side's lower edge, a cigarette burn or two. A mask and 2 to 3 decals.
6. **Touch points:** grime round the light switch, the door's push plate and handle (unverified as a period rule, true of any used door). Small decals.
7. **Wall bases and the door frame:** scuffs and chipped paint, from the occlusion and curvature mask.
8. **Paper:** notices a little curled, faded and off square, yellowed tape. Mesh and roughness.
9. **Dust** on the tops of cupboards and fittings: roughness.

## The room lit: four views judged by fresh reviewers (25 October, 23:20 to 23:37)

Pictures: F:/LedgerTools/gate/new/office/2026-10-25-lit3 (production/previews/office-lit-2026-10-25.jpg: the door, the bench end, the window and the bench, by day above and at night below). One fresh reviewer per view, against the Hook sheet; no earlier picture exists for these views. **All four fail.** Lived-in, 2 of 5 in every view.

**Faults (the glitch list for item 5):**
1. **By day the lit room reads near-black** (high; the door view's mean brightness 12 of 255, the bench view 82% of the room under 6%): the day's exposure follows the bright window, not the room.
2. **By day the ceiling fittings show unlit,** grey boxes with no tubes (high): the room's lights read as off by day.
3. **By day the shop glass from inside is a dark mirror,** the room reflected in it, the street not seen from the door view (medium-high).
4. **At night the back wall and the counter top blow out** (high and medium): the map 38% and the drivers' board 53% clipped, the counter's laminate 39%; the desk lamp throws a hard hotspot and its own shadow up across the map.
5. **The "wear" on the walls reads as stuck-on rectangles** (medium): hard-edged streaky patches behind the bench (one tile per bench section, across the dado rail, over the calendar's dates) and in the far corner, smearing round it.
6. **Rings on papers hung on the wall** (low to medium): the fares card, the licence, the calendar photo: meant for papers on a desk.
7. **Placeholder blocks** (medium): the books and binders on the shelf, flat colour, no spines; the radio desk a bare box (low).
8. **A flat pale-grey patch on the carpet** at the counter's foot (medium): no texture.
9. Low: Ron's shoes cast no contact shadow at night; a round shadow outside with nothing to cast it; the blind's slats grainy; the posts outside ending at the blind's rail.

**Known shortfalls** (listed, not failing): the warm amber night light against cool fluorescent tubes; the Hook sheet's muted grey palette; fine detail below Kingdom Come II (the bench's vinyl, the counter, the notices without pins); little clutter; my camera too high and steep (the bench cut off: next time eye height and a gentler tilt; in this mode a positive pitch looks down).
