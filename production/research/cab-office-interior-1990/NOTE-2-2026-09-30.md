# Inside a minicab office, 1985 to 1995: second pass

Research note, 30 September 2026, second pass, by a separate research helper (about thirty minutes). It follows NOTE-2026-09-30.md and does not repeat its sources, except one that it could now actually see. Every page was read on 30 September 2026.

Labels: OPENED (I saw the picture, or read the page itself), SNIPPET (a caption or search summary only), HOLE (not found).

Short version: still no readable photograph of a British minicab office interior from 1985 to 1995. But one route that failed last time works: the stock libraries (Alamy, Getty) load in the browser pane, though the plain page reader gets refused. So I could see the 1980 Brixton minicab office picture for the first time. I also found two 1987 London office photographs that are good for surfaces, and two period TV dispatch offices to grab frames from. Three period photographs seen in all.

## 1. Photograph links

### Minicab or taxi offices

1. John Heseltine, "Man in minicab office, Brixton, south London, 1980". Alamy D99KY1. The only real interior of a minicab office found in either pass. Ten years early. https://www.alamy.com/stock-photo-man-in-minicab-office-brixton-south-london-1980-57376229.html (OPENED this time, in the browser pane, after declining non-essential cookies. Black and white. Contributor, date 1980 and London are on the page.)
   What I saw: a man bent over a desk or ledge just inside a shop window, writing on papers with a pen. The window has a wooden frame. A big phone number, "582 5104", is painted in white on the glass, with more painted words below it, not legible at that size. The glass carries street reflections. The number reads the right way round, so the photographer seems to have stood in the street (my reading, not the caption's).

2. "run-down minicab firm office", Gary Roebuck, Alamy A55REH. A "CP Radio Cars" office: a white-painted boarded cabin under a concrete deck, sign with phone numbers, barred window. Taken 2007, outside only. https://www.alamy.com/stock-photo-run-down-minicab-firm-office-11206552.html (OPENED. Out of period; listed only as a sign-and-frontage style.)

### The nearest kin

3. Anna Fox, "Assistant Office Manager and Director of HG Peggs paper merchants, Hammersmith", 1987, from her series Work Stations (Museum of London and Camerawork). A small firm's office. https://collections.londonmuseum.net/media/col/178/69/mid_in15616.jpg (OPENED)
   What I saw: a woman at a desk with a white top, papers in front of her, hand to her face. Behind her, a glazed partition hung with pale venetian blinds, and a second office beyond with a man at a desk. Pot plants (a spider plant and a leafy plant in terracotta pots), a glass vase and an orange tape dispenser on a low blue shelf unit full of binders and books. Red plastic letter trays stacked on the right.

4. Anna Fox, "Office employer hard at work at desk", 1987, same series. https://collections.londonmuseum.net/media/col/178/71/mid_in15623.jpg (OPENED)
   What I saw: a woman writing at a light wood-effect desk covered in printed forms, with an open drawer of pens and highlighters. Dark brown carpet. Swivel chairs with mustard-brown fabric seats and black five-star bases. Grey metal drawer units. More desks along the back wall with papers and trays. A coiled telephone cord on the right.

   Both Fox pictures are listed on the museum's page "Office life in the 1980s": https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/office-life-1980s/ (OPENED). These are London commercial offices, better off than a provincial cab office. Use them for materials and fittings, not for mood.

5. Henry Grant, a photograph of two people seated at a desk in a "CAB office". The museum's record says CAB here means Citizens Advice Bureau, not a cab firm. It is a small public-facing office, the nearest kin in the museum's search. Date not given on the record. https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/v/object-1084214/photograph-gelatin-silver-print/ (record OPENED; picture not seen, so SNIPPET for the picture)

### Surfaces

6. Tefal electric convector heater No 64-41, about 1975, Science Museum Group. A free-standing electric convector of the kind a small office might have. https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co538648/tefal-electric-convector-heater-no-64-41 (the search listing was OPENED; the object page and picture were not seen. Tefal is French; whether it was sold in Britain: HOLE.)

That is the whole haul for surfaces. No readable photograph of lino, carpet tiles, a suspended ceiling, woodchip, wood-effect panelling or a wall heater in a small British shop-front office, 1983 to 1995, was found in this pass (HOLE). The two Anna Fox pictures (3 and 4) are the best surface evidence found: carpet, blinds, desks, chairs, trays.

## 2. Film and TV (sets, not real rooms)

- Boon (ITV, 1986 to 1995). In series 2 Ken Boon runs a courier firm, "The Texas Rangers", sending riders out by two-way radio, with a secretary in the office. Episode "Texas Rangers", shown 17 February 1987: he buys a radio set and a client list, and the radio turns out to belong to a rival firm. A late-1980s Midlands dispatch office on screen, the closest set to ours in period. https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0530270/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boon_(TV_series) (SNIPPET; no stills found)
- EastEnders: Kevin Masters (Colin McCormack) owned a minicab firm, and Peggy Mitchell started working for him in 1991. A minicab office set from inside our window. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_EastEnders_characters_introduced_in_1991 (SNIPPET). The fan wiki's pages on Walford's cab firms (for example https://eastenders.fandom.com/wiki/Walford_Cars) refused the reader (HOLE).
- Minder, "Come in T-64, Your Time is Ticking Away" (Candy Cabs) is dated 1979 by IMDb, so it is ten years early. https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0648666/ (SNIPPET)
- Roger Roger: a search summary says the Cresta Cabs office was a studio set. That summary came from no page I could check, so treat it as unsourced. No stills found (HOLE).

## 3. What these show about such a room

- The 1980 Brixton picture (1) fits the first note's picture of paper and voice: the booking man sits at the front window writing jobs down by hand. The firm's phone number is signwritten large on the glass, so the window is the sign. No radio, map or board can be seen in it.
- Offices of 1987 (3, 4) had dark brown carpet, light wood-effect desk tops, fabric swivel chairs on five-star bases, grey metal drawers, plastic letter trays, venetian blinds and pot plants. Phones had coiled cords. A run-down provincial cab office would likely be shabbier and older than these. That is my inference, not a source.
- Boon (1987) and EastEnders (1991) each put a small radio or minicab office on screen inside our window. Frames from them would show how designers of the time dressed such a room.

## 4. What did not work, so the next person does not repeat it

- Plain page reading (WebFetch) was refused by Historic England's archive (403), reportdigital (403) and the EastEnders fan wiki (402).
- Alamy and Getty do load in the browser pane. Alamy's "minicab office" search gave one period interior (1, Brixton 1980). The rest were 2000s to 2020s shopfronts. Its "taxi office 1980s uk" and "taxi controller radio 1980s" searches gave nothing. Getty's search matches loosely. Its "minicab office", "minicab" (oldest first) and "minicab controller" searches gave no British cab office from the period on the first page.
- Flickr's "minicab office" search: about twenty shopfronts, all from the 2000s or later. "minicab controller": two recent pictures. "cab office 1980s" and "taxi office 1980s england": nothing relevant.
- Homer Sykes' archive has no picture captioned "minicab". The London Museum has none for "minicab". "cab office" there found only the Citizens Advice picture (5). "office interior" from 1983 to 1995 found nothing.
- Leodis (Leeds) search stuck on "Loading images" in the browser pane.
- Not reached in time: Peter Marshall's Flickr stream searched inside, Janine Wiedel, Paul Reas, Daniel Meadows, Chris Killip, Paul Trevor, Nick Hedges, Tish Murtha, Picture Sheffield, Picture the Past, the Bishopsgate Institute, Amber and Mass Observation.
