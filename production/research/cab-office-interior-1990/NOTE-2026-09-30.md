# Inside a provincial minicab office, 1988 to 1992

Research note, 30 September 2026, by a separate research helper (about thirty minutes). Every source was read on 30 September 2026. Labels: OPENED (page read), SNIPPET (search summary only), HOLE (not found). The money, licensing and VAT side is in ../cab-office-1990/SUMMARY-2026-09-28.md and is not repeated here.

Short version: in thirty minutes the web gave no openable photograph of a real British minicab office interior dated 1985 to 1995. It gave one 1980 interior (a stock library that blocked reading), two dated shopfronts, some TV and stage references, and photographs of the period's phones and a desk microphone. Most of the room's contents are holes.

## 2. What was in the room

The premises

- The 1987 planning order excluded "a taxi business or business for the hire of motor vehicles" from every use class. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/764/article/3/made (OPENED) So a shop turned cab office needed its own planning permission. (SNIPPET, a modern planning guide: https://planwatch.co.uk/guides/sui-generis-use-explained)
- Room size, counter or hatch, and whether customers waited inside: HOLE for 1985 to 1995. A modern Norwich firm (2013) has a waiting area seating 50, but it is a big city-centre office and not our period. https://www.justregional.co.uk/taxi-firms-starring-role-on-tv/ (SNIPPET)

The records the law put in the room

- The operator had to keep a record of every booking "in such form as the council may, by condition attached to the grant of the licence, prescribe". He also kept records of his vehicles. He produced both to a council officer or a constable on request. Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1976, s.56(2) and (3). https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1976/57/section/56/enacted (OPENED)
- The Act required him to produce his operator's licence on request (s.56(4)). It did not require it on the wall. (OPENED, same page) Whether 1990 councils added a display condition: HOLE.
- At one Fareham firm (1989 to 1990), drivers logged every job on daily sheets and radioed it in, and the office logged it too. (From the 28 September note, source S13, OPENED then)

How jobs were sent out

- A London radio circuit began in 1974 "manually with paper dockets and voice radio dispatch", then moved to computer screens in the early 1980s. https://www.taxi-point.co.uk/post/comcab-s-journey-london-s-black-taxi-booking-firm-celebrates-50-years-on-the-road (OPENED; London black cabs, a large circuit)
- Autocab, later the main UK supplier, was founded in 1989 and sold its first system in 1991. https://www.zoominfo.com/c/autocab/3474591 (SNIPPET) So a small provincial office in 1990 very likely ran on paper and voice radio. (Inference, not sourced)
- An American firm (Portland) used handwritten order slips, a magnetic board with a numbered button for each cab, and two-way radios until the mid-1990s. https://www.radiocab.net/about-radio-cab/ (SNIPPET; American, not British. A British drivers' board: HOLE)

The radio desk

- The radio licence cost £120 a year per channel for up to 10 mobiles, from 1 November 1989. (From the 28 September note, source S11, OPENED then)
- Pye/Philips mobile sets by year: M290 1979, MX290 1982 to 1984, FM1000 and PRM80 1989. https://www.pyetelecomhistory.org/prodhist/mobiles/mobiles.html (OPENED) In the late 1980s Philips also sold the "PRF10 low cost fixed station". https://www.pyemuseum.org/divisions/communications/pye_telecom/ukhistory/ukhistory_pt3.php (OPENED) Neither page mentions taxi firms.
- Storno was bought by Motorola in 1986 and the brand was kept for six more years. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storno (SNIPPET)
- Which base station or desk microphone a provincial cab office actually used: HOLE. The Pye "Tulip" (link 11 above) is one period desk microphone, but it is not tied to taxis.
- A wall map of the district: HOLE.

Phones

- The Viscount (link 9) was the BT phone of the 1980s in homes and offices. (OPENED, MoDiP) A BT Tribune: not checked.
- BT rented indoor payphones in 1989 and 1990 (link 10). Whether cab offices had one for customers: HOLE.

Furniture, walls, light, heat, floor, kettle, ashtrays

- Seating, notices (fares, licence, "No smoking"), lighting, floor, heating, kettle and mugs, ashtrays: all HOLE. Nothing openable described them. Whether smoking indoors was normal in such an office in 1990 was not checked in this pass.

## 3. Night and day

- The office ran through the night in the sources that describe work at all: a London driver's memoir covers shift lengths and account work, but says nothing about the office itself. https://revsoc21.uk/2020/01/17/what-a-way-to-make-a-living-minicabbing-since-1979/ (OPENED)
- The stage picture of a controller alone at a microphone at night comes from Pinter (link 8, OPENED). The busy picture of one man swamped by phones comes from Minder (link 5, OPENED).
- A photograph of such an office lit at night, 1985 to 1995: HOLE.
