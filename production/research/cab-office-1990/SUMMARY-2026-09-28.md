# A minicab office in 1990: money, records, VAT, licences

Research note, 28 September 2026, by a separate research helper (about thirty minutes), for rewording Act III for Mickey's as a cab office (D58, queue 400) and deriving its laundering ceiling again. Each fact has a source number (list at the end) and a label: OPENED (page read), SNIPPET (search summary only) or DERIVED (the helper's arithmetic, assumptions stated). HOLE means not found.

## 1. How the office made its money

- Most minicabs were driven by their owners. They paid a weekly fee to a radio circuit for the radio and the bookings. [S22 SNIPPET]
- There were two models. In the agent model, self-employed drivers own and run their cars, pay the firm a fixed weekly sum and keep the rest. In the principal model, the firm owns the cars and takes a share of the takings. [S12 OPENED]
- The weekly sum was called "rent" [S16 OPENED] or "settle" [S14 OPENED].
- Principal model, 1989–90: Hamiltax, Fareham, had 22 firm-owned cars and 42 drivers. Drivers paid the firm 70% of cash takings daily and received 30% of account fares weekly. By 1992 account work was over half the work, at a rate per mile "substantially less than taxi rates". [S13 OPENED]
- Agent model, 2000s: Argyle Park Taxis, Birkenhead, charged a settle of £80 a week for cash work and £72 for contract work. The office invoiced account customers itself and paid drivers after deducting the settle. [S14 OPENED]
- In 1990 money that settle is about £49–£54 a week. [DERIVED: RPI 126.1 in 1990 against 186.7 in 2004 (S25 SNIPPET); assumes the rates applied around 2004.] A contemporary 1990 figure: HOLE.
- In the 1990s one London firm waived next week's rent if a driver did £200 of account work. [S16 OPENED]
- Account customers were invoiced monthly, plus VAT (Camberwell Cars, VAT Tribunal, 1 April 1993). [S15 SNIPPET]
- Councils set hackney fares only. Private hire fares were unregulated. [S8 OPENED]
- A 1990 survey in a university town priced a 2.5-mile daytime taxi trip at £3.00–£3.70. [S9 OPENED] That is about £1.20–£1.50 a mile. [DERIVED: assumes the survey's prices bracketed the real fare.]
- London cabs from 9 June 1989 charged 40p, then 20p per 384 yards up to £6. [S10 OPENED] That is about 92p a mile. [DERIVED]
- A Private Mobile Radio licence cost £120 a year per channel for up to 10 mobiles, and £250 for 11 to 25, from 1 November 1989. [S11 OPENED]
- Controller's wages, office rent, advertising and the office's insurance: HOLE. Hire-and-reward insurance was "very expensive" for drivers. [S16 OPENED]
- Typical provincial firm size: HOLE. The standard survey (Coe and Jackson, TRRL Research Report 68, 1986) is cited in S8 but is not online.

## 2. Records

- The operator had to record every booking before the journey, in the form the council set. He also kept the vehicle records the council required, and produced them to a council officer or constable. [S7 OPENED]
- At Hamiltax, drivers logged every job on daily sheets and radioed it in. The office logged it too. [S13 OPENED]
- At Argyle Park, the driver got a ticket from each account customer showing the price. [S14 OPENED]
- For VAT, a trader kept the records the Commissioners required, and a VAT account, for up to six years. [S4 OPENED]
- A named drivers' rent book or cash book: HOLE (likely, but unsourced).

## 3. VAT, 1988 to 1992

Registration threshold [S1 OPENED; confirmed in S2, S3]:

| From | Annual | Quarterly |
|---|---|---|
| 16 Mar 1988 | £22,100 | £7,500 |
| 15 Mar 1989 | £23,600 | £8,000 |
| 21 Mar 1990 | £25,400 | none |
| 20 Mar 1991 | £35,000 | none |
| 11 Mar 1992 | £36,600 | none |

- The standard rate was 15%, then 17.5% from 1 April 1991. [S23 SNIPPET] The VAT inside a gross sum is 3/23 at 15% and 7/47 at 17.5%. [DERIVED]
- Money drivers pay the firm (radio rent, bookings, commission, controller fees) is the firm's taxable supply. [S12 OPENED] Tribunals split on cash fares: the firm was treated as agent in some cases (1980, 1993) and as principal in Hamiltax (1992). [S12, S13 OPENED] When the office invoices account work as principal, the whole fare counts as its turnover. [S14 OPENED]
- A 12-driver office on about £50 settle takes about £30,000 a year. That is just over the 1990 threshold and under the 1991 one. One driver's fares (about £15,000 a year) fall below it. [DERIVED]
- HM Customs and Excise ran the tax through Local VAT Offices. [S17 OPENED]
- The legal basis was the Value Added Tax Act 1983, Schedule 7, as in force on 1 February 1991 [S4 OPENED]:
  - para 4: assess "to the best of their judgment" when returns look wrong;
  - para 7: keep records;
  - para 8: furnish information and produce documents on demand;
  - para 10: enter business premises "at any reasonable time"; with a justice's warrant, enter by force and seize documents;
  - para 10A: court order for access to records.
- Assessments could reach back six years, or twenty for dishonesty. Against a dead person's estate they had to be made within three years of the death. [S6 OPENED]
- The civil penalty for dishonest evasion equalled the tax evaded, and could be halved for cooperation. [S5 OPENED] New misdeclaration penalties and default interest began on 1 January 1990. [S17 OPENED]
- In 1992–93 there were 350,000 audits [S18 OPENED] across about 1.6 million traders, so roughly one visit every four to five years. [DERIVED]
- How visits were arranged in 1990, and how long they took: HOLE. Today HMRC usually gives seven days' notice, and can come unannounced. [S19 OPENED, modern]

## 4. Licensing outside London

- Part II of the 1976 Act was adoptive. Where a council adopted it, vehicles, drivers and operators were all licensed. [S8 OPENED] By 1998 "virtually all" councils had adopted it. [S21 OPENED]
- Operating without an operator's licence, or using an unlicensed car or driver, was an offence. [S7 OPENED]
- Operator: the council must find him "fit and proper". The licence ran up to five years, could carry conditions and could be appealed to magistrates. There is no transfer provision. [S7 OPENED] Councils today treat the licence as personal: a new owner applies afresh. [S20 OPENED, modern] So the heir needs his own licence. [DERIVED] Whether executors had a grace period in 1990: HOLE.
- Driver: must be "fit and proper" and have held a full driving licence for twelve months. [S7 OPENED] Knowledge tests varied, and many areas had none. [S8 OPENED] Police record checks in 1990: HOLE.
- Vehicle: must be suitable, safe, insured and not like a hackney. The licence lasted a year and the plate was displayed. A transfer of the car had to be notified to the council within 14 days. [S7 OPENED]

## 5. What made declared income implausible (general)

- Customs compare returns with what similar businesses show ("credibility checks"). Ratios are guides only. [S26 SNIPPET, modern]
- For a cab office, the comparisons that follow [DERIVED] are:
  - takings against licensed cars, drivers and radios;
  - account invoices against the booking record and drivers' sheets;
  - takings per car against what one car can carry;
  - takings against costs.
- The tell is income out of proportion to the cars, drivers and bookings.

## 6. A plausible ceiling [DERIVED]

Assumptions: a £3 fare, 1.5 jobs an hour when busy, 10-hour shifts.

- One car takes about £270 a week in fares single-shifted over six days, and about £630 double-shifted over seven. The hard ceiling is about £34,000 a car a year.
- An owner-driver office with 8 cars and 12 drivers takes about £30,000 in settle, plus perhaps £30,000 of account invoices. Its own turnover is about £45,000–£65,000 a year.
- With firm-owned cars and every fare billed by the office, 8 cars take at most about £270,000.

## Canon notes

- Night work from pubs and clubs, and any betting-shop account, fall outside canon.
- Council contract work often meant school runs, which involve children, so it also falls outside canon.
- The inspector is a VAT officer. A cab office has no excise duty, cellar or stock. His powers come from the 1983 VAT Act, not the Customs and Excise Management Act.
