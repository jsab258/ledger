# Shop glass at night: why it reflects nothing (8 October 2026)

**Question.** Mickey's window is near black at night, though it shows the street by day. **D** read at source, **S** search summary, **I** inference.

**Measured** [9]: the night glass averages 5.7 of 255, against 65 by day. Brightened six times, the hook night view of the same window shows brick: the reflection is there, about 2.5 stops too dark to see (D).

## 1. Cause

1. **The cube is refreshed at night.** Each change of light re-catches every window (VignetteShot.cpp ~7847, from ApplyCondition and ApplyPlayCondition), and the shot waits for it (~9162) (D).
2. **It holds linear light.** A Scene Colour HDR capture has post-processing off (SceneCaptureRendering.cpp ~749), so its pre-exposure is 1 (PostProcessEyeAdaptation.cpp ~1528-1541), in a float cube. The glass is multiplied by the main view's exposure (BasePassPixelShader.usf ~2493) (D). The reflection is exposed with the street, so night darkness alone cannot explain it (I).
3. **The strength is the same day and night:** 2.5 × Fresnel 0.04, so 10% face on (D).
4. **That 10% falls on dark things** (I, from the positions in vignette-pieces.json):
   - **No lamp can appear in this pane from the office camera.** The west lamp (x 18) reflects at x 9.0, beyond the window's end at 8.61. The east lamp (x 8) reflects 39° up, above the frame. A real pane would show neither.
   - **What it does see:** the west terrace, lit only at its foot; the near road; the night sky (gain 0.15; 13 by day). No window opposite is lit.
   - **Our light sources are too dim.** Lit windows and rooms glow at 0.07-0.13 cd/m², while the paving under a lamp is about 1 cd/m² (465 cd, so 20 lux, albedo 0.15). On screen the lit windows (about 100) are darker than the pool (190) (D values, I).
   - **In reality** lit windows average 6 cd/m² and shop windows 50 [3] (D). A residential road gets 2-15 lux [4] (S), so 0.1-1 cd/m² (I). Real lit windows are 10 to 100 times the lit pavement; ours are a tenth.
   - **Lamp heads are the exception:** lit from 2 cm away, they clip to white (measured), and the float cube keeps them.
5. **The gilt lettering** sits about 44° off the near lamp's axis, outside its 42° pool, with a dim room behind (I).

## 2. The professional method

- A pane reflects about 4% a face, so at night only light sources and lit surfaces show. The craft is setting each emitter's brightness in proportion (Unreal's emissive is in cd/m² [5], D); a faithful reflection then follows (I).
- Spider-Man pairs screen-space and cube reflections with interior-mapped rooms [7] (S).
- **Cheats:** capture-only proxies ("Visible In Scene Capture Only", PrimitiveComponent.h ~715, D), or a night strength. Either makes the reflection disagree with the street seen directly, so they come second (I).
- **Routes set aside before** [8] would reflect the same dark street (I).

## 3. Steps, smallest first

1. **Split test, 0.5 hours. First, because it proves the cause before the look changes.** Run at night with -GlassCatchDump, and read eg_bay0's cube: medians toward the terrace, the road and the sky, and the maximum. Compare them with the reverse view's HDR screenshot. **Pass:** within 25%. If the cube is darker, the capture itself is at fault; research that instead.
2. **Light the side opposite, 2 hours, plus his page (it is the look).** Within canon, decide which windows and doorway opposite Mickey's are lit at 20:30. Raise lit windows and rooms to 3 to 10 times the lit paving (room_glow_gain_night 0.08 → about 1-3). Where the room pictures wash out, as they did at 0.25, give the pane its own glowing curtain instead. **Verify:** the reflected rectangles reach at least 40 of 255 in cam_mickeys at night, and a fresh reviewer names them.
3. **Judge lamps in the hook and reverse views, 0.25 hours**, where they can reflect.
4. **The gilt, 0.5 hours:** Mickey's room lit at night (a 24-hour office; a canon check), or the pool's cone opened to 46°. **Verify:** the letters stand out at least 2:1 from their surround.
5. **Fallback, 0.5 hours:** glass_cube_strength_night at 5 or 10, kept only if no reviewer sees a mirror.
