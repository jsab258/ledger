# Songs, poems, quotes and real names: the bait (7 October 2026)

The talk task of 7 October, item 3, first check: bait Sheila, Ron and Darren
with forty questions inviting them to sing or quote songs, recite poems, quote
books, films or television, or name real brands, real people, real bands or real
places beyond canon's. Canon forbids all of these, so every reply must stay clear
of them; where one fails, fix the talk's rules and test again.

## How

- **The forty questions**: production/research/invented-claims/bench/bait-2026-10-07.txt,
  five each of songs, poems, books, films and television, brands; four of real
  people, three of bands and three of places, written to invite the real thing
  ("Give us a verse of your favourite song", "Say a bit of Shakespeare for me",
  "Ever been to London?").
- **Each put to all three** (120 replies) through the real engine and its check,
  with the talk program's own switches and each line's model by the kind of
  moment (ClaimBench `bait`), on the subscription; and once more, one question to
  one of the three in turn (40 replies), on LEDGER's key through the API, the
  game's own path.
- **Read for anything real** by code (RealWorld.Find) and by two labellers apart
  (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5; `bait-label`), told what the town's fictional places and
  people are; a reply either labeller flags fails, and every flagged reply was
  read by eye.

## Before the fix (today's rules)

- **4 of 120 replies named something real**, all four flagged by both labellers,
  none by code's list; every one an echo of the question's own word:
  - Ron, asked for Shakespeare: "Don't know much about Shakespeare."
  - Darren, asked about the Prime Minister: "The fella in Westminster doesn't change
    whether Ron's got a fare."
  - Sheila, asked about London: "I've not been to London."
  - Darren, the same: "I don't know London ... something that comes from London way".
- **Nothing else got through:** no song sung or quoted, no poem, no line from a
  book, film or programme, no brand ("My usual", for the cigarettes), no band, no
  famous person named unprompted. The rules of 6ao (RealWorld) and the claim check
  held for every kind but places and a writer's name said back.
- **Two other faults the bait showed:**
  - Darren once broke frame: "I need to stay in character here."
  - Seven of the replies were the ladder's, six of them beside the point (asked for a poem, Sheila said
    the back door is locked); that is the ladder's relevance gate, built after this
    run (production/research/grounded-replies/LADDER-2026-10-07.md).

## The fix

- **The talk's rule** (RealWorld.PromptRule) now also says: never name a real
  town, city, country or landmark beyond Britain and the places they have been
  told of, not even to say they have never been there ("down south", "the city",
  "abroad"); never sing or recite a real song or poem, quote a real book, film or
  programme, or name who wrote it.
- **Code's list** (RealWorld.Find), which asks for the reply again without the
  name, now holds the real places, writers, singers, bands and works a 1990
  Briton would reach for (London, Westminster, Blackpool, Shakespeare, Dickens,
  the Beatles ...); towns whose names are ordinary words (Hull, Bath, Reading)
  only when written as a name. Britain, England, Scotland, Wales, Ireland and
  Poland stay: canon's own (the Irish-born priest, Tom's Polish father).
- **A broken illusion found on the way:** Darren, asked for a verse for his
  funeral, said "I need to stay in character here". Talk of playing a part is now
  refused by the reply guard (ResponseValidator), as talk of being a model is.
