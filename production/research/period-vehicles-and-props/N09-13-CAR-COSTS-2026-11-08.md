Helper read all pages on 8 Nov 2026. Prices are what Fab and Meshy showed this PC (Swiss francs, ex tax). Hours are the helper's own estimates unless a source is linked; marked "est.".

## Questions

1. Which Fab or free CC0/CC-BY car models fit a British street of 1990 (hatch, saloon, estate, van; 1978-90 look): price, licence, AI-usage tag, triangles, interior/damage/LODs, work to turn real badges into a fictional make?
2. Image-to-3D: which current tools (output and weights licence, cost per model) could make a car from period photographs; what quality and cleanup hours does public evidence show for vehicles?
3. Per car: money and hours for each route, bought (needs his yes) against made.

Reading: every Fab car page read carries "Allows usage with AI: No". Under the allowlist and his 13 Oct ruling, NoAI items are refused unless Epic's own; none of these is Epic's. So buying them is not open, whatever the price. The licence field showed no terms to a signed-out reader (unverified what a signed-in buyer sees). The pages did not show a period British estate or van. No CC0/CC-BY British 1978-90 car was found (the 1 Oct note found none; this pass did not repeat that search in full).

Badge stripping, had a model been usable: real badges are mostly texture or small geometry (est. 1-3 h per car to paint out grille, boot, wheel centres, plates, plus 2-4 h to reshape the silhouette enough that it is no longer one recognisable real model, as the allowlist forbids real car models). A bought pack of different shape would not meet the "fictional make" rule by badge removal alone.

## Recommendation in three lines

1. Fab cars are closed: every one read is tagged NoAI, so price is moot; no free canon-safe British car was found.
2. The made route (existing script, Epic's materials) costs no money and is the only fully allowlisted one; image-to-3D does not cut the cleanup much for cars and adds a real-shape problem.
3. If he wants a test of image-to-3D, one Meshy Pro month (CHF 18, his yes) on one small hatch, timed against the scripted one.
