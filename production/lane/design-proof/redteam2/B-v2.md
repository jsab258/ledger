# Red team 2, helper B

Source: GAME-DESCRIPTION-COPY-2.md only, read in full (914 lines). Ordered by importance. Deliberate choices (one small town, rare guns, no meters, minimap, stat numbers or game over) are not counted as gaps.

1. MISSING. Act III morning grading (section 38) has a heading and a pass line but no body. Act III is the deciding week, so nothing says how each morning's item is graded, what counts as "the books hold", how many mornings there are, or what each grade feeds into. The team cannot build the climax.
2. UNDEFINED. The ending function. Five "built states" sit under three shown endings, but nothing gives the conditions that select one: what "the business survives" means, what "the books hold" means, how a friendship "holds" or is measured, how Ellis's charges (read at the ending) change the result, what "the street holds his rackets as fact" means, and what exactly sends a player to Burn Both. Endings are the payoff of 15 to 20 hours and right now they would be guessed.
3. UNDEFINED. Rival ladder movement. Only the first rung (noticed, on the first sighting) and the stall rule (one rung a day after week eight) are given. Nothing says what moves a rival from visit to pressure to threat to sit-down: deeds, time, rung of the firm, or how far Tom pushes back. This ladder paces all of Act II and decides when it ends.
4. UNDEFINED. Sit-down outcomes. "Accept, defy, or counter" is offered at the end of Act II and in rival play, but no outcome is written for any rival and any answer: what Tom gives up, what he gains, what the rival does next, how Act III changes. This is the end of the longest act.
5. MISSING. The main story's written content. The four asks of Act I are staged only for nights one and three; nights five and seven "carry what the measured story says". Who the old firm and the landing men are, what is in the envelopes, the truth of the warehouse fire (said to be never the street's, but the team needs it), the eight Act II beat scenes and the collision scenes are all handed to documents that are not included. The story cannot be built from this page.
6. MISSING. The economy's numbers. There are no figures for a driver's weekly wage, the per-mile part of a fare ("80p plus distance"), Tom's cut from the landing, a crewman's cut, the dock gate tax, a car's price, coats, the barber, the doctor, a bribe, a front's share, the Fixer's prices, the cold-store contract's value, a front's wash amount, or what the shops stock and charge. Only Act I's rent and debts are given. The rent-versus-income curve, the wash rule and the "sinks never run dry" rule cannot be tuned or tested without them.
7. MISSING. What happens when rent goes unpaid, wages go unpaid or the book runs dry. "Doing nothing misses Friday's rent" is stated, but not who the landlord is, what follows (a warning, eviction, losing the office, losing the flat) or how a broke player recovers. Rent is the first pressure and the opening says it is "never a dead end", yet no floor is defined.
8. UNDEFINED. Killing named people the story needs. Ron (58), Danny Cammack, Darren, Kevin, Terry, the Fixer, the bought officers and others are not covered by Tom's line and can be killed for good. Nothing says what happens to the acts, the sit-down, the asks, the radio or the rival who loses its head. Open-world games need either protection or written fallbacks for every essential person.
9. UNDEFINED. Broken cross-references and documents outside this file. "Section 37" (Act II beats; really 31), "section 36" (envelopes), "section 24" (the map; really 20), "section 32" (the rung table; really 26), "section 14" (three keys; really 13), "section 40" and "section 34" do not match this file's numbers. RT-04, GD-02, GD-07, GD-08, A38, A41, S41, QUESTIONS 6, 8.2.1, "the brand bible", "the measured story", "the melee sheet", "the casting sheet" and "the teardown" are all cited but not included. A team building from this file alone hits dead links.
10. MISSING. Hold-ups as a verb. They are named three times (a detective's crime, Tom's line holding for hold-ups, "A41's target list"), but there are no rules for targets, how a hold-up starts, what a keeper does when he stands, what is taken, or how it is reported.
11. MISSING. How break-ins work. Break-ins are a core job family and a first-night option, but there is no lockpicking and no climbing, and the page never says how Tom gets in (force a door with a crowbar, a window, a key, a lifted key, Ian's locks), how long forcing takes, how loud each way is, what is inside to take, how a room is searched, or what an occupant does. Only "a forced door is the loudest thing" is given.
12. UNDEFINED. Contradictions in the firm's rung rules. The cold-store contract is "won at rung 3", but rung 3 needs the contract held, which is circular. Rung 2 needs "his answer to Sheila, a third car, a first recruit", while the transitions say any one of several states gives it. "Wind it down" still gives "the firm is his", but the page never says what winding down does to the cab business itself.
13. UNDEFINED. How gossip spreads and cools. The page says the town's memory is the engine, that talk "cools", that a deed can be "hot in talk", that a street can be "loud" (which brings Ellis), and that a telling can become "fact". No rules say who tells whom, how fast, how it degrades, when it cools, or what loud and fact mean. The page claims the prototype holds this code, but the design does not state it.
14. UNDEFINED. Nerve, loyalty, readiness to talk and regard. These are hidden values, which is fine, but nothing gives their range, what raises or lowers each, the thresholds (a crewman breaks, a debtor stands, a witness walks to the phone), or how "regard" picks a greeting (afraid, angry, likes him, his appearance). Every reaction in the game rests on these values.
15. MISSING. A map specification. Section 1 lists areas, but where the chapel, the caff, the launderette, the pawn, Rita's, the boxing club, the doctor's terrace, Ada's house, Hal's shop, the ironmonger, the outfitter, the barber, the solicitor, the car lot, the police station and the hospital sit, and which of them are playable or off the map, is never given. Nor is the list of interiors Tom can enter, the addresses and landmarks Pauline uses, or where the vault places are. Wayfinding by landmark depends on all of this.
16. UNDEFINED. The hospital and the police station. Tom "comes round in hospital" and is taken "to the station", but it is not said whether either is on the map, how many days each injury costs, where he is put back afterwards, or what the town and his jobs do during those days.
17. UNDEFINED. The clock in talk contradicts itself. "The clock stops in talk" sits beside talk while driving, a sit-down in the back of a moving cab, passengers talking on the way, and a witness's walk that should go on. Whether the world freezes in moving talk needs one rule.
18. UNDEFINED. The rule for a fall that kills "rarely" breaks the rule of no chance. The town "uses no chance" and pickpocket feel is set by fixed limits, yet a fall against a kerb kills "rarely". It is not said what deterministic condition kills (speed, surface, blow type, state of the victim). The same applies to "rarely" anywhere else.
19. UNDEFINED. Finishing a man who is down, and kicking. Finishing is "a separate decision, made in the quiet afterwards, never one press", but its input and flow are not given. Kicking a man on the ground is used to set how a deed is told, yet there is no kick input.
20. UNDEFINED. Guns, how they reach Tom. Guns come "bought, stolen, taken, or found once at Mickey's", but not from whom, at what price, where the one at Mickey's is or when it can be found, how much ammunition exists and how it is restocked, or whether there is any weapon besides the revolver. The aim and hit numbers are deferred to footage.
21. MISSING. Numbers that rest on footage or measures not yet made. Hidden fight capacity per fighter type, the gun timings, travel times ("provisional bands"), street density, jobs per hour, branch counts, the money curve's shape, and "the agreed share" for reading typed talk are all left "to come". These are the tuning core.
22. UNDEFINED. Fronts and rounds. "A share of a real business got by money, a favour or pressure" and "a set of payments collected on a day": the page does not say which businesses are fronts and at what cost, who pays a round, how much, how a round is set up, what trouble on it looks like, or how a rival takes one. Rungs 3 and 4 need at least one and two of each.
23. UNDEFINED. What the voices are. "A subtitle appears only with its audio", and every named person has live talk, but it is never said whether live replies are voiced (by synthetic voice, recorded barks, or text only), which crowd lines are recorded, or how a voiced line and a written line agree. Tom is not voiced, but everyone else's voice is undefined.
24. UNDEFINED. The live-talk service. The service, the "small model" for suggested lines, latency and cost per hour of play, whether the game needs to be online, the fallback for decision lines and haggles when offline, and how a live reply turns into a game act (an offer, a promise) are not given. The whole talk pillar rests on this.
25. MISSING. Inventory and item list. The page gives "two things under the coat, one in the hand or a holdall", but there is no full list of items (weapons, tools, keys, goods, the torch, cash), no holdall capacity, no rule for carrying many lifted goods, nothing on where goods from a break-in go, and no rule for whether the cash on him is limited.
26. UNDEFINED. What one counts as an "Open" item when lost. The help for a lost player measures progress "towards the place in the Open list", but Open holds many jobs and appointments. It is not said which one is the target or how the player chooses it without a marker.
27. UNDEFINED. Whether Tom can drive cars other than the firm's. The page says "no garage of cars", and cars are bought from people or the lot, but it never says whether Tom can enter or take a stranger's car, the police car, or a rival's van. Open-world players will try this in the first hour.
28. UNDEFINED. The calendar overflowing. The calendar runs "about nine game weeks", yet Act I (1) plus Act II (7, or 8 plus the stall days) plus Act III (1) can pass that. Nothing covers dates after the calendar ends, or what happens to the calendar in post-game play.
29. UNDEFINED. Post-credits play. Play returns "at the ending's state with no new main beats", but not what still runs: rivals, police stages, fares, side stories, the rent, sends, or whether a hunted man is still hunted.
30. UNDEFINED. The phone as a verb. Phone boxes, the office phone, answering machines and pagers exist, and a job can come by "a phone box call he makes back", but who Tom can ring, how a number is known, what a call costs, and whether he can ring the police or a rival are not given.
31. UNDEFINED. Distraction and the torch. "O throws a stone or rattles a bin" and "I the torch" are bound to keys, but there is no rule for how an NPC reacts to a noise (looks, walks over, how long), nor for whether the torch makes Tom easier to see.
32. UNDEFINED. Charges and bail. A charge "joins Ellis's case and is read at the ending", but it is not said what bail limits, what several charges do, what a charge does to the ending, or what happens after the 22 hours for a killing beyond "bail".
33. UNDEFINED. Revenge and grudges. A man beaten or robbed "remembers", but the page never says whether he retaliates, avoids Tom, goes to a rival or to the police later, or when.
34. UNDEFINED. Being robbed when beaten. Tom comes round "sometimes robbed": what is taken (cash, the coat's contents, a weapon), by whom, and whether it can be got back are not given, nor what "sometimes" means without chance.
35. UNDEFINED. Crew limits and order outcomes. There is no cap on crew size, no rule for how "fetch him", "lean on him" and "watch that" resolve, nothing on what a crewman does when Tom is arrested or in hospital, and no rule for picking which man answers a digit order.

37. UNDEFINED. The office break-in and the tin. The tin can be stolen by a rival or a thief, but it is not said when, whether Tom can guard it (a man on the door, a lock, a front), or whether it can be got back.
