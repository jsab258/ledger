# Fit check and the players' questions, 21 November 2026

Which parts of the design depend on each other's numbers, checked with arithmetic, then what three experienced players (GTA V and VI, RDR2, KCD2) would test first, answered from the design. Numbers by line: fit-check/numbers.md; players' lists: fit-check/panel-*.md.

## In plain words

34 pairs checked: 13 fit (one only if most nights are played), 5 do not fit, 14 cannot be judged yet, 1 is a plain gap and 1 a contradiction in the text (a second sits inside a fitting row). Ten gaps where the design has no number. The players raised 105 points, merged into 61 questions: 35 answered, 16 gaps (7 already listed by red team 2 or the coverage map), 10 fit problems.

**Is the map big enough for car chases?** Not as written.
- The Hook has no size in metres, and its two crossing times barely agree: a 3 to 5 minute walk at 1.4 m/s means about 350 to 590 m of road, a 60 to 100 s drive about 540 to 1,340 m. Only about 540 to 590 m fits both (F01).
- A car chase lasts up to 90 s. At 35 to 45 mph that is 1.4 to 1.8 km, two to three lengths of the Hook, so it must run round loops or it reaches the soft edge in 30 to 45 s. A chase at the edge is not covered (F02, G3).
- "Lost after 7 s or 70 m out of sight": if 70 m means distance driven, a car covers it in about 4 s, so a car chase ends about 4 s after the first corner; the text does not say which it means (F03).

Fix: a road plan in metres with a 1.5 km loop; define the 70 m; time real chases in four reference games (about 2 PC hours).

**The fast clock (45 s a game hour) causes most other misfits.** The staged first hour reaches the landing when the clock reads about 03:30 to 07:00, though it is open ten till one (F07). A fare uses 1.3 to 2.7 game hours, and the "last ferry in four minutes" works in neither game nor real minutes (F08). A townsperson crossing the Hook on foot spends 4 to 7 game hours walking (F09). A two-hour job window is 90 real seconds, shorter than walking to it (F10). Each has a design fix; the clock is his open C11, and slowing it lengthens the acts, so both go together (question 1).

**Also:** 30 lines per regular, one a day, over 63 days repeats from about week five for people seen daily (F18). The story bible's 14-hour main line plus 25 to 40 side stories makes 19 to 27 hours against his 15 to 20; the design does not say whether its act hours include side stories (F15). Contradictions: "about half" against "every" fare with a wrinkle; Act II ending at week 7 against a fail-safe at week 8.

**All three players fear:** a small map worn out by week two, repeated lines and happenings, a rushed clock, low stakes, and money that stops mattering after week one (not judgeable until spending has amounts, GD-07).

**New gaps:** the road plan, car speeds and the stop speed, a chase at the edge, Tom's and constables' speeds, the beat's round, counts of happenings and fare people, music, birds and animals, open rooms, night opening hours.

**Four scope questions for him** are at the end, each with a recommendation; the first is his open C11.
