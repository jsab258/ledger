## Gap rows added (GV-01 to GV-06)

### GV-01 The boot, seen
Definition. The cab's boot holds what is not on him: the envelope, a spare coat, a box. Opening it is an act with the seven slots; a constable who stops the cab with cause may look in it; a spare coat there is preparation (raised by Y1.3, Y1.6; idea 5).
Standard. Opening the boot is perceived like any act; a stop with cause searches it; what is in it survives save and load.
Test. B: boot opened in sight of a watcher x 20 (memory written); a stop with cause finds the boot's contents.
Parent parts: 1.6.9. Status: MEASURED (design decision, pass or fail).

### GV-02 Shedding what a witness holds, slot by slot
Definition. Each slot a witness can hold is shed its own way, and only against witnesses who have not yet told anyone: the coat (change it at the office), the car (the second car, the roof sign off, new plates or a respray), the face (a cap and scarf at night, a barber for a week), the companion (walking apart from Ron), the weapon (got rid of). Tom's guess of what each person holds of him shows in words on the Ledger's People page, never as icons (raised by Y1.4, Y2.2; idea 1).
Standard. A changed slot no longer matches for witnesses who had not told; gossip already carried is never undone; the Ledger shows Tom's guess per slot in words.
Test. B: deed, change one slot, meet the same witness: only that slot fails to match; a witness who told first still names it.
Parent parts: 1.4.4, 1.6.10, 6.2.1. Status: MEASURED (design decision, pass or fail).

### GV-03 The passenger who knows the way
Definition. Whoever rides with Tom (Ron, a crewman, a fare who knows the town) says the way by landmark in a chase and when asked on a fare: "left after the chapel, he cannot follow you through the yard". No marker; the words carry the where (raised by Y3.3; idea 4).
Standard. In a car chase with a passenger, at least one landmark line per 20 s of chase; every line names a real landmark on the way.
Test. B: car chases with each passenger; lines logged and checked against the map.
Parent parts: 1.2.7, 6.9. Status: MEASURED (design decision, pass or fail).

### GV-04 Places stay open
Definition. Every building used in a job stays enterable afterwards, as a place in someone's life, unless the story closes it and says so (raised by Y4.2).
Standard. Every interior used once can be entered again after its job, or its closing is said in the world.
Test. B: after each job, a walk back into every interior it used.
Parent parts: 4.1.3. Status: MEASURED (design decision, pass or fail).

### GV-05 Bugs at release
Definition. The release build has no blocking fault: never stuck, no lost save, no crash in a whole evening (raised by Y8.2: critics found almost no bugs in GTA VI; players punish bugs and ports most of all in the walkthrough games: GTA IV port 25.9%, bugs 12.7%; Forza Horizon 4 port 22.0%).
Standard. Three nights running, the AI tester's whole-evening and whole-week walks in the packaged build find zero blocking faults and zero crashes.
Test. The nightly tester walk, three nights in a row before the gate is called.
Parent parts: 7.8, 8.2.1. Status: MEASURED (design decision, pass or fail).

### GV-06 How a deed is told: restraint and cruelty
Definition. The telling carries how a deed was done, not only that it was: a man put down with three blows who is left alone is told differently from one who was kicked on the ground. The worst telling, once carried, is never undone (raised by Y2.4; idea 9; derived from people's beliefs, never a stored meter).
Standard. Two seeded fights differing only in restraint produce different told versions and different greetings within a game day.
Test. B: paired fights; the told lines and greetings compared.
Parent parts: 2.4.1, 2.12.4. Status: MEASURED (design decision, pass or fail).

## Bars raised

- 1.2.4 Crashes, damage and unsticking: Raised by Y1.1: front damage over the crash threshold pulls the car to one side until repaired, and a broken lamp darkens the road ahead at night; a dent is a mark witnesses remember.
- 1.2.10 Taking a parked car: Raised by Y1.2: the two ways in differ by the car: a brick through the quarter-light (about 3 s, loud) or a tool at the door and the ignition (about 10 s, quieter); a car with a steering lock cannot be taken; times to be checked in the footage pass.
- 1.2.9 Repairs, plates and paint: Raised by GTA VI's respray rule: new plates or a respray clear the police's noted plate for that car (see GV-02).
- 6.1.1 The prompt: Raised by Y1.8 (GTA VI fails it): with two usable things in reach the wrong action never fires; a tester walk presses the key 500 times in crowded spots and counts misfires (pass: zero).
- 6.2.1 People: Raised by Y2.2: Tom's guess of what each person holds of him, slot by slot, in words (GV-02).
- 2.6.10 Police after a deed: Raised by Y2.3: the place stays warm for days, not minutes: next morning a constable knocks along that street and asks at windows, and for two nights the spot is walked more often; no circle on screen.
- 2.5.3 Crowd reaction to a deed: Raised by Y2.6: a person with the nerve for it (their temper) may step in, unarmed, at a hold-up or a beating in the street; Tom's line holds if that person is one it covers.
- 2.7.6 Fencing: Raised by Y2.7: buyers differ in what they take (the second-hand shop radios and video recorders, the scrap yard car parts, a man at the back of the caff jewellery) and pay less for each further piece the same week; each is also a listener. No price table on screen.
- 3.6.3 Written street lines: Raised by Y2.5: no shared street line heard twice in one real hour of play anywhere (GD-02).
- 7.8 Saving: Raised by Y8.2: see GV-05 (no blocking fault three nights running).
