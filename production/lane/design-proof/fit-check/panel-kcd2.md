# Fit check: a Kingdom Come: Deliverance II player reads LEDGER

Read from the one-shot prompt only, as a player who has put a few hundred hours into KCD2. These are the things I would try in my first evening and first week, and what I am afraid will not hold.

## Size, time and the living town

1. One street for 15 to 20 hours. "Not a hundred square miles: one port, dense, known by heart" sounds brave, but KCD2 earns density with two villages, a city, monasteries and forests between them. By week three I expect to be walking the same Quay Street for the hundredth time and I want to know what is still new about it.

2. "A game hour is 45 real seconds, so a game day is 18 real minutes." That is several times faster than KCD2's clock. I will stand and watch Ada's routine, the caff and the dock shift, and I am afraid people will look like they are sprinting between places and that a routine I am meant to learn by watching will be over before I notice it.

3. I will follow one regular for a whole game day to see whether the routine is a real life (meals, work, a chat, home, lights off) or four positions they teleport between. KCD2 people go to work, eat and sleep in a bed; I expect the same here and the description only promises "where they are, what they do, which door they use".

4. "About thirty of their own street lines" per regular and "at least six lines per situation" for the crowd, across "about nine game weeks". I will hear every line in the first week. KCD2 repeats barks too, but it has thousands of them; I am afraid the street goes stale and starts sounding like a loop by the second Saturday.

5. I will try every door on Quay Street. "Rooms are enterable where jobs and stories go" reads like most doors are painted on. In KCD2 nearly every house can be entered, robbed and slept in; a street of locked fronts will feel like a film set the moment I notice.

6. No children, no pubs used, no betting, a closed school. I accept the content rule, but a 1990 British port town with nobody under adulthood and no place where men gather in the evening risks feeling hollow at night. I want to see where the town actually spends its evenings and whether the caff and the boxing club are enough to make it feel lived in.

7. "Uses no chance: the same week plays the same way twice." With autosave after every deed and manual slots, I will reload a bad night and replay it until the witness never looks. KCD2 limits that with Saviour Schnapps. Here I fear every risk becomes a puzzle I solve by reloading, and the tension the whole design is built on disappears.

## Stealth, crime and the law

8. No crouch, no sneak skill, no noise meter. "Standing still in shadow is the hiding." I will try to rob Iveson's hut and expect sneaking to feel like a real activity, not just standing in a doorway and walking when nobody is facing me. I am afraid stealth is one rule I learn in ten minutes.

9. Pickpocketing is "one held press of about 2 s" and whether he feels it "follows fixed limits, never a roll". Once I know the rule (distracted, crowd of three, poor light) every lift succeeds. KCD2's pickpocket circle is fiddly but it is a skill; this sounds like a checkbox.

10. No lockpicking game, no safe dial. Locks open by key, back way or force. Fine for pace, but I will want something to do with my hands at a door; holding E for 6 seconds is not a verb that "feels good the moment it is touched".

11. The search times ("a drawer 2 s, a desk 4 s, a wardrobe 5 s") and what is inside: "authored per job". I will open every drawer in every room and I expect most to have something, even rubbish. If only job containers work I will stop searching by the second night.

12. Chases "always end, caught or lost, within 90 real seconds" and are lost once Tom is "unseen for 7 s or 70 m". I will run round one corner and stand still behind a van. If that always works the police are a nuisance, not a threat, and every chase plays the same.

13. "One patrol car works the Hook" and one beat constable. I will try leading the constable away with a rattled bin and then doing everything behind his back. With one man on the beat I fear the law is a single walker I can learn and avoid forever.

14. Arrest costs "about six hours" for a window, "twenty-two for a killing and then bail", no court, no prison, no fines. In KCD2 being caught hurts (stocks, fines, branding, a jail stretch). Here I am afraid the only cost is losing part of an 18-minute day, which means I will stop caring about witnesses by the second week.

15. Killing. "A fall against a kerb, a wall or a step within 3 s of a blow can kill, rarely." I will try to kill someone on purpose with a knife or the cosh, and I will try to hurt Sheila, Ron or a rival head. The description never says what happens if I go after a named character the story needs, or whether I simply cannot.

16. Tom's own line: he "never lays hands on a woman, on anyone of sixty or over, or on the priest", and "the input does nothing". That covers a big share of the town, including the Widow's side. In a sandbox crime game, pressing a key and getting nothing will feel like an invisible wall every time I hit it.

17. The quay edge "stops him like a kerb that cannot be stepped off", vaulting only at "set places", "no jump key and no climbing walls". KCD2 has its limits too, but this many invisible kerbs on a small map will be noticed in the first chase.

## Fighting

18. Fists first, guns late and rare, no swords. Jab, heavy, guard, slip, counter, grab is a decent core, but KCD2's directional combat, combos and master strikes give me hundreds of hours. "A few kinds" of fighter type (boxer, brawler, big man, knife man) sounds like I will have seen every fight by week two.

19. "No stats, levels, skills, perks." Tom "never gets better at fighting or driving; the player does." KCD2 lives on Henry getting better. I will play a week and ask: what have I got for it? Money, clothes and a few more cars may not feel like progress if Tom fights exactly as he did on Monday.

20. "Two against one is deadly; three is a beating." Good, but I will test whether a patient player can still win two against one with slips and counters, the way you can in KCD2 once you are good. If the answer is always no, fights become something I avoid rather than enjoy.

21. Things to hand break after "about 2 blows" for a chair, "3" for a brick, "4" for a bin lid. I will pick up everything on the street. If only those three things and the cosh are usable, the "street as a weapon" is a short list rather than a street.

22. The boxing club is the only pastime. KCD2 has dice, hunting, archery, alchemy, smithing, horse racing. Without gambling or drink that is understandable, but I need a second and third thing to do in free time or the evenings between jobs are just walking and waiting.

## Driving and money

23. A map this small and fares that last "1 to 2 real minutes" and pay "80p plus distance". I will drive the whole map in the first evening. I am afraid I will know every route by Wednesday and that driving on one port's streets cannot carry "about a fifth" of 15 to 20 hours.

24. The fare rule contradicts itself: the play story S07 says "about half the fares carry one wrinkle", the game section says "every fare after the first carries one wrinkle". With seven wrinkles and many fares a week, I expect to see each wrinkle dozens of times. How many authored fares are there before they repeat?

25. Money has little to spend on: coats, a torch, a crowbar, a car, a barber, bribes. "No price tables, stock or supply chains." KCD2's economy is not deep either, but there is always armour, a horse, a better sword. I am afraid that after the rent is safe, money stops mattering, and the rise is just a bigger number in words.

26. The wash rule ("£10 plus 80% of the fares really driven") is clever, but I suspect I will understand it on the first count and never think about it again unless something forces me to.

## Talk and story

27. Live AI talk with a typed line, "a reply is heard within 2 s or the written line plays" and lines slower than "8 s" dropped with a brush-off. I will type silly, off-topic and period-wrong questions to every named person. I expect at least one to break character, repeat itself, or sound like a chatbot, and in an immersion game that breaks the spell harder than any missing feature.

28. Tom is not voiced and his lines are text. KCD2's Henry is fully voiced, and the cutscenes carry the story. Here scenes are capped at "60 s" and "under 5% of the hour is watched". I am afraid the big moments (the sit-down, Ada's evening interrupted, the inspection) will play as text boxes and subtitles with no cinematic weight.

29. "Side stories: 25 to 40", "each 3 to 5 steps", "mostly light". KCD2 side quests are long, branching and memorable. Forty short ones across 15 to 20 hours is roughly two an hour; I expect them to feel like errands unless a few are proper stories with a twist.

30. The finale is a VAT inspection: one item each morning for a week. As a reason to have built an empire, that sounds anticlimactic. I want a climax I can act in with my hands, not a week of producing paperwork while the books are graded.

31. Act II is "about seven game weeks, 11 to 14 hours" driven by states, with "no dead weeks" promised by one rival rung, one firm change and one return a week. That is a thin minimum for two hours of play a week. I fear the middle of the game sags into fares and collections with a rival visit now and then.

32. The rivals. Three heads are named but I never read where they live, whether I can walk into their places, tail them or meet them before the sit-down. If they only exist as pressures arriving by letter and men at the gate, they will not feel like people.

33. Crew orders on the digit keys (seven orders) and sending (eight verbs) "an order is given and a line is written". If I send a man to beat someone and only read a Ledger line, the power fantasy of running the port is happening off screen, which is the opposite of "verbs first".

## The opening

34. The first hour is a timetable: control at 0:20, the first fare at 2:30, the first deed at 5:30, the carry at 9:00, the fight at 20:30. KCD2 earns its world slowly. I will ignore Sheila and wander off at minute two, and I want to know whether the town still holds when I break the order, or whether it is a ride that only works if I follow it.

35. With "nothing on screen says hidden", no meters and no markers, I welcome the KCD2 feel, but I will test whether the light cue in the corners and the sound mix are actually readable on a normal monitor with the music and rain going. If I cannot tell when I am seen, every witness will feel unfair rather than my fault.
