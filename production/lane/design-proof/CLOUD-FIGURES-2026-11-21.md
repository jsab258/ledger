## B. 1990 figures

### B1 Rent and running costs (row 2.7.4)
| Figure | Value | Source and date |
|---|---|---|
| Council rent, England, April 1990 (provisional) | £23.36 a week (April 1989 £20.70) | Hansard written answer, 27 June 1990, api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1990/jun/27/council-rents |

| London cab fare from 8 June 1990 | £1.00 minimum for the first 0.58 mile, then 20p each 340.5 yd (about £1.04 a mile) to 6 miles | Hansard written answer, 18 May 1990 (per-mile figure our arithmetic) |
| Diesel, early 1990s | about 45p a litre (drivers' recollection, not data) | TaxiPoint, 26 Sep 2025 |

Reading: the description's £18 a week rent for the office sits below the 1990 council average (£23.36) and well below private rents (£65 in 1991). It is defensible for a run-down port office let by an old acquaintance, but it is cheap; the 80p fare base sits below the London cab minimum of £1.00, as a minicab's should. Not changed here: the £18 is the description's and canon's; noted for the economy curve (GD-07). Status: MEASURED (1990 figures in hand; the standard "a week of fares alone pays the rent" is a design rule, now checked against a real band of £18 to £65).

### B2 Custody (row 2.6.4)
| Figure | Value | Source and date |
|---|---|---|
| Legal limit without charge | 24 hours, 36 for serious offences (a superintendent); 8 hours' rest overnight | Hansard written answer, 8 July 1987 |
| Held over 24 hours, 1990, England and Wales | 542 people (465 released without charge); 401 held over 36 hours under warrant | Statewatch, citing Home Office Statistical Bulletin, 15 July 1991 |
| Typical length, 1984 to 1987 | about three quarters (73%) held under six hours | Bottomley and others (Br J Criminology 1991), as relayed by Kemp, Balmer and Pleasence (Nottingham repository; second hand) |
| Mid-1990s suspects | mean 6 h 40 min | Phillips and Brown 1998, as relayed by the same paper |

Reading: the description's hours (about 6 for a window, 8 a wounding, 12 a robbery, 22 a killing) sit inside the legal limits and the record: a minor deed about the typical stay, serious ones longer, under 24 except the killing, and holding past 24 hours was rare (hundreds a year nationally). One correction: after a killing, "then bail" should read "then released under investigation or charged and remanded", since a man held 22 hours for a killing in 1990 is either charged or let go pending inquiries; the game's existing rule (never twice for one deed) stands. Status: MEASURED (limits and typical length found); the wording point goes into the definition below.

### C2 Day length (row 7.13)
| Game | Real minutes a game day | Source and date |
|---|---|---|
| GTA V | 48 (2 s a game minute) | PowerPyx (Oct 2018); Player.One (7 Jul 2026) |
| RDR2 | 48 on average (slower by day, faster at night) | PowerPyx (Oct 2018) |
| KCD1 | about 96 (15 to 1) | the official forum, players' timing (2018, 2019); KCD2's own figure not found |
| GTA VI | about 144 reported from footage, not confirmed by Rockstar | All Things How (Aug 2026) |
| LEDGER now | 18 at 45 s a game hour (the description, the prototype); 12 at 30 s (the Unreal clock) | DESIGN §15 |

Reading: both LEDGER clocks run far faster than every reference (48 to 144 minutes). That is a choice, not a fault: the evening's shape (dusk, the job, home, the count) has to fit a sitting. But the 30 s clock gives a 12-minute day, a quarter of GTA V's, and should not be the one built. Recommended to the advisor for his open feel decision C11: keep 45 s (18 minutes a day) for the prototype, and have the PC's footage check (A5) time how long a reference evening (dusk to midnight) runs in real minutes, so the evening, not the whole day, is matched. Status: PENDING (figures in hand; the choice is his open decision C11 and the A5 footage check, 23 to 28 Nov).

### C3 PC display options (row 8.3.1)
RDR2 on PC (Shacknews, 19 Nov 2019; PC Gamer, 30 Dec 2019): output monitor, resolution (16:9, ultrawide, double-wide and 4:3 offered), refresh rate, screen type (windowed, borderless, exclusive full screen), V-sync, triple buffering, one quality preset slider (21 steps), resolution scale, anti-aliasing (TAA, FXAA, MSAA) and later DLSS (July 2021), motion blur, about 40 options in all, a built-in benchmark. Not shown by any page read: HDR, FSR, a frame-rate cap, a field-of-view slider. Standard set: the prototype and the release offer resolution with ultrawide 21:9 at 3440x1440 (his screen), windowed, borderless and full screen, V-sync, a frame-rate cap (30, 60, 120, none), a quality preset with per-option overrides, resolution scale, an upscaler where the engine has one, motion blur off by default, and a benchmark or a settings preview; HDR and field of view are judged with the Unreal build (field of view is his open point AX-03). Status: MEASURED.
