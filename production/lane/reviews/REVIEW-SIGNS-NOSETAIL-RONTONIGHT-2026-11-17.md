## 1. tools/lane/street_signs.py and test_street_signs.py: PASS WITH FIXES

Checked and right:
- Tests: 38 passed (pytest), 0.07 s.
- Sourced sizes are in the research: flag 300 x 450 mm and 1 in numerals (Commercial Motor, 30 Apr 1971, KERBSIDE section 2); flag look from The Beauty of Transport. Every other size (pole, case, shelter, plate size, letters, mount height, interval, posts) is marked est, as the research gives none. The plate colour is marked est and the docstring repeats the research's warning to check SI 1981/859 first.
- Hansard re-read today: "for at least eight hours during the period between 7 am and 7 pm on at least four days per week"; timings "displayed on plates placed at regular intervals". The four-day and eight-hour parts are coded right. "at any time" is banned, matching the research (no plate-less double yellows in 1990).
- Placement: greedy, plates on columns where they reach, a post at the limit otherwise; ends within half an interval of each end. Probed edge cases (empty kerb, kerb shorter than the reach, a column at 0 and at the end, columns close together) give sound results.

Fixes:
1. validate_restriction is stricter than the source. The rule is at least eight restricted hours inside 7 am to 7 pm, not that the hours must lie inside it. "Mon to Fri, 7 am to 8 pm" meets the Hansard rule but is refused ("hours must lie within 7 am to 7 pm"). Count the overlap with 07 to 19 and require at least 8; drop the start < 7 / end > 19 test. Add a test for 7 am to 8 pm (passes) and 9 am to 5 pm (fails, 8 h exactly passes; 10 am to 5 pm fails).
2. The docstring's "Plates normally on lamp posts (Commercial Motor, 8 Jan 1965)" is a quote about loading-ban kerb marks, not waiting lines. Say so, and mark putting waiting plates on columns as inferred.
3. Flag orientation. The comment says the flag's face is "across the footway so people walking along can read it", but the flag is offset along x (along the kerb) with its width on x, so its face points across the road, at the carriageway and the buildings. Give the flag an explicit facing (normal along x, standing out from the pole toward the carriageway in y), and mark the facing and "tall, not wide" as est in the output. The size stays sourced; which side is the width is not (the module says so in prose, but the output marks both as plain "sourced").
4. _hour_text(24) gives "12 pm" and _hour_text(0) gives "0 am". Single-line plates can reach these (validate_restriction lets single lines run 0 to 24). Use "midnight" for both, and add a test.
5. check_text only matches a word preceded by a space and followed by space, full stop, comma or slash, so "(pub)", "pub-side" and "beer's" pass. Split on non-letters before matching. Add the children words the content rule needs: "children", "playground", "nursery", "school keep clear" (only "school" is there).
6. Minor: the greedy placement can put two plates on columns 2 m apart (kerb 100 m, columns at 29 m and 31 m: plates at 29, 31 and a post at 91). The post count is still right; say in the docstring that extra plates on columns are allowed, or prefer the column that leaves the next gap shortest.
7. Before the operator name "Meridian District Buses" goes on a visible flag, run the usual no-real-name check on it (not checked here).

## 2. production/lane/P9-NOSE-TAIL-BRIEFS-2026-11-17.md: PASS WITH FIXES

Checked and right:
- The lighting figures, re-read today in SI 1989/1796 Schedule 7 as made: for cars first used from 1 April 1986, front, rear and side repeater indicators; at most 400 mm from the side; at least 500 mm apart (400 mm under 1,400 mm width); 350 to 1,500 mm high; repeater within 2,600 mm of the front; the gap to the dipped headlamp only from 1 April 1991. All match the brief's table.
- Base numbers match the loft: ROUND3_PROPOSAL gives nose_top_z 0.72, tail_top_z 0.84; sill_z 0.30, width 1.66, nose_taper 0.86, tail_taper 0.92; car_kit's lamp_z = nose_top_z - 0.10 = 0.62, tail lamp tail_top_z - 0.12 = 0.72, bumper rises 0.22 and 0.25, plate_rear at tail_top_z - 0.30. Every design number is marked est; plates and colours trace to the class file. Every module sits well inside the legal limits.
- The P9 brief's order is kept: nose and tail run the same blind brief, one complete sample (N1 + T1) before any other, every shipped pair run.

Fixes:
1. T1 does not fit the kit. car_kit's tailgate runs from the rear bumper top (0.55 m) up to the roof; there is no rear panel below the hatch opening. T1 says the clusters sit "in the rear panel below the hatch opening" and the plate "on the tailgate between the clusters", at the same height: both cannot be true. Add a NoseTail parameter for the tailgate's lower shut line (tailgate_cut_z, est) and say which panel each part is cut into. If the clusters end up on the tailgate, the Golf II escape ("clusters wide and low in the body, not the tailgate") no longer holds and needs rewording.
2. T2: "the upright stops at about 0.94 m, about a third of the way from belt to roof" is wrong for this body. Belt is 0.625 x 1.40 = 0.875 m and the roof edge about 1.38 m, so a third is about 1.04 m; 0.94 m is about an eighth. Fix the words or the number. The upright rises above tail_top_z (0.84), so state that it must stay on the rear quarter, outboard of the rear screen's edge.
3. The parameter table misses what the modules use: lamp_h as two values for N3 (inner 0.11, outer 0.14), the T2 upright's height and width, the T3 panel's height and rib count, the plate lamps, and the tailgate shut line (fix 1). Add them, or the Blender side will invent them.
4. Tie heights to the body, not to absolute metres. The values are worked from ROUND3_PROPOSAL, which the file's own source calls "not tried in a blind round"; if P9 moves nose_top_z or tail_top_z, absolute lamp_z, tail_lamp_z and plate_rear_z go wrong. Give them as offsets (as car_kit does: nose_top_z - 0.10).
5. N1 arithmetic: with lamp_inner_y 0.17, lamp_w 0.38 and indicator 0.09, the indicator centre is 0.595 m from the centre, not 0.62. Still legal. The regulation's 400 mm is measured to the outer edge of the lit area; N1 uses the centre, N3 the outer end. Use the edge throughout.
6. N2: "a grille the same height as the lamps" but grille_h 0.15 and lamp_h 0.17. Pick one.
7. Schedule 11 (rear fog) and Schedule 4 (headlamp height) are marked UNVERIFIED; read them before fog_rear_y and lamp_z are modelled, not after. fog_rear_y negative = offside agrees with car_kit (right side is y -1).
8. Risk of resembling a real car: the "must not resemble" lines are from memory and no picture was looked at (the file says so). The highest risk is N1, the first sample. Wide rectangular lamps, a body-colour bar with one slot and wrap-round indicators is close to what this reviewer remembers of the Opel Kadett E / Vauxhall Astra Mk2 nose, and the brief describes that nose as having a "slatted grille", which may be wrong. Also unconfirmed is N3's claim that the Escort had a grille-less nose. Before modelling N1 and N3, put a sheet of period photographs of each named car beside the brief (front and rear, from a licensed or fair-reference source per the allowlist) and redo the "stays clear" lines against it. P9 stays the final check.

## 3. production/lane/RON-TONIGHT-LINE-PLAN-2026-11-17.md: PASS WITH FIXES

Checked and right:
- Ron's routine in the plan matches hook-cast.json exactly: daily [0 off, 7 cafe, 8 mickeys_rank, 12 fish_front, 13 mickeys_rank, 17 quay, 19 mickeys_office, 23 off]; "sun" [0 off, 10 allotments, 13 off, 19 mickeys_office, 23 off]; no "sat" key. Day 0 is Monday (CastDay.Weekday, GameTime).
- Golden rows R1 to R6 and R8 to R11 give the right token for his real routine under the plan's choice rule (worked through by hand). Building TonightStays from PlaceOf only, with the next day's real routine after midnight, is the right one-home design.
- The "tonight" window 17:00 to 05:59 matches Claims.TimeWordFits "tonight" for evening hours.
- Content: no drink, pub, betting or children; the ferry landing kept out; lines go through the content gate.

Fixes:
1. The claim check is misread, and section 5's change is not needed. Claims.WhereHeSays and the helper's check (Program.cs, about lines 825 to 850) judge Tom's own answer when a person has asked him "where were you" about a deed, against where that person saw Tom. They never check what Ron says about Ron. WhereWords only adds the "Where you are:" text to the scene (line 697). So "Ron could say something the check then calls a lie" cannot happen. Drop the claim-check change in section 5, the Claims tests in section 8 and the 1.5 h. Keep the CoreTests sweep (chosen place equals PlaceOf in tonight) as the real guard. Total about 4.5 h, not 6. (The source note RON-ROUTINE-TALK makes the same misreading in its table: "the claim check reads that as the scene".)
2. R7 contradicts the window the plan says it copies. In TimeWordFits, "tonight" means NightDay == Today, where Today is the calendar day now (DeedWhen(deedDay, deedHour, now.Day)). Asked at 01:00 on Friday, the check's "tonight" is Friday evening, not what is left of Thursday night. Once fix 1 removes the matching reason, choose a rule and write it down. Recommended: follow TimeWordFits, so R7 becomes mickeys_office ("Office from seven tonight, boss"), with the cafe at seven allowed as the morning extra. Change section 6's first edge case to match.
3. "this evening" in TimeWordFits ends at midnight (Day == Today, hour 17 or later), not 06:00; section 2 says it is the same window. State the difference, or say the line deliberately reads it as tonight.
4. "No new word list is needed" is not true. "later", "later on", "you about" and "you around" are not in TimeWordRx. Give the trigger reader its own small list, and do not add "later" to TimeWordRx: that would change NamesAnotherTime and LooseTime and move the claim-check goldens.
5. R12's test routine cannot give the expected answer. "0 off, 7 cafe, 17 mickeys_office" puts him off from 00:00, so he is not in the office all night. Use [[0, "mickeys_office"], [6, "off"], [7, "cafe"], [17, "mickeys_office"]].
6. Small: R2's routine column says "now quay till 19". Under the plan's own rule (stays from now) the quay is one hour and the office four, which agrees with the token. Write "18 to 19" so the test author does not count two hours.
