# Review: target for the empty unit to let, east parade bay 3 (17 Nov 2026)

Reviewed: production/research/phase2-plan/TARGET-EMPTY-UNIT-2026-11-17.md, against canon.md, RULINGS.md (3 Oct trades and posters, 8 Oct kit pieces, 3 Nov review sizes, 8 Nov phase 2) and outside evidence. Fresh reviewer, no part in writing it.

**Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES.** Nothing breaks canon or the content rule, and no real firm or logo is used. The weak points are the letting board (a size with no source and blank fields that will become placeholders) and that the look of whitewash in 1990 still rests on no dated photograph.

## Findings

1. E4, board size 900 x 450 mm. The only source is fascia-signs TARGET 6.3, where it is itself a judgement with no photograph behind it. Fix: label it "Judgement" in the table, and measure it against the 1989 Hull photograph (R05) once someone can see the picture.

2. E4, board wording "TO LET" only, with agent and number "minted later". In 1990 a commercial letting board normally carried the agent's name and a phone number as large as, or larger than, TO LET. A board with no name looks like a private sign or a placeholder, and blank fields would fail the gate's "no placeholder" rule. Fix: mint one fictional agent now (a made-up surname firm, no real brand or logo), add a line such as "SHOP PREMISES", and set the number line before the build starts. Source: general period knowledge, not verified today (see Unverified).

3. E4, number format. Not covered by the target. In 1990, codes outside London began with 0 and had no 1 after it. The 1 was added on PhONEday, 16 April 1995 (Wikipedia, "PhONEday", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhONEday, read 17 Nov 2026). London's old 01 code went out of use in May 1990 (same page). The project already uses "TEL. 0632 960nnn" on the skip (street-dressing/skip TARGET) and 0632 960418 on Mickey's glass. Newcastle gave up 0632 for 091 from 1982, over about five years (Wikipedia, "091", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/091, read 17 Nov 2026), so in 1990 the code belonged to nobody. Fix: use the same town code, written "TEL. 0632 960nnn". Never use 01, 071, 081, 01xxx, 0191 or 555. A number in the 1990 format cannot be dialled today, which is a further safeguard.

4. E4, the law (SI 1989/670, Class 3A: one board, at most 2 m², under 4.6 m, taken down within 14 days). Unreached today: legislation.gov.uk did not respond to the fetch. Not contradicted, but not confirmed by this review either. Fix: none until it has been re-read. The figures do fit the board (0.405 m², fascia top at 3.40 m).

5. E3, whitewash in 1990. I found no dated photograph of a whitewashed empty shop from 1988 to 1992 either. The only dated period text I found points to grilles: Kirkcaldy in 1992, "some 20 unoccupied premises are protected by drab security grills", and "several major sites still display 'to let' signs" (Fife Today, https://fifetoday.co.uk/retro/kirkcaldy-high-street-plunges-downmarket-in-1992-946934, read 17 Nov 2026). The ruling stands. Fix: add this to the open question, and add "whitewash plus grille" as a choice for his page only if a dated photograph turns up. Do not change the build.

6. E3, how the whitewash looks (cloth swirls, thinner beside the beads, the street seen in the glass). Physically sound: the wash goes on from inside, so the outer glass stays plain glass and reflects. The bead fall-off and the band widths are judgement, not measured. Wikipedia "Whitewash" was unreached today (the fetch timed out), so the "whiting, chalk or lime" claim is unverified. Fix: mark the bead and band numbers "Judgement", and add a fail condition: no writing or finger marks on the outside of the glass, because the wash is on the inside.

7. E2, painted-out fascia "no letters". Alan Dein's dated 1988 East End photographs include a sign "partly erased" where "Lip" still shows, because the painter "ran out of paint" (Spitalfields Life, https://spitalfieldslife.com/2010/06/28/alan-dein-east-end-shopfronts-1988/, read 17 Nov 2026; text read, image unseen). Fix (optional, cheap): allow a faint ghost of 2 to 4 letters of a fictional old trade through the buff, very low contrast, drawn from the fascia target's ghost-word list. Otherwise keep "no letters" and record that it was a choice.

8. Unreached list, Historic England MBC01/04/092. It was reachable today. It shows a former Burton store in St Albans with its "shop windows and lower facade covered by weatherboarding", dated "1970s - 1980s" (https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/photos/item/MBC01/04/092, read 17 Nov 2026). It names a real firm, so it is reference only. It shows boarding rather than whitewash, and its date is not inside the window. Fix: move it from "Unreached" to the period checks, with that note.

9. E4, Marshall R05. The title, "Shops, Princes Avenue, Hull, 1989", confirms the date (https://www.flickr.com/photos/petermarshall/51039473258, read 17 Nov 2026). The page text does not mention a letting board, and the image is unseen. The board rests only on the project's earlier description. Fix: keep the target's "image unseen" note, and do not cite R05 for the board's layout until someone has looked at it.

10. E6, fly-posting. Double crown (508 x 762 mm) and quad (762 x 1016 mm) are the standard British sizes. I did not re-read them today. The poll-tax bill fits 1990 well. The Flashbak Swanfield Street caption and the "corrugated iron" quote were unreached today (fetch timed out). Fix: put the cinema quad up for a fictional film at the Tivoli (canon's minted cinema), with nothing about drink or gambling in the title or art.

11. E9, the blind. Agreed. Rolled-out canvas on an empty unit contradicts "to let". Fix: remove awning_02 at x 24 rather than keep a box. This takes one item off his page.

12. Real organisations. None is used. QUAY FIRM is canon's tag for the Hook. Pass.
