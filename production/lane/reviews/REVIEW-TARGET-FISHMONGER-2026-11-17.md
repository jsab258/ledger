# Review: target for the fishmonger, east parade bay 1 (17 Nov 2026)

Fresh reviewer. I did not write the target or build the shop. I read the target, canon.md, RULINGS.md (look and street, trades, reviews), the FISHMONGER note (3 Oct), both fish market reviews (15 Oct), the shopfronts and fascia-signs targets, vignette-scene.json and the licence allowlist. Tool reads text only; I saw no image.

VERDICT: PASS WITH FIXES. Nothing breaks canon or the content rule: no drink, betting or children, no real names on the board. Most sizes trace to the kit targets and the two reviews. But the target leaves out three fixes the reviews ordered, gets the night window wrong, and leans on two sources it has not seen.

## Findings

1. Night and afternoon window (F5, F9). The target shuts the shop at 14:00 but never says what the window holds once it is shut. Fish and ice left on a slab overnight is wrong for any period shop. Fix: from closing time the slab is bare and washed, no fish, no ice, tickets down, CLOSED card showing. Mark it Judgement (no dated photograph found).
2. Real maker names on tins (F6). Both reviews found FENWICK, a real firm, on three tins and jars, and asked for SALTHOUSE, TIDEMARK and BRIGHTWATER to be checked. The target's "no real name" rule covers only the fascia. Fix: add to F6 "tins and jars carry only invented makers, checked against Companies House and the trade-mark register; FENWICK struck". Source: REVIEW-fish_market-v1-close.md point 1, street point 8.
3. Tickets (F5). The close review found twelve tickets standing in a fixed zigzag over bare ice. F5 says one ticket per fish but not where. Fix: add "each ticket at the front of its own tray; LEMONS by the lemons; no fixed spacing". Source: close review point 5.
4. The price (F5). The ONS series is "White fish fillets, per kg (cod prior Feb 02)": 1990 average 574p, January 533p, December 628p. That is 2.60, 2.42 and 2.85 a lb, so the target's sums are right. But it is fillet, not whole cod, and no other fish is priced. Fix: the ticket reads COD FILLET 2.60 lb; prices for every other fish marked Judgement, set below cod fillet for whole or cheaper fish. Source: https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/timeseries/czol/mm23, read 17 Nov 2026.
5. Metal fronts evidence (F1). The Flickr page's only text is "Shops, Princes Avenue, Hull, 1989". Nothing there says metal, aluminium or fish. A title is not evidence of a metal front. Fix: drop it as evidence for F1, or have someone look at the picture and record what it shows. Source: https://www.flickr.com/photos/petermarshall/51039473258, read 17 Nov 2026.
6. The pale fascia (F2, open question 1). The only dated 1990 photograph (t13138) shows a dark board. The pale ground comes from the Hook sheet (fascia-signs D1). The rulings say the Hook sheet governs palette but photographs govern what things looked like and win. Fix: keep the pale ground only as marked Judgement, and look for one dated pale-ground fish fascia from 1985 to 1995. If none turns up, take it to his page as a choice. Don't let it pass in silence.
7. The blind (F8). The target says canvas stowed. The scene still hangs awning_02 out at x 12, projecting 1.735 m, and the target doesn't say whether that changes. Shop blinds existed to shade goods in windows from the sun (Historic Environment Scotland, quoted by Designing Buildings). That supports out in sun, in when dull. Fix: say plainly "canvas in by default, never out at night; awning_02 at x 12 retracted to its box". The same page says the box usually sits above the cornice or is built into the shop front. The scene hangs it under the fascia, so check which fits the Victorian front. Source: https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Shop_blind, read 17 Nov 2026.
8. Fish scans (F5, open question 3). The window's main stock depends on scans whose state is unknown since 12 Oct. The street review said the shop is not a finished fishmonger without them. Fix: name the scans' state, or put them in Needs you as blocked. Sketchfab CC0 is allowed by the allowlist, so licence is not the issue.
9. "Northern" (question 1). Canon says only "a fictional British port town". Fix: write "a British port-town fishmonger". Sheffield (t13138) is not a port, so call its photographs a period inland shop.
10. SMOKED legibility (F3). The target asks for a legible M at 1.5 m but not the cause. The close review found the M's middle point too high, so it read SHOKED. Fix: add "the M's middle point at 0.55 to 0.65 of the cap height". Source: close review point 6.
11. Historic England listed as unreached. It answered today. The only fishmonger fronts I found there are from 2000 (Hertford 15 Oct 2000; Dorking 6 Mar 2000), so they are not period evidence. Fix: update the Unreached list. Sources below.
12. Content rule. No pub, off-licence, betting or child items appear in the dressing list. No fix.
