## Check 5: usability from chapters 0 and 1 alone

No. A fresh session would know the owner's eight decisions and the rules, but not how to start. The three biggest holes:

1. **No start kit.** The records and roles the method leans on are never set up or defined: the canon file, the rulings, the notebook's entry form, the order ledger, the owner's standing page, the data schema, the keeper, the advisor, helpers against sessions, the pace line and weekly points, the folder and storage map. Chapter 0 needs a first-day list naming each with a one-line form.
2. **No phase plan scaled to scope.** The chapters follow the order a game is made, but give no entry and exit for research or design (only the start gate before stage 1), no order for the fourteen passes of 1.4 (which first, which repeat, which a small game skips), and no rule for scaling them: 14 lenses of 50 questions and a 100-angle hunt are this game's scale, not a small roguelike's. Stages are never sized and stage 1's contents are never described.
3. **No technical foundation and no pack template.** Chapter 0 asks for the platform but not the engine, the models and tools, the session set-up or the asset route (licence, generated, made sits buried in 1.9 step 6). A genre with no pack "gets one from its first design passes", but nothing says what a pack holds (laws, measures, references, production notes) or how to fill the areas DISTILL-NOTES calls silent: many-unit pathing, opponent AI, netcode, balance, traversal and combat feel, set pieces, a fast camera. Both far games would start with their core production discipline blank.
