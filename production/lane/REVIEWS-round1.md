# Reviewer panel, round 1 (18 Nov 2026, with the calibration)

The simulated Steam panel (tools/lane/prototype/reviewers/, eight player cards grounded in PLAYERS.md) reviewed two builds of the loop prototype from the AI playtester's record alone: build A, the page as published at 12:00 (before the eight fixes), and build B, round 1 (the eight fixes). Both were played by the same playtester the same day; the reviewers saw only "build A" or "build B", never which was which. Answers: production/lane/reviews-panel/reviews-1200.json and reviews-round1.json (field summaries of the reviewers' own words). A signal for choosing the next round, never a gate.

## Calibration: can the panel tell the builds apart on the faults fixed?

Yes, by what the reviewers cite and how long they stay; not by the thumb.

- Thumbs up: 0 of 8 for both builds.
- Hours before stopping (mean of eight): A 0.19, B 0.36. Two reviewers (story-first, British crime drama) did not quit within the hour on B; on A they quit at 24 and 18 minutes.
- A person repeating one line within one talk (fix 1): cited by 7 of 8 reviewers on A (Sheila's "The weather isn't in the book" to four or five questions, Ron's dock line four times, Ada's window line up to six times); by none on B.
- Paying for nothing (fix 8): cited on A (Darren's three pounds for "Nothing going round that I've heard"); not on B.
- The theme scorer (PLAYERS.md's forty themes, word lists rebuilt) cannot see this difference: both builds score "bugs that break the illusion" and "slow opening, too much talk" high, because repeats across people still read as bugs. So the scorer is kept for comparing with KCD2 and Mafia, and the calibration rests on the cited evidence and hours. No change of method was needed.

## Round 1 (build B): the panel's results

- Thumbs up: 0 of 8. Hours before stopping: 0.05 to 1.0, mean 0.36.
- Quit points: impatient player at 2.4 to 3 min (the opening speeches); sandbox player and AI sceptic at 4 to 5 min; immersion fan at 7 min; period stickler at 11 min; systems player at 17 min; story-first and crime-drama fans did not quit.
- Most exciting moments named: Sheila reading out the debt book (1.9 to 2.1 min, five reviewers); the reporter tracing Sheila's answer to the police, "a fare book learns a sentence" (41 min, three); taking the first fare (4.3 min, one).

## Recurring complaints backed by the playtester's evidence (the next round's candidates, ranked)

1. Nothing tense to do: no fights, chases or escapes, and a third of the hour is waiting (all eight in some form; record: pursuit 0, fight 0, escape 0; waiting 37%; tense moments 6.0 per 30 minutes, all of them sightings of Tom out at night). This is the next round's target: one non-talk activity with tension and stakes.
2. The shared stock lines come from many different people over the hour ("It's been wet since August", "Aye, well. There it is", "Same as ever. Less of it."; six reviewers). Each is now once a talk and one person a day, but the hour spans four game days. Candidate: more of each person's own lines, or a shared line once per week per street.
3. Every debtor refuses with the same line (systems player, crime-drama fan, immersion fan; record: Darren, Lee, Wayne, Julie, Dennis all "I haven't got it. You can see I haven't got it."). Small: give each debtor their own refusal.
4. The opening is four minutes of speeches before the first thing to do (impatient player, sandbox player; record: first action at 4.1 min). Touches canon's opening; for the main session to weigh.
5. Period slips: Darren's closing line "Laters, yeah?" (written in round 1, fixed before publishing to "See you about, yeah?"); "washeteria" (a real British word of the period, so kept, noted).

## Expected friction, not to fix

- "No clear first job" and "no direction" where the record shows the to-do note: the design has no waypoints or minimap on purpose.
- "Talk is the consequence": reviewers asking for less talk overall is the pillar's own aim (talk fell from 36% to 22% of the hour), not a request to remove talk.
