# LEDGER: the vision

One page, for his yes. 18 November 2026. Canon outranks it; his rulings stand.

## The fantasy

Britain, October 1990. Tom Nowak steps off the ferry in Meridian with one suitcase and a letter. His uncle Mickey is dead and has left him a minicab office on Quay Street: two tired cars, a radio, a bookkeeper who will not use his name, an old docker on the door, a book of debts nobody can collect, and an envelope that is expected at the ferry landing after ten. He has £23.50 and the rent is due on Friday.

Fifteen to twenty hours later the port does not move without him. His cars are on every rank, his men are on the doors, and people step aside on the pavement. From a minicab office to running the town.

What is new: no game has been built on a minicab firm, and it is the perfect criminal machine. A cab is cover (nobody looks twice at a minicab at two in the morning), eyes (drivers and passengers hear everything), an alibi (every fare goes in the book) and the risk (the firm's name is on the roof).

## The feeling

- Immersion, as Kingdom Come: Deliverance II has it. The town lives by its own clock whether Tom is there or not. No markers, no meters, no minimap. He learns its streets, its hours and its people the way a newcomer does.
- Tension, because the town is watching. Every job is done in front of windows. The player's questions are always the same three: who can see him, how far did that carry, and who is walking to the phone box.
- A rise to power that is felt and never counted: what people call him (the new owner, Nowak, Tom, Tommy), who works for him, what he owns, what he wears, which doors open.

The town's memory and gossip are the engine under the hood. The player never manages them. He meets them as a look held too long, a fare refused, a constable at the door, a rival who knows too much.

## The loop at three scales

- A minute: read the street, act with the hands, the street answers, get clear. He carries, drives, breaks in, fronts a man up, fights or runs; a head turns, a lamp comes on, somebody walks off towards the phone box; so he finishes it, drops it, stops her, or goes.
- An evening: by day the cab earns clean money and learns the town. At dusk somebody brings a job. At night Tom does it under watch. Then the count: the tin on one side, the fare book on the other. Next morning it comes back, said to his face by somebody who was not there.
- A playthrough: three acts. The Inheritance, one week with nothing and nobody. The Rise, with cars, crew, fronts and rackets, while three rivals each squeeze a different part of him. The Ledger Comes Due, when an inspector opens the books and the ending is whatever the town and the books then hold.

## What makes a moment worth retelling

A plan meets a person. The player chose it, it could have gone another way, somebody with a name was there, and it came back later. A friend tells it like this: he was walking the envelope down the quay when the constable turned round, so he cut through Mickey's yard, and next morning the woman at the window asked what he had been doing in her alley. Every evening is aimed at one such moment, and the first thirty minutes must hold one.

## What LEDGER is not

- Not a gossip simulator. The player acts; talk is where the consequences surface.
- Not a shooter, a driving game, an economy game or a story on rails.
- Not a hundred square miles. One port, dense, known by heart.
- No stats, levels or skill trees. Tom rises in position, never in numbers.
- No game over. A mistake becomes a story or a choice.
- No drink, no gambling, no children.

## The bar

Kingdom Come: Deliverance II for immersion: the town runs without him and nothing breaks the spell. Grand Theft Auto for why people play for hours: there is always something to do with the hands, and it feels good the moment it is touched. Every system carries plain rules a tester can check: never stuck, smooth input on every device, no failure without warning, the camera never inside a wall.

The measure is a panel of eight kinds of player. In round 5 two of the eight recommended it. The vision is met when most of them do, after a whole evening and a whole week of play.
