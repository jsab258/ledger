# LEDGER: the whole game, addendum of 20 November 2026

The design of the gameplay parts the parts tree found missing or thin (production/lane/parts-tree/, GAPS-2026-11-20.md). Written in DESIGN.md's style and read with it. Canon outranks it and his rulings stand; DESIGN.md still governs wherever this addendum is silent.

How to read it:
- Each section names its part ids in the tree, where the gap was found, the design, and its experience rules with their checks (B the clumsy-player bot, S a scripted test, H the AI hands-on session, as DESIGN section 19).
- Numbers are first values, to be tuned by play, and live in the one tuning table (DESIGN section 19). A number marked "to measure" waits for the feel measurements of next week (RESEARCH-NEEDED.md).
- Every choice here has one line in production/lane/parts-tree/DECISIONS-PENDING.md. Three sections are built to a recommended answer to a question for him (QUESTIONS-FOR-JAFAR-2026-11-20.md) and are marked so: A26, A27, A28. They flip if he picks otherwise.
- Nothing here adds a stat, a meter, a marker, a game over, drink, gambling or children, or a fourth rival.

## A1. Hiding and keeping out of sight (1.1.5)

Found by the parts tree's own walk of group 1 (DESIGN gives the light scale but no verb). No crouch-walk and no stealth mode: hiding is what a man in a street can really do.
- Standing still in shadow is the hiding. A still figure in a doorway counts at the doorway's light (0.12) and is caught as a shape at most beyond 8 m. Moving through the same doorway counts at the street's light.
- Turning away. With his back to a watcher, Tom's face cannot be caught; a mark (the coat) still can.
- Among people. In a group of three or more people within 2 m, a watcher's glance at Tom takes twice as long (the 0.35 s glance becomes 0.7 s).
- Behind things. A parked van, a stall, a skip once real (ruling 2 Oct), a corner: no line of sight, nothing caught. The same line-of-sight test the watchers use.
- Nothing on screen says "hidden". The light cue (DESIGN section 2) and the watchers' bodies are the only signs.

Experience rules:
- ER-HIDE-01 Standing still in a dark doorway is caught less than walking past it, at every distance band. Check S: golden rows, still against moving, at 8, 18 and 35 m.
- ER-HIDE-02 Hiding never needs a special key or posture. Check S: the rows above hold with only the move keys.
- ER-HIDE-03 A newcomer can say where to stand to be least seen. Check H: asked at five spots, right 4 times in 5 against the simulation.

## A2. Tailing, on foot and by car (1.1.6, 1.2.6)

Found by the walk of group 1 (DESIGN names tails, no rule). A tail is the town's eyes in reverse: the target is a watcher who looks back now and then.
- The target looks back on a rhythm from his temper (DESIGN section 2's glance), about once every 20 to 40 s on foot (to measure), and at every corner and stop.
- "Made" uses the same ladder as any watcher: a shape is nothing, a mark is a doubt (he speeds up or changes route), a face he knows ends the tail (he turns and says so, or runs).
- No hidden distance fail. Losing sight is not failing: the target keeps his routine, and the player can pick him up at the next place on it, which the Ledger notes once Tom has heard it.
- By car the same, with the car as the mark: a cab behind him twice is a doubt; the firm's roof sign makes it worse.
- A tail ends in a Ledger line naming what was learned (a place, an hour, a person met).

Experience rules:
- ER-TAIL-01 A tail never fails on a distance the player cannot see; it ends only by being made, by arriving, or by the player stopping. Check S: a scripted tail at every distance from 5 to 60 m.
- ER-TAIL-02 Being made always shows first: a doubt (a look back held, a change of pace) comes before the tail ends. Check S: seeded tails, the doubt before the end in every case.
- ER-TAIL-03 What a tail learns is in the Ledger within a game minute. Check S.

## A3. Pursuing on foot (1.5.2)

Found by the walk of group 1. Somebody runs: a runner fare, a man from a fight, a witness heading for the phone.
- A runner runs at the pace of a running man (to measure; first value Tom's run speed minus 5%), so a straight chase closes slowly and a clever line closes fast.
- A runner takes the routes he knows: through yards and alleys of his own routine.
- Catching him is a hand on the collar when Tom is within 1 m from behind, then the choice: let go, talk, or the lower rungs of the ladder.
- Breaking sight for 7 s or 70 m loses him, the same rule as the police use on Tom (DESIGN section 8). He goes home or to a friend, where he can be found later.

Experience rules:
- ER-PURSUE-01 Every foot chase ends, caught or lost, within 90 real seconds. Check S: 50 seeded chases.
- ER-PURSUE-02 A runner can always be caught by a better line and always lost by a slow one. Check S: a direct-line pursuer catches; a lagging one loses.

## A4. Searching and taking (1.6.2)

Found by the walk of group 1 (DESIGN names rooms and keys, not the search). No search minigame.
- Every container a job or story needs has a search time: a drawer 2 s, a desk 4 s, a wardrobe 5 s, a safe needs its key or combination from a person, never a dial game.
- Searching makes a noise at a reach set by the container (a drawer about 2 m, a forced cupboard about 10 m at night).
- What is found is authored per job: the thing sought, a line for the Ledger, and sometimes something nobody asked for (a letter, a photograph) that feeds a story.
- Taking is one press. What is taken is carried (1.6.9) and can be found on him.

Experience rules:
- ER-SEARCH-01 Every container a job names can be searched and each search's time and noise match the table. Check S.
- ER-SEARCH-02 No search is a test of reflexes or puzzles. Check S: every search completes with the use key held.

## A9. Finding the way (6.9)

Found by both red teams (fares cannot work without it). DESIGN section 4's guidance without markers is kept and made concrete.
- Every door that matters has a number and every street a name plate at its ends (4.1.3). A job, a fare and a letter give an address and a landmark.
- People asked the way give it by landmark if they know it, and say "no idea, try the caff" if they do not. Shopkeepers and the constable always know.
- In the car, a fare says "left here" a junction before, as well as Pauline's radio line.
- Lost: after 90 real seconds without progress toward the place in the Open list (moving away from it or circling), Tom's own line notes the landmark again ("the chapel, then second left"), once. After three minutes, the next person he passes within 5 m offers the way.
- The paper map marks the places he has been or been told of (6.3); opening it pauses nothing but stops Tom.

Experience rules:
- ER-WAY-01 A newcomer reaches ten named places unaided within 3 real minutes each. Check H.
- ER-WAY-02 Every place a job, a fare or a letter names has an address and a landmark in the data. Check S.
- ER-WAY-03 The nudge when lost fires once per goal and never inside 90 s. Check S.

## A10. Street manners, stopping and ground rules (1.4.7, 1.1.7, 2.2.6)

Found by the red teams. Verbs first means the smallest acts are verbs too.
- Street manners on one key, picked by the stick: a nod, a greeting, a hard look, a shove. Each is an act with the seven slots and is answered in body or voice within 1 s. A greeting raises nothing; a hard look and a shove are rungs 1 and 4 of the ladder.
- Stopping: sit on a bench, a wall or a step; lean in a doorway; light a cigarette. A stopped man who belongs (sitting, smoking, waiting at the bus stop) is caught as a shape like anyone, and draws no second look; a man standing about at night without a reason draws one.
- Ground rules: every place carries a label: public, open by hours, private, forbidden. Public is free. Open by hours is free in hours, private out of them. Private: the owner or a neighbour warns, warns again, then calls or acts within half a minute (DESIGN section 4). Forbidden (the customs shed, the Harbour Board's back office) skips the first warning.

Experience rules:
- ER-MANNER-01 Every gesture is answered within 1 s and remembered like any act. Check S.
- ER-STOP-01 A seated or smoking Tom is never caught more than a walking one at the same spot. Check S.
- ER-GROUND-01 Every place has a label and the warnings come in order. Check S.

## A11. The water's edge (4.2.4)

Found by both red teams: a port makes it certain on night one.
- Tom never walks or runs into the water: the quay edge stops him like a kerb that cannot be stepped off (ER-CORE-01 holds). Cars cannot leave the quay into the water: bollards and the edge stop them, and a car stopped there backs out (ER-DRIVE-01).
- In a fight the quay edge is one of the seven street places (DESIGN section 7). A man put in the water climbs out at the nearest ladder within a minute, soaked and humiliated; the town talks. Water never kills. Tom, put in by others, climbs out the same way.
- Things can be thrown in: a weapon, goods, a bag. What goes in is gone, and anybody who saw it keeps it.

Experience rules:
- ER-WATER-01 Thirty minutes along every quay edge on foot and by car, and no fall in. Check B.
- ER-WATER-02 A man in the water is out within 60 real seconds and alive. Check S.

## A12. Traffic police and stops (2.6.7)

Found by both red teams and the mechanics check. DESIGN section 5's rule stands: reckless driving in a police car's sight starts a stop.
- One patrol car works the Hook by day and night on a route; more only at the dial's War.
- Reckless means: a hit, driving on the pavement, a red light crossed at speed in sight, or a speed far over the limit (to measure against the game's band). Ordinary fast driving is not stopped.
- A stop: the car flashes and sounds; pull over and the constable looks at the car and the driver (a search if he has cause), notes the plate, gives a caution; refuse and it is a chase (ER-POL-04). No fines as a system (DESIGN section 18).
- The plate noted joins what the police know of the car, as a witness's sighting does.

Experience rules:
- ER-TRAFFIC-01 A stop starts only on a reckless act a police officer saw, and costs under 2 real minutes. Check S.
- ER-TRAFFIC-02 Ordinary driving inside the game's speed band is never stopped. Check S: 40 minutes of lawful driving.

Found by both red teams and the mechanics check. Canon: police are corruptible as individuals, never as a thesis.

Experience rules:

## A14. The firm grows: fleet, drivers, ranks, other firms (2.15.1 to 2.15.3)

Found by the red teams (the vision's last picture is his cars on every rank).
- A car is bought from a man selling one, or from the used-car lot on the Hook's north road edge. A driver is hired from the regulars (DESIGN section 12's two roads) or a stranger who asks at the counter. Drivers leave if unpaid or frightened.
- Ranks: the rank outside Mickey's is his; the ferry rank and the station rank are won by being there with a car on the board enough days, or by a word with whoever holds them. Accounts: the cold-store shift contract (DESIGN section 14), then the Harbour Board's late men, then a solicitor's office.
- Other firms: one other minicab firm works the Hook, run by a regular, with its own two cars. It takes the fares people stop giving Mickey's after a hit-and-run and gives them back as the talk fades. It is a business, never a fourth rival and never a crime faction.

Experience rules:

- ER-FIRM-02 The board takes under a minute a day. Check H.

## A15. Fronts and rackets held (2.17)

Found by both red teams: Act II rests on it. DESIGN section 14 already gives a first front and a first round at rung 3.
- A front is a share of a real business on the street (the launderette, the caff, the empty unit once let), got by money, a favour or pressure. It washes night money through its till (each front raises what can be washed by a set amount a week), hides men and things, and is a place his men are.
- A round is a set of payments collected on a day (DESIGN section 4's collections, repeated): a man from his crew collects it, and trouble on it comes back to Tom through that man.
- Each front and round has its man, his nerve and loyalty (DESIGN section 12), and is a target for the rivals' pressure (DESIGN section 13).
- Not an economy: no stock, no prices, no sliders. Money arrives as the man brings it, and is short if the man is.

Experience rules:
- ER-FRONT-01 Every front and round is run by a named man, and its money and trouble reach Tom only through him. Check S.
- ER-FRONT-02 No front or round is managed on a screen. Check S: screen inventory.

## A23. Casing, drawing a watcher away, the quiet blow and yard dogs (1.6.11, 1.4.8, 1.3.6, 4.11.1)

Found by the red teams and the mechanics check.
- Casing: watching a place from across the street or a parked car for a while learns its hours, who holds the key and which way is left open, each written in the Ledger as Tom sees it. It is a tail on a place (A2).
- Drawing a watcher away: a thrown stone or a rattled bin makes a noise at its reach; a watcher inside it turns his head or walks to look, and remembers there was a noise.
- The quiet blow: a cosh or a fist from behind on a man who has not seen Tom knocks him out (10 to 30 s, the melee sheet). It is a strike with the seven slots like any other, and the man may have caught a shape. No prompt, no finisher shown at length (canon: no cruelty as spectacle).
- Yard dogs: a dog in a yard barks at a stranger; a bark is a noise that draws the owner. A scrap of food quiets it for a while. Dogs are never hurt.

Experience rules:
- ER-CASE-01 What casing learns matches the simulation's routines. Check S.
- ER-DOG-01 No verb harms a dog. Check S: the verb list.
