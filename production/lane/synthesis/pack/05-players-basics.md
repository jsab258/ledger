### 2.2 Method

Source: PLAYERS.md, "What was read, and how far to trust the counts".
- English Steam reviews through Steam's public review interface, one request at a time with 3 to 8 second pauses, in five pulls: Steam's default most helpful list; the 500 most recent of each vote; the most helpful of the past 365 days; the most helpful per week for the first five weeks; the most helpful per month to 12 Oct 2026. Overlaps removed.
- Sample: KCD2 5,701 reviews (3,112 positive, 2,589 negative); Mafia 3,634 (2,088 positive, 1,546 negative). Raw text kept outside the repository.
- Not random: leans on most helpful and newest, and holds about as many negative as positive reviews although the real negative share is 7% (KCD2) and 28% (Mafia). Counts show what is said inside each side and the order of themes, not shares of all players.
- A script splits reviews into sentences and tags themes by word match, with praise or complaint from word lists and a flip for negations. Checked against 300 hand-read reviews (150 a game): on a fresh frozen set of 100, 53 of every 100 script tags matched the hand reading, and the script found 65 of every 100 hand tags; absolute counts rough within a factor of about one and a half. Rank correlation of script against hand order 0.84 for praise, 0.91 for punishment, over 39 themes.
- Ranking: by the average of each game's share of its own reviews, so the bigger KCD2 sample does not outweigh Mafia.
- Quotes under 15 words, at most two reviewers per theme. Patch notes: KCD2 1.2 to 1.5.6 and Steam announcements (159 KCD2, 82 Mafia). Critics: 12 KCD2 and 13 Mafia reviews coded by hand.

### 2.5 Theme list: which game each belongs to, and just below the line

Rule: a theme counts as one game's when its share there is more than three times its share in the other (PLAYERS.md, "The twenty things players praised").
- Praise, mostly KCD2: skill-based melee; side quests as whole stories; crafting, skills and perks; choices and roleplay. Mostly Mafia: weighty gunfights; driving and riding; knife duels. Both: the other thirteen (story, looks, living world, ran well, price and length, period detail, music and sound, voice acting, slow burn, stealth, humour, polish, challenge).
- Punish, mostly KCD2: combat; quest design with no direction; the costly save; systems nobody explains. Mostly Mafia: stutter and crashes; linear and empty map; the ending; repeating knife fights; weak gunplay; racing. Both: story, short or dear, cutscenes and slow opening, boring, bugs, the same loop, stealth, difficulty, hollow world, interface (PLAYERS.md, "The twenty things players punished").
- Just below the line, praise (PLAYERS.md, "Just below the line (praise)"): the town reacts to you (KCD2 2.9%, Mafia 1.9%; seven of twelve KCD2 critics praise it; players name it rarely but notice when it fails); an ending that landed (1.2%, 3.8%); the love story (1.3%, 3.1%).
- Just below the line, punish (PLAYERS.md, "Just below the line (punish)"): the look called poor or dated (1.6%, 3.2%); hunger, sleep, wear and washing as friction (KCD2 3.8%); stiff faces, repeated models and voices (2.4%, 2.6%); choices that change nothing (2.1%, 2.2%); thin paid add-ons (2.2%, 2.3%); the studio replacing a translator with AI (KCD2 81 of 5,701, 1.4%, 49 of them in March or April 2026); culture-war complaints (KCD2 1.6%, off-topic); slow or missing fixes (1.6%, 0.6%).

### 2.6 Other findings

- Where critics and players differ (PLAYERS.md, same-named section): all twelve KCD2 critics praise the living world, players name it far less than story and looks; players fault combat and saves much more than critics. Mafia critics are harder on story (nine of thirteen), stealth, gunplay and knife fights; players punish length, price and the ending more. Critics rarely name price.
- What the patch notes show (PLAYERS.md, same-named section): both studios fixed progress-blockers and crashes first, then interface traps, then the structural complaint they could answer with content (Mafia's Free Ride, KCD2's hardcore mode). Among negative KCD2 reviews, saves went 12% to 11% and combat 39% to 38% (not changed by patches); bugs 36% to 18% and performance 11% to 6% (fixed). Mafia's linear complaint 29% to 21% after Free Ride (direction, not size). KCD2 patch 1.2 listed 811 fixes, 433 for single quests, 106 town-behaviour items.
- What players asked for that LEDGER has ruled out (PLAYERS.md, same-named section): free roam and a bigger map; crafting, skills, stats; waypoints and a minimap; original-language audio; difficulty modes; cars and races; more weapons; paid expansions. Asked for and not ruled out: saving at will, a photo mode, mods (3.8% of KCD2 reviews mention mods).
- Content rule (PLAYERS.md, "Content rule"): alcohol (KCD2 197 reviews, 3.5%), gambling (KCD2 116, 2.0%), children (KCD2 5 reviews say there are none or towns feel empty without them; Mafia 0).

### 2.7 What this changes for LEDGER (PLAYERS.md, same-named section, condensed)

1. Players' three words are story, looks and a world that feels alive; the first half hour needs one scene a friend would retell (R6, P4) before more street.
2. Commonest punishments: short or dear, slow or cutscene heavy, boring and repetitive. Keep P4's empty minutes as the headline number and the cutscene rule.
3. KCD2's save rule is the costliest single rule in the data, unchanged by patches; basics 19 and 20 are right; no destructive choice one click from Resume.
4. Players punish no direction; LEDGER rules out waypoints; basic 6 must carry the load; test with newcomers in P4.
5. Mafia's eleven identical knife fights and forced race read as padding; add no scripted set piece twice in thirty minutes.
6. Critics rate the town reacting highest; players punish it only when inconsistent or all-knowing; the claim check and N3 matter more than more reaction types.
7. Add the move between play and speech to P1's list (Mafia's cutscene stutter).
8. KCD2 players punished the studio's AI use; the AI notice, report-a-line key and Steam disclosure are part of the product.
9. Players notice absent children; put the street's life into residents' routines.
10. Question for Jafar: put price and length on the Monday risk list? (a) yes, now (recommended); (b) wait for phase 4; (c) leave it out.

### 3.3 Conclusions

Source: CENSUS.md, "In plain words" and "6. What the gap means".
- In total size LEDGER is 2 to 7 percent of KCD2; per square kilometre and per hour it is close or ahead on people and story threads, far behind on lines, items and length. A yardstick for scope, not a target.
- Far from AAA: total size; written words (half of KCD2's recorded lines are reactions to the player, the kind LEDGER leans on, and LEDGER has no hand-written bank that size); things (no playable inventory planned); interiors (one built).
- Close: people per km2; people and threads per hour; every one of LEDGER's 41 simulated people is named, against roughly one in four in KCD2.
- Cautions: the close ratios are flattered by the small area and short play time; the most important numbers (buildings, interiors, animations, lines) were published by neither studio, and the sure way to fill them is the study of the game files on the PC (RULINGS 11 October).
- Content rule (CENSUS.md, "7. Where the games touch the content rule"): KCD2's taverns and dice and Mafia's wine business and gambling den are ruled out for LEDGER; Mafia's child backstory noted for the PC study only.

## 4. TALKS.md

Source for every line: production/research/aaa-reference/TALKS.md, the numbered item named. One line each: speaker, studio, lesson.

### 4.1 NPC life and schedules

- 1.1 Martin Černý, Tomáš Plch, Matěj Marko and others, Warhorse with Charles University (2015 paper): a schedule names only a kind of activity, the area picks the place and the place says what to do; quests take over one slot; budget up to 5 ms a frame, measured under 1 ms for 30 complex NPCs and under 2 ms for 300 simple ones; one scripter filled a village in two days.
- 1.2 Tomáš Plch, Warhorse (PC Games, 2015): things carry their own thinking (a door tells an NPC it is open or locked); theft is noticed by the owner's own AI, who goes to report it; testers must follow NPCs to check where and why they go.
- 1.3 Viktor Bocan and Tomáš Plch, Warhorse (E15, 2015): the player is "basically another NPC" with no privileges; each NPC holds relations to people, things and places, so someone thrown out three times stops going there; 4 AI programmers and 7 scripters then.
- 1.4 Tobias Stolz-Zwilling, Warhorse (Press Start, 2024): about a thousand NPCs in the city on daily cycles; day cycles are hard to keep once an RPG layer disturbs them (doors open by day, locked by night); a poisoning makes another NPC dislike you.
- 1.5 Martin Ziegler, Warhorse (Epic interview via Wccftech, 2024): after violence or theft in the region NPCs stay in, arm themselves or call more patrols; all of it runs whether the player is there or not.
- 1.6 Patrik Papšo, Warhorse (X post via GamesRadar, 2025): NPCs pick up dropped items by value against their social status, so a noble leaves what a beggar takes; a rule small enough to copy.
- 1.7 Prokop Jirsa, Warhorse (PC Gamer, 2026): NPCs steal a passed-out player's shoes and cap and wear them; players mistaking systemic clashes for designed scenes is why costly systems are worth it.
- 1.8 Matej Marko, Warhorse (GDC 2025 via GamesRadar): only two NPCs owned horses yet riding needed full pathfinding; "you have to support it", so a rare case costs the full work.
- 1.9 Seth Rosen, Hangar 13 (GDC 2016 slides): behaviours in five kinds (activity, idle, conversation, locomotion, combat); activity picks by priority, position, direction and cooldown with a saturation point; look-at rules include "don't stare"; the world is dead without AI and failing performance kills the AI.

### 4.2 Crime, law and witnesses

- 2.1 Viktor Bocan, Warhorse (Game Rant, 2026): testers called the crime system broken when a shopkeeper named them the thief unseen, because the player was the only one in the shop; "a lot of hidden mechanics"; pulls against RULINGS 30 Sep unless the record states how sure it is.
- 2.2 Tobias Stolz-Zwilling, Warhorse (IGN via 80 Level, 2024): a witness fights or runs to the nearest guard; if the player has gone, citizens become more vigilant; the city ran out of beds so some people sleep outside it.
- 2.3 Jiri Holba, Mafia III AI team (Game AI Pro 360 chapter, 2020): a sight detector on each head and five detection points on the body; recognition speed depends on angle; hearing separate from audio, footsteps count only after several; 18 archetypes and 120 variants; crouching no longer slowed recognition.

### 4.3 Dialogue and quests

- 3.1 Petr Nohejl, Warhorse (Perforce podcast, 2026): one quest tool outside the engine holds quests, encounters, overheard chatter and cutscenes; a switchboard of wired graphs saved as XML; a server check before saving and a build farm playing quests day and night; two programmers full time on the tool.
- 3.2 Rémy Boicherot, Hangar 13 (GDC 2017 slides, Mafia III): lines are assets, not branches (about 850 lines, about 120 used in one playthrough), chosen by conditions, priority, least played and least recently played; no loyalty bar, moods shown only in speech and movement; characters know only what they were exposed to.

### 4.4 Streets, towns and world building

- 4.1 Ondřej Bittner, Warhorse (Xbox Wire, 2025): research order is geographic data, then old military maps, then surviving buildings, then general period knowledge; a lost tower rebuilt from woodcuts; refuse dumps tell what was common.
- 4.2 Alex Cox, Josh Zammit, Devin Hitch, Steve Noake, Hangar 13 (official diary): rare photographs, accounts and a trip to Sicily; real places broken into kinds, then blended into one map; research changed design (knife combat, horses beside cars).
- 4.3 Nick Baynes and Alex Cox, Hangar 13 (VGC, 2024): a rural, deliberately linear story, not "bustling city streets"; rebuilt on Unreal Engine 5; for LEDGER, this game has no town that runs by itself.
- 4.4 Alex Cox and Devin Hitch, Hangar 13 (Player 2, 2025): Mafia III's police came faster to affluent districts than poor ones as an authenticity statement; a ready idea for canon's districts, not ruled; the knife never runs out, guns are approximations.

### 4.5 Combat and animation

- 5.1 Hangar 13 (Community Q&A posts): not open world; three knife classes with durability spent and restored; firearms less forgiving; difficulty changes only damage, accuracy, loot and vehicle damage.
- 5.2 Josh Zammit and staff, Hangar 13 (official diary): systemic movement and non-speaking performance captured in Brno, cinematics and speech in California.
- 5.3 Nick Baynes, Alex Cox, Tomáš Hřebíček, Hangar 13 (PAX East panel, 2025): a golden-path story with free roaming; knife duelling new; a collector let them photograph, drive and record the earliest cars.
- 5.4 Tobias Stolz-Zwilling, Warhorse (GGRecon, 2024): guns and heavy crossbows are slow "ultimate weapons" so the game is not a shooter; an in-house historian works with museums and reenactors; a city was wanted from the start but was out of reach at 35 staff.

### 4.6 Production and tools

- 6.1 Prokop Jirsa, Warhorse (Edge via GamesRadar, 2026): the sequel built on what the first game learned; about 250 staff, 100 more; new pipelines to see project state and how systems fit.
- 6.2 Tobias Stolz-Zwilling, Warhorse (Gamepressure, 2019): the engine at one point allowed no more than six people on one screen, so idle "artificial NPCs" filled frames; the studio kept the engine for its tools.
- 6.3 Michal Drimalka and staff, Hangar 13 (official diary): one building over one million triangles against 20 to 30 thousand before; MetaHuman, Nanite and Lumen "to the max"; lighting as part of the cinematic feel.
- 6.4 Alex Cox, Hangar 13 (Epic interview via Gameranx, 2025, unverified against the original): photographic reference from Sicily; Megascans for rocks and terrain; layered lighting.
- 6.5 Hangar 13 audio staff (official diary): period weapons and cars recorded; singing labourers, street musicians and musical NPCs tuned to the score; Sicilian voice and ambience with Stormind Games.
- 6.6 Ondřej Bittner, Warhorse (GamesRadar, 2025): every mini-game immersive as if the hero does it; realism cut where it hurt play; variety from layered systems because same sessions bore; tied to RULINGS 4 Oct (the twenty basics).
