## Summary in five lines

1. Professionals do this, and it has a name: the horizontal slice. Every area and every step of the main story exists in rough (grey boxes, stand-in people, stubbed talk, placeholder mechanics) so the whole game can be walked start to finish; it "eventually becomes the game as content fills in" (Tim Cain, The Outer Worlds).
2. For a simulation game the matching milestone is the connected-systems playable: every system in at its lowest working fidelity, each feeding another, judged over minutes and hours of play (Borut Pfeifer, 2013).
3. Half of his question is already answered by his own decision of 17 Nov (21:57, advisor 211): the browser prototype becomes the whole game in rough and the spec of record, a source project from the order of 23 Nov at about 10% of a week. That is the story skeleton; it is in hand and not offered again here.
4. What stays open is the Unreal side: whether the Hook goes grey end to end (every street and door, stand-ins, the AI tester walking from arrival to an ending) before its people are multiplied, or stays street by street as now.
5. RECOMMENDED, option A: a grey Hook in Unreal after the friends' evening, docks greyed last or not at all, about 2 to 4 W (estimate, low confidence), most of it kept as the Hook's layout. It stays inside one district, so it keeps the words of his "deep before wide" ruling, but it changes the order of the depth work, so it is his call.

## What the professionals do (read 18 Nov 2026)

- Tim Cain's stages: test rooms, prototype (all features together in grey, "won't look like the final product, but it should play like it"), beautiful corner, vertical slice, horizontal slice ("all areas are playable, but none are complete"), alpha, beta. The horizontal slice shows the real amount of maps, art, talk and scripting, how much of each area the main story uses, the play time ("if the horizontal slice takes 20 hours, the final game will be at least that long"), and order problems in open play. Fallout and Arcanum had no formal process and placed content by feel; Bloodlines ran out of time and left the Warrens thin; at Carbine it failed partly because artists wanted to finish levels instead of accepting grey boxes; on The Outer Worlds it worked.
- Borut Pfeifer (Game Developer, 30 Aug 2013): in a simulation, "building 2-3 minutes of polished gameplay is largely impossible until the game is in post-production", because pacing comes from how systems interact. Connect every system with minimal function, about 5 to 7 systems, each with at least one input from and one output to another; one consistent low-fidelity look so nobody fixates on one asset; then judge play, cost and technical risk.
- Ron Gilbert (8 Jul 2011): vertical slices waste money; build foundations and build up, "Da Vinci didn't paint the Mona Lisa one strip at a time".
- Supergiant on Hades (Greg Kasavin, 17 Jan 2019): monthly milestones: code changes early in the month, then locked, then content, voice, testing and polish. (The article calls the Early Access launch a "pilot episode" with the story unfinished; it does not say a whole game existed early.)
- The risk every source names: people judge grey boxes as the finished look. Cain's answer is a separate beautiful corner that proves the look; Pfeifer's is one consistent plain style.
- Reference first (CLAUDE.md): the reference studies (production/research/aaa-reference, studies and TALKS.md) were searched for slice, blockout and grey-box notes on Mafia or Kingdom Come II: none found; no public source on their production order came up in this run's searches.

## Options for the Unreal side

A. Grey Hook first (RECOMMENDED). After phase 4 (friends' evening), before the Hook's 14 principals and about 30 regulars are multiplied: every street and door of the Hook as grey boxes, stand-in bodies with simulation identities, talk live or stubbed, crime and empire at Pfeifer's minimum (one punch, one consequence; one business, one racket), the story beats taken from the browser skeleton. The AI tester walks from arrival to an ending each night. The docks greyed last, or not at all while they are his first cut.
- Size: about 2 to 4 W (estimate, low confidence). Basis: one street's rough layout is 26 to 52 h and the Hook has several streets; past guesses ran 2 to 4 times over (progress.json, phase 2 note), so the upper end may be low.
- Gains: walking times, crowd memory and play time in the real engine measured many months sooner (estimate); the monthly re-forecast gets a measured content count; the 1 Feb scope options chosen with the whole district in view; polish then goes where play goes.
- Costs and fit: most of the grey layout becomes the Hook's layout and is kept (Cain), but some of it will be redone. It stays inside one district, so it keeps the words of his 5 and 6 Nov ruling ("deep before wide: one dense district proves the method, then the game goes wide"), yet it puts layout across the whole district ahead of finishing streets one by one, which changes the order that ruling implies.

B. Grey Hook later. The same, but after the Hook's first block of people passes (station 5's "one more block"). Proves the person method first; the skeleton comes 4 to 8 W later (estimate).

C. Keep street by street (today's plan). Keeps the depth order exactly and puts every hour on finished pieces; the browser skeleton alone carries story pacing and endings, and Unreal's walking times and crowd load are first known street by street.
