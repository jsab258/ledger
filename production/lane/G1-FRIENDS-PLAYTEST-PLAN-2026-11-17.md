## The method, end to end

The professional pattern is small rounds: find problems, fix, test again. About six players a round finds most problems (six allows one no-show); more rounds beat more players per round. Nielsen's "five users find about 80%" is only a starting point for complex games [READ gamesuserresearch.com, Bromley, 17 Nov 2026].

1. The AI tester's pass on the frozen package (below); friends only once it is clean.
2. Round 1: three friends, one an evening, think-aloud as RUNBOOK.md.
3. Fix blockers two or more hit; re-run the AI tester.
4. Round 2: three new friends on the fixed package, same sheet.

Six friends, six sessions of about 45 minutes (30 playing, 15 talking), over two to three weeks.

Speaking while playing can change how people play; asking afterwards keeps play natural but memories drift. The middle way is asking afterwards over the replayed recording ("cued retrospective") [READ casrai.org, 17 Nov 2026]. In LEDGER players speak to characters aloud, so live think-aloud may get mixed up with in-game talk [I]. Suggest: round 1 as RUNBOOK.md; one round-2 friend plays silent and talks over the recording afterwards, to compare.

## How the build reaches them

Recommended, and already ruled: at his PC, his account, the shortcut. Nothing new; his key and cap stay in his control.

Remote options, only if a friend cannot come round:
- Steam Playtest: a separate app linked to a main game already set up on Steam; Valve reviews only capsule images and icons; no charging; no secrecy agreement; sign-ups pick players at random, so friends would get keys, which work with the store page hidden [READ partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest, 17 Nov 2026]. Needs a Steamworks account and main app (fee not on that page). The real block: live talk runs on his key, which never leaves his PC; remote play needs talk offline or a hosted service, a scope and money question outside G1.
- A zip of the shipped folder plus the 4.3 GB voice: the same key problem, and unsigned-game warnings [I]. Not recommended.

## The one-page sheet (adds to RUNBOOK.md's four notes)

Watch, with the minute noted:
- first moment lost, and for how long;
- each question asked aloud;
- each talk with a person, and any reply that surprised or confused;
- any line breaking 1990 or the content rule;
- any visible fault: hole, stutter, wrong face or voice;
- silences over a minute.

Ask after: RUNBOOK.md's questions, then "would you play it again, and for what?"

## How findings are sorted

Each observation is one row: minute, what happened, how many friends hit it. Two or more friends, or play stopped: FINDINGS.md, and a NOW.md item if it blocks round 2. Story, canon or content: his page, one line, recommended. Design ideas: DECISIONS.md if acted on, else dropped; new ones wait for the next order. The evening's summary to FOR-JAFAR.md; the method's lessons to RECIPE.md. Raw notes and recordings stay local, deleted on the agreed date.

## What the AI tester does first

Before any friend, on the frozen package: phase 4's two newcomer routes and one checked-live route; a careless run that skips instructions (like P4 run 1); a run that pushes live talk against the content rule and uses the report key; the cap from a cold start; rain at night for performance. Friends then judge fun and whether people feel real.
