## Claims and verdicts

| # | Claim (target or brief) | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | A broken shopfront in Britain c.1990 was made safe by boarding | Confirmed as practice, material not | Yorkshire Evening Post photo set: "All the shop fronts are boarded up for protection" (early 1980s, Leeds). Board material not named. |
| A2 | Board, tape or crack in 1990 | Judgement | Nothing read on tape or time to boarding. |
| A3 | The shop window would be ordinary (annealed) glass, not toughened or laminated | Judgement, supported by law: no detailed safety glazing rule until 1 June 1992 (Hansard 27 May 1993) | BS 6262 was a voluntary code in 1983 (Hansard 7 Dec 1983); Part N came into force 1 June 1992. |
| A4 | Float glass, not polished plate, by 1990 | Confirmed | Pilkington: float announced 1959, reliable and profitable by 1962-3; before it, distortion-free glass came only from the "costly and wasteful plate process". |
| A5 | Break pattern: one sheet with jagged edges left in the frame, shards on sill and pavement | Judgement, consistent with A3 | Toughened glass breaks into "small, blunt-edged particles"; laminated holds fragments on its interlayer (Designing Buildings). Annealed glass fails in "long sword-like shards", toughened by "dicing fragmentation" (British Glass, fractography page), so try 2's small four-to-six-sided chips read as toughened and are wrong for the period. The in-frame pattern (radial cracks, a hole at the blow, dagger pieces held at the edges) is judgement, not sourced. |
| B1 | Sky's first service began 5 Feb 1989; the rival's square antenna came in 1990 | Squarial in 1990 confirmed; launch dates not re-checked (see Unreached) | Squarial page: inception 1990, 38 cm white plastic flat plate. Launch dates not re-read today. |
| B2 | The dish is 60 cm | Corrected | Standard Sky analogue dish was the "60cm" (Rewind Museum). The first dedicated Sky dish, the 1989 SDX60, measured 711 x 625 x 40 mm (Science Museum Group). An oval about 0.71 by 0.63 m (taller than wide is judgement; the record does not give its orientation). |
| B3 | Dish colour and type | Confirmed: white, offset | "At first dishes were white", fitted with an LNB on "the white offset dish"; black mesh came "a little later" (Rewind Museum). |
| B4 | LNB arm | Confirmed | Offset dishes have a "single strong feed support arm at the bottom"; the LNB sits on a circular clamp at its end, out of the beam (Satsig). The arm should rise from the bottom rim. |
| B5 | Would a 1990 dish's centre read black | Corrected: no | An offset dish has nothing at its centre: the bowl face is plain painted reflector and the feed sits off to the bottom on the arm (Satsig, Rewind). The centre reads white, shaded by the curve and grime, so black there is likely a rendering fault (target section B diagnosis, not yet proven). |
| C1 | Paint fails first at edges | Confirmed | Surface tension pulls paint off sharp edges, leaving them "only half as thick as the rest of the paint system"; edges become drip points; peeling at "the edges of joinery" (Resene Architect's memo 13, Sept 1981). |
| C2 | Chips at door bottom rail, lock rail, sill nose | Confirmed for bottom rail and sill; judgement for lock rail | Bottom rail is "where water intrusion is most likely"; sills, end grain and joints fail first (JLC). The lock rail wears from hands, not water: judgement. |
| C3 | Widen chips to outer arrises of pilasters, cornice, mullions, not every moulding | Confirmed in principle | Sharp outer arrises carry the thinnest film (C1). Add cornice and transom tops, pilaster and mullion feet, joints. |
| C4 | Primer and undercoat colours under the gloss | Judgement | Fleetwood confirms primer, two white undercoats, gloss; no primer colour. Pink wood and red oxide metal primers seen only as unopened search titles. |
| C5 | 3 to 10% wear within 5 cm of an edge | Judgement | Mafia game data, not a 1990 measurement. |
| C6 | Rust | Judgement | Rust only on iron or steel; timber shows grey wood, so rusty flakes on timber (7 Nov) were wrong. |

## Against canon

- No Sky, Amstrad or BSB name or logo on the dish (no real logos). Ship it plain white; if a mark is wanted, a fictional one from the brand bible (the regional TV channel is still owed).
- Posters or graffiti on a board: no alcohol or gambling, no children, fictional names only.
- No issue found with glass or paint.

## Fixes the target should take

1. A: annealed float glass. One sheet in the frame, radiating cracks, a hole at the blow, dagger pieces held at the edges; long sharp shards on sill and pavement, not small chips. Plan a boarded second state for the morning after (timing is judgement).
2. B: white oval bowl about 0.71 by 0.63 m (taller than wide is judgement), arm from the bottom rim, LNB on a round clamp, plain white centre. Black in the bowl is likely a mesh or shading fault, not yet proven.
3. C: weight the edge mask to bottom rails, sill nose, cornice top, pilaster and mullion feet, joints and outer arrises. Under chips: white undercoat, primer, grey wood; rust only on iron or steel.
