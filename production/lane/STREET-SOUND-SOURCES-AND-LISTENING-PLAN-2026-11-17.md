## Licence findings

- Freesound: licence per sound. CC0 and CC BY allow commercial use (CC BY with credit); CC BY-NC does not; the retired Sampling+ is on old uploads (Freesound FAQ).
- BigSoundBank: CC0 1.0, commercial use without asking (its rights page). Unit 10's 8 BigSoundBank sources stand.
- Sonniss #GameAudioGDC: commercial projects, unlimited copies, no attribution; the sounds may not be sold as they come; no AI clause on the page; licence page undated. The 2026 bundle is 7.47 GB from 17 vendors (rekkerd, 16 Mar 2026). Off the list: needs a pick, and a disk check before any download.
- Pixabay: free, no credit needed, no standalone distribution; the summary does not say whether a game counts as creative effort. The full terms page did not load: unreached, not evidence. Needs a pick.
- BBC Sound Effects: non-commercial reuse only (Broadcast; a 2024 blog quoting the BBC's licensing page). The BBC page itself was not read. Out.
- British Library: copies, including commercial, by request, assessed case by case. Not a free source.
- Fab audio: not read; the allowlist names Fab only under 3D; buying is his alone. Needs a pick.

## Period-wrong or banned sounds

- Banned by canon: pub crowds, clinking glasses, fruit machines, betting-shop commentary, racing on a radio; children, playgrounds, an ice-cream van's chime.
- Wrong for 1990: an electric car's whine, hybrid buses, stop-start silence, modern common-rail diesels (vans after about 2000), bus door chimes and spoken announcements, mobile phone rings and texts, key-fob chirps.
- Reversing bleepers: legal on lorries over 2 tonnes and buses from 28 Feb 1985, little taken up by private hauliers (Commercial Motor, 14 Dec 1985); not made compulsory (Hansard, 14 Nov 1988). Rare: a council lorry at most. The hiss-type alarm is later (date unverified).
- Car alarms are period-right (Hansard, 13 Apr 1989); keep one rare and far.
- A pelican crossing's bleep is period-right (named in the 1985 article).
- American sounds: US sirens, yellow-cab horns, US train horns, non-British gulls.
- Music: none. Copyright, and music waits on P18. Radios are speech only, no word caught.

## Professional method

Every source found uses three parts: quiet looping beds; short sounds spawned around the listener at random gaps, pitch and loudness, capped by place and hour; sounds fixed to objects, each heard within a set distance. Mafia: The Old Country's files (our study 8, 28 Oct: a quiet street spawns at most 3 far voices and 3 activity sounds by day, none at night; gulls to 150 m); Prototype's New York, crowd layers rising with people near (Game Developer, 4 Jun 2009); an Audiokinetic post. Mafia: Definitive Edition's own method was not found.
