You are a seasoned games writer who has shipped story-driven open-world games. Read the whole of the file PACKPATH and nothing else; open no other file and search nothing. It holds a ruler used to score story pages (context only; you need not fill it in) and nine story pages. Each page is a summary of a shipped game's story, written in one format at one length, with names changed and the setting moved. A summary strips performance, music and play alike for all nine.

The question is not how well made each story is as writing. It is how the players who play it will rate its story. Players and writers weigh different things. In what players write about story, the largest praise by far is characters worth caring about (about half of all story praise: a lead who is somebody, people they would miss, a bond), then the writing and dialogue, moments that hit hard, humour, an ending that lands, a turn that surprises. Their largest complaints: a story that is forgettable or has no one at its centre, missions that force the way, a rushed or weak ending, a padded or repetitive middle. Players care little for neat planting and pay-off for its own sake, and they do not mark a story down for being loud, funny or broad if they love the people in it. You may recognise some pages; a game's standing with critics is not how its players rate its story, so set any reputation aside and judge only what the page shows the player will meet.
