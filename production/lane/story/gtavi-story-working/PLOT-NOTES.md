# Grand Theft Auto VI: plot notes from public written sources

Compiled 21 November 2026 (two days after the 19 November 2026 release). Research notes only. Spoilers throughout.

Confidence tags: [2+] two or more reliable outlets; [1] one reliable source; [U] unreliable or unverified only.
"Reliable" here means a named outlet with a dated review or guide (Kotaku, VGC, NPR, Red Dead-style walkthrough sites with named authors, Rockstar's own pages). Wikipedia is counted as reliable but has no plot section.

## 0. How complete the sources are

- Thin. Two days after release there is no full, verified plot summary on any page reached. Wikipedia's article (checked, whole text) has only a premise paragraph and a cast list, with no plot section.
- Reviews (Kotaku, VGC, NPR) were published 18 November under embargo and deliberately avoid the third act and the endings.
- The only detailed plot content is the Dork walkthrough (20 Nov), which covers Prologue to the start of Chapter 5 in outline but says no full ending breakdown has been published. Its Endings section is explicitly "reports say".
- Mission lists exist (Dexerto, Red Bull, Beebom, Dork), but the four disagree on counts. Dexerto, Red Bull and Dork give 91 missions (22/11/26/25/7, Dork says 21 for Ch1) with some titles redacted as [Spoiler]. Beebom's own running list gives 118 plus Prologue (25/18/31/34/10), because it counts sub-objectives and second halves as separate entries. Treat 91 as the story-mission count, [2+], and Beebom's 118 as objective-level.
- The endings are the weakest area. Beebom (20 Nov) and Europeans24 (21 Nov) describe a bridge finale, but both are secondhand and they disagree with each other on who survives. Kotaku says only that there are several endings and the reviewer saw two. No source gives a verified full list.
- No source describes the side stories ("Strangers"-type content) in any depth. No source I reached names a single "most retold" scene with evidence.

## 1. Setting and premise [2+]

- Leonida, a fictional Florida: the Keys, Port Gellhorn, Vice City, swamp and rural north (NPR, VGC, Kotaku, Wikipedia).
- Two playable leads, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, a criminal couple. Rockstar's Rupert Humphries calls it a "Bonnie and Clyde-type narrative" (Wikipedia). Reviewers compare it to Badlands, Heat, True Romance (NPR).
- The couple want out of crime and a normal life (a bank account, a home, a family), not crime-lord status or revenge (Kotaku, NPR). The wish keeps failing: an ex-serviceman and a robber on an ankle tag cannot get legitimate work, so crime pulls them back (VGC).
- Wikipedia's premise: after a failed bank heist the pair uncover a statewide conspiracy and must protect each other. The released game's structure (below) shows this is the Fuentes/hard drive thread.
- Writing: first main GTA not written by Dan Houser (Wikipedia).

## 2. The leads

- Jason Duval: Army veteran (NPR says dishonourably discharged), flies drug runs for Brian Heder in the Keys, fishes with Cal. Reserved, loyal, unsure he is a good man, the voice of reason (Kotaku, NPR) [2+].
- Lucia Caminos: petty thief and car thief, paycheck to paycheck; less troubled by her flaws, pushes for bigger risks and goals (Kotaku). Opens the game in jail after robbing a payday-loan office (Kotaku, NPR, Dork) [2+]. VGC praises her performance.
- Player can switch between them, play the pair, or phone the other to drive the getaway (NPR, VGC).

## 3. Supporting characters (role, wants, fate)

- Cal Hampton: Jason's best friend and fishing partner; recurring contact for side work and fencing. Wants a quiet life (paranoid, per Wikipedia). Fate unknown. [2+ on role]
- Brian Heder: long-time drug runner and Jason's landlord/employer in the Keys; hires him through Chapter 1; hinted retirement (Dork). Fate: not stated. [1]
- Donnie: Brian's associate; killed by the Kingfishers before "Swamped"; the player clears out his trailer and car (Dork). [1]
- Mia Caminos: Lucia's mother, spends all her money on a rundown house flip; attacked by an alligator in the opening (NPR). Fate not stated. [1]
- Lori: rescued in "Swamped", then taken onto another boat whatever the player does (Dork). [1]
- Arnold: chased in "Hey Arnold!"; player kills or spares him; he leaves the story either way (Dork, Dexerto's mission guide, Beebom endings piece on the profile effect) [2+ that the choice exists].
- Captain Méndez: police captain who pins the Visitor Center killings on the couple; reappears in the finale (Dork). [1]
- Raul Bautista: charming veteran bank robber from Port Gellhorn; recruits talented, risk-taking people; reckless; "crew must commit or walk" (Rockstar's own page). In the released game he sets up the Cartel Heist and then the airport heist and decides to take the score himself; Valentina sells him out; Jason and Lucia find him tortured on his houseboat and "end it" (Dork). Fate: dies. [Rockstar + Dork]
- Dre'Quan Priest (Only Raw Records), Boobie Ike (Vice City businessman), Bae-Luxe and Roxy (Real Dimez): Vice City employers/contacts in Chapter 3, owed favours in Chapter 4 (Wikipedia for roles; Dork for chapter use). Fates not given.
- Abe Gonzalez: lawyer whose fee the couple must work off (Dork, Beebom list). [2]
- Valentina: Raul's associate who houses them at the Y Hotel, later betrays Raul (Dork). [1]
- Kordell Grant: crew boss planning the airport vault heist, posing as a wine authenticator; his crew is turned on by Raul's (Dork). [1]
- Nate, Andrés de León, Gustavo (de León's head of security, the inside man, killed in "Child's Play"), Karim (dies in the airport heist), Senator Camila Fuentes (footage surfaces in "Close Surveillance"), Giorgio Gallo (cocaine smuggler; introduced end of Ch4; offers pardons; betrays them), Vinny and Liz (killed in "Operation Savage Glory"), Mattia (named in Ch5), an Albanian hitman (Dork). [1 each, single walkthrough]

## 4. Structure and opening missions

Five chapters plus Prologue, 91 missions [2+]: 
1. Just Like Paradise (22; Dork says 21)
2. Paradise Lost (11)
3. Tits Ass Drug Money (26)
4. Vice City, Baby (25)
5. Top of the World, Ma (7)

- Prologue: Lucia in prison (Hamlet Correctional per Dork; the Beebom list says counsellor interview and escape attempt as control tutorial); flashback to the payday-loan robbery and arrest; control passes to Jason (Dork, Beebom) [2].
- Chapter 1: Jason does odd jobs and smuggling in the Keys for Brian ("Gone Fishing", "Gear Up", "Lab Fees", "Pipework", "Island Dreams" with a shark and a harpoon). A no-crime mission has Jason and Cal clean a living room and move a TV (Kotaku). Lucia's release ("Coming Home"/"Day Release") puts her in Jason's house; they open a BuckMe joint account. The chapter ends with a police ambush at the Grassrivers Visitor Center; Jason is hospitalised in Vice City and Lucia gets him out (Dork) [1 for the ambush]. Opening scene praised by NPR: Mia's house, a mattress in a pool, an alligator (NPR) [1].
- Chapter 2: Lucia hides a wounded Jason at the Starlet Motel, Port Gellhorn, on an ankle tag, finding rent, painkillers and a car. "Checking Out" is a point of no return and needs $15,000 for the move to Vice City (Dork, Red Bull, Dexerto lists agree on titles) [2 on structure, 1 on detail].
- Chapter 3: both work from a borrowed Bayside apartment in Vice City for Dre'Quan, Boobie Ike and Raul: drug deals, car thefts, a bank job (Sinfrontera National Bank), ending on "The Cartel Heist" (Red Bull, Dexerto, Dork) [2+].
- Chapter 4: they live in room 701 of the Y Hotel, work off Abe's fee, and are forbidden to be seen together from "Making Gainz" until "VCIA Takedown" (Dork) [1]. Airport heist: "VCIA Takedown". "Sunset" is the Valentina betrayal. "Starting From Zero" (Beebom's name for the last mission; Dexerto/Red Bull redact it) has the police hunting them and accounts frozen.
- Chapter 5: "Bird in the Hand", "Taking Liberties", "VC Baby", "Doggie Bag", "Operation Savage Glory", "Protective Custody", finale "The End of the Road" (title from Beebom and Dork) [2].

## 5. The long middle

- Chapters 3 and 4 are most of the game (about 51 of 91 missions). The repeated shape: couple-run jobs for Vice City bosses, then heists, with a robbery-escape-celebrate loop. Reviews cite robberies that escalate from corner stores to banks (Kotaku, NPR, VGC) [2+].
- Length: VGC says about 60 hours main story; Kotaku logged 152 hours with two endings and most side content; Dork says 50 to 80; Rockstar-sourced pre-release reports said up to 80 (TechRadar, others, not re-fetched).
- Pacing: Kotaku says the later chapters "spin their wheels" before picking up near the end; NPR says the third act "drags a bit". [2]
- Relationship and Criminal Profile systems run through the whole game (see section 9).
- Side/stranger content: conversations with NPCs lead to side missions (VGC); post-story completion list includes scanning cars, observing animals, clearing rival gang operations and fencing contraband (Screen Rant). No named stranger-mission arcs in any source reached.

## 6. Funny and grave scenes

Funny [1 each unless stated]: Lucia imitates Jason's deep voice (Kotaku); the living-room/TV mission with video games (Kotaku); a polar bear escaping a zoo transport and killing people (Kotaku); a stuntman's backflip and crypto scam (Kotaku); drag queens flirting with police and streamers jumping off a bridge (VGC); a mission to spray manure on a farmer's boss's house (NPR); podcast satire (VGC, NPR) [2].

Grave: reviews name no specific tragedy. From Dork: Donnie's killing, the Visitor Center ambush, Gustavo's death, the airport massacre, Raul's torture and death, Vinny and Liz killed. NPR's only ending hint is "final operatic moments" and a closing note on predators and prey.

Spectacle set-pieces: a street-to-highway shootout (Kotaku); a net-dangling job under a cargo helicopter carrying cocaine, a bank robbery in the rain (NPR); a 5-star chase ended in a railway yard and the first central Vice City mission "among the best in series history" (VGC).

## 7. The turn and last act (single-source: Dork)

- The turn is the Chapter 4 airport job: Raul decides to take the vault score himself, his crew turns on Kordell's, Valentina has sold Raul out; the couple end Raul. Nothing forces this; no reviewer calls it "the twist".
- The hard drive and a statue that the couple go back for in "Starting From Zero" lead to Giorgio Gallo, who says the drive is blackmail on Senator Camila Fuentes, protecting his cocaine smuggling. He offers presidential pardons for a last job: destroy a rival shipment ("Operation Savage Glory"). In "Protective Custody" he betrays them; they are arrested separately, fight corrupt police and face Méndez. "The End of the Road" sends them to Giorgio's penthouse for the drive.
- Wikipedia's "statewide conspiracy" premise matches this. No second source for the Gallo/Fuentes chain.

## 8. Endings

What is solid [2+]: there are multiple endings (Kotaku, VGC, Beebom, Dork); the game then reverts to a point before the final mission, with the couple outside a law office, so free roam continues and there is no New Game Plus at launch (Screen Rant; Dork: free roam after credits; Europeans24 says it returns before Chapter 5's mission, which differs) [2].

What is disputed or unverified:
- Beebom (20 Nov, "confirmed as one of two main branches"): a bridge roadblock in "The End of the Road", choices "Swim for It" / "No Surrender". Ending A: Lucia escapes to raise their son, Jason goes to prison. Ending B: both die in a shootout, echoing Bonnie and Clyde. It also says at least four variants exist and Rockstar said "a lot more than two".
- Europeans24 (21 Nov, self-described fan-sourced): both die at a bridge roadblock (two variants), or a good ending where both live and move to Liberty City with their son. It disagrees with Beebom on the survival outcome of the "Swim for It" path, and its labels are its own interpretation. [U]
- Dork's finale description is a penthouse trip for the drive, which could be the run-up to the bridge; no source reconciles them. Dork says social claims of two or four endings are unsupported.
- Kotaku reviewer saw two endings. NPR hints at a tragic-operatic close.
- Pre-release leak articles (Sportskeeda "Jason and Lucia kill each other", "ends on a depressing note", Gaming Bible "ending spoiled" 2025) appeared in search results; I did not use them, and their claims should be ignored.

What decides it [2+ that these factors matter; exact mapping unknown]:
- Relationship level between Jason and Lucia (Kotaku, VGC, Beebom, Dork; four levels, per Beebom).
- Criminal Profile of each character, Professional to Hardened to Irredeemable; Irredeemable is permanent (VGC, Beebom, Dork).
- Story decisions (Arnold kill/spare feeds the profile) (Beebom, Dork).
- Money kept or invested in the BuckMe account (Beebom, Dork).
- Per Dork, save before Chapter 5 and before the final mission; gold trophy "You Gotta Know When to Get Off".

## 9. Choices versus fixed story

Choices [2+ unless noted]: who to play and when to switch; couple vs friends and dates, clubs, robberies (Kotaku, VGC); stealth or gunplay, lethal vs non-lethal (Kotaku, VGC); the final bridge choice (Beebom only, [1]); Arnold (kill/spare); saving Dobbs from the shark with two harpoon hits (only the closing dialogue changes; Dork [1]); whether Lucia goes with Jason on "Day Release"; Lucia's Chapter 1 errands set the starting relationship; the car wash defuse in "Girls' Trip" (Dork [1]).
Fixed: nearly every plot event above, including the arrests, Raul's fate, Giorgio's betrayal and Lori's capture, whatever the player does.

## 10. Most retold scene

No source reached states this with evidence. The candidates mentioned in print are the alligator opening (NPR's chosen emblem), the polar bear (Kotaku), and the ending bridge (secondhand coverage). I cannot say which players retell most.
