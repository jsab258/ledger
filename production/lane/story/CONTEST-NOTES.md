# Contest notes: what each version chose, so the next one differs

Not shown to any reader. Written before versions B and C, as the order asks.

## Before B: the choices A ("Twice") made

- Spine of the middle: the club's asks grow kinder, then one ask is the crime. First half, the club gives; second half, people come to read one line in a book.
- The outfit: real, open and criminal. Cyril the tallyman runs it and says so in week one. No hidden hand at all.
- Last year's fire: the club lit it, as the street nearly has it. No lie about who.
- Midpoint: a deed by the player. An ordinary account fare on Bonfire Night is the arson run; he drives it, hands it to Terry, or works it out.
- Central turn: Sheila writes his fare in the book, and it is word for word the line she wrote for Mickey a year before, with one line more.
- Main opponent: Cyril and the account; Danny as the wronged rival. The Widow and Agar small.
- Tom's test: complicity. He owes a named person.
- Ada: only a witness. Sheila: incorruptible recorder.
- Climax: the same fare booked a third time; what he does at the landing with his hands.
- Roads: one line of scenes for both.
- Tone: the darkest of the three.

So B must not: turn on a deed of arson by the player; make the outfit real; make the tallyman the opponent; repeat a line twice as its turn; end on a third fare. B should be about something other than complicity.

## Before C: the choices A and B made

B ("No Charge"):
- Spine of the middle: a reputation. First half, the town fears the firm for a fire and the fear pays; second half, Tom knows there is nothing behind the name and has to live on it or give it up.
- The outfit: does not exist. Three old parts each think another part is alive; a bookkeeper fills the envelopes from the cab takings by writing paid fares as "no charge".
- Last year's fire: nobody's. A watchman's heater. The firm let the street believe it.
- Midpoint: car two is burned by Danny, openly; the insurance claim puts the fare book in the player's hands and his own paid fares are in it as free. He follows Sheila.
- Central turn: Sheila is the keeper, and the first night's carry was his own money. The noose is round her.
- Main opponent: Agar, a respectable man at war with gangsters who are not there. Danny calls the bluff; the Widow knew first.
- Tom's test: asking. He must take from the poorest people in the Hook.
- Climax: the Friday round driven in reverse against the clock; forty-one envelopes on the counter.
- Tone: the warmest and funniest; a con in the middle.

So C must not: be about a line repeated (A) or about money and a false reputation (B); must not put the turn on Sheila's deed (both A and B rest on what Sheila wrote or did, in different ways); must not make Cyril or Agar the main opponent. Left unused: the Widow as the main opponent (his people); the friendship as the plot itself; a midpoint that is a loss and not a find; a turn that sits on Ron; a climax in a car with the seats changed over.
