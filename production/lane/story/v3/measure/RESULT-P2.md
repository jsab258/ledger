## Blindness: mended in one half, not in the other

Mended: for the first time no reader could tie our page to this project. All twelve answered "none". Starting readers with no project attached works and is the method from now on.

Not mended: every reader still judged our page an unshipped plan (75 to 90% sure), and said why: they recognise the other nine and not this one; the page gives exact counts and minutes ("325 of the main line's 720 minutes"), states rules ("only a plain yes counts", "no screen offers an ending"), plants and pays off everything, and has none of the "sources differ" hedging of a page made from a real game. So the marks are read knowing it is a plan. That can lean either way; readers said the page looked "built to the ruler". Next round the summariser is told to write the page as a report of play (events, no minutes, no rules stated as rules), which removes the tell that can be removed. The first tell (a story they have never heard of) cannot be removed.

## What the readers said (their words, shortened)

X4 The Burial Club. For: "the people are vivid and specific: the doorman, the old mourner's marks out of ten, the bookkeeper's four words"; the doorman "someone they would miss", his death "prepared and lands"; "the ending reads back the player's whole record"; quiet comedy (the coffin "like a wardrobe on a staircase"). Against: "the weekly funeral loop is quiet, slow and repetitive"; "a slow, grief-heavy weekly cycle with small stakes"; "humour is sparse"; "the lead is withdrawn by design"; the ending "mostly withholds catharsis ('nobody stands behind the lead')"; "open-world players reward this less than writers would".

Y5 The Arrested Ship. For: "full of people a player would miss: the master's 'To nowhere, please. One hour'", the motorman calling turns as he dies "a devastating, earned moment", a lead with a creed that is tested. Against: "quiet, ledger-driven and dense", "almost no comedy", "the nightly fare cycle across 42 nights risks feeling padded", "players are likely to admire it more than love it".

Z4 The Knowledge. For: the bond "the kind players fall for", the driving lessons "real comedy", "You'll have to do the door, boss" a line players retell. Against: "dense with counts and systems", "the stakes are small and intricate", "slow and hard to follow", "niche rather than broad", no central threat.

Across all three, the same four things hold our pages under 8: a quiet repeating week, small stakes with no danger a player feels, too little comedy on the page, and a lead less vivid than the people round him. What the shipped pages above us are praised for in the same replies: a lead with a loud want that visibly changes, a bond that bickers and is funny, big prepared scenes that hit, and a last act with a reckoning.
