**A. Expected player marks**

**K: 9.0.** The lead is somebody: loyal, dying, and visibly changing what he does with the time he has left. The bonds with the leader, the younger gunman and the widow all break or mend on the page. The fall of the leader and the diagnosis give the story moments that hit, and the funny strands give relief. The epilogue is long and has a rail-like dive scene, but players forgive that when they love the people.

**M: 8.5.** The bickering friendship between the lead and his friend runs through the whole story, with fights, walk-outs and reconciliations, and ends in a possible romance. The revenge question is answered by a parents' vision that weighs the player's own deeds. Crude comedy and memorable side stories add a lot. Points come off for the twenty-minute servant stretch that throws you out for mistakes, and for a crowded middle.

**B: 8.0.** Three leads with clear, clashing wants and a buried betrayal between two of them. It is loud, funny and broad, which players enjoy. The repeating heist cycle and choices that change nothing hold it back, but the final choice pays off the triangle with three distinct endings.

**R: 7.5.** The lead has a strong want, and the cousin is a bond players would miss. Facing the traitor and the wedding-day death both land hard. The middle is a chain of employers that could largely be reordered, which players notice as padding.

**T: 7.0.** The divided-loyalty premise works, and the boss, his mother and the childhood friend are vivid. The wedding massacre and the friend's death hit hard. The middle repeats one turf-war pattern, the endgame becomes a run of vengeance set-pieces, and the single ending is somewhat tidied away by a recording.

**P: 6.5.** The couple's bond is the whole point of the story, and the closeness system makes it the player's concern. The page says the turn and last act are fixed and that the pair are forced to work apart for a long run. The supporting cast is thin, and the ending hangs on hidden meters rather than on a scene players will feel they earned.

**W: 6.0.** The war secret and the posed-murder cover-up are strong, and the twist is real. But the lead is stiff and hard to love, and the affair arrives without being built up. Control shifts to another character late on, the lead dies offscreen in a flood, and his betrayer gives the eulogy. Players tend to call that a deflating finish.

**H: 5.5.** The bond between the doorman and the lead is tender and carefully built, and there is gentle comedy in the lessons and the bollard. But the stakes are quiet and procedural: dock hiring counts, kerbs and ledgers. Most players would find it obscure, slow and hard to follow, and the endings are subdued.

**F: 5.0.** The handler bond and the clone backstory give it a spine, and the disguises are funny. But the lead is nearly blank and the middle is a chain of interchangeable contracts. The cabal plot is mostly delivered between missions, so players remember the levels rather than the people.

**N: 4.0.** There is no lead. Recruits are swappable and the story scenes are identical whoever plays them, which is exactly the "no one at its centre" complaint. The organiser twist has some punch, and the AI's quips help, but the leads give players nothing to hold on to.
