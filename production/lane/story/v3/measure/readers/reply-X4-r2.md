# Player ratings of story, predicted from ten summary pages

## A. Marks

**B: 8.2.** Three leads with clear and clashing wants: boredom and family respect, getting out of the street, chaos and a grudge. The betrayal in the snowy prologue sits under the whole story. The family scenes, the self-help cult chain and the broad satire give players plenty of comedy, and they forgive the noise because they enjoy the men. The heist-cycle middle and choices that change nothing hold it back, and two of the three endings punish the player, but the "all three live" ending lands.

**K: 9.4.** The most complete page for what players praise. A lead who changes in a way they can watch (the diagnosis turns him outward), a father figure falling apart over a long arc, and a large camp of people with their own wants. Several scenes hit hard: the partner's death, the illness, dying on the ridge. There is real comedy (the menagerie, "is this when I hop on?"), and the endings settle the central bond. The island stretch and the long epilogue are the only drags.

**R: 8.6.** A lead with a bitter voice, a buried want (the traitor) and a warm, foolish cousin whose bond runs from the dock to the wedding steps. The traitor reckoning and the wedding shooting are prepared and hit hard. The middle is a chain of employers and errands, which players note. The comic threads and the strong supporting cast carry it.

**P: 7.4.** The couple's want (an ordinary life) is sympathetic, and the bond meter puts the relationship at the centre. But the page gives few scenes that make either partner vivid. Its strongest beats, the airport betrayal and the bank robber's death, are fixed. The middle repeats job, escape and celebration, and the endings depend on hidden measures. Players will like the pair but may call the story thin for its length.

**M: 9.0.** The friendship between the commoner and the nobleman is shown, broken (the pillory) and mended across many scenes, with crude, comic bickering throughout. The revenge arc is paid off morally in a vision where the parents judge him. The foster-brother reveal, the siege and the cheeky side stories add more. The servant stretch where he can be thrown out is a rails complaint, but players forgive it.

**W: 7.2.** The war secret is a strong slow-burn turn, and the posed-murders case, where the player charges innocent men, hits hard. But the lead is stiff and hard to love. Partners rotate desk by desk, so no bond lasts. The affair arrives with little build-up, and control shifts to another man in the last act. Players tend to call that kind of ending alienating, even though it is bold.

**T: 8.2.** The undercover premise gives the lead a clean inner conflict. The bond with the district boss, his mother and the childhood friend is built and then destroyed at the wedding and in the alley, and both scenes hit. The villains are vivid and the revenge is satisfying, with a little comedy. The middle repeats turf jobs, and the ending is more tidy than moving.

**F: 6.5.** The killer is almost wordless and most targets live for one contract, so there is little to care about beyond the handler bond. That bond does pay off well in the train finale and the poison ruse. The disguise comedy is real fun, but players come for the levels, and the story mostly connects them.

**H: 7.8.** On the page it is rich in exactly what players praise. The doorman is someone they would miss, and the old mourner, the bookkeeper and the crew boss are distinct. The doorman's death is prepared and the turn is real, and quiet comedy runs through it (the coffin "like a wardrobe on a staircase"). Against that, it is a slow, grief-heavy weekly cycle with small stakes. Its ending mostly withholds catharsis ("nobody stands behind the lead"), which suits a devoted few rather than a broad audience.

**N: 5.0.** No lead is the players' biggest complaint written into the design. Recruits are interchangeable and their lines are the same whoever speaks them. The organiser's betrayal surprises, but it lands on no one the player was attached to. The best moments, the program's quips and the mother trapped in the house assistant, sit at the edges, and the final stretch is on rails.
