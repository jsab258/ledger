# Story marks: player ratings for ten story pages

## A. Marks

**K: 9.4**
The lead has a clear want, an arc that changes direction at the diagnosis, and a final act he spends getting others out. Several bonds run through the whole story and are broken or tested: the leader, the younger gunman, the widow and the principled gunman. The ending is earned and has a last image, and players forgive a slow open, a padded island stretch and a forced dive when they care this much about the people.

**M: 8.9**
The bickering, crude, loyal friendship between the lead and the young nobleman carries the page, and players love a bond like that. It has real comedy (the goats in the churchyard, the hatchet and the ear), choices that come back in the parents' verdict, a foster-brother reveal and a big siege climax. The twenty-minute servant stretch, where repeated mistakes throw the lead out, is the main thing players will complain about.

**R: 8.6**
The lead has dry humour and a buried want (the traitor), and the cousin is a warm, foolish man players will miss. The traitor scene gives the story a reckoning, and the wedding loss lands hard whichever branch the player chose. A long chain of employers makes the middle feel like a string of jobs, but the people in it carry it.

**B: 8.3**
Three leads with clashing wants, loud satire and plenty of comic set pieces, plus a grave-reveal turn that recasts the opening. The third ending gives players the payoff they want to see. Heist choices that change only money, and work forced on the leads by the agents, make the middle feel run for others.

**T: 8.1**
An undercover lead with a personal wound, a friend players come to care about, and a boss's mother who is unforgettable. The wedding massacre and the friend's death hit hard, and the superintendent's betrayal pays off. The middle repeats one gang-war pattern, and the ending is tidy rather than costly.

**H: 7.6**
The page is full of people only they could be: the master asking leave to board, the doorman logging in docker's hours, the bookkeeper's "As directed". The motorman calling turns as he dies is a strong hard moment, and the endings settle the bonds. But it is quiet, sombre, has almost no comedy, and runs a nightly cycle over seven of twelve hours. Players are likely to admire it more than love it, and many will find it hard to follow.

**W: 7.2**
Gripping cases and a dark war secret, but the lead is stiff and kept at a distance. Choices change only ratings, and the long case loop repeats. Late on, the story switches the player to another man, kills the lead off, and ends on a funeral where nothing the player did matters. Players tend to feel cheated by that.

**F: 6.5**
Disguises and comic kills are fun, and the handler bond gives the story a spine. But the lead is nearly wordless and the plot runs in the background between levels. The circle's members are names more than people, so players remember the levels rather than the story.

**P: 6.2**
The couple's wish for an ordinary life is a good hook. On the page, though, the people around them are thin, the turn is "all fixed", the endings come from hidden meters, and the middle is a repeating cycle of job, escape and celebration. Players are likely to call it forgettable apart from the leads.

**N: 4.4**
There is no lead, and story lines are voiced by whoever the player picked, which is exactly the "no one at its centre" complaint. The organiser twist is undercut by a mostly faceless cast, and the ending is fixed and bureaucratic. The program is the only real character.

## E. Unshipped pages and ties to known projects

**H is most likely an unshipped plan or draft (about 80% sure).** My reasons:
- I don't recognise it, unlike every other page.
- It reads like a design document rather than a summary of a released game. It gives exact counts ("forty-two of forty-seven nights", "about seven of twelve hours", "twenty-five optional side stories, five from the debt book") and describes what a player "may" do in a way that reads as planned branching.
- It looks built to pass the ruler almost item by item. There is a labelled "comic scene" and a labelled "grave one". Things are planted and paid off (the tins, the nozzles, the hook on the nail, the bell). The Sunday answer is "quoted back to him twice". There is an accusation that can come early, choices that come back, and three endings that each settle the central bonds.
- It has a draft-like seam: the last act opens "in week five" after the middle has already reached week six.

**P could also be unshipped or only just shipped (about 35% that it is a pre-release or projected summary).** If it is the game I named, that game was due in late 2026, and a page built from "the sources" this early may rest on pre-release material.
