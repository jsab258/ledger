## A. Expected player marks

**F: 5.5.** The lead is deliberately blank, and four fifths of the story is a chain of self-contained contracts while the plot "advance[s] in the background". That is the "no one at the centre" and "string of jobs" complaint. The handler bond and the late turns (the shared laboratory, the train, the serum choice) give it a spine, and the disguise comedy will be enjoyed, but players will mostly remember places rather than people.

**N: 4.2.** There is no fixed lead and the recruits are interchangeable by design. That is the biggest single player complaint. The organiser twist reframes the opening, but the page gives no bond to protect, and the opening and finale are both on rails. The dark side strands (the organ theft, the mother in the house assistant) will be remembered, but there is little humour beyond the program.

**W: 6.4.** The lead is specific, with a hidden war past and a gut-punch homicide reckoning. But he is stiff, the affair arrives with little build, and the last act often hands control to another man before killing the lead off. The cases repeat on a pattern, and the radio crimes have no thread. Players will respect it more than love it.

**P: 7.2.** A couple who want an ordinary life is an instantly warm centre, and the closeness meter puts the bond at the heart of play. Comedy is plentiful. Against that, the middle is long and repeats "a job, an escape, a celebration". The couple are forced apart for a long run, the turn and arrests are "fixed", and the ending hinges on hidden meters, which some will feel is arbitrary.

**K: 9.3.** It hits every top player item. The lead visibly changes after a terminal diagnosis, and the leader's slow decay plays out across many scenes. The camp has a large, distinct cast with their own wants. Moments hit hard (the partner's death, the veteran's horse, the ridge), the humour lands (the fake menagerie, "is this when I hop on?"), and the ending follows from who he has become. The long epilogue and the island stretch are the only drags.

**R: 8.3.** The lead is sharply drawn: bitter, funny, and trapped by the one thing he is good at. The cousin is a bond players will miss, and the traitor scene is a real reckoning. The employer-chain middle is reorderable and repetitive, but the comic support and the deal-or-revenge loss at the wedding make the ending sting.

**B: 8.1.** Three loud, distinct leads with clashing wants and a buried betrayal between two of them give real drive. It is broad, funny satire, which players do not mark down. The heist cycle repeats, and some forced agency work feels like errands. The third ending, where all three survive "still friends, awkwardly", is very satisfying.

**T: 7.6.** The undercover premise creates constant personal stakes. The district boss and his mother, the friend, and the sister backstory make it personal, and the wedding massacre and the friend's death hit hard. The middle is a repetitive turf war, and the friend is somewhat underused. The ending is tidy and lands quietly.

**M: 9.0.** The lead and his friend bicker, joke, split and mend across the whole story, which is exactly the bond players praise. The lead has a clear revenge want that the ending weighs morally, and the comedy (the pillory, the goats, the cup and hatchet) is concrete. The long servant stretch and the throw-out failure state will draw some "rails" grumbling.

**H: 6.8.** The doorman bond is unusual and touching, the people are vividly specific, and there is real humour (the bollard lesson, the gulls joke). The page, however, is dense and procedural. The stakes are small and intricate, the gate-hut accusation turn may confuse, and players who want momentum may find it slow and hard to follow. It could be loved by some and bounced off by many.
