## A. Expected player marks

**K: 9.5.** It has the strongest centre of the ten. The lead has a clear want, loyalty pulling against decency, and the terminal diagnosis changes what he does. The gang is a large cast players will miss, and the leader decaying from father figure to betrayer is a bond that breaks across many scenes. The deaths of the partner and the veteran, the dying-alone ending and the comic menagerie and "do I hop on?" beats give players everything they praise. The only likely complaint is the long epilogue and the island stretch.

**M: 9.0.** The lead wants revenge, his parents' killer and the stolen axe, and the ending measures whether revenge made him someone his parents would know. The bickering, crude friendship with the hot-headed nobleman is a bond players love. It is broken in the pillory, mended, and can even become a romance. Humour is plentiful (the goats in the churchyard, the hatchet and the ear). The twenty-minute servant stretch, where repeated mistakes get the lead thrown out, will draw some "on rails" grumbles.

**R: 8.6.** It has a lead who is somebody: dry, bitter, hunting a traitor, and failing at "I don't want to kill any more." The warm, boastful cousin is a bond players will feel. The final split that kills either the cousin or the sister hits hard, and the traitor reckoning is anticlimactic in a way that suits the theme. The chain of employers makes the middle feel like a string of jobs, which will cost it a little.

**T: 8.4.** The divided loyalty of an undercover cop is easy to grasp and well personified. The district boss, his mother and the childhood friend are people players will grieve. The wedding massacre and the friend's death land on the lead directly, and the revenge payoffs are visceral. The middle is a repetitive turf-war loop and the dates are thin, but players forgive that when they like the people.

**B: 8.2.** It has three loud, funny, distinct leads, and players love the broad satire. The dynamic between the robber and the partner, built on a buried betrayal, is compelling. The heist structure gives satisfying set pieces and a crowd-pleasing "everyone lives" ending. The forced agency work, a padded middle, and a young lead whose want is thinner than the others' keep it below the top tier.

**H: 7.6.** The page shows players exactly what they praise. There is a lead with a fixed creed ("It's owed") that is tested and redeemed, and a nightly fare with the master that is a bond with real tenderness. Four distinct older people have crossing wants, and the motorman's death on the hospital drive is a devastating, earned moment. The risk is scale and tempo. A quiet, counting, seven-week story about a debt and a ship may feel slow and small to an open-world audience, and its finest effects are ledger-level subtleties that players may not notice.

**W: 7.3.** The case-by-case structure and the face-reading interviews are memorable, and the war secret is a strong late reveal. The lead is held at arm's length, though, being "stiff with people." The affair arrives with little build-up. The switch to another protagonist and the lead's offstage drowning are exactly the "rushed ending / lost the lead" complaints players voice. The procedural middle also repeats.

**P: 6.8.** The couple who want an ordinary life is a likeable core, and their closeness is tracked as a system. The page, though, gives few concrete scenes of the bond itself, and the middle is long, repetitive robbery cycles with an extended stretch apart. The last act is fixed ("the player cannot change...") and handed off to a late smuggler and senator, an antagonist players barely meet. The ending depends on hidden variables rather than on what the couple have become.

**F: 6.3.** The handler bond and the cloning backstory give a skeleton, and the train finale with the self-injection ending is striking. The lead is almost wordless, though, and the plot advances mostly in short scenes between self-contained contracts. Players typically describe a story like this as background to the sandbox. The disguise humour helps, but few will say they cared.

**N: 4.8.** There is no lead. Recruits are interchangeable, and the page says story scenes are the same whoever plays them, which is the "nobody at the centre" complaint at its purest. The organiser twist has some punch and the AI side story is the strongest thread. Even so, the middle is a repeating district cycle, and the opening and finale are both explicitly no-choice stretches.
