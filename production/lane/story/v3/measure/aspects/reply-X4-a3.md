# Section A: Aspect scores

**A1 The lead**
SCORES A1: P=6 N=2 R=9 M=9 H=6 B=8 W=6 F=5 K=10 T=8
BEST A1: K
The lead has a stated want, loyalty to a failing leader. A personal cost arrives mid-story and turns that want into helping others get out. His decency is partly in the player's hands, and the story tracks it to the end, so who he becomes is visible in what he does.

**A2 The people around him**
SCORES A2: P=5 N=5 R=8 M=8 H=8 B=8 W=6 F=6 K=9 T=7
BEST A2: K
A large cast each has a want of their own: the planner who can argue back, the recruit hungry for favour, the runaway with a family, the widow who becomes capable. Each is told apart by what they do in camp and on jobs, and several change sides or leave over the story.

**A3 The central bond**
SCORES A3: P=7 N=2 R=9 M=9 H=7 B=6 W=3 F=6 K=8 T=7
BEST A3: M
One relationship runs from the first scene to the last. It is shown in banter, rescue and betrayal (one walks out after the pillory), then mended, and it is still being talked through in the final conversation. A test: the bond must break and repair on screen at least once.

**A4 The opponent**
SCORES A4: P=4 N=5 R=7 M=6 H=6 B=6 W=5 F=7 K=8 T=7
BEST A4: K
The antagonist is planted early as a rival for the leader's favour and grows in presence. He is exposed through a clue the lead carries back, and he is faced in person in both endings. The leader himself is a second, slower-turning opponent.

**A5 Writing and dialogue**

The page shows lines that carry weight in very few words ("I'm not him", "Forty walked. Five.", "Mind his height."). Small details are planted and paid off: returned coins, the roof sign, the doorman's count. A test: each recurring line or object must return changed.

**A6 Moments that hit hard**

BEST A6: K
Losses are prepared and things are different after each. A mentor's death unravels the leader. A fatal diagnosis changes what every later job is for. The ending lands on a death the player has been walking toward.

Comedy is built into the main line as well as the side stories: the fountain, the cult-like self-help chain, the digger through the house. The satire keeps one tone even through its darkest scene.

**A8 The turn**

BEST A8: W
Two reveals land together, a public disgrace and a hidden war atrocity. Together they recast every earlier scene in which the lead was called a hero, and the medal itself. A test: the turn must make the player reread the lead, not just the plot.

**A9 The ending**
SCORES A9: P=6 N=4 R=8 M=8 H=8 B=8 W=5 F=7 K=9 T=7
BEST A9: K
The ending follows from who the lead has become: the player's last choice is whether to help the friend or go for the money. The bond with the gang and the leader is settled face to face. The last image (heron or vulture) lands either way.

BEST A10: B
The middle repeats a cycle (plan, set-up, heist, payout), but each heist is larger than the last and builds visibly toward one named final job. Family and enemy threads are closed in the last act rather than dropped.

The core physical act, keeping step and holding the coffin level, is the story's measure of the lead. How well he does it feeds the marks, the households and who comes at the end. A test: the main play verb must be the thing the story is about.

Optional strangers get arcs with turns of their own that return across visits: the widow's lessons ending in a letter, the inventor's death and the bird on the church roof, a dying veteran's gift. A test: a side story must have a second visit that changes the first.

The period's politics shape every quest: a usurper, an absent king, a persecuted minority quarter, mines and a mint. Daily life, such as having to wash before people will talk to you, is part of play.
