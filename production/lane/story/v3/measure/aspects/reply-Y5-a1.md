## A. Scores by aspect

**SCORES A1:** W=7 H=8 M=7 K=9 B=6 P=6 R=8 T=7 N=3 F=5
**BEST A1:** K
The lead has a stated want, and a blow arrives that he cannot fix. That blow changes what he works for, while the same hands keep doing the same work. Test: does a fixed event force the lead to choose a different use of himself, and is that choice what the player plays out?

**SCORES A2:** W=6 H=8 M=6 K=8 B=7 P=5 R=8 T=7 N=5 F=5
**BEST A2:** H, R
Every named person has a want that is not the lead's and a habit of action that marks them out (logging in docker's hours, chalking a slate, asking leave to board). Several of those wants conflict with each other, not only with the lead. Test: can each supporting person be identified by what they do, with their name hidden?

**SCORES A3:** W=4 H=8 M=7 K=8 B=6 P=7 R=8 T=6 N=2 F=6
**BEST A3:** R, K
One relationship is present from the first scene to the last. It is strained by the lead's own work and then ended or nearly ended, and in R's case the player's choice decides which bond survives. Test: is the bond tested at least twice, and does its fate depend on something the lead did?

**SCORES A4:** W=5 H=7 M=5 K=7 B=5 P=4 R=6 T=7 N=6 F=6
**BEST A4:** H, T
The opponent's warmth or authority is real and present early. She wants something coherent and is met face to face at the climax. She is not killed off stage. Test: does the opponent appear in person in act one and in the final confrontation, with a want the player can state?

**SCORES A5:** W=6 H=9 M=5 K=7 B=6 P=4 R=7 T=6 N=5 F=6
**BEST A5:** H
Short lines are planted early and paid back changed: "It's owed" returns as "Not to me", and the misspelt names return as a name spelt letter by letter. Test: does each key line return at least once with new meaning?

**SCORES A6:** W=7 H=8 M=6 K=9 B=6 P=6 R=8 T=8 N=6 F=6
**BEST A6:** K
The losses are prepared over many hours, and each one changes what follows: the leader comes apart, the diagnosis redirects the lead, the death on the ridge closes the story. Test: after each grave scene, does the story behave differently than before?

**SCORES A7:** W=3 H=6 M=7 K=6 B=8 P=6 R=7 T=5 N=6 F=7
**BEST A7:** B
The comedy is sustained satire built into whole optional chains and family scenes, and it sits beside grave material without breaking the tone. Test: is humour a recurring thread with its own payoffs, not isolated gags?

**SCORES A8:** W=7 H=7 M=6 K=6 B=6 P=4 R=6 T=7 N=8 F=7
**BEST A8:** N
The guide who has spoken in the player's ear all game is revealed as the attacker from the opening. The reveal recasts every earlier mission as work done for her. Test: does the turn change the meaning of what the player already did, not just what comes next?

**SCORES A9:** W=5 H=8 M=6 K=9 B=7 P=6 R=7 T=7 N=4 F=7
**BEST A9:** K
The ending follows from who the lead has become: he spends himself to free the younger man. It settles the father-figure bond with an abandonment, and gives a quiet final image. Test: does the last act require the lead to act out his change, and does it close the central bond?

**SCORES A10:** W=4 H=8 M=5 K=6 B=6 P=4 R=5 T=6 N=4 F=6
**BEST A10:** H
A recurring nightly scene shows steady decline (lights out, cranes falling), so the middle escalates instead of repeating. Every thread is called back by the end. Test: does each week change the state of the world, and does every named person get a closing beat?

**SCORES A11:** W=6 H=9 M=8 K=6 B=7 P=6 R=5 T=7 N=7 F=9
**BEST A11:** H
The story is kept in the player's hands. The books, the slate and the tins are records the player writes, and they become the evidence and the ending conditions. Test: are story facts produced by player actions and then read back as plot?

**SCORES A12:** W=1 H=9 M=7 K=5 B=4 P=6 R=6 T=1 N=3 F=4
**BEST A12:** H
Choices from the first week are quoted back, alter later scenes (the empty nail, who holds the gangway) and decide among distinct endings. Test: can a playtester name three early choices that visibly return in the finale?

**SCORES A13:** W=3 H=8 M=8 K=8 B=7 P=4 R=8 T=6 N=4 F=6
**BEST A13:** H, M
Side stories carry their own turn or choice (reason vs. old custom; a pawned hook), and some feed back into the main line. Test: does each side story have a decision or reveal, and does at least one alter the main story?

**SCORES A14:** W=8 H=9 M=8 K=8 B=7 P=6 R=8 T=7 N=6 F=5
**BEST A14:** H
The period's economic change (deregulated docks, severance sums, labour pen) is the engine of the plot, with exact prices and customs. Test: would the story collapse if moved to another time and place?

**SCORES A15:** W=8 H=8 M=4 K=1 B=1 P=2 R=2 T=4 N=3 F=5
**BEST A15:** W
Clue-gathering and questioning are the core verb of most of the game, and wrong accusations cost something. Test: must the player use collected evidence to challenge a claim, with the reasoning their own?
