In a large city on the warm coast of an invented southern republic, three criminals are pushed together towards one enormous job on a store of gold. There are three leads: a retired bank robber living under a false name, bored and wanting respect from a family that gives him none; a young repo man wanting out of street crime and into bigger money; and the robber's violent old partner, a desert drug maker wanting chaos and nursing a grudge. Around them stand a housebound planner, two government agents who force jobs on them, and a billionaire who uses them and does not pay. The player's hands switch between the three almost at will, drive, shoot and fly through a cycle of heists planned, set up and pulled, crew and approach changing only money and difficulty. The turn comes when the old partner finds the crewman he thought jailed lying in the robber's grave: the robber bought his new life from an agent at that man's cost. The two draw guns, then make an uneasy peace under attack. The gold job succeeds, and the billionaire and an agent each order the young repo man to kill one of the others. His permanent choice decides three endings and who is lost for good: he kills the old partner, he kills the robber, or he refuses, and the three kill their enemies, drop the billionaire into the harbour and walk away rich.
