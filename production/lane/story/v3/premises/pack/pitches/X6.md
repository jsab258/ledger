In the old harbour quarter of a northern port, the strike that followed the harbour labour register's abolition failed in three weeks, and nobody rides the radio-taxi office's cabs, which in its second week carried managers through the picket to unload a foreign ship. The dead owner's uncollectable debts are week-one loans to strikers, and nobody pays a scab. The lead, a stranger and his nephew, asks what the quarter owes the dead man, and he the quarter for something before his time. The doorman is spat at for staying; the bookkeeper minds silent phones; the dead owner's estranged daughter, a night nurse, still rides home in the cab; her man, a strike leader called Strike Anywhere, stands in the pen as a leftover. Only outsiders ride, the landowner's surveyors and bussed casual men, each fare a side taken. The player's hands collect hard from men with cheques. In week four, the office burns with the bookkeeper inside. The strike leader had promised it; the lead runs him down, laming him. The fire was the young crew boss's, for the rank; the daughter never speaks to the lead again. That month's dispatch book, opened by the bookkeeper, shows the managers' money all lent to the strikers: the strike ate scab money. The quarter, remembering whom he drove and squeezed, answers at the gate's last call, where he reads out the page and cancels the book, or burns it and collects.
