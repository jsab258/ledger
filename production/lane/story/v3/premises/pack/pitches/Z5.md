In a northern port in 1991, a dead radio-taxi owner has left his office not to his daughter but to his stranger nephew, the lead. The daughter, a night nurse, rides his cab home each dawn past the pen, never saying why she was cut out, and he asks what he owes her. The doorman says his uncle knew his mind; the bookkeeper typed the will and keeps silent; the gate boss says the arrangement stands; she will see him on the fourteenth of December. The player's hands run what his uncle ran, night runs off the last ships, a harbour office break-in, harder fights each week, and money comes too fast. The office is twice turned over, a solicitor writes, a lock changes. Sure the daughter wants it back, the lead spreads cab talk that she steals from the ward's cupboard, and she is struck off. She had been trying to make him leave. His uncle borrowed thirty-five stevedores' severance cheques from the gate boss to keep the men working a year; the sum falls on the heir on the fourteenth, hence a nephew nobody would miss. The lead holds the office against the gate boss, and the jobless daughter takes the morning train and never writes. What the player earned, from whom, and who saw decides whether the men stand with him. He pays with the office, or the doorman, or becomes the gate boss's man.
