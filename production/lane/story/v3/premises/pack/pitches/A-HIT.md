A secret bureau sells killings to the very rich from no fixed country, and over contracts in invented cities someone outside it starts steering names onto its list, until the bureau itself ends. The lead is a cloned assassin in late middle age, conditioned in a mountain laboratory and missing most of his memory, who kills only the paid target and is torn between obedience and a wish to know what he owes those who made him. His handler, a cool woman who directs every job by radio, is his one bond; the hidden client, a former soldier with a private army, shared the laboratory with him and once tried to escape with him; a soft-spoken go-between speaks for a secret circle of old money. The player's hands watch routines, steal uniforms, pick locks, poison food, rig accidents and hide bodies, choosing the route to a fixed target. At the turn the lead learns the bureau was tricked into the hidden client's work, then that the circle founded it, and the three set out to kill the circle's partners. The hidden client shoots himself rather than expose the lead, and the bureau falls when the lead publishes its contracts. The handler seems to betray him to the go-between, but it is a ruse. In a train's last carriage the player chooses: inject the go-between and work freelance with the handler, or inject himself and wake empty in a white cell.
