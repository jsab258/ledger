In a northern port in 1991, eleven stevedoring firms have failed and casuals and managers work the ships, so last year's theft is a job and the old job is theft. The lead, a stranger left his dead uncle's radio-taxi office, meets this when the doorman's old gang, six tradeless stevedores, ask for midnight cabs to work the ships themselves by the old rules and keep what they discharge. He wants an equal's share, enough to buy the office's freehold, and weighs who is owed: the gang, their dead, or the dead man whose cabs carry it. The doorman runs the hatch; the bookkeeper turns cargo into clean fares; a stevedore's son denied registration rides up front. The player's hands break sheds, drive loads through a watching town, fight the gate boss's men and take cars off a foreign ship, the men talking in the cab at four. Finding the detective at a shed, the lead decides a man always gone before midnight informed and beats him past working. He was the watchman, leaving doors unlocked; the detective had read the dispatch book, written in the player's hand. The lead takes one more ship, unlit, and the son falls down the hold and dies. What the player wrote truly, and who saw his cab, decide what she can prove. She demands the book: the true one convicts the lead and clears the gang, the false one the reverse.
