In the old harbour quarter of a northern port, the harbour labour register was abolished by law two summers before; half the quarter's crimes became somebody's job, the rest arrested before the land sale. In week one the detective reads the radio-taxi office's dispatch book standing, and that night the first fare is lifted at his kerb. The lead, a stranger and the dead owner's nephew, weighs what he owes a bargain he never made. The bookkeeper writes each booking first, the doorman holds the door, the young hustler drives car two, and the detective comes Mondays. The player's hands carry men, dock parcels, the young crew boss's getaway and the gate boss's night run, breaking in and fighting. A fare's words in the back, and where he alights, are the deepest talk and a line in ink. Arrests follow the cab, growing weekly, until the firms want a name. Seen in the detective's car, the young hustler is beaten behind the rank and handed to the go-between. Nobody talks; the uncle's bargain was only an honest book for the detective, and the chosen fares of weeks two and three were the evidence. The lead has the bookkeeper write one false line, her first; the detective proves it, and she is charged as keeper, gone for good. On the last night the gate boss and young crew boss both book cars; he writes that page, true or blank.
