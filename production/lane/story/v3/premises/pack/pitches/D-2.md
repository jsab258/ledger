In a ruined harbour district of an invented city, decades after a failed revolution, foreign powers administer the streets, a dock strike is coming to a head, and a grey nothing at the world's edges is slowly eating it. The lead, a police detective, wakes in a cheap hotel after a week of self-destruction that has erased his name, past and badge, and owes the district one thing: a mercenary's body hanging from a tree behind the hotel. Those who matter are his calm, exact partner from another precinct; the union boss who runs the docks; a woman at the hotel who knows more than she says; and the absent fiancée whose loss broke him. The player's hands mostly talk: they search scenes, argue with his warring inner voices, whose checks decide each exchange, and choose what he believes, from revolution to the free market. The turn comes when the dead man's armed comrades hold a tribunal against the union men, and a shootout leaves several dead and may leave the partner wounded. The truth lies across the bay: from an island ruin, an old deserter of the revolution, hidden there for half a century, shot the mercenary out of envy. The fiancée does not come back, and the dead stay dead. When colleagues arrive by boat the ending is fixed, but their verdict on him, and whether he is kept on, follows how he worked the case.
