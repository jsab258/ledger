In a crowded harbour city of an invented southern republic around 2010, police share the streets with an old sworn brotherhood of oaths, ranks and district bosses, whose ailing chairman's hold is ending as a syndicate from across the border pushes in. The lead, a police officer who left as a boy and whose sister died after a brotherhood man put her on heroin, is sent home undercover and must stay a policeman while living as a sworn brother. His handler warns he is getting too close; the superintendent wants results; a childhood friend brings him in and later wants out; a district boss treats him as a brother. The player's hands fight unarmed with counters and throws, chase over rooftops, drive and leap between vehicles, shoot, pick locks and plant bugs, scored by police and gang alike. At the turn, gunmen dressed as the foreign syndicate kill the district boss and his bride at their wedding and wound the chairman, who makes the lead boss in the groom's place. The lead learns a rival boss arranged it, hands the man who ruined his sister to the groom's mother, who kills him, and finds his friend murdered. The friend, the boss and the chairman are gone for good. The one ending is fixed: the lead kills the rival boss, exposes the superintendent's murder of the chairman and returns to the police, and the brotherhood's new chairman lets him live.
