In a northern port in 1991, the last ship cannot leave: a bankrupt stevedoring firm owed the dead owner of a radio-taxi office, whose lawyer had a foreign freighter arrested for the debt, the writ taped to her mast. The lead, a stranger and the nephew, meets the dock's end as nine unpaid seamen, now in law his problem. He wants the debt paid, then her to stay, since while she lies there the berth is a berth and his, and he owes strangers who cannot go home until he signs. The master books a nightly cab to nowhere, for an hour off his ship; the doorman becomes her watchman, happy for the first time in two years; the bookkeeper costs the ship by the day. The player's hands carry stores aboard, fight off the young crew boss's men, who want her burnt, and burgle the landowner's office for the berth plans; fares come in tinned fish, and the ship keeps home time, so the night fare is always tomorrow's. When brass and cable vanish, the lead blames the doorman's old workmates and breaks a man's arm. The crew sold their ship to eat. Refusing the landowner's settlement, the lead sees the owners abandon her, the crew flown home and the master deported, his log listing the player's every kindness and charge. The doorman's old gang discharge her to pay the debt, or she is towed for scrap, cargo aboard.
