In the old harbour quarter of a northern port, three firms divide dock land sold by the hectare. The go-between brings the dead owner's share, paid by all three to keep his forty men off the quay. His book of uncollectable debts is the roster, forty nicknames the bookkeeper invented from stevedores gone or dead. For eleven years his radio-taxi office ran no crime, only rumour keeping the peace. Nobody tells the lead, a stranger and his nephew, left to weigh his debt to a lie that fed three people. The bookkeeper wrote it, the doorman is its one real fist, the go-between its bagman, and the young hustler wants in. Collecting, the player's hands beat a real man, a stevedore called the Drizzle, into paying from his severance. Once the bookkeeper tells, the player props up the bluff: borrowed faces in the cab, lit empty warehouses, a public fight the doorman must win. The doorman admits hitting nobody in six years. The young crew boss tests harder weekly until the lead does one real thing, burning his car lot. Sent to a meeting to stand for the forty, the young hustler is killed. The firms' last-night muster calls all forty to the quay at six. Who walks down comes from the record: fares chosen, the Drizzle repaid or not. The quay fills or stays empty; the lead becomes what his uncle only pretended to be, or says so aloud.
