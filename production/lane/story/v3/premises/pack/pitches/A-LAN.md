In 1950, a few years after a long overseas war, a hot southern port and film town swells with returned soldiers, new money and a police force that takes from both, a land scheme growing behind its boom. The lead, a former infantry lieutenant with a gallantry medal, rises through the police desk by desk, wanting to prove an honest man can rise in a crooked force while hiding what he did in the war. Partners change with each desk; around him are a narcotics sergeant on a developer's payroll, a refugee singer with whom he has an affair, and the old company sergeant who disliked him, now an insurance investigator. The player's hands search crime scenes, watch faces in interviews and accept, press or accuse with evidence, drive, chase and fight, a worse case giving only a weaker charge. He charges innocent men for a killer whose name is then buried. At the turn his partner leaks the affair, and the lead loses rank, wife and standing, while flashbacks show he had sheltering civilians in a cave burned and the survivors shot. He and the investigator expose the developer's fund, which burns houses to buy land cheaply for a motorway, and in flooded storm channels the lead saves the singer and the investigator and drowns. There is one ending, unchanged by anything the player does: at his funeral, the sergeant who exposed him gives the eulogy.
