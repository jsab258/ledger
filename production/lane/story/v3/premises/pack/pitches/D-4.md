In a sun-baked desert city of an invented continental republic, an old boom town gone to sprawl is split between a private military company, a car-stealing gang and anarchist club kids, and what is ending for its young people is any honest way to pay the rent. The lead, a graduate deep in student debt whose face and voice the player makes, is hired by the private army and fired after a botched job, and wants money, standing and a life of their own with the friends sharing the flat. Three flatmates matter: a getaway driver tied to the car gang, a disc jockey and cook tied to the club kids, and a business student who draws up plans. Together they found a gang in an abandoned church. The player's hands shoot, drive, glide in a wingsuit, steal cars and buy empty lots for crooked businesses that bring income, while the gang takes ground from all three rivals. At the turn the friends' old ties catch up with them: they quarrel over what the gang has made of them, one is taken, and the others get him back. Little is lost for good: no one in the crew dies, and what goes is the three factions' leaders and the friends' former lives. The crew defeats the private army in a final assault, and there is one ending, unchanged by the player's choices: the four friends run the city together.
