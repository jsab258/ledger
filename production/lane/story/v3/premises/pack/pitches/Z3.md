In the old harbour quarter of a northern port, the docks are cleaned for sale and the detective has seven weeks to clear it. Meeting the lead, a stranger and the dead owner's nephew, off the ferry, she knows what is in his suitcase; the player never does. Her price is one name every Monday from the radio-taxi office's dispatch book. What does he owe those who took him in, when the law wants them? The doorman, four weeks a picket, says you never scab out the man next door. The bookkeeper notices a turned page. The young hustler sells radios, wanting a job. The player's hands earn names driving the gate boss's night runs, breaking in with the young crew boss's men and fighting; men talk in the cab; each Monday he gives one, each bigger. In week four the back of the office burns, and the doorman's hands with it. Blaming a stevedore known for leaving before midnight, the lead hands him over, adding a lie; he goes down. He leaves early to nurse a dying brother; the fire was the gate boss's, to find the informer. He gives a truer name; the detective wants the last. Whoever he names is gone for good; if he names nobody, the doorman is told. Fares he was kind to witness for or against him. He writes his own name in the book, or the doorman's or the bookkeeper's.
