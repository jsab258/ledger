In a northern port's old harbour quarter, a dock bonded store burned last year, in the winter the ambulance crews stopped work, with a radio-taxi cab on the row; now the last registered stevedores sign for cheques and the plot is for sale. The lead, a stranger and the dead owner's nephew, meets it in last year's dispatch book: car two, out and back, eleven miles, no name. What does he owe a night he missed? The doorman, who cannot drive, learns beside him on the empty dock road, the player holding the handbrake. The bookkeeper makes him write one true line a night. A burnt stevedore in grey gloves is kept by a sick club the office secretly funds; the player's hands do a growing weekly job to keep it paid. In the car the doorman almost tells. Evidence says the doorman drove off and left the burnt man; the lead accuses him and puts him out on the road. On foot, the doorman carried the man out; the bookkeeper drove off, turning back at a bollard. The lead drives one last job; it goes wrong with the doorman inside, and the player alone decides whether to go and how far before turning. The distance jails or cripples the doorman; the burnt man dies regardless. The detective reads out the dial's miles and every false line. The bookkeeper signs for her night only if the lead signs for his.
