In a northern port in 1991, the docks empty, the cheques are spent and the young crew boss takes the cab rank. The quarter says a radio-taxi owner did not just die: one of the crew boss's collectors was seen leaving his yard that morning. The lead, the stranger nephew, hears it from four sure fares in week one and asks what he owes an unmet uncle. The doorman found the body and says leave it; the bookkeeper says nothing; the collector's wife, a cleaner, rides home at six each morning. The player's hands fight weekly for the rank until he has the collector alone on the fish quay; how far he goes is his: the collector dies or never works again. At two in the morning, engine off, the doorman tells it. The collector was the only debtor in nine years to come and pay; he left forty crowns on the desk and the owner alive. The doorman took the envelope from beside the body for the widows' tin, so could not say who had come. Seven people saw some of it; each wants something, the detective is asking, and the lead pays in worsening jobs. The wife still books the six o'clock. The lead decides whether the doorman owns up, which would finish him here for robbing the dead. He writes that morning's line in the dispatch book, and drives the wife home or lets her walk.
