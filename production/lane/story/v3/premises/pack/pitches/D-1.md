Late in the eighties, the capital of an invented eastern island nation is swollen by a property boom, and in its neon pleasure district one small empty lot, the last piece of a great redevelopment, is worth killing for. The lead is two men, played in alternating chapters: a young syndicate enforcer framed for a murder on that lot, who leaves the syndicate to clear his name and shield the jailed patriarch who raised him; and a one-eyed former lieutenant, exiled to run a dance hall in a southern city after taking a fall for his boss, who owes that boss and wants back in. Those who matter are a blind woman who unknowingly owns the lot, a property broker who helps the enforcer and proves to be her brother, and three rival lieutenants racing for the lot. The player's hands brawl in switchable styles, take dozens of strangers' side stories, and run a business for each man: buying up shops, or making the dance hall the city's best. The turn comes when the exile, sent to kill the woman, protects her instead, and both men's roads meet over her. Her brother dies of a beating; the woman regains her sight but never learns the exile's face. The ending is fixed: the lieutenants fall, the lot passes to the syndicate's chairman, the enforcer stays in the life, and the exile returns to his boss behind a wild new manner.
