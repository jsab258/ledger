In a northern port in 1991, the last registered stevedores are paid to go, up to 35,000 crowns each at a rate closing in the new year, and one in five refuses. The lead, a stranger left his dead uncle's radio-taxi office, inherits them too: his uncle's debts are theirs, uncollectable until they sign. The landowner pays a fee per signature, which the lead needs for the mortgaged office bearing his name; what he owes the living is set against what they owe him. The doorman signed and is called a seller of his registration; the bookkeeper knows why each loan was made; a stevedore who never lifts a hand leads the holdouts, riding his cab each morning and paying in old coins since reissued smaller. The player's hands collect, forcing doors, taking a van, and in the cab the men say what the cheque would end. Believing the gate boss pays him to hold out, her smuggling needing a working dock, the lead reads his debt page aloud, and the men sign. He had borrowed to pay sick men, who now get nothing. The lead takes his signature too; he pays in full and leaves for good. How the player collected, and whether he took a widow's tin of old coins, is recited at the gate. He banks the fees and keeps an office in an emptied quarter, or burns the pages and drives for another.
