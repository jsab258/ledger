In a northern port in 1991, as the dock closes, the debts in a dead radio-taxi owner's book are uncollectable because of who owes them. For thirty years he lent to nobodies: a harbour clerk, a constable's wife, a young solicitor now the landowner, the gate boss's late husband, then a stevedore. Now they own what is left; the lead, the stranger nephew, meets the dock's end as a list of the powerful, a sum by each nickname, asking what he owes his uncle's patience. The doorman knows the names, the bookkeeper the debts, told only after, and the go-between carries answers. Each call is a job for the player's hands, a door, a yard, a safe, a fight on a ship, lifting the lead to the rank, the gate, the cold store and a seat at the dock's sale, while those he ruins confess in the cab. Losing the gate overnight, he decides the go-between sold him and breaks him in the yard. The go-between was obeying his uncle: never the last three pages. The last is the bookkeeper's: at twenty-two she owed his uncle an unpriced debt, and calling it gives the lead the whole quarter. Called, she pays in full and leaves by morning, book squared; if not, the gate boss retakes the gate and the doorman goes into the pen. His ruined sit at the sale; he buys the dock or closes the book unpaid.
