In a northern port in 1991, two summers after the harbour labour register was abolished, the docks hire by the day again, a foreman picking each dawn from a pen of men registered for thirty years. The lead, a stranger who has inherited his dead uncle's radio-taxi office, meets it because his one contract drives picked men to the berths. He wants the gate, the quarter's last employer, where the gate boss's people sell the tap on the shoulder, but once it is his, every man he picks pays the rent and every one he leaves is a leftover by his hand. The doorman, once in that pen, writes the list, hating it; the bookkeeper prices each man; the young hustler runs the names and wants a tap of his own. The player's hands drive the cabs, break her collectors and hold the gate in a fight, and a man in the back explains why tomorrow must be him. When cargo walks and the detective reads the dispatch book, the lead blames a leftover he long passed over and breaks his hands before the pen. The thief was the doorman's mercy, letting unpicked men carry a little out. Hitting the gate boss's yard, the lead leaves the hustler alone at the gate to be killed. Men picked most stand by him; those never picked talk to the detective. He keeps the call or hands the list to the pen.
