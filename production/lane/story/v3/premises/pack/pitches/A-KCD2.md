Late in the fourteenth century an invented kingdom of forested hills and walled towns is at war with itself: the rightful king is a prisoner, and his half-brother, the usurper, holds the land with foreign horsemen. The lead, a blacksmith's son of low birth whose village was burned, serves as a man-at-arms and wants three things: to win over a wavering castle lord, to kill the officer who murdered his mother and foster father, and to recover the axe his foster father forged. Those who matter are his friend, a hot-headed young nobleman; a bandit chief who is in truth a rebel captain; a spy who frees him from prison; and a broker in the quarter of a despised minority in the mint city. The player's hands ride, fight, talk, sneak, steal and spare, and must wash to be spoken to. The turn comes when, serving at the city council, he hears the usurper order the minority massacred; he leads the broker's people out, and the broker proves to be his foster brother. The group steals the usurper's coin from under the city and holds a hill fort through a siege, where the lead kills the officer or leaves him to die. There is one ending: the siege breaks, the usurper withdraws. A vision of his parents weighs his deeds and remorse; they bless him or leave him for good, and he takes the axe or settles down.
