In 1893, on the frontier of an invented southern republic, railways, wires, banks and federal police close in the open country, and a gang of outlaws is breaking apart while its leader promises one last score. The lead, the gang's enforcer, raised in it since boyhood, loves the leader like a father and wants to stay loyal, keep the others alive and become better than a debt collector. Those who matter are the leader, his older partner and planner, a loud, violent new recruit, and a younger gunman with a former lover and a son. The player's hands ride, hunt, collect debts, rob trains and fight from cover through four camps, while a measure of good name tracks his conduct. A harbour-city bank robbery fails, the partner is killed, a shipwreck strands the survivors on an island, and the leader, sure of an informer, grows cold, fixed on one more robbery. Then the lead learns he has an incurable blood disease, and turns to helping others escape. The partner, the camp and the leader's trust are lost for good; the recruit was the informer, and the leader sides with him. Cornered on a mountain, the lead helps the younger gunman escape or goes back for the money; that choice and his good name shape the end. He dies either way, alone on a ridge or shot by the recruit; an epilogue follows the younger gunman's new life.
