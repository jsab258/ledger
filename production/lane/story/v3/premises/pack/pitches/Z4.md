In the old harbour quarter of a northern port, the doorman's trade was abolished by law, and he means to drive a cab before the top cheque rate closes, though he cannot drive. The lead, a stranger and the dead owner's nephew, cannot find a street, and owes the doorman every door opened to him. Beside him, the doorman names every building by what it was and calls each turn a street late. The bookkeeper books his test for the seventh Friday and prices his collisions. The gate boss, who sells the gate's hiring, wants him for foreman. The player's hands do the radio-taxi office's work, the doorman knowing which yard, which wall, who is in. He talks during night lessons on the dock road. Then the doorman is seen hiring at the gate in a foreman's coat, and the gate boss waits at the lead's next job. Before the pen the lead brands him a seller of the book and gives his seat to the young hustler. The doorman was picking the dead owner's debtors, one a morning, so the uncollectable could pay. The lead does the big job alone; it goes bad, the doorman walks in for him, and the gate boss's men break his hands for good. Streets learned and men picked decide who stands at the gate on the last morning. On the seventh Friday the examiner waits; the lead drives him there anyway, or not.
