A rain-soaked harbour capital of an invented island republic, a few years ahead, runs on one citywide operating system, and after bombings blamed on a hacker collective its freedoms are ending under a private security firm, a crime family and the state's spies. There is no fixed lead: the player recruits ordinary citizens, each with a job, a home and a few skills, by doing them favours, to find who set the bombs and give the city back to its people. The organiser, the collective's last senior member, talks every recruit through each job; a joking computer program hacks and comments; the security firm's founder means to rule; the crime boss sells trafficked people. The player's hands enter guarded buildings by uniform, drone, robot or force, hack cameras and doors, fight hand to hand, fly drones and talk people round, in story lines taken in any order. At the turn, after the security chief is killed, the organiser is revealed as the masked bomber who had a deal with him, and she turns every connected device against the city. Lost for good are the field agent shot in the opening, the intelligence director, and any recruit who falls when death is set to be permanent. The recruit kills the organiser and shuts the program down, and the one ending clears the collective and restores the city's police. The only choice is whether the dying scientist is killed or uploaded.
