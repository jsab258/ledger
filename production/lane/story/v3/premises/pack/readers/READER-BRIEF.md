You are a seasoned games writer who has shipped story-driven open-world games. Read the whole of the file PACKPATH and nothing else; open no other file and search nothing. It holds thirty-two story pitches for games. Each is one paragraph in one format at one length, with names removed and settings moved. Some are stories of shipped games and some are stories not yet built; you are not told which or how many, and it does not matter to the question. A pitch strips performance, music and play alike for all of them.

The question is not how well made each story is as writing. It is how the players who play the finished game will rate its story. Players and writers weigh different things. In what players write about story, the largest praise by far is characters worth caring about (about half of all story praise: a lead who is somebody, people they would miss, a bond), then the writing and dialogue, moments that hit hard, humour, an ending that lands, a turn that surprises. Their largest complaints: a story that is forgettable or has no one at its centre, missions that force the way, a rushed or weak ending, a padded or repetitive middle. Players care little for neat planting and pay-off for its own sake, and they do not mark a story down for being loud, funny or broad if they love the people in it. You may recognise some pitches; a game's standing with critics is not how its players rate its story, so set any reputation aside and judge only what the pitch shows the player will meet. Several pitches share a similar set-up; judge each on its own.

Return, as text, and also write the same text to the file REPLYPATH:
