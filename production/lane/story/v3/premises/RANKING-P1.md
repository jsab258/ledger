## The blindness fix: failed, and why

The pack itself held: no reader found a British word, a name or the cab office's real trade in it. But all three readers said in their last line that the twenty looked like variants for LEDGER, and all three said how they knew: the project's standing instructions are loaded into every helper this session starts, whatever folder it runs from and whatever file it is given. Moving the working folder outside the repository did not remove them. All three also sorted every pitch correctly into shipped or unbuilt.

So: readers knew which twenty were under test. That does not touch the order inside the twenty (all twenty were equally known). It may lean the twenty's marks against the shipped games, either way. This cannot be mended from inside a session that carries the project's instructions; it needs readers started from a session with no project attached (an empty folder and no repository), or the advisor running the three readers himself. That is the one open method fault and it is named for the next run.

## What the readers said, across all three (for Phase 2)

1. What lifted a premise every time: one warm, funny, specific bond at the centre (the doorman who cannot drive beside a lead who cannot find a street; the stevedore who marks funerals out of ten; the master who books a cab to nowhere; the doorman who has hit nobody in six years).
2. What sank a premise every time: money as the plot (deposits, leases, signatures, a cost book), a lead who is the oppressor or a function, and people who stay roles.
3. Idea 4 as written is a fault in play. Nearly every premise was marked down in the same words: the wrong blow is "forced", "imposed", "unearned", and it makes the lead hard to care for. A wrong the player is made to do reads to players as a mission on rails. In the bibles the false belief must be one the player reaches himself from evidence that honestly points there, with more than one person he could fairly choose, and a road for the player who holds back.
4. Bleak endings cost marks; an ending image that pays off the player's own record (the turnout at a funeral, the quay that fills or stays empty, the examiner waiting) gained them.
