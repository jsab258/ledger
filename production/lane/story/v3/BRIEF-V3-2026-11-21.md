THE ELEVEN IDEAS (the brief):
1. The end of something. Great game stories are about a world dying (RDR2: the end of the outlaw era). LEDGER's is real: the National Dock Labour Scheme was abolished in 1989 and container ports were killing the old ones; Ron lost his docker's job that year. Tom arrives in the Hook just as its reason to exist goes. Research it first, dated, with sources (WebSearch first, WebFetch only on URLs from your own results).
2. Theme you play. Every system is already a ledger of obligation: fares, debts, rent, favours, what witnesses know. The story's one question: what does Tom owe the dead man and the living? The ending settles accounts, and the systems tell the story.
3. Fewer people, deeper. Players praise characters most (46% of story comments in the eight-game study). Three or four relationships carried through the whole game (Ron and Sheila above all) rather than twenty people with a scene each; the rest light.
4. Make the player do it, then show what it meant. The player commits to a wrong belief with his own hands (accuses or hurts the wrong person) and only then learns the truth.
5. Losses that cannot be undone. Within the content rule, someone the player cares about is lost for good, as a result of the player's own choices.
6. The cab as the confessional. The deepest talk happens in the cab: Ron in the passenger seat at 2 a.m., a fare who says too much. Driving is a large share of the hour; let it carry the story.
7. The town remembers what you did. The authored story keeps taking up the player's own record (the witness from week two returns in act three): name which acts are picked up, where, and the fallback when the player did something else.
8. Many premises cheaply, then the best few: twenty one-paragraph premises within canon, all through the checked reader, the top three developed in full, then one picked.
9. Judge key scenes in play, not on paper: playable sketches of the chosen story's key scenes.
10. Push against AI habits: too many characters, everything mended, tidy arcs. Specific, strange and restrained beats complete.
11. Real ingredients: dockers' oral histories and local papers of 1989 and 1990 (from the outside, by search), so the details are ones nobody would invent.
