## 1. Minicabs and private hire
- Operators needed a licence under section 55 of the Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1976, granted by the district council. The operator must produce the licence on request to an authorised council officer or a constable (s.56(4)). https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1976/57/section/56/enacted/data.xht
- RECORDS (the key point): under s.56(1) the operator keeps a record "in such form as the council may by condition prescribe". Before each journey starts he enters the particulars the council prescribes, for every booking he accepts, including bookings he passes to another operator. Under s.56(2) he must produce that record on request for inspection by an authorised officer or a constable. Under s.56(3) he also keeps whatever records the council prescribes for each vehicle he operates, producible the same way. The Act sets no retention period; councils set it by licence condition. https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1976/57/section/56/enacted/data.xht
- So the booking book is a legal document, and a constable may ask to see it. What goes in it varies by council. A modern example (Chorley, NOT 1990): time and date of booking; hirer's name and address; pick-up point and time; destination; vehicle and driver; remarks including sub-contracting; records kept at least 2 years from the latest hiring in them. Use as a model only; the fictional town's own condition is the writer's to set. https://chorley.gov.uk/licensing/conditions-attached-issue-private-hire-operators-licence/6

## 2. Money and the times
- Base rate: held at 15% through the third quarter of 1990; cut to 14% in "early October" with entry to the Exchange Rate Mechanism. The page gives no exact day (5 Oct 1990 is UNVERIFIED here). 1989 rates: not on that page, UNVERIFIED. https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/1990/q4/operation-of-monetary-policy
- Poll tax: Scotland from 1989/90; England and Wales from 1990/91, taking effect 1 April 1990. Per-head amount set by each council, so it differed by town. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poll_tax_(Great_Britain)
- Non-payment: about 1.2 billion pounds uncollected in England and Wales in year one; up to 30% of former ratepayers refusing in some areas; Strathclyde issued 250,000 summary warrants on 15 Jan 1990. Protesters urged people to contest liability orders and skip court. In Nov 1990 South Yorkshire Police said arresting all defaulters would be physically impossible. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poll_tax_(Great_Britain)
- Protest dates: 6 Mar 1990 Bristol (5,000 marched, 26 arrested); 7 Mar 1990 Hackney (about 50 windows smashed, 56 arrested); 31 Mar 1990 Trafalgar Square (113 injured, about 339 arrested). Anti-Poll Tax Federation formed Nov 1989. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poll_tax_riots
- Ambulance dispute: 7 Sep 1989 to 23 Feb 1990. Overtime and rest-day bans, then emergency-only from November 1989; army on standby 30 Oct, first army ambulances in London 8 Nov; army in 18 service areas by mid-February; settled in the early hours of 23 Feb 1990 at 16.9% over two years. About 80% of the public backed the crews. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%931990_British_ambulance_strike
- Burns' Day storm, 25 Jan 1990: 47 dead; gusts over 92 mph on coasts from west Wales to Cornwall and Kent; top gust 107 mph at Aberporth and Gwennap Head; over half a million without power; phone lines down; roads blocked by trees and overturned vehicles; railways, airports and ports disrupted. https://rmets.org/metmatters/day-burns-day-storm-1990

## 5. Ferries, fishing, dock hiring
- The National Dock Labour Scheme was abolished in July 1989 (Dock Work Act 1989). The Employment Secretary told the Commons on 24 July 1990 that the 15 former scheme ports showed "substantial changes in the composition of the labour force" and more flexible labour use. This is a government claim, with no figures on the page. https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1990/jul/24/dock-labour-scheme
