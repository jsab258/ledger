## 2. Funerals, collections, sick and benevolent funds

2.1 Preston Dock, "Images of a Port: Life and Times on Preston Dock" (University of Central Lancashire).
- On attendance: after nationalisation in 1947 dockers had to "present themselves at the call stand to get their books stamped A.P. (Appearance or Attendance Proved)" to qualify for the flat wage. A man who arrived late could "lose his A.P. money for that shift."
- On death or injury: when a docker was killed the men "walked off the dock" as a mark of respect, and "organised a collection at the call stand to try and help the families of those killed or injured."
- URL: https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/37191/1/Images%20of%20a%20Port%20Life%20and%20Times%20on%20Preston%20Dock.pdf (first listed by search at clok.uclan.ac.uk, which redirected here)
- Sure: read page (summary). Period is post-1947; exact decade of the walk-off not established. The word "whip-round" was not in it.

2.2 Colin Ross, docker, Spitalfields Life, 21 March 2012 (and a 2022 follow-up).
- Words: "we had so many dockers who were dying and getting pauper's funerals". His father's "Distress Fund": "Ten thousand people joined the fund at a shilling a week"; "The work they put in to collect that one shilling a week."
- This is the 1960s to 1970s London docks (the page's dates run to 1978), pre-1989. Useful as the habit of mutual aid, not for 1990.
- URLs: https://spitalfieldslife.com/2012/03/21/colin-ross-docker/ and https://spitalfieldslife.com/2022/09/25/colin-ross-docker-x/
- Sure: read pages (summaries).

## 3. Daily working details

3.1 Brass tally (pre-scheme casual hiring): a metal token that gave a man the right to work that day. Foremen handed them out each morning; "tallies were sometimes knocked from workers' hands or grabbed by others." Casual labour in London lasted, the blog says, until the late 1970s (this looks loose; the earlier file has casual hiring ended in 1967 under the Scheme, so treat the blog's claim as doubtful).
- URL: https://bremenbod.blogspot.com/2024/04/social-history-in-50-objects-number-2.html
- Sure: read page (summary). A blog, not an archive. By 1990 the brass tally was history for a Scheme man; the "book" and the AP stamp were the live objects.

3.2 Attendance money: see 2.1 (A.P. stamped book; late means lost A.P. money). Cullen (1.1) agrees: "If there was no work available in the morning you had your book stamped 'AP' which meant you had attended."

3.3 Tally clerk, checkers, Customs, port police (London): Museum of London page "The jobs that made London's docks run".
- Words: "the tally clerk seated on the left is recording the weight of the crate of tea suspended on the right" (a 1951 photograph). Warehousemen did "piling, checking and sampling goods". Customs officials "calculated the import or export duties to be paid on the goods handled at the docks." The docks had their own police force, which existed "even before the Metropolitan Police was created in 1829."
- URL: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/jobs-that-made-londons-docks-run/
- Sure: read page (summary). London, mostly pre-1980.

3.4 Not reached: a first-person tally clerk's account of the 1980s; bonded store routine (keys, Customs lock, officer's seal) at any named port; port police duties at a non-London port in 1990. The Spectator "The Tally Clerks", 10 May 1957 (https://archive.spectator.co.uk/article/10th-may-1957/5/the-tally-clerks) came up in search, snippet only, not read.

## 5. Prices

5.1 Cigarettes, pack of 20, UK, rates as at 12 February 1990: retail price 161p (£1.61); total tax 118p (73 per cent); duty 97p; VAT 21p. In 1985 it was 127p. This is the most popular price category, from a Treasury table.
- URL: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written_answers/1990/feb/14/cigarettes (HC Deb 14 February 1990, vol 167, cc296-7W)
- Sure: read page (summary). Firm, but February; the March 1990 Budget moved duty, not checked.

5.2 Loaf, white sliced, 800g: £0.50, the calendar-year 1990 average, ONS series CZOH, via a website.
- URL: https://salarytax.uk/basket/1990
- Sure: read page (summary). Second-hand ONS number from a hobby site; a corner shop could differ.

5.3 Taxi fare: London black cab, from 8 June 1990: minimum fare £1.00 (includes a 40p hire charge) for the first 1,021.5 yards or 3 minutes 36 seconds, then 20p for each 340.5 yards or 1 minute 12 seconds, up to 6 miles. Fares rose by about 9.2 per cent that day.
- URL: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1990/may/18/london-taxi-fares
- Sure: read page (summary). It is London licensed taxis, NOT a minicab and not a port town. No 1990 minicab fare found. Unverified guess for the story: a minicab for a short town hop would sit near or a little under the taxi figure, perhaps £1.50 to £3; mark this as invention until a source is found.

5.4 Funeral: in December 1983 the minister (Dr Boyson) estimated the average cost of a simple funeral at £350 to £600; no London and provinces split.
- URL: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1983/dec/14/funeral-and-cremation-costs
- Sure: read page (summary). 1983 not 1990. No 1990 figure found; Royal London's index starts too late (its 2017 report gives £3,784 for 2017 only). For 1990 an unverified range of roughly £700 to £1,000 would be a reasoned guess, not a source.

5.5 Cigarettes as a share of an average manual weekly wage, October 1990: 0.7 per cent (Hansard, 11 March 1991, https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1991/mar/11/cigarette-prices). Read, firm, but a ratio only.
