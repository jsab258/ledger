# LEDGER: the spine as cards

Written 24 November 2026 by the lead's helper, from the lead's brief of the same day (PAPER-BRIEF.md, which carries the owner's binding decisions), the story frame of 26 November (section 5, the spine; section 6, the director; section 7, memory) and the side stories of 8 November. Where the brief and the frame disagree, the brief wins. Nothing here is built; every card is the lane's design, for the lead to overturn.

## What a card is, and how the director uses it

A card is one authored beat of the spine. The story director holds every card in one pool. It never moves the world: the Core moves the dial (0 Fists, 1 Shown, 2 Fired, 3 War), the rivals' ladders (noticed, a visit, pressure, a threat, a sit-down), Ellis's stages (nothing, asking round, investigating, manhunt), the rungs and the name ladder (the new owner, Nowak, Frank, Frankie), all on Frank's deeds. The director only chooses which card, among those whose Conditions are already true, plays next, and when. It takes the highest Priority first (5 plays as soon as it is true and the pacing allows; 1 waits for a gap), then the card whose sign has stood longest, then checks Weight against the pacing. Weight: light (a rest, a laugh or a small deed, a few real minutes), middle (a job or a scene with a cost), heavy (only four: the Landing's night, the night between, the yard, the quay at four). Cooldown is the game time after a card ends before the director may start another spine card; side stories and the street fill it. Conditions name world state only, never real time. Time: a game day is 48 real minutes, a game hour 2 real minutes, a game week about 5.6 real hours.

The pacing rules, in both clocks:

- After Act I something reaches Frank at least every 15 game hours (30 real minutes).
- Act II runs in at least four stages of at most 5 game days each (4 real hours), each ending in a rung change; on the wound-down road, where an honest firm stops at rung 2, a stage ends on a rival's rung instead (proposed here).
- At most one heavy card in a game week (Monday to Sunday), a light or funny card after each, and at least 60 game hours (2.5 game days; 2 real hours) between two heavy cards.
- Cooling: if the dial or a ladder has risen twice in one game day (48 real minutes), only light rest cards until the next game morning. If nothing has risen for a game week (about 5.6 real hours), the director reaches for the highest card whose sign has been shown.
- Each game week: one rival rung, one firm change, one return of a deed.
- No card plays twice. Neighbouring cards differ on at least three of the seven shape fields. A gap is filled by a side story or a street happening before any filler job.
- The Ledger always holds one aim in a person's words (who, where, what for) and, beneath it, Frank's aim for the rung in his. No markers.
- Fail-safes, all in game time: the Landing by the end of game week 4; Ellis's tip by Thursday evening of game week 5 (so the search falls inside week 5); the sit-down's fail-safe from four game days before Act IIb ends; the Customs letter by noon on the Monday of game week 7.
- The three chairs: if Danny is dead or gone, Craig Fewson has his ladder, his key and his grudge; if Ron is gone before the turn, the most loyal man stands in his place and Sheila has the last word (a rival's harm to Ron stops at the hospital until the turn); if Sheila has left, the book lies on the counter with the pen on it.

The likely weeks follow the town's calendar of 1990: game week 1 opens on a Monday in mid-October; week 4 has the quay's display on its Saturday and Remembrance Sunday after it; week 7 ends on the Saturday the Harbour Board chains the old dock gate (1 December, proposed in the frame). Dates appear in play only where a person says them.

Shape card fields: opening (how it starts), turn (what changes inside it), ending (how it closes), action (what the hands do), place, company (who is beside Frank), mood.

## The checks

Sixty-one cards, S01 to S61, in story order.

Neighbouring shapes. Every pair of neighbouring cards differs on at least five of the seven shape fields as written, so the rule of three holds everywhere. The opening, turn and ending fields always differ, so the honest test is the four concrete fields (action, place, company, mood). On those, these neighbours share two, and pass only on the other five: S09 and S10 (the office, Sheila: Friday's rent, then Sunday's question; kept, since a rent day and a reckoning two days apart are the act's spine, and one is a wait, the other a talk); S17, S18 and S19 (the office and Sheila, three in a row in week 2 to 3; the director should put a street card or a side story between them when it can, and the conditions allow it, since none of the three wakes on the same state); S20 and S21 (both drives at the dock gate, four game days apart, the first cold and with the pen, the second tense and against the clock; kept, since the contract is built as one fifth-morning run after the first); S11 and S12 (drives with Ron, a dawn farewell and then a comic lesson); S32 and S33 (Ron, quiet: the gun and lesson five; S32's cooldown of 4 game hours does not keep them apart, so the director should not play S33 in the same game day as S32); S40 and S41 (talk at Grace's, Garbutt's aim and then the turned man, who may be met in her kitchen; the turned man can be met at the corner instead).

Heavy beats. One heavy card a game week: the Landing's night (S24, week 4), the night between (S38, week 5), the yard (S44, week 6), the quay at four (S57, week 7). Each is at least 60 game hours (2 real hours) from the last. Three places where the rule needed a choice:

1. The frame names five heaviest scenes, the fifth being the gate when they corner Ron (S30). Its own director lists only four heavy beats and calls the post crises middle weight, so S30 is middle; week 5 then holds one heavy card (S38). Played, S30 will feel heavy; that is its job.
2. The counter at five (S58) is the last choice of the game, but is carded middle and quiet, so week 7 has one heavy card. It also stands in for the rest beat after the quay: there is an hour between them by design, and nothing lighter fits.
3. The night between must stay in week 5 for the yard to fall in week 6 with 60 game hours between them. The frame's tip fail-safe ("by the end of week five") could push the search into week 6, so the cards set it by Thursday evening of game week 5. If the search still slips into week 6, the yard waits for week 7, and the five mornings start no earlier than 60 game hours after it.

Week 7 is the fullest week: the sit-down on its Monday (the brief's "Monday week" from the week-6 aim), the letter that afternoon, the five mornings Tuesday to Saturday, the quay and the counter on the Saturday, then the close. That is about six game days, under 5 real hours.

Length. With the field labels (about 40 words a card), the cards run from about 150 to 265 words. Without the labels, all but eight are under 200; S06, S14, S20, S23, S24, S28, S38 and S44 run to between 204 and 225, the night between (S38) longest because its three ways and their costs have to sit on one card.
