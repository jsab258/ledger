# LEDGER: the cast

Written 24 November 2026 by the story lane, from the revised story frame (frame/STORY-FRAME-2026-11-26.md), the spine and side cards (paper/SPINE-CARDS.md, paper/SIDE-CARDS.md), the 23 scenes (paper/scenes/), the casting index (production/casting/CASTING.md) and the street's regulars (production/casting/regulars/REGULARS.md). The first pass was a helper's extraction (working/cast-extract.md, working/cast-diff.md); every doubtful cell in it was checked against those sources, and the quoted lines are copied from the sources as they now stand. The changes against the earlier frame and the people files are in CAST-CHANGES.md.

The people data files (blueprint/data/people/) read this file. Three layers:

- Layer 1, core: six people carried through every week. Frank and five.
- Layer 2, supporting: 29 living people with a thread of their own (a spine card, a side story or a speaking part in a key scene), and Mickey, who is dead and offstage.
- Layer 3, residents: everybody else. About 200 to 300 in the end, plus the named ones already written into the story, plus the roles nobody names.

Ages: the approved casting sheets (CASTING.md for the principals, REGULARS.md for the thirty regulars) win on age, look and name. Where a person has no sheet, the age is the people file's, or a scene's where the scene states one; the line says which. Where a scene disagrees with a sheet, the scene owes the fix (listed in CAST-CHANGES.md).

Names: the lead is Frank Nowak, never Tom or Tommy. His name ladder is the new owner, Nowak, Frank, Frankie. Quotes below carry their source in brackets: a scene number, a card id (S for the spine, D for the side stories), the frame's section, or REGULARS.

## Layer 1: core (six)

### Frank Nowak

- Age: 32 (CASTING.md; the sheet is still headed Tom Nowak, a name fix owed).
- Role: the player. Mickey Suddaby's nephew, the son of a Polish settler and Mickey's sister. Steps off the ferry with £23.50 and a dead uncle's cab office. He missed the funeral and brought the suit.
- Want: to be the Hook's name by Christmas, so that when they say Mickey's they mean him (scene 2; frame section 1).
- Flaw: pride, and a stranger's need to be seen to win.
- The one deed: in week one, in his funeral suit, he drives Danny's abandoned car down Quay Street, parks it with the front wheels over the quay's edge, hangs the keys on Mickey's board and chalks CAR 3 (frame section 3; scene 5).
- Ties:
  - Ron: the bond. Ron carries the envelope, the door, the lessons.
  - Sheila: the book. She makes him say what he did, in words she can write.
  - Danny: the opponent, and the only man who treats him as an equal from day one.
  - The Widow: she sees what he is short of, and owns him by it.
  - Ellis: the one person who wants to hear everything and would never pass it on as talk.
  - Garbutt: sells him true reads and one truce. Agar: writes to him (Nowek), meets him once at Grace's.
  - Pauline: says his name on the air, rung by rung. Mickey: the uncle he never knew.
- Voice: short and in order (what it is, then what he will do about it). He counts aloud. His temper is quiet: he does up the middle button of his jacket, goes formal (Mr Cammack, Mrs Jensen) and does it with his hands. He never asks the way. Lines:
  - "Nowak. N, O, W, A, K." (scene 14)
  - "Forty-one doors on this street, Ron. I counted. Not one opened." (scene 2)
  - "That leaves five pound fifty. I'll try not to go mad." (frame section 3)
- What he is called: the new owner (Pauline, week one), Nowak (the street), Frank (Pauline from week four; Sheila the day she trusts him), Frankie (the town's top rung, for the man who runs the Hook; the Widow says it to own him, Ron once if ever; Danny says "nephew" until he is under Frank). Never Tom, never Tommy. He gives everybody both names.
- What he calls others: Ron, Sheila, Pauline by first name; Mr Cammack, Mrs Jensen, Mr Agar when he means it.
- Data id: frank_nowak (the internal cast_id "tom" and home "toms_flat" may stay; the brief allows it).
- Path notes: he can be hurt, arrested and held for hours; he cannot die. Honest firm: the same want by daylight. His second deed, week five, is his own name signed to keep Ron out (or not).

### Ron Kirby

- Age: 58 (CASTING.md).
- Role: the bond. A docker until the scheme ended in 1989; Mickey kept him on the door and the rank. Cannot drive until Frank's lessons: every morning since Mickey died he has started car two and sat in it with the handbrake on, so the battery keeps.
- Want: to be a man who is sent for.
- Flaw: he does not ask and does not tell. He carries a thing for years before he says it is heavy.
- The one deed: the day the office is searched he puts on the coat he wore to Mickey's funeral, walks into the station, asks for DS Ellis by name and offers a statement nobody asked for (frame section 3; scene 14).
- Ties:
  - Frank: boss. Sheila: his name is down the whole third column of her red book.
  - Danny: they bicker down the dock road. The Widow: his old gate, her second blanket in the cells.
  - Frank Stathers: offers him his old place and brass tally 39 at the gate. Ellis: the statement.
  - Father Walsh: carries his messages out of the cells. Lily Tang: two of number nine on Fridays, then one.
  - Rita and Hal: Mickey's watch, pawned by Ron for the funeral flowers. Mickey: the arrangement was his to carry.
- Voice: slow, northern, "aye", "nowt", "boss". He names only what he has seen. After the night between he stops naming buildings. Lines:
  - "Coming up on the ice house. That's the ice house. Leaving the ice house." (frame section 3)
  - "Saying's nowt, boss. You've a firm to stand in. I've a door." (scene 14)
  - "I've told her nowt that isn't so. I've just not told her you." (scene 14)
- What he calls Frank: "boss". "Mr Nowak" at the gate in the Burn Both close (scene 23). "Frankie" once, if ever. In the Kingdom close Frank asks him to say "Frank" once, and he says "Boss" (scene 21).
- Data id: ron_kirby.
- Path notes: Ron is one of the friends the ending counts (owner's decision 4), with Sheila, Alison and Danby. Ron lives on every path (can_die false; a rival's harm to him never goes past the hospital). Free or inside, he has a beat every week; if he is gone he is on the remand wing, charged with conspiracy to evade duty on the landing's loads, and the Straight Life close has him driving on his L-plates. On the Widow's path his week-5 go is on the ferry rank, not at the gate: he stands at the wing of car four and will not answer the council man (S30b).

### Sheila Dunn

- Ties:

- Voice: dry, exact, short; "love". Lines:

- What she calls Frank: "love", and "new management" until she trusts him. "Frank" is a rung of its own: "Goodnight, Frank." (scene 22).
- Data id: sheila_dunn.
- Path notes: Sheila is one of the friends the ending counts (owner's decision 4), with Ron, Alison and Danby. Trusted, she is the firm's brain and hands him the pen. Asked to put the books off three counts running, she leaves. Never trusted, she keeps the red book anyway. If she has left, the book lies on the counter with the pen on it.

- Ties:

- What he calls Frank: "nephew" (outside the name ladder). "Frankie", to needle him, once the ladder reaches it: "Evening, Frankie." (scene 21).
- Data id: danny_cammack.
- Path notes: pact, he is the best earner the firm has and turns in sight from the phone box. Refused, the loudest rival; where the Widow is Frank's ally his week-5 go is at Ron on the ferry rank, with two hackneys on rented plates and the council's licensing officer (S30b). ### Maureen Jensen, the Widow

- Age: 57 (CASTING.md; scene 16 agrees).
- Role: head of the dockside syndicate. The dock gate and the men at it; taxes cars £5 a week. Trawler-owner's widow from a Hessle Road family.
- Want: the dock gate and the men at it. For herself: to owe nobody and be owed by everybody.
- Flaw: she cannot ask. She can only give, and count.
- The one deed: the night he comes back from casualty she sits in Mickey's chair in the dark office in her fur-collared coat, facing the door, with a pot made and his dinner under a plate, cut up for a fork. She has not walked up Quay Street in years (scene 16).
- Ties:
  - Frank: pressed and fed by her. Ron: her old gate man; she has had a second blanket sent to him.
  - Frank Stathers: her man at the gate. Mark Billany: her ferry-rank taxman.
  - Kevin Needham and Lee Drewery: her eyes. Colin Mastin, Brenda's porter, Gary Mawer: her berths, her cut, her question.
  - Garbutt: her load, her place at the table. Agar: the cold-store road past her gate. Mickey: told her no, most politely, for thirty years.
- Voice: low, slow, never asks, "Mr Nowak". Lines:
  - "I never ask anybody for anything, Mr Nowak. I find out what they're short of." (scene 16)
  - "Sit down. It's been in the oven twice." (scene 16)
  - "Leave him. He can ask." (scene 17)
- What she calls Frank: "Mr Nowak"; "Frankie" is the fourth rung and she says it first, at the chained gate (scene 21).
- Data id: maureen_jensen.
- Path notes: ally, her men stand his posts and are gone in one morning at the fourth rung. Rival, the wall he breaks at the Landing. Alone or honest, she taxes, poaches and waits. She cannot die (maureen_jensen.json has can_die false, by the data's own cover rule); a gate with nobody at it is her fear.

### DS Carol Ellis

- Age: 40 (CASTING.md).
- Role: CID (the casting sheet says Humberside CID; the game's text says CID only). The law. Wants one case joining the warehouse fire to the street's crimes.
- Want: one case. For herself, the fire file closed in her own handwriting.
- Flaw: she moves only on paper. A thing is not so until somebody has signed it, which is why the street always knows more than she does.
- The one deed: in the searched office she reads Frank his own first line aloud, turns the book round on the counter, holds out her pen and says, "Write it again. Underneath. Same words." (scene 13).
- Ties:
  - Frank: the line, the trade, the cells. Ron: his statement, which she did not ask for.
  - Sheila: the mint. Wardell and Garton: her constables, and the two she cannot see are bought.
  - Norman Dent and Dennis Iveson: her witnesses. Alison Sedman: the paper. Mickey: the fire file.
- Voice: level, patient, exact; never jokes on duty. Lines:
  - "I'm not asking you to remember everything, Mr Nowak. I'm asking you to remember it the same way twice." (scene 13)
  - "Somebody always saw, Mr Nowak. My trouble is finding the one who'll sign." (frame section 3)
  - "The same way twice, Mr Nowak. I'll say that for you." (scene 22)
- What she calls Frank: "Mr Nowak", always; she says it right from the first.
- Data id: carol_ellis.
- Path notes: she can be bought once, only with exactly £800 in cash in Frank's hand that night (not from the tin), then £100 every Friday ("A hundred, Fridays. I write everything down; that's what you're paying for."). A missed week ends it, her case jumps, and she is never bought twice. Bought, she writes every envelope in a notebook of her own, and the notebook is the case.

## Layer 3: residents

### The rule

- 200 to 300 in the end, added after the first loops (Jafar's ruling; cast.json residents_rule). Made in one seeded pass, so the same town comes out every time.
- Each has a name, a trade, a routine and at most a line or two (shared street lines by speaker kind; no own lines).
- None carries a spine beat. A resident may be named on a card as a step or a fallback (Ombler, Ian Megginson, Gordon Leckenby, Hal Metcalfe, Colin Mastin), but a person from layer 2 can always stand in.
- Ages 18 to 90, no children anywhere; first names and surnames from the 1990 northern port lists with Irish and Polish origins as the casting research gives; never a name already in people/; no alcohol or gambling in any routine or line.
- The named residents below are the ones already written into the frame, cards or scenes. Thirteen of them give a side story (one giver, one tie, one return); the story is theirs to open and a layer-2 person's to end. That is a side story, not a spine beat.
