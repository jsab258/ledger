# 20. The counter at five

- Where it sits: Act III, the last day, five o'clock. The books open. The last choice of the game.
- What wakes it: the quay has been played; it is five by the office clock.
- Who must be present: Derek Hebblewhite of Customs and Excise. Sheila Dunn. Pauline at the radio. DS Ellis, unless nothing of hers is live (nobody charged, nobody on remand, no trade offered), which is rare. Ron on the door, if he is free. Danny Cammack, if he won on the quay or was agreed with. Fallback: if Sheila has left the firm, the book lies on the counter with a pen on it, and nobody turns it round.

Frank is trying to get one line written under a name he can live with. He will give up the firm, or Ron, or his standing with every door on the Hook: one of the three, and he chooses which.

## Staging

The office, the strip light, the street dark. The board is behind the counter as the game has left it: so many lines, such names, such hooks with keys.

Hebblewhite stands at the customers' side with his case on the bench and a list of five on a clipboard. Four are ticked. He is fifty, neat, in a raincoat he has not unbuttoned all week, and he has never sat down.

The day book is on the counter, open at the first Monday. At the foot of the page is the line: Pauline's start, Sheila's finish. Beneath it, in another hand, dated the sixteenth of November and initialled, is the same line, or one that is not the same. Beneath that the page is clean.

Sheila is behind the counter. Her handbag is on the desk at her back.

Ellis is by the door with her case, out of the light.

Pauline has the wall clock over her head and her hand on the microphone switch.

The camera is across the counter, where it stood on the first morning, in week three and at the search.

## The scene

PAULINE (on the air, to every car): Books open at five. Real five.

If Frank is carrying Mickey's watch, it says five now. The wall clock says five to.

HEBBLEWHITE (who has looked at both): I have five to, Mr Nowak. I don't open a book early. It isn't fair to the book.

Nobody moves. There are five minutes, and they are the player's: he can speak to anybody in the room, and each has one thing to say.

- Sheila: "Mickey was always stood there five minutes before anybody wanted him. I never found out what he did with them either."
- Pauline: "I've wrote down where every fare in this town was going for nineteen year. I never once knew where any of them was really going."
- Hebblewhite: "Four mornings. Two I would call sound. I'm not at liberty to say which two."
- Ellis: "Somebody always saw, Mr Nowak. My trouble is finding the one who'll sign."
- Ron, on the door: "Boss."

Without the watch he has come on the minute, and there is no wait.

The clock's hand comes up.

HEBBLEWHITE: Week one. (He opens the narrow cash book first.) Cash introduced, twenty-three pounds fifty. Is that all of it?

SHEILA: It's what was there.

HEBBLEWHITE: Then the firm began on a week and a third's rent.

SHEILA: It did.

He ticks something. He turns to the day book, and reads without touching the page.

HEBBLEWHITE: Monday the fifteenth of October, ten in the evening. I have an entry in one hand and, beneath it, an entry in another, a month later, initialled F.N. I am obliged to ask which of them is the firm's.

Sheila turns the book round on the counter to face Frank. She unscrews her pen, lays it in the gutter of the page, and takes her hand away.

SHEILA: Thirty-one years I've written what I was told. You write this one.

If she has ever called him by his name, she says it now, after: "Frank." If she has not, she does not.

The player has the pen, the clean inch of paper, and three things he can do.

Leave it as it stands. He does not write. He can cap the pen and give it back, or leave it lying.

HEBBLEWHITE: Then the entries stand as written.

He goes down his list, and what he says is the week's verdict, plainly. If every morning held: "I am shown books that are in order. Good evening." If one fell: "I shall be writing to you, Mr Nowak. It will be a longer letter than the last."

ELLIS (closing her case): Then the only signed account of that night is Mr Kirby's, and it's the one I'll take to court.

If Frank signed in week five, she says instead: "Then your own stands, Mr Nowak. Monday."

Write it true. He writes a third line beneath the two, in his own hand and his own words: what was carried, where, and that it was his.

Sheila reads it upside down. She turns to the desk, opens her bag, moves the knitting, and lays the red book on the counter beside the day book. She opens it at the ribbon and then turns it back to page one.

SHEILA: Then you'll want the other one.

Hebblewhite looks at a third book he was not told of. Ellis has come forward into the light. She reads down the last column with her finger, not touching it. R. Kirby. R. Kirby. And here and there, on the nights Frank went by himself: F.N.

ELLIS: There's no column for who they were brought to.

There is a ruler in Sheila's pen tray. The player's hand takes it. He rules a line down the right-hand edge of the page, top to bottom, to make a fourth column that the book has never had. At the head of it he writes two words, and under them his name.

SHEILA (of the lines marked F.N.): Those you'd fetched yourself. They want nothing writing beside them.

The more of them there are, the less of the new column there is to fill.

HEBBLEWHITE: This is not the book I was sent to inspect. (He takes his spectacles off.) It is a great deal more honest, and a great deal more expensive. I'm sorry.

ELLIS: A man can't sign for another man's column. Mr Kirby will be out by eight. (Or: That takes most of the weight off your own, Mr Nowak. Not all.)

Put Danny's name to it. He writes, beneath the two lines, that the job was brought in by D. Cammack, who drove car two and held its key.

Sheila does not read that one. She looks at the board.

ELLIS: And you'll sign to it. (He does.) One charge goes. Whose?

PLAYER (own words): his own, or Ron's.

Pauline takes her headphones off and lays them on the desk. In the street doorway Terry Hodgson has come in for his half of the night's takings. He has heard. He looks at Frank, says, "Now then," to nobody, and goes out without his money.

HEBBLEWHITE: That is not a matter for me. The entry is completed. Thank you.

In every case he clips his pen to the board, picks up his case, and stops at the door.

HEBBLEWHITE: Produce it, or ask for it in writing. You produced it. Not everybody does.

He goes out. Down the street a Harbour Board lorry passes the end of the parade toward the dock gate, with a length of chain rattling in the back.

## What the player can do

- Use the five minutes, if he has them. Hands: his own words, to anybody. Sign: two clocks that disagree. Nothing said here changes what can be written.
- Leave it. Hands: nothing, or the pen handed back. Sign: Hebblewhite has asked which entry is the firm's, and silence is an answer. What follows: the firm, if the books hold. Whoever was carrying the line goes on carrying it.
- Write it true. Hands: the pen; then the ruler. Sign: Sheila has said, every Sunday for four weeks, whose name stood by each night. What follows: Ron walks, or Frank's own charge lightens. The firm does not survive its books. The nights he fetched alone are already his; every other is his from now.
- Put Danny's name to it. Hands: the pen, and a signature. Sign: Ellis offered it on Thursday, and said then that the street would know. What follows: one charge goes; Danny is taken on the quay or at his door; Terry has told the rank before the ink is dry.
- Try to write something else. A fourth thing (a new lie, another man's name, a joke) is read by the Core as leaving it, and Hebblewhite says so: "I'll take that as no alteration."
- Ask Sheila what she would do. "I'd write what I was told, love. That's why it's you."

## What the town remembers

- Pauline heard all of it, and the microphone was under her hand. She did not open it. What the cars got was: "All cars. Books are shut." The Hook knows from that only that it is over.
- Terry, if Danny's name went down: "He give Cammack to her. Across his own counter. I was stood there." By seven every door has it.
- Sheila has the pen back. Whatever was written, she blots it herself, and initials the foot of the page for the last time. S.D.
- Hebblewhite's letter comes on Tuesday, addressed correctly.
- Ellis holds a line in the owner's hand, or a signature under a rival's name, or one man's statement and nothing else.
- Ron, on the door, heard a ruler drawn down a page, or did not. Nobody tells him which. He is told by what happens to him.
- The close is read from what is on the page and the board at this minute.

## Variants by path

- Danny's hand on the book. He won on the quay, or was agreed with. He is at the counter before Frank, with the day book shut under his palm. "Evening. I've fetched your book up." Hebblewhite: "And you are?" "Depends what he writes." The three ways are all there, and each has his price on it.
  - Leave it: Danny lifts his hand only when his name is on the top line. He chalks it himself, behind Frank's back, while a man from Customs watches a firm change hands in chalk. "Partners. I'll do the talking."
  - Write it true: "Write what you like. It sinks you, not me. I'll have the rank outside when you've done." Sheila writes one line more: the rank, to D. Cammack.
  - His own name: he lifts his hand to let Frank write, expecting anything else. He reads it upside down. Ellis is already beside him. "You've brought me the exhibit yourself, Mr Cammack. I couldn't have asked you to." Danny looks at Frank. "He's moved my motor." He is not laughing. "Mickey would've said sorry."
- The book went in the dock. There is nothing on the counter but a clean oblong. Ellis lays a certified copy of one page in its place, and her own pen. Everything is as written, on her paper, and she takes the paper away with her whichever way it goes. Hebblewhite: "For the remainder I must use my best judgment. People seldom care for it."
- Ellis is bought. She is by the door as before. If every Friday was paid she offers no trade and reads no column aloud; when the red book is opened she says only, "I'll need a copy of that," and writes in a small notebook that is not the station's. If one Friday was missed, the small notebook is on the counter beside the day book, open. "There are two books opened at five, Mr Nowak."
- Sheila has left. The book is not turned round for him, and nobody says thirty-one years. If he writes it true there is no red book to open; he has only his own line, and Ellis says, "It's a start."
- Frank refused the pen at the search. There is one line and a gap. Hebblewhite: "I have an entry, and under it a space somebody was asked to fill." The choice is the same, an inch higher up the page.
