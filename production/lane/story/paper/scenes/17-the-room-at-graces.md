# 17. The room at Grace's

- Where it sits: the close of Act IIb: the Monday that Garbutt named a week ago, in the evening. Tomorrow at nine the inspector starts.
- What wakes it: Garbutt's day has come. Frank has fetched one, two, three or none of what the three heads want: a man of Danny's, a paper of Agar's, a load of the Widow's.
- Who must be present: Keith Garbutt. Geoffrey Agar, seen here for the first time in the game. Maureen Jensen. Danny Cammack. Grace Rinaldi at the hatch. Fallback: a head who is dead or gone is sat for by his second (Simon Ombler, Frank Stathers, Craig Fewson), who can accept terms and cannot alter them. A head in a pact with Frank sits on Frank's side of the cloth and says so.

Frank is trying to walk out still holding the rank, the gate and the licence. He will give up, a head at a time, the thing he fetched for each of them, or keep his coat on.

## Staging

The back room at the caff, six o'clock, the front in darkness and the blind down. A gas fire. A table with the good white cloth on it, and five chairs.

There was a funeral tea in here until four. A plate of ham sandwiches is under a damp cloth on the sideboard, with half a fruit loaf, and the urn is still warm.

By the door is a coat stand. Three coats are on it already: a camel overcoat, a dark one with a fur collar, a leather jacket. The rule of the room is Garbutt's, and he said it a week ago. A coat on the stand means a person has come to deal. A coat on his back means he has not.

Garbutt is at the head of the table in his car coat. He never takes it off. He is not anybody's.

In the wall behind him is the hatch to the kitchen, open. Grace's face is in it from time to time, in her hairnet. Behind her, if Frank turned him, a young man with his sleeves rolled is at the sink doing the big pans.

Frank comes in, if the yard went that way, with his left arm in plaster under the good overcoat, which is buttoned over the sling because that is the only way it will go on.

The camera sits at the foot of the table, level with the cloth.

## The scene

GRACE (through the hatch, to nobody): I've had one funeral in here today. If there's to be another I want notice.

GARBUTT: Coat on the stand, Mr Nowak, or coat on your back. I did say.

With two arms, Frank takes it off or does not. With one, he cannot get out of it alone, and the three of them watch him find that out.

DANNY (up at once): Here, nephew. I'll have that off you. I've had most things.

THE WIDOW (not moving): Leave him. He can ask.

Agar looks at the fire.

PLAYER (own words, or his hands). He can let Danny. He can ask one of them by name. He can work it off himself, button by button, which takes a full minute with nobody speaking. He can leave it on.

- Danny takes it: he hangs it on top of his own jacket. "There. We're sharing a peg."
- He asks the Widow: she gets up, undoes it as she would a parcel, and hangs it by its loop. "That's one."
- He asks Agar: Agar is so surprised that he does it, badly, and says "Not at all" to Garbutt.
- Himself: when it is on the stand Garbutt nods once.
- On his back: see Variants.

GARBUTT: Oldest firm first. Mr Agar.

Agar is seventy, pink, and soft-voiced. He has not touched his tea. He speaks to the head of the table.

AGAR: Perhaps you would tell Mr Nowek that the office's licence falls to be renewed at the January committee, and that Mr Sowerby could be brought to recommend. In consideration, the yard reverts to the freeholder at Lady Day, and the firm's cars keep off the cold-store road.

If Frank has the paper, the player can lay it on the cloth. It is one sheet with Agar's signature at the foot, as agent, on an option to buy the cold-store road. Agar looks at it for some time. Then, for the first time, he looks at Frank.

AGAR: Mr Nowak.

He has said it right.

- Hand it across. Agar folds it in three and puts it away. "The licence will be renewed. The yard may remain as it is. I think that is everything."
- Keep it. Frank puts it back in his pocket. "Then I must take it that it may one day be read." Agar gives no terms and can press none. They sit and look at one another.
- Counter. PLAYER (own words): he keeps the paper and names one thing (the yard, the licence, Ada's rent, the road). "That is a different conversation, Mr Nowak. But it is one I am able to have." He gives that one thing, and nothing else.

Without the paper, Frank takes Agar's terms or reaches for his coat.

GARBUTT: Mrs Jensen.

THE WIDOW: Five pound a car is five pound a car. The landing goes back to who had it. And whoever stands my gate, I call them.

If her load came home:

THE WIDOW: Four cases came to my door on Thursday night, counted, and not one opened. (To Garbutt.) I owe him for that. I don't care to owe. I'd as soon it was settled before I've had my tea.

- Take what she offers: the tax struck off his cars from tonight, or her name behind his gate entries on Friday. He says which.
- Take nothing. "I want nothing of yours." Her mouth goes thin. "Then I'm left owing, and I shall pay it my own way, and you'll not choose the day."
- Counter. PLAYER (own words): if he names Ron. "I never had a hand on him. I had a hook he could hang on. (A pause.) I'll not ask him again. If he asks me, that's his own."

Without the load: her terms as she said them. "And there's the haddock."

GARBUTT: Mr Cammack.

Danny has eaten four of the funeral's sandwiches.

DANNY: Top line. Half. And the ferry rank Saturdays. I'm not greedy, I'm just first.

If the man is at the sink, Garbutt tips his head at the hatch. Danny turns in his chair and sees one of his own up to the elbows in Grace Rinaldi's water.

DANNY: That's mine. What's he doing with his sleeves up?

THE YOUNG MAN (not turning round): She made me do the big pans.

- Give him back. "Take him home." Danny laughs, and means it less than usual. "Third line, then. A third. And I'll leave your board alone."
- Keep him. "He's on mine now." "Then he's told you things, and I'll have to change them all, and that's a week's work, nephew. I'll want paying for my week."
- Counter. PLAYER (own words): he says the thing the man told him (who has been paying Danny's crew since the Landing; where the brass key hangs at night). Danny stops chewing. He does not look at Fewson's empty place by the door, which is how everybody knows. "Who's been talking to you? No. I can see who." His terms come down to whatever Frank says next, and he remembers every word.

Without the man: his terms, or the coat.

GRACE (a plate through the hatch): He's gone grey. There's ham. It's only been to one funeral.

DANNY: Whose?

GRACE: Depends how you all get on.

When all three have been round, Garbutt lays both hands on the cloth.

GARBUTT: Then that's the table. Mr Agar first out; he's furthest to go.

They leave in that order, each taking a coat from the stand. The Widow, passing Frank's chair, looks at his boots.

Garbutt goes last. He is still wearing his.

GARBUTT: Nine tomorrow you've a man from Customs and Excise. I can do nothing for you there. I've known him eleven years and I've never once got him to sit down.

## What the player can do

- The coat: who has it off him, or nobody, or it stays on. Hands: his own, or his words. Sign: Garbutt's rule, a week old, and three coats on the stand. What follows: it is the thing the town tells.
- For each head: accept, defy or counter. Hands: lay a paper on a cloth; say a word about a load; look at a hatch. Sign: each head says terms aloud before anything is asked of Frank. What follows: with the thing fetched, he sets the terms or holds that head still through the last week. Without it, the terms are theirs.
- Eat a sandwich. Grace watches until he has.
- Say nothing to one of them. Garbutt gives it ten seconds. "Then Mr Nowak accepts."
- Ask for Garbutt's truce, if it has not been spent. "One. For one of them. A fortnight, which sees you past Saturday. Say which." It is the only way to hold a head he fetched nothing for.

## What the town remembers

- Grace heard every word through the hatch and repeats none. What she tells the caff at breakfast is the coat. "He'd one arm and three of them sat watching. Guess who had it off him." If it was himself: "A minute and ten. I had it on the wall clock."
- The young man at the sink goes home with whoever won him. If Frank kept him, he is on the board on Tuesday under a new line, and one of Danny's crew spits when he passes.
- Agar has been made to say a name correctly, and to somebody's face. Simon Ombler types "Nowak" from Tuesday.
- The Widow holds what was struck or what was refused. Left owing, she pays it on Friday at the gate, unasked, with her signature, and says, "That's us level. I didn't enjoy it."
- Danny holds the counter, if it was used, and who gave Frank the words. Fewson has taken the brass key off its nail by Wednesday, or Danny has moved it.
- Every head whose terms Frank took has them by the weekend: a notice on the yard gate, a car turned back at the dock, a name at the top of a board. Every head he defied with nothing in his hand is against him through the five mornings.
- Garbutt holds that everybody is still at the table. It is all he wanted.

## Variants by path

- Coat on. Frank sits in it, or stands. Garbutt: "Then we'll not keep you." Each head states terms once, to the room, and they stand as stated; he may answer none of them. All three are against him in the last week, and nobody lays a hand on him tonight. Going out, Danny: "It's a nice coat. I'd have kept it on."
- The arm was not lost. The coat is a matter of two seconds. The Widow still looks at his boots.
- A pact holds with one of them. That head's chair is beside Frank's. Garbutt skips it: "Mrs Jensen is spoken for." She says only: "For the present."
- Danny on the top line. He is on Frank's side, and the third chair is Fewson's. "Mr Fewson, for the new crew." Danny, to the cloth: "Since when?" Fewson's terms are Danny's old ones, without the jokes. The man at the sink is Fewson's to lose.
- He signed first. Agar opens differently: "I am not certain the committee can renew for a proprietor under charge. One would need to see." The paper is worth more for it.
