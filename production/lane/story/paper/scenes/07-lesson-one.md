# 07. Lesson one

- Where it sits: Act IIa, week two. The first of Ron's five lessons, and the start of the naming.
- What wakes it: Frank holds the second rung; the ferry rank is not yet his; it is daylight; car two is on the rank and Ron is on the door. Then either the player asks Ron to take a car down to the ferry rank, or Sheila says it to the room: "That rank wants a car stood on it and I've nobody to stand it."
- Who must be present: Ron Kirby. Sheila at the start, Brenda Laverack on her step, Mark Billany on the ferry rank. Fallback: none; this is Ron's. If the player never takes it up, Ron asks once, on a wet morning: "I'd stand that rank for you, boss. I can't get her there."

Frank is trying to get a third pair of hands that can move a car. He will give up his own seat at the wheel, which is the one thing he is better at than anybody on this street, and some of car two's paint.

## Staging

Quay Street and the dock road, mid-morning, drizzle.

Car two stands on the rank with a white square and a red L on its bonnet and another on its boot, tied on with string through the grille, because magnets will not hold on filler. Sheila found them at the back of the stationery drawer in a paper bag with the receipt still in it: 1984.

Ron is in the driving seat with the seat run right back and both hands on the wheel at ten to two. He has taken his cap off and laid it on the dash. On the passenger side, by Frank's right knee, is the handbrake.

The road ahead: down Quay Street to the fish dock corner, where an iron bollard leans; left along the dock wall past the ice house, whose doors are chained; over the dock railway, four wet rails set in cobbles with a white board that says STOP LOOK LISTEN; past the pen and the dock gate; and on to the ferry rank.

The camera sits low between the seats and shows both men and the road. In the windscreen the wipers go, one a beat behind the other.

The player's hands are few and exact. He can pull the handbrake. He can say one word that Ron will always obey, which is "Stop". And he can talk.

## The scene

SHEILA (in the doorway, with the paper bag): Mickey bought them. He had hopes.

She goes in. Ron sits.

RON: Door. Mirror. Belt.

He does each as he says it. Then he winds his window all the way down, and the drizzle comes in on his sleeve.

PLAYER (own words). If he asks about the window:

RON: For signalling.

He puts his right arm straight out of it, flat, palm forward, and holds it there.

RON: I am intending to move out.

He lets the clutch up. The car jumps a yard and dies. Across the pavement Brenda Laverack turns her hose off to give it her whole attention.

BRENDA: Big lad in a little car. (She waits until he has it started again.) Mind, I've always said it's not the size of the car.

RON (eyes front, arm out): Morning, Brenda.

The second time it holds. Car two goes down Quay Street at a walk, with a docker's arm out of it.

At the foot of the street the bollard is dead ahead. Ron steers toward it the way he would walk toward a man he expected to step aside.

- If the player says "Stop", the car stops as if it had hit a wall. Ron's cap slides off the dash into Frank's lap. The bollard is a foot from the bumper.
- If he pulls the handbrake, it slows and stalls, a yard off.
- If he does neither, car two takes the bollard on the near-side wing. It is not fast. It is a dent, and it stays on the car to the end of the game.

Either way Ron looks at the bollard for a while.

RON: That's the bollard. Mickey had that. Twice.

He finds reverse at the second try, and looks over the wrong shoulder.

RON: I'll say what's coming, boss. Then you'll know I've seen it.

He turns left onto the dock road with his arm out and turning in a slow circle, which is the signal for left in the book he learned it from. The needle comes up to eleven and stops there.

RON: Coming up on the ice house. (The chained doors go by.) That's the ice house. (A pause of one wiper.) Leaving the ice house.

This is the lesson. What Ron names, he has seen, and the player can leave alone. What he does not name, he has not seen.

RON: Coming up on the railway. Stop, look, listen.

He stops at the board, by himself, short and square. He looks left along rails that have grass in them. He looks right. He listens. The wipers go. Nothing is coming, and has not been for some time.

RON: Nowt coming.

Over the rails. A milk float comes up behind, whining, bottles ringing, pulls out and goes past them. Its driver lifts a hand.

RON: He knows the road.

A fish lorry begins to back out of a gateway ahead on the left, slowly, its reversing horn going. Ron says nothing about it. He is looking at his speed.

- "Stop": the car stops dead, two lengths short. The lorry driver leans out and gives an opinion of learners that takes in the L-plate, the firm and the weather.
- The handbrake: the same, softer, and Ron says, "I'd have seen him."
- Neither: the lorry stops for them, on its horn, and the opinion is longer.

RON: Coming up on a lorry. (After.) Leaving the lorry.

Then the dock wall opens on the right. There is a railed square of ground with a tin roof over half of it, and eight or nine men standing in it with their hands in their pockets, not together. Beyond it, the gate, and a man at the gate in a white hard hat, with a list.

Ron does not name it. He has both hands on the wheel and his eyes on the road and his arm in. The car goes on toward it at eleven.

The player has just been taught what silence means.

- If he says "Stop", Ron stops. Instantly, as taught, cap off the dash, dead level with the pen, with an L on the bonnet. Every man in the pen turns his head. One of them calls it, not unkindly: "Now then, Ron. They letting you out on your own?" Ron does not look. He looks at the road. Then, low, to the windscreen:

RON: I'd seen it. (He finds first.) I just didn't say it.

- If he pulls the handbrake, the same, slower, and the same words.
- If he does nothing, car two passes the pen at eleven miles an hour and nobody in it says a word. The men watch it go by. A hundred yards on:

RON: Leaving the gate.

He never says he was coming up on it.

The ferry rank is a painted bay by the ticket window with room for three. One car is on it already, and Mark Billany is leaning on its wing with his cap pushed back. Ron has to stop behind it. He brakes a length early, and then a yard at a time. The handbrake is the player's if he wants it.

Ron switches off. He takes his cap from wherever it has got to, puts it on, gets out, and goes and stands by car two's front wing with his hands in his pockets, squarely, facing the boat queue. It is the first thing he has done all morning that he knows how to do. The camera, from inside the car, sees it through the wet glass: nobody could move him.

BILLANY: Whose is that?

RON: Mickey's.

BILLANY: Is it. With that on the front?

RON: Aye.

Billany looks at him, and at the L, and goes back to leaning. Ron comes round to Frank's window and bends to it.

RON: This one's wanted on Quay Street. Get a third car on that board and stand on the ferry rank till they stop asking whose it is.

The Ledger takes it, in his words. He walks round and gets in on the passenger side, and puts his belt on, and waits to be driven home.

On the way back, with Frank at the wheel, he names nothing. If the player asks why:

RON: I only say it when it's me.

## What the player can do

- Say "Stop". Hands: one key. It always works and it is always total. Used where Ron had named the thing, he says "Aye," and sets off again; nothing is lost but his cap.
- Pull the handbrake. Hands: hold. Softer, slower, and Ron says he had seen it.
- Do nothing. Sign: Ron has not named what is ahead. What follows: a dent, a horn, an opinion. Each dent is kept on car two for good, and in the last week the tax inspector walks round the car and counts them.
- Talk. Ron on the arm signals: "It's in the book. I've had the book since I were called up. Never had the car." On the naming: "On the tugs they sing out what they see. Then nobody has to shout." On the pen, once, and no more: "I stood there. Thirty-six year." On Mickey's L-plates: nothing; he looks at the road.
- Take the wheel back. Hands: say so, or get out at any stop. The lesson ends where it is; Ron walks the rest and stands the rank. Nothing is lost. He will ask again in his own time.
- Come back for lesson two. Any dry morning, from Ron: "Right turns, the book says. I've only done lefts."

## What the town remembers

- Brenda has her line and gives it to the whole parade by noon. It is said to Frank's face for a week, by people who do not know they are quoting her.
- The lorry driver tells the fish dock that Mickey's is putting learners on the road now.
- The pen saw Ron Kirby stopped dead in front of them in a little car with a red L on it, or saw him drive past without turning his head. They hold whichever it was. Stopped: "Had him sat there like a parcel." Passed: "Went by us. Never looked." The man at the gate with the list is Frank Stathers, and he saw it too. A week on, at dawn, he opens with it (scene 08).
- Billany tells the Widow's people that Kirby stood the ferry rank for ten minutes under an L-plate and would not be stared off it. It is the first report she has that the old man is being put to use.
- Kevin, on the air, all week: "Car one. I'm behind car two on the dock road. I'll be a while."
- The naming is in the car from now on, every time Ron drives, and the player learns to drive by it: the thing Ron names is the thing he has seen. Four weeks later it is still the sound of the car.

## Variants by path

- Danny is on the board (scene 06, the top line or the third). He is leaning on the hatchback outside the caff as they go by, and calls: "I've been overtaken by a hearse, Ron." Ron, arm out: "It were in a hurry."
- The bollard was hit. Ron does not hit it again in any later lesson. He names it every time, coming up, at, and leaving.
- The ferry rank is Danny's already (the player waited at the board). The car on the rank is Fewson's, and the questions are his. Ron's answers are the same.
