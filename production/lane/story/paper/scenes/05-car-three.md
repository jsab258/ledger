# 05. Car three

- Where it sits: Act I, week one, the Thursday, late morning. Danny Cammack's first move, and the deed only Frank would do.
- What wakes it: Danny's people have something on the new owner (the speech on the rank has reached him, or the carbon found his lock-up); it is daylight; the rank is in use. The day before the rent.
- Who must be present: Danny Cammack, Craig Fewson, Ron Kirby, Sheila Dunn. Ada at her window and Shaun Hewson on his ladder are the street's eyes. Fallback: if the player leaves the car where it stands for a game day, Danny moves it himself and tells the street the new owner asked him nicely; Fewson is met beside him then, on the wall, the same.

Frank is trying to get his rank back with the street watching, and a name with it. He will give up a quiet first week, and risks a hiding and another man's car in the dock.

## Staging

Quay Street, about eleven. Dry, grey, a wind off the water that moves the painter's dust sheet.

A white hatchback with a body kit stands across Mickey's rank at an angle, nose to the kerb, so that car one cannot get on and car two cannot get off. Its doors are unlocked and the keys hang in the ignition. Across the top of the windscreen runs a green sun strip of the kind couples have, with a name each side. The driver's side says DANNY. The other side says nothing.

Kevin is double-parked in car one with the engine running, one hand flat on the horn and not pressing it.

On the low wall across the street sit two of Danny's crew. The nearer is Craig Fewson: thirty, heavy in the shoulder, a leather jacket zipped to the neck, looking at the hatchback and not at the office.

Inside, Danny Cammack is sitting on the counter with his heels on the customers' bench, eating a bacon sandwich out of its paper. He is twenty-seven, quick, gold at the neck. The day book is open beside his thigh and he is turning its pages back with one clean finger. Sheila is at her desk with her handbag on her knee. Ron is in the doorway, filling it.

The camera is wide in the street, so the wall, the window and the ladder are all in frame, and close at the counter.

## The scene

DANNY (not looking up from the book): Twenty to ten, boarding house, cold-store road. She's a lovely clear hand, Pauline. You could read that by a torch.

He looks up, pleased to see Frank, as if Frank had come into his office.

DANNY: Nephew. Mickey's runs the Hook, I heard. So I've come down to be run. (He nods at the window.) Don't mind the motor. Nobody'll touch it.

Frank does up the middle button of his jacket.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own):

- "Move it, Mr Cammack." "I will. I'm having my breakfast."
- "You're on my rank. That's eighty pence, and ten pence a minute after the first five." "Mickey used to say please. For about a fortnight."
- Nothing. He turns and walks out to the car.

Threatened, in the player's own words, Danny smiles past Frank at Ron. "Morning, Ron. You've got thinner."

The player can go on talking. Or he can walk out to the car.

When Frank's hand is on the driver's door, Fewson is off the wall and halfway across the road. Danny has come out to the step behind Frank with the sandwich.

DANNY: Sit down, Craig. I want to see what he does.

Fewson stops in the road. He goes back and sits. Danny crosses, wiping his fingers on the paper, and sits on the wall beside him.

Frank gets in, in the suit he brought for a funeral. The seat is set for a shorter man. The player has the car.

Ron comes off the doorway and walks beside the driver's window with one hand flat on the roof, as a man walks a load across a quay. He says nothing. Down the street he lengthens his stride and gets ahead of the bonnet. Where the cobbles stop and the coping stones begin, with no rail and three metres of brown water beyond, he turns and faces the car, and his hands come up: both palms beckoning, slow. Come on. Come on.

This is the only guide the player gets. The camera drops to the driver's eye line: bonnet, Ron, water.

- If the player stops short, round the corner by the chandler's, Ron lowers his hands and waits. Nothing more happens there.
- If he comes on, Ron steps aside off the line at the last and goes on beckoning with one hand, eyes on the front wheels. The wheels reach the coping. They go over it, one and then the other, and the car's belly comes down on the stone with a bang.

RON (his fist closes): Whoa.

- If the player brakes on the fist, the hatchback sits on the quay's edge with its front wheels hanging over the water, turning slowly.
- If he does not, Ron's other hand has the driver's door open before the nose dips. Frank goes out onto the stones on his shoulder, and the car goes in, lights first, and floats for longer than seems right.

Frank takes the keys. He walks back up Quay Street with Ron half a step behind him. Nobody on the parade is doing anything else. He goes in past the wall without looking at it, reaches over the counter and hangs the keys on the third hook, the bare one. He takes the chalk from the ledge and writes a third line on the board under the two that are there.

FRANK: It's on my rank, so it's on my board. Car three. Two pound forty to the quay. Sheila, put it in the book.

Sheila has her pen out already.

SHEILA: Minimum fare, eighty pence. Twenty-one minutes stood on our rank, first five free, sixteen at ten pence. Two pound forty. (She looks over her glasses at the doorway.) Does Mr Cammack want a receipt?

Danny is in the doorway. He looks at the board. He looks at the hook his keys are on, and for a moment longer at that. Then he turns to the street and tells it.

DANNY: He's moved my motor. (To Frank, delighted.) Mickey would've asked nicely for a fortnight and then said sorry.

He is laughing, and it is real. He takes a folded twenty from his shirt pocket and lays it on the counter.

SHEILA: I've not change for that.

DANNY: I know you've not. I've read your book. (To Frank.) Call it an account, nephew. I'll be on that rank again.

The twenty lies on the counter. The rent is eighteen, tomorrow. See what the player can do.

DANNY (over his shoulder): Craig. Fetch it.

Fewson walks down to the quay with every window on him. He does not get straight in. He opens the door, runs the seat back two notches to fit himself, tilts the mirror, and sets his hands on the wheel. Then he starts it and puts it in reverse. The front wheels spin in the air over the water. The car does not move.

DANNY (from the step, enjoying it): Leave my mirror alone.

If the car is in the dock, none of this plays. Danny stops laughing at the splash, puts the twenty back in his pocket and says, evenly, "That's a fortnight of your nights you owe me," and Fewson stands at the edge looking down at the sun strip under the water.

## What the player can do

- Talk, and leave it. The car stays. Sign: Kevin on the horn; Pauline, "Car one, I've a fare stood waiting." What follows: each game hour it stays costs a fare, and by tomorrow Danny has moved it and the street has his version.
- Drive it to the corner. What follows: the rank is clear and nobody is shamed. Danny pays £2.40 in coins and says, "He's tidied me up." Brenda's version by noon: "He moved it for him."
- Drive it to the edge on Ron's hands. Sign: the palms, then the fist. What follows: as written. This is the telling the town keeps.
- Drive through the fist. Sign: Ron's fist, and the bang of the sill on the stone. What follows: the dock; no fare; Danny moves against the office a week early; the Harbour Board's crane lifts it on Friday with the parade watching, and sends Mickey's the bill.
- The twenty, by one of Frank's lines. "Mrs Dunn, open Mr Cammack an account." The rent is paid by Cammack's money, Sheila writes "D. Cammack, on account, £17.60 in credit", and he parks there seven more times as of right. "Seventeen pound sixty. Count it." Frank counts it out of his own pocket if he has it; the twenty goes in the book as the firm's, and he has nearly nothing of his own for tomorrow. "Two forty. I don't sell my rank." Danny pats his pockets, has nothing smaller, and says, "Then I owe you." Sheila opens the debt book and writes him in, under Mickey's five. "That's the only one in there that'll pay," says Danny, "so you'll want to look after me."
- The car on the edge. It is front-wheel drive and cannot back itself off. Tow it with car two (Fewson has to come into Mickey's and ask for a rope); leave them to it (six men from the caff lift the front, Fewson calling the count); or hand Fewson the keys off the hook yourself, or say, "They're on the board, Mr Fewson. Ask Mrs Dunn." What follows: how Fewson was treated goes with him.

## What the town remembers

- Ada saw all of it from her window; Shaun from the ladder; Brenda from her step with a haddock in her hand. By noon it is said to Frank's face: "You're never him that put Cammack's motor on the edge." Lee Drewery has it third-hand: "A man from Mickey's did it. Was it Ron?" The player can leave Ron's name on it or claim it.
- Danny tells it himself, better than anyone, at the caff: the suit, the sun strip, the wheels going round. It is his best story for a week, which is why he comes back.
- The board keeps CAR 3. Sheila does not rub it out. Under CAR 2 the old paint still shows the ghost of a name that was chalked there for years and rubbed off with a sleeve; the player may not read it yet.
- Danny saw which hook his keys went on. A week later he comes for Frank in Mickey's own car (scene 06).
- Fewson holds what was done to him in front of the street: towed, left, handed the keys or made to ask. He set that seat to fit himself, and Danny saw. In week five he has one of Frank's men in a van; in the yard, or on the quay at the end, he is there with this morning on him.

## Variants by path

- He told Ron no on Monday. Danny's opening changes: "No envelope, I hear. So who's reading your book now, nephew? I'll tell you. Me."
- Ron is not on the door (sent on a debt). Nobody walks the car down, and the edge is harder to find than the water.
- The corner, or the dock. Frank chalks the third line on his way in all the same.
- The ask first reached him on Wednesday. Danny comes on the Friday, and the fare he reads out is Wednesday's.
