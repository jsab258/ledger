# 04. The line in the book

- Where it sits: Act I, week one, the morning after the first ask that reached Frank (the Tuesday, when night one was played straight).
- What wakes it: an ask reached him last night, whatever he did with it; it is morning; Sheila is at the counter cashing up; the day book holds Pauline's half line. Frank comes within reach of the counter.
- Who must be present: Sheila Dunn. Pauline Kitching arrives at the end. Fallback: if Frank keeps away from the counter, Sheila asks it from the foot of the stairs at noon; if nobody has answered by five she finishes the line herself, and it is stored as the third kind below.

Frank is trying to get the morning's paper off the counter and himself out of the door, where £18 has to be found by Friday. He will give up £2.80 of his own, or the say-so over a line, without knowing yet that he has given up anything.

## Staging

The office, a quarter past eight. Grey daylight through wet glass and the strip light on over the counter, humming. The street outside is being hosed down in front of the fish shop.

On the counter: the day book, open at last night. It is a duplicate book, each white sheet with a pink one under it; last night's pink has been torn out along its perforation and the white stays. The page is ruled in six columns with printed heads: Time, From, To, Car, £, Booked by. Beside it Terry's night bag, a blue cloth bag with a drawstring, emptied into piles; a stack of clear bank bags; an adding machine with a paper roll; a mug of tea with the bag still in.

Sheila is ticking each line of the sheet with a pencil against a pile of coins, and bagging as she goes: pound coins in twenties, fifties in tens. She does it without looking at her hands.

Through the window Ron's back is against the wall beside the door, where it was last night.

Sound: the adding machine's handle, coins, the strip light, the open radio channel hissing with nobody on it.

The camera sits at Frank's shoulder across the counter. It can read the book whenever the player looks down at it. This is the only thing in the room that will matter later, and nothing in the room says so.

## The scene

SHEILA (to the coins): Ten fares. Twenty-four sixty. Half's the drivers'. Twelve thirty to the tin.

She cranks the handle and tears the slip, pins it to the sheet, and runs her pencil down the last column of ticks to the bottom of the page. The pencil stops. She does not look up; her other hand is already reaching for a bank bag.

SHEILA: Pauline's started a line and not finished it. Ten o'clock, booked by Ron, then nothing. I can't carry half a line forward. What was it?

The camera can read the line she means: 10.00, Office, three empty columns, R.K.

PLAYER (own words). She reads back whatever he says in book words, pen held a little above the paper, and waits for a plain yes. Anything but a plain yes and she says, "Then say it how you want it," and he can say it again. The scene reads three kinds of answer.

1. A fare, or any account that is not the envelope (a run to the ferry, a walk, an errand, a favour for Ron). She makes a booking of it, because that is the only thing the page is ruled for.

SHEILA: Ten o'clock. Office to ferry landing and return. Waited. Cash. (She looks up over her glasses.) That's what you want in?

On a plain yes she writes it, in ink, across the three empty columns. Then she pulls the adding machine round.

SHEILA: It's a mile and a bit there and back. Two pound ten. Waiting's ten pence a minute after the first five, and I'll say you waited twelve. Two pound eighty.

She holds her hand out flat across the counter, palm up, and goes on reading the slip with her eyes.

SHEILA: The book says the tin had two pound eighty off that job. So the tin wants two pound eighty.

Frank pays it out of his own pocket. The player watches £23.50 become £20.70 three days before the rent. She drops the coins into the fifties bag and the pound bag, where they belong.

SHEILA: I don't mind who the passenger was, love. I mind that he paid.

2. The envelope, said as it was (a packet to the landing, Mickey's arrangement, the man who asks for Mickey's).

SHEILA: Ten o'clock. Office to ferry landing. One packet, by hand. Mickey's account. No charge. (Over her glasses.) That's what you want in?

On a plain yes her pen stays where it is for a moment longer than writing needs. Then she writes it, smaller than the lines above, and blots it.

SHEILA: That account's been shut three weeks. I'll have to open it again.

She turns back a page to a list inside the cover, writes one word beside a name there, and turns forward. She asks for no money, and none is owed.

3. Nothing ("leave it", "put what you like", "that's my business", or twenty seconds without a word).

SHEILA: I can't leave half a line, love. If you've nothing to say, I'll say it.

She turns the book a quarter toward herself and curls her left hand round the top of the page, as a person does who was taught young to guard her sums. She writes one line, quickly, in a hand that has written it before. She blots it and turns the page over to today's clean sheet.

SHEILA: There. It's a whole line.

If he asks what she wrote:

SHEILA: What I always write.

Whichever it was, the line is whole and the page is finished. She initials the foot of the sheet, S.D., and squares the bank bags into the tin.

From under the counter she brings a second book, older, with a cloth spine, and puts it on the counter between them with the flat of her hand on it.

SHEILA: That's the other book. A hundred and fifty-six pounds, five names, and not one of them has it. Your eighteen's in there somewhere. Ron knows the doors.

The street door opens. Pauline comes in backwards, shaking an umbrella out onto the pavement, and hangs her coat on the hook next to Ron's.

PAULINE: Morning. Did somebody finish my line?

SHEILA: Somebody did.

PAULINE (to Frank, reaching past him for the kettle): I only write what I'm told, love. He said he was off the rank at ten, so I wrote him off the rank at ten. What he was off it for's upstairs' business.

She puts her headphones round her neck and sits down to the radio.

PAULINE: Car one, Kevin, are you out of your bed?

## What the player can do

- Answer. Hands: his own words, then a plain yes. Sign: none beyond what the night gave him: a torch stopping on this same line at the landing. What follows: the line is stored word for word, with its kind and the facts in it (where, what, for whom, what was paid).
- Change the answer before the yes. She reads the new one back the same way. After the yes it is ink.
- Read the page. Hands: look down. The ten fares above are all in Pauline's blue with P.K. in the last column. The one line that is not a fare has R.K. No column on the page is headed for an owner.
- Ask what Mickey put. "Mickey didn't have a line. Mickey went himself, and Pauline was told he'd gone for a paper." "At ten at night?" "He liked a late paper."
- Ask her to rub out Pauline's half. "It's ink, love. And it's on the pink as well, and the pink's gone."
- Keep away from the counter. She asks from the stairs at noon, the same words. By five the line is hers.
- Pay, in the first kind. He cannot refuse it and keep the line: "Then it wasn't a fare, and you'd better tell me what it was." If he has spent below £2.80 already, she writes the fare and writes him into the debt book beneath Mickey's five names. "That's six."

## What the town remembers

- Nobody saw it but Sheila, and Sheila tells nobody. Nothing is said to his face about it that day or that week. It is meant to be forgotten.
- Pauline holds that somebody finished her line, and that Ron was off the rank at ten. On the nights of the later asks she writes the same half line without being told, and Sheila finishes each the way the first was finished. By the end of week one there are up to four of them in the book, alike.
- Sheila, on Sunday, with the week's sheets in front of her, reads the nights back before she asks what the firm is to be: "Monday, the landing. Wednesday, the landing. Friday, the landing." She does not yet say the name that stands beside each.
- It returns three times. In week three her pen is up over the first night money and she turns back to this page: "Same as your first night?" (scene 09). In week five DS Ellis reads the line aloud in the searched office and holds out her pen. In the last week Mr Hebblewhite keeps week one for his fifth morning.
- A fare paid from his own pocket is remembered by the tin. On Friday, if he is short for the rent, Sheila counts what there is and says, "You'd have had it but for a waiting fare."

## Variants by path

- He told Ron no last night. The half line is the same; it was Ron who was off the rank. The first kind reads back as written. The second: "Ten o'clock. R. Kirby, office to ferry landing. Message, by hand. No charge." Ron's name is then in the line itself as well as in its last column.
- He stood at the landing and would not answer to Mickey's. As written; the envelope came back up the street, and in the second kind she says, "Not delivered," and writes that too.
- The ask reached him first on Wednesday. This scene plays on Thursday morning, word for word, with Wednesday's sheet.
