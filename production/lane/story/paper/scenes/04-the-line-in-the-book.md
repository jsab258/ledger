# 04. The line in the book

- Where it sits: Act I, week one, the morning after the first ask that reached Frank (the Tuesday, when night one was played straight).
- What wakes it: an ask reached him last night, whatever he did with it; it is morning; Sheila is cashing up; the day book holds a line Pauline began and did not finish; Frank comes within reach of the counter.
- Who must be present: Sheila Dunn, Pauline Kitching, and Ron Kirby, in for his tea. Fallback: if Frank keeps away from the counter, Sheila asks it from the foot of the stairs at noon; if nobody has answered by five she finishes the line herself, and it is stored as the third kind below. If Ron was sent out early, his lines are not said.

Frank is trying to get the morning's paper off the counter and himself out of the door, where £18 has to be found by Friday. He will give up £2.80 of his own, or the say-so over one line, and think no more about it.

## Staging

The office, a quarter past eight. Grey daylight through wet glass and the strip light on over the counter, humming. Outside the fish shop is hosing its step.

On the counter: the day book, open at last night. It is a duplicate book, each white sheet with a pink one under it; last night's pink has been torn out and the white stays. The page is ruled in six columns with printed heads: Time, From, To, Car, £, Booked by. Beside it Terry's night bag, a blue cloth bag with a drawstring, emptied into piles; a stack of clear bank bags; an adding machine with a handle.

Sheila is ticking each line of the sheet with a pencil against a pile of coins and bagging as she goes. She does not talk while she counts.

Pauline is making the tea and has not stopped talking since she took her coat off.

Ron is inside the door, where he stands for ten minutes every morning, with a big white mug that has R.K. on its base in red nail varnish. Pauline did that, so nobody else would use it.

The camera sits at Frank's shoulder across the counter and can read the book whenever he looks down.

## The scene

PAULINE: ...so my sister's to keep it up on a stool, and I said, you've never sat still in your life. Sugar's in. Is that him down? Morning. There's one in the pot if you don't mind it stewed, Mickey liked it stewed, you could have tarred a boat with it.

SHEILA (to the coins): Ten fares. Twenty-four sixty. Half's the drivers'. Twelve thirty to the tin.

She cranks the handle, tears the slip, pins it to the sheet, and runs her pencil down the ticks to the bottom of the page. The pencil stops.

SHEILA: Pauline. You've started a line and not finished it.

PAULINE: Have I? Which? Oh, him. (She nods at Ron.) He said he was off the rank at ten, so I put him off the rank at ten, and then the phone went.

The camera can read it: 10.00, Office, three empty columns, R.K.

SHEILA: Off the rank where?

RON (into the mug): I weren't off it. I were at the wall.

SHEILA: Is the wall the rank?

RON: It's forty yard off it.

SHEILA: Then you were off the rank. And a man's off the rank for a job, or he's off it for nothing, and I don't carry nothing forward. What was it?

RON: That's the book. Book's not mine to say.

He takes his mug out to the step and pulls the door to behind him. Through the glass his back settles against the wall.

SHEILA (turning the pencil toward Frank without looking up): He brought it to you. What was it?

FRANK (the player picks one of his lines, or speaks his own):

- "A fare. Landing and back. I'll pay it."
- "Mickey's envelope. To the landing. Write that."
- "Not my line. Put what Mickey put."

Whatever he says, she reads it back in the words the page is ruled for, pen an inch above the paper, and waits for a plain yes. After the yes it is ink.

A fare, or any account that is not the envelope.

SHEILA: Ten o'clock. Office to ferry landing and return. Waited. Cash. That's what you want in?

FRANK: Yes.

She writes it across the three empty columns. Then she pulls the adding machine round.

SHEILA: A mile and a bit there and back. Two pound ten. Waiting's ten pence a minute after the first five, and I'll say you waited twelve. Two pound eighty.

She holds her hand out flat across the counter, palm up, and goes on reading the slip.

SHEILA: The book says the tin had two pound eighty off that job. So the tin wants two pound eighty.

Frank counts it out of his own pocket.

PAULINE: So Ron's booked a fare. First in thirty-six year. Does he get the half?

SHEILA: The driver gets the half.

PAULINE: Who drove?

Nobody says who drove.

SHEILA: Then there's no half.

FRANK: That leaves me twenty pound seventy.

SHEILA: It does. I don't mind who the passenger was, love. I mind that he paid.

The envelope, said as it was.

SHEILA: Ten o'clock. Office to ferry landing. One packet, by hand. Mickey's account. No charge. That's what you want in?

Pauline has stopped with the kettle in her hand.

FRANK: Yes.

Her pen stays where it is a moment longer than writing needs. Then she writes it, smaller than the lines above, and blots it.

SHEILA: That account's been shut three weeks. I'll have to open it again.

For once Pauline has nothing to say. It lasts as long as it takes her to reach the tap.

PAULINE: Well. I'll fill this.

Nothing, or "put what Mickey put".

SHEILA: Mickey didn't have a line. Mickey went himself, and Pauline was told he'd gone for a paper.

PAULINE: At ten at night. Every other night. I never knew a man read so much.

SHEILA: I can't leave half a line. If you've nothing to say, I'll say it.

She turns the book a quarter toward herself and curls her left hand round the top of the page, as a person does who was taught young to guard her sums. She writes one line, quickly, in a hand that has written it before. Pauline leans. Sheila moves her elbow.

PAULINE: What's it say?

SHEILA: It says a whole line.

She blots it and turns the page.

In every case she initials the foot of the sheet, S.D., and squares the bank bags into the tin. From under the counter she brings a second book, older, with a cloth spine, and lays the flat of her hand on it.

SHEILA: That's the other book. A hundred and fifty-six pounds, five names, and not one of them has it. Your eighteen's in there somewhere. Ron knows the doors.

Outside, Ron finishes his tea, comes in to rinse the mug, stands it upside down with its letters showing, and puts his cap on.

PAULINE (already back at the radio, to nobody): I just put what gets shouted at me. I never ask what for. I'd be here while Christmas. Car one, Kevin, are you out of your bed?

## What the player can do

- Say which it was. Hands: one of Frank's lines or his own words, then a yes. Sign: nothing beyond what the night gave him, which was a torch stopping on this line at the landing. What follows: the line is kept word for word, with what kind it is and what is in it (where, what, for whom, what was paid).
- Change it before the yes. She reads the new one back the same way.
- Read the page. Hands: look down. The ten fares above are in Pauline's blue with P.K. in the last column. The one line that is not a fare has R.K. No column on the page is headed for an owner.
- Ask her to rub out Pauline's half. "It's ink, love. And it's on the pink as well, and the pink's gone."
- Keep away from the counter. She asks from the stairs at noon, the same words. By five the line is hers.
- Pay, in the first case. He cannot refuse it and keep the line: "Then it wasn't a fare, and you'd better tell me what it was." If he has less than £2.80 left, she writes the fare and then writes him into the debt book under Mickey's five names. "That's six."

## What the town remembers

- Sheila tells nobody. Pauline tells everybody, and what she tells is the argument: "Forty yard. He stood there with his tea and said forty yard, as if he'd paced it." Nobody outside the office hears what was written.
- Ron went out to the step on purpose. He does not know what was written after his letters, and he chose not to. Five weeks on, in a car, he asks (scene 12). It is the only time.
- On the later nights of the ask Pauline writes the same half line without being told, and Sheila finishes each the way the first was finished. By Sunday there are up to four in the book, alike.
- It comes back three times. In week three Sheila turns back to this page: "Same as your first night?" (scene 09). In week five DS Ellis reads the line aloud in the searched office and holds out her pen. On the last Saturday a man from Customs and Excise asks which of two entries is the firm's.
- A fare paid from his own pocket is remembered by the tin. On Friday, if he is short for the rent, Sheila counts what there is and says, "You'd have had it but for a waiting fare."

## Variants by path

- He told Ron no last night. The half line is the same; it was Ron who was off the rank, walking the answer down. The fare reads back as written. The truth reads: "Ten o'clock. R. Kirby, office to ferry landing. Message, by hand. No charge."
- He stood at the landing and would not answer to Mickey's. As written; in the second case she adds, "Not delivered," and writes that too.
- The ask reached him first on Wednesday. This scene plays on Thursday morning, word for word, with Wednesday's sheet.
