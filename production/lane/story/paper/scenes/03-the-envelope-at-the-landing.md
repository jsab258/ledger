# 03. The envelope at the landing

- Where it sits: Act I, week one, the Monday night, between ten and one.
- What wakes it: Frank comes onto the ferry landing with the envelope on him while Mickey's arrangement stands.
- Who must be present: the old firm's man, who is by the ticket window from ten till one on every ask night. He has no name and no cast voice; his lines show as plain text. There is no fallback carrier and none is needed. If Frank told Ron no, this scene is not played: Ron comes down alone (see Variants).

Frank is trying to be dealt with as the owner, not as a dead man's errand. To hand the thing over he has to give up his own name and answer to another's.

## Staging

The ferry landing, a little after ten. Rain. The last boat lies against the floating stage with her deck lights on and her engine turning over; she sails at twenty to eleven, and until then her lights are most of what there is to see by. After that there is one lamp, over the glass case with the timetable in it.

The ticket window is shuttered. Karen Grummitt is on the inside of it cashing up; a line of light shows under the shutter and sometimes the sound of coins. The brow runs down to the stage and creaks as the stage lifts. At the kerb a plain van stands with no lights and its engine off. Somebody is in it: a cigarette brightens and fades behind the windscreen.

Across the basin the timber ship lies dark against the wall with one cabin light.

The man stands under the overhang beside the shutter, out of the lamp: a raincoat, a scarf, a cap, a cigarette cupped in his hand the way men smoke who have worked outdoors. He is past sixty. In his top pocket is a pencil torch.

In Frank's hands: the envelope. On his back the dark coat, or the suit he came in.

The camera stays behind Frank's shoulder and moves in when the torch comes out. It may read the carbon when the torch is on it. It never shows what happens up the cold-store road.

## The scene

Frank comes down past the timetable. The man does not move from the wall.

THE MAN: Mickey's?

PLAYER (own words). The scene reads one of four kinds.

- A yes, a nod, or the envelope held out without a word. The man takes it. If nothing was said: "That'll do for a yes."
- His own name, or that he is the owner:

THE MAN: I didn't ask who you were. Mickey's?

He will ask a third time. If Frank still will not answer to it:

THE MAN: Then you've no business down here.

He turns his shoulder. The handover is over for tonight and counts as a night Frank stayed away, although he stood there.

- A question, any question: "I don't do talking." Then, again: "Mickey's?"
- Nothing in his hands (he left it, or took the carbon out and brought the envelope empty; the man weighs it on two fingers before he opens it): "Nothing for me? Then you've no business down here."

When the envelope is in the man's hands he does something Ron did not say he would. He does not put it away. He steps out to the lamp over the timetable, untucks the flap with his thumbnail, draws out the pink sheet and unfolds it against the glass, over the sailing times. The pencil torch clicks. The camera reads it with him: Pauline's hand, in blue, ghosted by the carbon. A time, a pick-up, a drop, a price. He goes down it with the torch, and his lips move. He is counting.

THE MAN: Eleven.

The torch goes back up the sheet and stops two lines from the bottom. The camera reads the line it stops on: 9.40, boarding house, cold-store road, 2.10. He does not turn round. He says it flat, to the van, whose window is down an inch.

THE MAN: Boarding house. Cold-store road. Twenty to ten.

The van's engine starts. It pulls out from the kerb without lights and goes up toward the cold-store road, in no hurry. Its tail is gone before the sound is.

The man has not looked at Frank. The torch moves down to the last line of the sheet and stops there too. The camera reads it: 10.00, Office, and then three empty columns, and in the last column, R.K.

He looks at that for some time. Then he looks at Frank: at the coat if Frank is wearing it, at the face if he is not.

In the coat:

THE MAN: Somebody's started writing you down.

In the suit, the torch comes up into Frank's face for a second first, and goes off.

THE MAN: So that's you. (A breath of smoke.) Somebody's started writing you down.

He folds the carbon in four and puts it inside his raincoat. He holds the envelope out again, empty, flap open.

THE MAN: He always had that back.

Frank takes it. It weighs nothing.

THE MAN: Right. Wednesday, same again.

If the player tries to talk now:

THE MAN: You've done your bit. Go home.

Under the shutter the line of light goes out. A side door opens and Karen comes out belting her jacket, key in her mouth, and locks up. She looks once along the landing, at a man in a cap and whoever is with him, and walks up toward the boarding house with her head down against the rain.

The boat sounds. Her ropes come in, and she takes her lights away across the basin. What is left is the lamp on the timetable, the rain going through it, and a cigarette end under the overhang. At the top of the street, by the wall, a big man is standing with his hands in his pockets.

## What the player can do

- Answer to "Mickey's" and hand it over. Hands: hold it out. Sign: Ron told him the man would say it. What follows: the arrangement stands; the next ask is Wednesday; the empty envelope is in his pocket, and Ron takes it from him at the wall without a word and puts it away for Wednesday.
- Insist on his own name. Sign: the man asks three times, the same word. What follows: no handover; it counts as staying away; tomorrow the town has "they waited on him at the landing".
- Hand it over opened and read. Nothing changes in the man's lines. The player watches the torch already knowing what it will find, and sees which line it stops on.
- Bring it empty. What follows: the man's line; it counts as staying away, and on Wednesday he opens with, "You kept us waiting last time. Don't make a habit of it."
- Follow the van. Hands: on foot, or back up the street for car two. Sign: it leaves slowly and unlit. What follows: he will not catch it on foot. By car he can find it parked, empty, outside a dark warehouse on the cold-store road with its doors shut, and Norman Dent's torch coming along the row. Nothing more is shown tonight. The Ledger keeps one line: a van, the cold-store road, after a fare from the boarding house.
- Stay and watch him. The man stands till one, smokes, and says "I've nothing to say to you" if pressed.

## What the town remembers

- The man himself tells it first, at his breakfast in the caff, to Darren: "Mickey's nephew brought the envelope down the landing." By the middle of the morning Darren says it to Frank's face: "Heard you did Mickey's run." If Frank would not answer to the name: "Heard they waited on you at the landing."
- Karen saw a coat, or, if he wore the suit, the man who got off her boat that morning. In the coat she holds a shape and tells nobody. In the suit she tells Sylvia Rennison at the boarding house that the new owner keeps Mickey's hours, and it is on the parade by Wednesday. Either way she has now seen him at the landing once, which is half of what opens her own story later.
- Terry Hodgson, coming back empty down the cold-store road at ten past ten, was passed by a van with no lights. He tells Kevin at the morning change, and Kevin tells the Widow's people, because Kevin does.
- Sylvia Rennison has a lodger, one of Danny Cammack's, whose bed was not slept in. He is back on Wednesday with his hand strapped and nothing to say. When Frank meets Sylvia on her step in Friday means Friday, this is the lodger.
- Danny Cammack knows by Tuesday night that somebody found his lock-up, and how. It is the first reason he walks into Mickey's on Thursday and sits on the counter (scene 05). "You've got the book, nephew."
- The half line is now in two places: in the day book on the counter, and on a pink sheet in the old firm's keeping, with Ron's initials in its last column. Sheila meets the first in the morning (scene 04). The second comes back in week five, in an old man's coat, in a police station.

## Variants by path

- Frank told Ron no. Ron walked it down. The player, if he follows at a distance, sees it from the wall with no words: Ron stops in front of the overhang, holds the envelope out, says one sentence, and stands while the man reads the sheet under the lamp exactly as above, van and all. The torch stops on the last line. The man says something; Ron says nothing; Ron walks back up. Asked what was said, Ron answers once: "He said they're done with us. And he said Pauline writes a neat hand." From then the man's line to Frank is, "Ron's been down. We're done, you and us."
- The ask passed unasked tonight (Ron never found him). If Frank comes down anyway the man says, "Nothing for me? Then you've no business down here." The scene plays on Wednesday as written, and "Wednesday, same again" becomes "Friday".
