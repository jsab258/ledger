# 22. Close: the Straight Life

- Where it sits: the close, Saturday 1 December, from five until a car pulls away from the kerb, some time after eight.
- What wakes it: at five Frank wrote the line true, ruled the fourth column and put his name at its head. The firm does not survive its own books. A friend still counts him: Ron, Sheila, Alison or Philip Danby.
- Who must be present: Sheila, Pauline, DS Ellis, Ron. Up to five who each hold a deed of his. Fallback: if Sheila left the firm weeks ago, the red book was never opened and the last word in the office is Pauline's; Ron drives in every case.

Frank is trying to get out of his own office with nothing in his hands. He has given up the firm to do it, an hour ago, with a ruler.

## Staging

The office, the strip light, the blind half down. The board has been wiped, not washed; the names can still be read in the paint.

Two books lie open on the counter, side by side, and nobody has shut them. One is ruled in six columns. The other is red, ruled by hand in three, with a fourth drawn down its edge an hour ago in fresh ink.

Over the door the sign says MICKEY'S.

Down the dock road a lorry's lamps stand at the gate.

At the kerb is car two. It has a white square with a red L tied to its bonnet with string, and another on the boot. The engine is running.

The camera stays inside until he leaves, and then rides low between the seats, where it sat in every lesson.

## The scene

Sheila has her coat on. Her handbag is over her arm, and it is lighter; the knitting has room. She looks at the red book lying open under the light where anybody could read it, and she does not reach to close it.

SHEILA: First line in thirty-one years I'd let anybody read.

She squares her pen with the edge of the counter and leaves it there.

SHEILA: Goodnight, Frank.

If she has said his name before, this is not the first time. If she never has, it is.

Ellis is at the door with her case. She has a certified copy of a page that now has four hands on it.

ELLIS: The same way twice, Mr Nowak. I'll say that for you.

Pauline tears the last pink sheet off the pad and looks at it.

PAULINE: All cars. That's your lot. Bring them in. (She lets the switch go.) There's a line on here I've started and can't finish. Twenty to six, Office, and him outside says he's booked. He won't tell me where to.

From the drawer under the counter she takes a brown window envelope gone soft as cloth, puts the pink sheet in it, and holds it out.

PAULINE: Have the envelope and all. He never bought one in his life.

Through the window of the envelope the last line of the sheet can be read: 5.40, Office, three empty columns, R.K.

Outside, as he crosses the pavement, each of these has one thing.

- Lily Tang, come down from the takeaway with a carrier bag, puts it through the car's open window onto the back seat. "Two of number nine. I remember. Everybody thinks I don't." She takes the money for them and counts his change.
- The Man in Black, passing on his way down to the gate, lifts his hat an inch. "Seven. Half a firm. I've marked the half I saw."
- Father Walsh, at the corner: "A parishioner of mine was let out at eight. I'm told he walked straight here."

Nobody sounds the horn. Frank gets in on the passenger side.

Ron is in the driving seat in the black overcoat, both hands on the wheel, cap on the dash. If Mickey's watch was ever redeemed it is on his wrist.

RON: I told you to keep the rank.

FRANK: I heard you.

Ron's left hand comes down between the seats, past Frank's knee, to the lever that was Frank's from the first lesson, and he lets the handbrake off, as he was taught.

He puts his arm out of the window. Car two goes down Quay Street at fourteen miles an hour.

He does not name the bollard. He does not name the ice house. The chained doors go by on the left and the car is as quiet as it has been since the middle of November.

At the end of the dock road the gate's two leaves are shut. Stathers is passing a chain through them. A man from the Harbour Board is wiring a notice to the ironwork. The pen stands empty under its roof. A woman in a fur collar sits on a kitchen chair on the cobbles, watching it done.

Ron slows for it. He does not stop.

RON: Coming up on the dock gate.

The lorry's lamps go across the windscreen.

RON: That's the gate.

The lock closes behind them.

RON: Leaving the gate.

The road goes on along the wall, away from the Hook. He has never driven it. The envelope is on the dash with its window toward them and a line in it that has no destination.

RON: Where to, Frankie?

## What the player can do

- Take the envelope from Pauline, or leave it on the counter with the two open books.
- Shut the red book before he goes. Sheila, from the door: "Leave it, love."
- Pay Lily. He has to; she will not let the bag go otherwise.
- Say "Stop", or name a building himself, on the drive. Ron stops, or says "Aye."
- Answer the last question, with one of Frank's lines ("Straight on, Ron." / "You pick.") or his own. Whatever he says, it is the drop that finishes the line, and nobody is left to write it down. The car goes there.

## What the town remembers

- Sheila left the true book open on the counter and went home. On Monday whoever opens the office finds it, and reads it.
- Lily counted two.
- Stathers, with the padlock in his hand, saw Mickey's car go by with an L on it and heard a man tell his gate what it was. He tells the Widow. She says, "That's Ron," and then, of the other: "And that's a man who's short of nothing I can sell him."
- The fourteenth of December, ten past two. Ron keeps it, or does not; the game does not say.

## Variants by path

- Somebody took the firm over first. An offer was made on Tuesday evening by Julie Kettlewell, or whoever had stood by him longest, and he said yes to it before five. The board is not wiped. Pauline does not say "That's your lot"; she says, "Car four, the Tivoli," and Julie, at the night desk, answers the telephone: "Mickey's. Two minutes. Real ones." The paint over the door is fresh. The half line and the envelope are the same.
- Nobody took it over. The cars are sold to pay the assessment, and the bay is whitewashed. Shaun has not been told to take the sign down, and will not until he is.
- Frank signed in week five. Ron has been on the door and silent for a fortnight. The car, the drive and the gate are the same, and the gate is the first thing he has named since the night between.
- Frank has number 39 from the Widow. He can hold it out in the car. Ron takes it, looks at it, and as they pass the gate hangs it by its hole on the end of Stathers's chain. "It can't be both."
- He is hunted for a killing. The car is at the kerb with its engine running and Ron in it, and nobody comes out of the office. Pauline, to Sheila: "He'll not wait all night." Sheila: "He will." The last line is Ron's, to the empty seat: "Where to."
