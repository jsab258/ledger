# 19. The quay at four

- Where it sits: Act III, the last day, Saturday 1 December, an hour before the books open. The opponent, faced in person.
- What wakes it: the inspector has asked for week one; it is afternoon; the day book is not on the counter. Danny Cammack has turned, from a cause the player made and watched (his name chalked third, or nowhere, and asked for twice more), and has the brass key he never gave back. If Danny was given the top line and stayed, Craig Fewson has taken the key off him; see Variants.
- Who must be present: Danny, or Fewson. Pauline, on the air. Ron, if he is free, at Frank's shoulder. The wall: Ada's window, Shaun's ladder, Brenda's step. Fallback: none is needed; one of the two men is always alive, and car two cannot be lost.

Frank is trying to get the firm's book back with an hour in hand. He will give up skin for it, with one arm if that is what he has; and then he has to say what the man at the edge is worth to him.

## Staging

It starts in the office. The strip light is on already; it is that time of year. Sheila is standing at the counter with her hands flat on it, either side of a clean oblong where a book has lain for thirty-one years. Car two's key is on its hook.

Then the quay. A quarter to four and the lamps coming on. High water, slack, a foot under the coping.

Car two is on the edge where the hatchback sat in week one: front wheels over, sill on the stone, its yellow sign lit against a sky the same colour as the river. The driver's door is open. Danny is in the seat sideways with one foot on the coping and the day book open across his knee, turning it back with one finger. He has chips in paper on the dash.

The wall has filled, as it did for car three. Craig Fewson is on it, in the same place, in the same jacket.

The camera is wide, low, from the landward side: the two men, the car, the edge, the water. It does not cut away from a blow.

## The scene

PAULINE (in the office, to the microphone): Car two? (Nothing.) Car two, your keys are on my hook. (She takes her hand off the switch and looks at Frank.) Car two is not answering.

SHEILA: He came in for change for the telephone. I turned round for the tin.

On the quay Danny looks up from the page, pleased, exactly as he did from the counter six weeks ago.

DANNY: Twenty to ten, boarding house, cold-store road. I'll never get tired of that one. (He lifts the handset off its clip.) Car two answering, Pauline. He's found me.

One line here is the car's. If somebody has run her every morning since Ron went in, or Ron is free: "Started first turn. Somebody's been sat in her every morning with the handbrake on. He's got you trained." If nobody has: "Flat as a plate. I'd to bump her down the street. He'd never have let her go flat. Where is he? (A beat.) Oh, aye."

One line is the night between's.

- Ron went in: "Mickey's man's doing Mickey's time, and Mickey's nephew's stood on a quay with his hands in his pockets."
- Frank signed: "Charged, and still come down himself. I'll give him that. Mickey'd have sent Ron."
- Wardell was paid: he says it to the wall, loud. "Ask him what two hundred pound buys at the station. Go on. Ask him what got lost."

Then he closes the book and holds it out of the car door at arm's length, over the water, by one cover.

DANNY: You had the book, nephew. I am the book. Five o'clock a man from Customs opens this, and if it's not there he guesses, and they guess high. Monday there's one cab office on the Hook and it's three doors up. (He lets the pages hang.) Or. You walk up there now, with them watching, and you chalk my name where it was. Top. And I'll carry this up myself at five and stand beside you like a partner.

PLAYER (own words, or his feet). The scene reads three things.

He agrees. He walks back up Quay Street past the wall and chalks D. CAMMACK on the top line with the office door open. Danny tosses the book on the dash, backs car two off the edge in one movement and follows. It is the losing end without the bruises; go to the end of this scene.

He rushes him while the book is over the water. Danny lets go of it. He means to. The day book turns over once and goes into the dock flat, and floats, open, and then does not. Nobody has a book at five. There is one page of it left in the world, copied and certified, in a detective's case.

He stands and waits. Danny looks at him, and at the wall, and laughs.

DANNY: No? (He drops the book on the dash, on top of the chips, and gets out.) Go on, then. I've wanted to since the hatchback.

Fewson is off the wall.

DANNY (not looking round): Sit down, Craig.

FEWSON: No. (He stays where he is, standing.) I want to see what you do.

Danny hears his own line come back, and for a second there is nothing on his face at all. Fewson does not come forward. He does not sit either.

It is fists, on the coping stones, beside an open car door, with water on one side. Danny is quick and will not stand still; he hits and is gone, and he talks. "That's for the receipt." "That's for car three." With one arm Frank is slower than him and has to make him come in. A man who goes down here goes down on stone. Either of them may be hit again while he is down, and the wall watches it.

If Ron is there, he does not join it. He stands at the bonnet of car two with his back to the river, between the fight and the edge, and nobody goes in the water. He says nothing.

If Frank wins. Danny is sitting on the coping with his back against car two's wheel arch and his heels over the water, blood from his mouth on the gold. He feels a tooth with his tongue.

Frank reaches into the car. He takes the book off the dash. He turns the engine off and draws the brass key out of the ignition, on its black fob, and the yellow sign over all their heads goes out.

Behind him Fewson walks along the quay to where the hatchback is parked, gets into the driving seat, runs it back two notches, tilts the mirror, and drives away up the cold-store road. He does not hoot.

DANNY (watching it go): Leave my mirror alone.

PLAYER (own words). What he says next is one of three.

- Go. "First boat's half six. (He gets a hand to the door and stands.) I'll want a cab to it. Two forty to the quay, I believe. Put it on my account." If Danny's twenty was kept in week one, Sheila strikes off the last of his seven. If he owed, she writes four pounds eighty under the two forty, and it is the only debt in Mickey's book that is ever paid: the money comes in an envelope from across the water, in the spring.
- Stay, under him. "Third line?" The player says. Danny holds a hand up to be pulled to his feet. "No arrow. You've my word, for what it fetches."
- Nothing said, and the brass key held up where he can see it. Danny understands whose pocket it is going to. "You'd not. (He looks.) Mickey'd not." He is still sitting there when a plain car comes down Quay Street at ten past five.

If Frank loses. He is on the stones. Danny stands over him getting his breath, one eye closing, and straightens the chain at his neck. He does not hit him again unless he was hit when he was down.

DANNY: Five o'clock. I'll bring it myself. I've never been late for owt that was mine.

He gets into car two, backs her off the edge, and takes her up Quay Street with the sign lit. He puts her on Mickey's rank, square to the kerb, at the first go.

PAULINE (on the air, after a moment): Car two's on the rank. Who's in car two?

DANNY (the handset): The book.

Ron, if he is there, gets Frank up by his good arm. They walk back up past the wall. It takes a while. Brenda, from her step, to nobody: "He's parked it better than you did."

## What the player can do

- Agree to the top line. Sign: he names the price, with the wall listening. What follows: no fight; Danny carries the book in at five (scene 20, Danny's hand on the book).
- Rush him. Sign: the book is over the water, held by one cover. What follows: the dock has it. At five the line is on DS Ellis's certified copy and nowhere else; she lays it on the counter, and the choice is made on her paper with her standing over it. The rest of the book the inspector has to guess.
- Wait him out, and fight. Hands: fists; one arm if the yard took the other.
- Hit him when he is down, or not. What follows: the wall saw. If he did, Danny is carried, and takes no boat and no line; and if Frank is the one put down later in the fight, he gets it back.
- Say what Danny is worth: the boat, the board, or Ellis. Hands: his own words, or the key held up.
- Use the handset first, from the office. "Car two, where are you?" Danny: "Where you put me, nephew. Come and look."

## What the town remembers

- The wall saw it, start to end, and it is the last thing the Hook tells about either of them. Won: "Put him sat against Mickey's own wheel and took the key out of her." Lost: "Cammack parked her on the rank himself. First go." The book in the dock: "Six weeks and it ends with a ledger floating past the chandler's."
- Ada saw Ron stand between two men and the water and not lift a hand. She says so in the close.
- Fewson went, in Danny's car, with Danny's seat set to fit him. By Monday the crew is his whichever of the two men won.
- The brass key is on the third hook by five, for the first time in nine years, unless Danny still has it.
- Pauline said "Car two is not answering" on an open channel, once, in the same words she used in week two. Every set on the Hook heard it.
- What Danny said about two hundred pounds, if he said it, is in the caff before the books open, and decides two of the three calls Frank made at noon.

## Variants by path

- Fewson has the key. Danny was put on the top line and never turned. He is on the quay beside Frank with one eye shut. "He's had it off me, nephew. Nine year I've carried that." Fewson is in car two with the book shut on his knee. He does not eat and does not joke. "I don't want your book. I want him off your board, and the lads told why. Rub him off, and this is on your counter by five." That is the price: give him Danny, or fight him. Danny fights at Frank's shoulder, badly: "I said I don't do lifting." If they win, Danny takes the brass key out of the ignition himself, looks at it, and holds it out. "Go on. It's nine year late."
- Guns have been fired this game. There is a revolver on the dash, on top of the book, and Danny does not get out. It cannot be fists. Frank agrees to his price, or leaves, or has come with Mickey's revolver, in which case he has come alone, because Ron will not ride with it. A shot on the quay is a killing or a wounding with the whole town on the wall, and the closes are played from that.
- Ron is inside. Nobody stands at the bonnet. If Frank is knocked toward the edge he goes in, and is pulled out at the ladder by Shaun Hewson, and has lost.
- The honest firm. Danny offers a wage instead of a partnership. "You do days. I do after. You'd be good on days."
