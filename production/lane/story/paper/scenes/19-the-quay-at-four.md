# 19. The quay at four

- Where it sits: Act III, the last day, Saturday 1 December, an hour before the books open. The opponent, faced in person.
- What wakes it: the man from Customs and Excise has asked for week one; it is afternoon; the day book is not on the counter. Danny Cammack has turned, from a cause the player made and watched (his name chalked third, or nowhere, and asked for twice more), and has the brass key he never gave back. If Danny was given the top line and stayed, Craig Fewson has taken the key off him; see Variants.
- Who must be present: Danny, or Fewson. Pauline, on the air. DS Ellis, in a plain car at the top of the street. Ron, if he is free, at Frank's shoulder. The wall: Ada's window, Shaun's ladder, Brenda's step. Fallback: none is needed; one of the two men is always alive, and car two cannot be lost.

Frank is trying to stop a man walking his firm's book forty yards up the street to a detective. He will give up skin for it, with one arm if that is what he has; and then he has to say what the man at the edge is worth to him.

## Staging

It starts in the office. The strip light is on already; it is that time of year. Sheila is standing at the counter with her hands flat on it, either side of a clean oblong where a book has lain for thirty-one years. Car two's key is on its hook.

Then the quay. A quarter to four and the lamps coming on. High water, slack, a foot under the coping.

Car two is on the edge where the hatchback sat in week one: front wheels over, sill on the stone, its yellow sign lit against a sky the same colour as the river. The driver's door is open. Danny is in the seat sideways with one foot on the coping and the day book open across his knee. He is going down a column with one finger, and counting.

At the top of Quay Street, by Mickey's rank, a plain car is parked with a woman in it. She is waiting for five, like everybody.

The wall has filled, as it did for car three. Craig Fewson is on it, in the same place.

The camera is wide and low from the landward side: two men, a car, the edge, the water. It does not cut away from a blow.

## The scene

PAULINE (in the office, to the microphone): Car two? (Nothing.) Car two, your keys are on my hook. (She takes her hand off the switch and looks at Frank.) Car two is not answering.

SHEILA: He came in for change for the telephone. I turned round for the tin.

On the quay Danny does not look up at once. He finishes his column.

DANNY: Nine, ten, eleven. Eleven nights with R.K. at the end of the line. (Now he looks up, pleased, as he did from the counter six weeks ago.) She's only got him for the one.

What he says next is the night between's.

- Ron went in: "One night, and he's sat in a cell for it. What's he get for eleven?"
- Frank signed: "She's got you for one, in your own hand. Here's the other ten of you."
- Wardell was paid, and has sold it to him: he says it to the wall, loud. "Ask him what got lost at the station, and who lost it. Then ask how long Ron stops out when she's seen this."
- Agar's man, or a paid detective: "Both of you walked and nobody's charged. I can't see how. She will, when she's read this."

If Frank fetched that Friday's load himself, along the dock wall, Danny has it from DC Wardell, who watched it from a plain car and wrote nothing down. He adds it, to the wall: "And one night that isn't in here at all. Astern down the dock wall with his lamps out, the night Ron was sat on her bench. I paid good money for that. I'd have paid to watch."

He shuts the book and lays his hand on it.

DANNY: Five o'clock her books open as well. So. You walk up there now, with them watching, and chalk my name where it was. Top. And this goes back on your counter, and I stand beside you like a partner while the Customs man reads it. (He nods up the street at the plain car.) Or I walk it forty yard, and hand it her through the window.

Frank does up the middle button of his jacket. With the arm in plaster he cannot; his hand goes to it and comes away.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own, or only stands):

- "Top line. Come and watch me chalk it."
- "You'll not get forty yards, Mr Cammack."
- Nothing. He walks round the bonnet and stands between the car door and the street.

To the first, Danny tosses the book on the dash, backs car two off the edge in one movement and follows him up. It is the losing end without the bruises; go to the end of this scene.

To either of the others, Danny looks at him, and at the wall, and laughs.

DANNY: Go on, then. I've wanted to since the hatchback.

He drops the book on the dash and gets out. Fewson is off the wall.

DANNY (not looking round): Sit down, Craig.

FEWSON: No. (He stays where he is, standing.) I want to see what you do.

Danny hears his own line come back, and for a second there is nothing on his face at all. Fewson does not come forward. He does not sit either.

It is fists, on the coping stones, beside an open car door, with water on one side. Danny is quick and will not stand still; he hits and is gone. For the first time since he walked into Mickey's he has stopped talking. With one arm Frank is slower than him and has to make him come in. A man who goes down here goes down on stone. Either of them may be hit again while he is down, and the wall watches it.

If Ron is there, he does not join it. He stands at the bonnet of car two with his back to the river, between the fight and the edge, and nobody goes in the water. He says nothing.

If Frank wins. Danny is sitting on the coping with his back against car two's wheel arch and his heels over the water, blood from his mouth on the gold. He feels a tooth with his tongue.

Frank reaches into the car. He takes the book off the dash. He turns the engine off and draws the brass key out of the ignition, on its black fob, and the yellow sign over all their heads goes out.

Behind him Fewson walks along the quay to where the hatchback is parked, gets into the driving seat, runs it back two notches, tilts the mirror, and drives away up the cold-store road.

DANNY (watching it go): Leave my mirror alone.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own):

- "There's a boat at half six, Mr Cammack. Be on it." Danny gets a hand to the door and stands. "I'll want a cab to it. Two forty to the quay, I believe. Put it on my account." Sheila does, and it is the only debt in that book ever paid from across the water: the money comes in an envelope, in the spring.
- "Third line. No arrow." Danny holds a hand up to be pulled to his feet. "You've my word. For what it fetches."
- Nothing. He holds the brass key up where Danny can see it, and looks up the street at the plain car. "You'd not. (Danny reads his face.) Mickey'd not." He is still sitting there when a constable walks down at ten past five.

If Frank loses. He is on the stones. Danny stands over him getting his breath, one eye closing, and straightens the chain at his neck. He does not hit him again unless he was hit when he was down.

DANNY: Five o'clock, nephew. Your counter, or her car window. You've an hour to think which you'd sooner.

He gets into car two, backs her off the edge, and takes her up Quay Street with the sign lit. He puts her on Mickey's rank, square to the kerb, at the first go, two lengths behind the plain car.

PAULINE (on the air, after a moment): Car two's on the rank. Who's in car two?

DANNY (the handset): Somebody who can park it.

Ron, if he is there, gets Frank up by his good arm. They walk back up past the wall. It takes a while. Brenda, from her step, to nobody: "He's parked it better than you did."

## What the player can do

- Agree to the top line. Sign: he names the price, with the wall listening. What follows: no fight; Danny is at the counter at five with his hand on the book (scene 20).
- Stand in his way, or tell him he will not get there. Hands: fists; one arm if the yard took the other. Sign: he has said how far it is to her window.
- Hit him when he is down, or not. What follows: the wall saw. If he did, Danny is carried, and takes no boat and no line; and if Frank is put down later in the fight, he gets it back.
- Say what Danny is worth: the boat, the board, or the detective.

## What the town remembers

- The wall saw it, start to end, and it is the last thing the Hook tells about either of them. Won: "Put him sat against Mickey's own wheel and took the key out of her." Lost: "Cammack parked her on the rank himself. First go."
- Ada saw Ron stand between two men and the water and not lift a hand.
- Fewson went, in Danny's car, with Danny's seat set to fit him. By Monday the crew is his, whichever of the two men won.
- The brass key is on car two's hook by five, beside her own, for the first time in nine years, unless Danny still has it.
- Every set on the Hook heard "Car two is not answering", in the words Pauline used in week two.
- What Danny shouted about the station, if he shouted it, is in the caff before the books open.
- Ellis watched all of it through a windscreen and did not get out. It was not on paper yet.

## Variants by path

- Fewson has the key. Danny was put on the top line and never turned. He is on the quay beside Frank with one eye shut. "He's had it off me, nephew. Nine year I've carried that." Fewson is in car two with the book shut on his knee. He does not joke. "I don't want your board. I want him off it, and the lads told why. Rub him off, and this is on your counter by five. Else she has it." That is the price: give him Danny, or fight him. Danny fights at Frank's shoulder, badly. If they win, Danny takes the brass key out of the ignition himself, looks at it, and holds it out. "Go on. It's nine year late."
- Frank has carried Mickey's revolver since the drawer (S32). The Hook has buried a man for the gun at the Landing, and it knows what Frank has in his coat. There is a revolver on the dash, on top of the book, and Danny does not get out. It cannot be fists. Frank agrees, or leaves, or has the revolver in his coat and lets Danny see it; nobody fires, and he has come alone, because Ron will not ride with it. If Frank left the revolver in the drawer, Danny brings no gun either: after the old man's funeral nobody on the Hook will be the next to fire one, and it is fists.
- Ron is inside. Nobody stands at the bonnet. If Frank is knocked toward the edge he goes in, is pulled out at the ladder by Shaun Hewson, and has lost. If somebody has started car two every morning since Ron went, Danny says so: "First turn. He's got you trained."
- A firm with no night money. There are four lines with R.K., all in week one. "Four's plenty. Or you do days and I do after. You'd be good on days."
