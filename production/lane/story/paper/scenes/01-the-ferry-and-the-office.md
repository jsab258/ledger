# 01. The ferry and the office

- Where it sits: Act I, week one, the Monday, the first minutes of play.
- What wakes it: a new game. Nothing else has to be true.
- Who must be present: Sheila Dunn, Ron Kirby, Pauline Kitching. On the way up: Gordon Leckenby at the landing, Shaun Hewson on his ladder, Ada Vane at her window. Nobody can be absent this early, so there is no fallback carrier; if the player skips Sheila's walk-round, it ends the same way.

Frank is trying to get the town to take his name the first time he offers it. He will give up asking anybody for anything (the way, a hand with the case, a welcome) to get it.

## Staging

The ferry landing, ten past nine. Dry, grey and low, the stones still dark from the night's rain. The boat's ramp is down on the floating stage and Gordon Leckenby stands at the top of the brow taking tickets back, because on this crossing you give your ticket up when you get off.

In Frank's hands: one suitcase and a typed envelope. In his pocket: two ten-pound notes, three pound coins and a fifty.

From the stage the camera, behind his shoulder, can see the whole of what he has come to: the quay wall, three cranes with nothing hanging from them, a timber ship lying against the far wall with no lights on, and Quay Street running up from the water. Halfway along the parade a painter is on a ladder. Over one doorway at the quay end the paint says MICKEY'S and has been saying it for some time.

There is one sound that does not belong to the water: a car engine idling, steady, somewhere up the street, and under it a woman's voice on a radio. It is how the player finds the office. Nobody points.

On the rank outside Mickey's stands one car with its engine running and a big man in the driving seat, both hands on the wheel, going nowhere.

Inside: a scuffed counter, the fare book, the radio set and its desk microphone, a kettle, two ashtrays, a telephone. On the wall a painted board with two chalked lines, CAR 1 and CAR 2, and a row of three brass hooks. Two have had keys on them for years; the paint is worn round them. The third is worn the same, and bare.

## The scene

Frank comes up the brow with the case. Gordon holds his hand out for the ticket, takes it, and looks at the envelope.

GORDON: Quay Street's straight up from the wall. You'll want telling which door.

FRANK: I'll find it.

GORDON: Aye. They all do. It's got his name on it.

The player has the street. If he stands for four seconds the walking hint shows. The engine and the radio get louder as he climbs.

Under the ladder, Shaun Hewson stops whistling and looks down, brush across the pot.

SHAUN: You'll be the new owner.

FRANK: Frank Nowak.

SHAUN: That's what I said.

He goes back to the board he is lettering. Across the street a net curtain moves and is still.

At the rank the man in the car watches Frank come in the wing mirror. He does not get out. He lifts one finger from the wheel. The engine runs on. The car is clean, and a bucket stands by the kerb.

Sheila Dunn is in the doorway before Frank reaches it, cardigan, glasses on their chain, a pencil.

SHEILA: New management. Sheila Dunn, I keep the books. Come on, I'll show you round before you start looking lost.

FRANK: Frank Nowak.

SHEILA: It says so on the letter. Come on.

Inside, Pauline Kitching is on the radio with her back to the door.

PAULINE: Car one, Kevin, the Tivoli for Fairview, two minutes. No, love, two real minutes. (She turns.) Is that him?

SHEILA: That's new management.

PAULINE: What do I call him on the air?

FRANK: Nowak. N, O, W, A, K.

Pauline has her pencil up. She looks at the fare book, which is ruled for fares. She looks at the board.

PAULINE: Board's for cars, love.

She puts the pencil behind her ear and leans to the microphone.

PAULINE: Car one, be told. The new owner's in.

KEVIN (on the radio, through traffic): What's he like?

PAULINE: He can spell.

Sheila walks him round. Each stop is one line; the player can stand and listen or walk on, and she finishes to his back.

SHEILA (the counter): Phone, radio, and the fare book. Every fare goes in the book. That was Mickey's rule, and it's mine now.

SHEILA (the board): One driver by day, one by night, and the radio in between. The takings have been thin since the docks laid men off.

If the player looks at the hooks:

SHEILA: Car one's out with Kevin. Car two's in the car. That one's been empty a while.

SHEILA (a shut door behind the counter): That's Mickey's office. It's been locked since he died, and I've the key. Not today.

SHEILA (the stairs): Your flat's upstairs, over the office. His things are still in it. I didn't have the heart.

She comes back to the counter, opens a narrow cash book at a clean page and unscrews her pen.

SHEILA: What did you come with?

Frank says nothing. He empties his pocket onto the counter: two tens, three pound coins, a fifty. Sheila does not pick any of it up. She moves each coin an inch with one finger as she counts it, the way a bank does.

SHEILA: Twenty-three pound fifty.

She writes. The camera can read it over her hand: the date, then New management, cash introduced, £23.50. She blots it and slides the money back across the counter to him.

SHEILA: I'm not taking it. I'm writing down that it was there. Somebody always asks what a man started with.

From under the counter she takes a buff rent book, limp with handling, and lays it on top of his money. Each week has a line, and each line a collector's initials, D.P., down to three weeks ago. After that the initials go on but the hand that filled in the amounts is hers.

SHEILA: The tin's paid it three weeks. The tin's done. Eighteen pound by Friday, love, or the landlord has the keys. And Sunday you tell me what this firm is.

She caps her pen. Through the window the engine on the rank stops. In the quiet the kettle can be heard, and the gulls.

SHEILA: That's Ron on the rank. Go and say hello. Everyone out there wants a look at you.

The AI notice shows, once. Then the talk hint. From here Frank's words are the player's.

Outside, Ron Kirby has got out of the car. He is wiping the driver's door handle with a leather. He looks at the suitcase, which Frank may still be carrying, and does not offer to take it.

RON: Boss.

PLAYER (own words). Whatever he says, Ron's first answer is the same.

RON: Aye. I know who you are.

What Ron will say if he is asked, each once, in his own time:

- About the engine: "She wants running or the battery goes. Ten minute. I've not missed a morning."
- About driving it: "Never had call. Docks is walking distance."
- About the key: "Stops in her. That's how he had it."
- About Mickey: one of the two approved lines, and then he looks up the street until the subject changes.
- About himself: "I do the door. And the rank. I'm here while eleven."

He does not ask Frank one question. He folds the leather into his coat pocket, stands with his back to the office wall beside the door, and is at work.

## What the player can do

- Walk straight up, or wander. Hands: walking, looking. Sign: the engine and the radio. If he has not come in after a game hour (two real minutes), Sheila calls from the door, "In here when you've done looking lost," and the walk-round plays from there.
- Stand through the walk-round, or walk on. Either way she has met him and the talk opens.
- Look at the board and the hooks. Sign: the camera lets the bare hook sit in frame while she talks. What follows: only her one line. It is the first sight of the key Danny Cammack kept (scene 06).
- Try Mickey's door. It is locked. Sheila, not looking up: "Not today."
- Talk to Ron, Pauline or Shaun. To each he can give his name. Ron goes on saying boss; Pauline goes on saying the new owner into the microphone; Shaun says Nowak from then on, because he was told it twice.
- Get into car two. Sign: the key is in it and the door is not locked. If Frank takes the driving seat, Ron gets in beside him without a word, pulls the seat belt across and says, "Where to." Any drive is free and unscored; Ron names nothing yet. Pauline, on the car's radio, after a moment: "Car two, is that you, Ron?" "It's him." "Who's him?" "Boss."
- Pick the money up, or leave it lying on the counter. Left, it is still there when he comes back, and Sheila has put the rent book square on top of it.

## What the town remembers

- Gordon, Shaun, Ada, Ron, Sheila and Pauline saw him come, and hold it at first hand. Within the day one of them, or somebody they told, says it to his face: "You'll be Mickey's nephew, then," or "Saw you come in. Mickey's nephew, is it?" Never Sheila, never June.
- Shaun, at the caff counter at noon, to Grace Rinaldi: "Gave me his whole name off the pavement. Both halves. Like he was stood for the council." This is where the street's joke about the name starts, and it feeds the name ladder: Nowak comes from people who were told, not from people who were asked.
- Pauline, to Terry Hodgson at the evening change: "N, O, W, A, K. I've nowhere to write it." Terry says "the new owner" until he is told otherwise.
- Gordon, to Karen Grummitt at the ticket window: "Wouldn't be told the way." It comes back in the last week, when Frank is carrying a dead man's watch that runs five minutes fast.
- Sheila's entry, cash introduced, £23.50, is the first line of week one. On the fifth morning of the last week Mr Hebblewhite reads it out and asks whether that was all of it. Sheila: "It's what was there."
- The Ledger takes its first aim, in Sheila's words: eighteen pound by Friday; and Sunday you tell me what this firm is.

## Variants by path

None. Every game opens here.
