# 18. Ron in the last week

- Where it sits: Act III, week seven, the Wednesday, between the second morning and the third. One scene in two halves; a game plays one of them.
- What wakes it: the night between has been played. If Ron is inside: Father Walsh comes to the counter with a folded paper, and it is visiting time on the remand wing. If Ron is free: Frank has somewhere to be and Ron is on the door.
- Who must be present: Ron Kirby. Inside: a prison officer, and Father Brendan Walsh beforehand. Free: nobody else. Fallback: if the player will not visit, Father Walsh brings what Ron wants done, in Ron's words, two days late; if he will not be driven, the drive happens on Saturday, down to the quay.

Frank is trying to get one easy word out of a man who has stopped saying what he sees. He will give up asking for it.

## Staging

Inside. The remand wing is the old gaol behind the Exchange. Visits for men who have not been tried are fifteen minutes, any weekday afternoon. Since a fight on his wing, his are in the closed boxes: a stool, a shelf, a pane of thick glass with a brass grille under it to talk through, and the same on the other side. An officer walks the row behind the visitors. On the wall is a clock.

Ron is in his own clothes, as a man on remand may be: the black overcoat, a clean collar. His lower lip is split and has scabbed. His right hand is bandaged across the knuckles and round the thumb.

Free. Quay Street, a dry grey afternoon. Car two at the kerb with its two L-plates, engine running, the driving seat run right back. The dock road is the lesson's road. At the far end of it the old dock gate has a Harbour Board notice wired to it, and a new chain lying coiled by the post, not yet on.

In both halves the camera is where it has always been with these two: level, a little below their eyes, and still.

## The scene

### If Ron is inside

Father Walsh, at the counter, that morning, holding a paper folded in four:

FATHER WALSH: From a parishioner. He says you'll know the door.

Inside, in Ron's capitals: WEDNESDAY. 2 TO 4. BRING NOWT.

The box. Ron is already on his stool when Frank sits. He looks at Frank's arm, if it is in plaster, and Frank can see him decide not to ask.

RON: Boss.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own): "Who did that?" / nothing.

RON: Walked into a door. (He nearly smiles, and it opens the lip.) I know about doors.

He will not say more. There are men from the docks on that wing, and to them he is the one who signed a paper for the police, whatever it said.

RON: Three things. I've wrote them down but they'll not let paper through.

He holds up the bandaged hand and counts on the fingers of the other.

RON: One. Car two. She wants running, ten minute, every morning. Nobody's doing it. You'll come out one day and she'll not turn.

RON: Two. Sheila's got a form of mine. There's a date on it. Fourteenth of December, ten past two, the test centre. I'll not be there. (He looks at the grille.) A man should say when he's not coming. There's a number on the form. Ring it. Tell them Kirby can't attend. Don't tell them why.

RON: Three.

What the third is depends on the tally.

- He kept it, and it is in his property at the gaol: "There's a brass number in my property here. Thirty-nine. I've signed for you to have it. Take it down the gate before Saturday. Hang it on Stathers's post yourself, where they can see you do it. If he asks, say Kirby says it can't be both."
- It was hung up, or it is in Frank's pocket from the Widow: "If she sends owt for me, a number, owt, send it back. I've got a door. It's this one."

FRANK (if the player wants it): That's three. I can count that far.

Then the thing Ron has not sat here to leave unsaid.

RON: You keep that rank going, boss. I've not sat in here for you to finish with nowt.

If Frank lied to him at the white board in lesson five, there is one line more. He says it to the shelf, without heat.

RON: It read different through a wall, boss.

He lets that stand, and does not go back to it.

If Mickey's watch came to Frank from the station desk:

RON: Have you got it on?

If he has, Frank reads the time off it aloud.

RON: Then it's five to. They shout on the hour. (He glances up at the wall clock, which agrees with him.) He liked to be early. You get five minutes nobody else has got. I never knew what he did with them.

The officer stops behind Frank's stool.

OFFICER: Time.

Ron stands. He does not put a hand to the glass; it would not occur to him.

RON: Mind the bollard.

It is as near as he comes to naming anything. He goes out by the door on his side, and it is shut behind him by somebody else.

### If Ron is free

Ron is in the driving seat when Frank comes out. He does not say he will drive. He has the engine going.

He does his door, his mirror and his belt, and says none of them. He winds the window down and puts his arm out, and moves off without a jerk.

The player has what he has always had in this seat: the handbrake by his knee, and one word.

At the foot of Quay Street the bollard comes up. Ron steers round it, close, and correct. He does not say it is coming, or that it is there, or that it has gone.

That is the whole of the scene, and it lasts as far as the gate. He drives well now, fourteen miles an hour, both hands. He sees everything. But the player was taught in week two that what Ron does not name, he has not seen, and now he names nothing, so every yard of the dock road has to be watched for him, or taken on trust.

A fish lorry begins to back out of a gateway.

- "Stop": the car stops dead, as it always did. Ron's cap slides off the dash. He does not say "Aye." He waits for the lorry, finds first, and goes on.
- The handbrake: the same, softer, and no word.
- Neither: Ron brakes for it himself, in good time. He had seen it.

The ice house comes up on the left. The camera gives it room: the chained doors, the grass at the foot of them. The car goes by at fourteen. The wipers are off. Nobody says it.

FRANK (if the player wants it): That's the ice house.

RON (after a while): Aye.

He can go on. He can name the railway, and Ron will stop at the white board, and look left and right and listen, and say nothing about what is or is not coming, and go over. He can name the pen, where two men are standing. To each thing the player names, Ron says "Aye," or nothing. It is not the same, and both of them can hear that it is not.

FRANK (if the player wants it): You've stopped saying them.

Ron answers once, by what was done on the night between.

- Frank signed: "There's a charge on you for a line with my letters on it. You'll not want me telling you what's coming."
- A statement was lost, or a detective paid: "I say what I've seen, boss. I've seen a thing I'll not say. So I've given over."
- Agar's man: "I were fetched out by a feller in a wing collar. I'd not know what to call that."

If Frank lied to him in lesson five, he adds nothing about it here. He says it on the door, some other day, once: "It read different through a wall, boss."

At the dock gate he stops in one go, short of the coiled chain. He reads the notice through the windscreen, moving his lips. He does not read it out.

RON: Fourteenth of December. Ten past two. I'll want that afternoon.

He turns the car in the gateway by the book, three movements, and takes Frank where he was going.

## What the player can do

Inside:

- Go, or not. Sign: the folded paper, and the priest who brought it. Not going: the three things come by Father Walsh on Friday, and Ron has sat in the box on Wednesday from two until four.
- Start car two each morning. Hands: the key, ten minutes, the handbrake on. It is Ron's first job in the game, done by Frank. On Saturday it decides whether the car starts for the man who takes it.
- Ring the test centre from the office telephone. Hands: the dial, his own words. A woman's voice: "Kirby. Fourteenth, ten past two. Cancelled. Does he want another date?" What the player answers is written on the form by Sheila, who is listening.
- Carry number 39 to the gate and hang it up, in front of the pen. Or keep it. Or take it to the Widow.
- Say the time by the watch, if he has it.

Free:

- "Stop", the handbrake, or his own eyes.
- Name the buildings himself, or leave the car quiet.
- Ask why, once.
- Take the wheel. With two arms he can. Ron gets out, walks round, gets in, puts his belt on, and looks out of the side window all the way.

## What the town remembers

- Father Walsh holds that he carried it and that Frank went, or did not. In the close he is at the chained gate, and says one sentence about a parishioner.
- The officer on the row tells the wing that Kirby had a visitor in a ninety-pound coat. It does Ron no good and he knew it would not.
- The pen, if Frank hangs the tally: nine men watch the owner of Mickey's walk to Stathers's post and put a brass number on a hook. Stathers: "That's his answer, is it? He's took his time." One of the nine, after: "He sent it back, then. I'd not have."
- Sheila has the form. She writes on it what Frank told the test centre, and keeps it under the day book to the end.
- Ada, at her window, sees car two go down Quay Street every afternoon with two men in it, and hears nothing through an open window that she used to hear. "He's stopped doing his buildings."
- Pauline, any time car two is out with Ron driving: she used to say, "Tell him two real minutes." Now she gives the fare and lets the channel go quiet.

## Variants by path

- Inside, and Frank came for him at the pen. Under the three things there is a fourth, said standing: "Thirty-eight pound. It's in my property. Give it Lily. That's a good few Fridays."
- Inside, with the Widow's blanket. He has found out. "Somebody's give me the cell by the pipes. I've asked to be moved. They'll not."
- Free, and Mickey's watch is on Ron's wrist. He looks at it at the gate. "Five to." It is the only time in the drive that he tells Frank something Frank has not asked.
