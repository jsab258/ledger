# 13. Write it again

- Where it sits: Act IIb, week five, the Friday morning. The turn.
- What wakes it: the Landing and lesson five have played; a tip has reached DS Ellis, by a leak the player watched this week; word came last evening, unofficially (a constable talking at the door, and Sheila: "Clear it by morning. Leave the book."); the night's clearing is done, or not. It is seven.
- Who must be present: DS Carol Ellis. Sheila Dunn. DC Graham Wardell and two constables for the drawers. Pauline at the radio. Ron on the door until near the end. Fallback: if Sheila has left the firm, her chair is empty and the day book lies on the counter with the pen on it; Ellis reads from it the same.

Frank is trying to keep his own first line standing under a detective's eye. He will give up the one cover he has had for five weeks, which is that nobody ever made him say it twice.

## Staging

The office, seven in the morning, the street lamp still on outside and the sky behind the chandler's going grey. Two plain cars at the kerb. Across the road a net curtain is held an inch open.

Ellis stands at the counter in a raincoat with a magistrate's warrant unfolded on the fare book. She has read it to the room once. It names these premises and the yard behind them.

The constables are working: every drawer out and stacked, the kettle lifted, the cushions off the customers' bench. Each thing taken goes into a clear bag with a brown label, and Wardell writes each label into a property book, a line an item. Wardell has had the keys off the board, written each into his book, and hung them back: car one, car two, and a Yale.

Sheila is sitting at her desk, which they have emptied round her. Her handbag is on her knee with both her hands on the clasp. Her knitting shows at the top of it.

Pauline is at the radio with her headphones round her neck, giving out fares in a voice for church.

Ron is where he is every morning, with his back to the wall beside the door, in his donkey jacket, looking at the street.

The camera is across the counter, in the place it stood on the first morning and in week three.

## The scene

A constable, finished with the desk, turns to the woman sat at it.

CONSTABLE: I'll need the bag, madam.

Sheila does not move her hands.

ELLIS (not turning round): It's a warrant for premises, Constable. Mrs Dunn isn't premises.

The constable goes to the stairs. Sheila opens the clasp, takes out a paper bag, and holds it up over the counter.

SHEILA: Mint, Sergeant?

The bag she took it from is open six inches from Ellis's elbow. Wool, needles, and under the wool the corner of something red.

ELLIS: No, thank you, Mrs Dunn.

She has not looked. She has a book of her own to look at. She draws the day book toward her and turns it back, a week's sheets at a time, as Sheila did in week three. She stops at the first Monday and runs a finger to the foot of the page.

What she lays beside the book depends on the leak the player saw. The old hand was lifted: a pink carbon sheet in a clear bag, its last line a time, "Office", and R.K. Danny in the phone box: her own notebook, open at a date. Iveson, or a crewman: a typed statement, two pages, face down.

ELLIS: I'm not asking you to remember everything, Mr Nowak. I'm asking you to remember it the same way twice.

She reads the line aloud, flat, every word, as it has stood in ink for five weeks. The player hears his own answer to Sheila in another woman's voice.

Then she turns the book round on the counter to face him, takes a pen from her inside pocket, and holds it out.

ELLIS: Write it again. Underneath. Same words.

The player writes. He has seen the line twice: when he said it, and when Sheila turned the book round in week three. Nothing on the screen offers it to him now. The game reads what he writes against what stands: where, what, for whom, what was paid.

1. The line is a fare.

If he writes the same fare, with the landing, the waiting and the cash:

ELLIS: The same way twice. (She writes one word in her notebook.) Who was the passenger?

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own): "He didn't give a name." / a name / nothing. A name is a new fact she can go and ask. No name:

ELLIS: A cab that went to the water at ten at night and waited, with nobody in it. I'll call that one.

If what he writes differs (no waiting, another place, the envelope):

ELLIS: That's two. (She lays her finger between the lines.) I don't mind which is true, Mr Nowak. I've got both, in the one book, and one of them's yours.

2. The line is the envelope.

He can write it without thinking, and the scene lets him. She reads it as he goes.

ELLIS: Thank you.

Her finger moves along the line he has just written, to the right, off the end of it, and comes to rest in the last column of the line above. R.K.

ELLIS: The book has a column for who brought a job in. It hasn't one for who it was brought to. (She looks up.) Who's R.K.?

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own): "It's a column. Ask the book." / "Ron Kirby. He works my door." / nothing.

If he says the name, he has said it to a detective, on the record, with the man ten feet away on the other side of the glass. If he says nothing, she turns to the inside of the cover, where Sheila listed the firm's initials thirty years ago and has kept the list up.

ELLIS: Kirby, R. Door.

She closes the cover.

3. The line is Sheila's.

He saw it once, in week three, for as long as she held the book round. If he can write it:

ELLIS: Her hand above, yours below, and you've her words by heart. Who told who, I wonder.

If he cannot:

ELLIS: Then these are Mrs Dunn's words.

She turns, with the book, to the desk. Sheila looks back at her over her glasses.

SHEILA: I write what I'm told.

ELLIS: And who told you?

SHEILA: Nobody. That's why it's in mine.

In every case Ellis turns the book back to Frank and puts her finger under what he has written.

ELLIS: Date it. Today's. And your initials.

He does. It is the sixteenth of November.

ELLIS: Five weeks I've had this firm in front of me, and not one line in its book in the owner's hand. (She caps the pen.) Now there is.

A constable photographs the page, twice, with a flash. Ellis will sign each print across the corner.

ELLIS: It's a working book. I'll not stop a business. The copies are certified; the book stops where it is. If a page leaves it, Mr Nowak, I'll know what that means, and so will a court.

She puts the film in her case.

ELLIS: Frank Nowak, I'm arresting you on suspicion of false accounting. You do not have to say anything unless you wish to do so, but what you say may be given in evidence.

FRANK (if the player wants it): Nowak. You'll want it spelt right on the form.

ELLIS: The sergeant does the forms.

A constable takes his arm. Sheila has not moved. Pauline has stopped giving out fares.

PAULINE (to the microphone, after a moment): All cars. Wait.

They walk him to the door. The wall beside it is bare. On the hook inside hangs a donkey jacket, and the man who is always in it is not on the street.

Somebody, across the road, lets the curtain fall.

## What the player can do

- Write it again. Hands: his own words, written. Sign: he has been shown the line twice, and she has just read it to him. What follows: as above, by kind. What he writes is stored beside the first, dated, in his hand.
- Refuse the pen. "Then I'll note that you declined, and I'll note the time." The arrest is the same. The second line is a blank in her notebook instead of a line in his book, and in the last week the inspector asks about the gap under week one instead of about two hands.
- Write something else on purpose (the truth under a lie, a lie under the truth). It is "That's two," and it is in ink.
- Answer her question about R.K., or the passenger, or not.
- Ask for a solicitor. "At the station. The duty solicitor is free. Mr Wardell will write down that you asked."
- Look at Sheila's bag. She looks at her mints.
- Speak to Ron on the way in or during. He says "Boss," and looks at the street. When Frank is led out he is gone.

## What the town remembers

- Ada saw two cars at seven and the new owner walked to one of them at eight with a constable's hand on his arm. By nine: "Saw the police put you in the car." By noon the fish shop is wrapping in it.
- Pauline said "All cars. Wait," on an open channel, and the Hook heard a cab office go quiet.
- Wardell's book lists a Yale from the third hook, hung back where it was. Asked whose door, Sheila said, "We never found out." Wardell did not ask twice. He knows whose unit is three doors up, and it is worth something to him to know it.
- Ellis holds two certified copies: one line in Sheila's hand, one beneath it in the owner's, dated. It is the best line she has ever had on the Hook, and she says so to nobody.
- Sheila holds the red book and Mickey's real book, under her knitting, where they were all morning. She tells nobody, and takes the bag home.
- Ron heard none of it and all of it. He was outside the glass when a detective asked who R.K. was. He has gone home for his other coat (scene 14).
- The page now carries three hands: Pauline's half line, Sheila's finish, Frank's copy. On the last Saturday a fourth entry is asked for.

## Variants by path

- A ride for the sergeant was taken. Before she opens the book she says back to him, word for word, one thing he told her in the back of Terry's cab. "So I know how you put things."
- The office was not cleared. The cocoa tin of unbanked cash is on the counter in a bag, and Mickey's office stands open: the arrest adds "and handling", and Mickey's real book, which nobody put in a handbag, is on the bench with a label on it.
- He has been fetching his own. Nothing in this room shows it; the F.N. lines are in the red book. They matter at five o'clock on the last day.
- Ellis is already bought. She has to come: the tip is on paper at the station and a superintendent reads. She sends the constables to the yard before she opens the book. Everything is said as written, in the same voice. She does not ask who R.K. is, or who the passenger was. As he is led out she says, for him alone: "This has to happen, Mr Nowak. What becomes of it is tonight's business. Have it with you."
- A firm with no night money. The line is one of four, all in week one, and nothing follows them. She reads the first. The rest is the same, and she is nearly civil.
