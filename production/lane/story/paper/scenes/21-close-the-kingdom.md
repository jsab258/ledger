# 21. Close: the Kingdom

- Where it sits: the close, Saturday 1 December, from a quarter past five until he sleeps. The Harbour Board chains the old dock gate tonight in every ending.
- What wakes it: the firm survives at five, whatever was written (the line left, or Danny's name put to it, with the books holding). A firm lost with a friend left is scene 22; with none, scene 23.
- Who must be present: Pauline; Sheila, unless she has left; the man who stands at Frank's shoulder now; Frank Stathers at the gate; Maureen Jensen; Lily Tang; Ron, by path. Fallback: if the Widow is gone, Stathers says the last name.

Frank is trying to walk home through a town that is his. He has already given what it cost; tonight he is handed the receipt.

## Staging

Quay Street under its lamps. The rank is full and the board is full. Over the door the paint is new: MICKEY'S, as it always said, and under it, if he told Shaun Hewson so, a small line with his own name, spelt right.

Three doors up, a shutter that once said CAMMACK in chalk a foot high says MICKEY'S. The only key it ever had is the Yale on the third hook.

On the counter is a spike, and on it a thick pad of pink carbon sheets. Nobody has carried one to the water in weeks. In the drawer beneath is a brown window envelope, soft as cloth, empty.

Down the dock road a Harbour Board lorry stands at the gate with its lamps on.

The camera walks behind him, as it did off the ferry.

## The scene

The office. Pauline is giving out fares faster than she can write them.

PAULINE: Car four, the Tivoli. Car six, boarding house. Car one, Fairview, he says the top road. Eighteen-ten.

She has not said who "he" is for a fortnight. She tears the first pink sheet of the night along its perforation and lays it on the counter in front of Frank.

He reads it with one finger, from the top, lips moving. He is counting. The finger stops two lines from the bottom.

FRANK (the player says it, or leaves the sheet lying): Chandler's. Landing. Ten past six.

The man at his shoulder nods and goes out. A car leaves the rank with its sign unlit. Frank puts the sheet on the spike.

Sheila writes the night's line in the red book. The camera can see the third column. It does not say R. Kirby tonight. It has a newer name, three times down the page, in the same small hand. She shuts the book and puts it in her bag.

SHEILA: Goodnight. (If she has ever said his name: Goodnight, Frank.)

The street. People on the parade step off the kerb for him and do not look up. By the phone box a man with his collar up takes his cigarette out of his mouth.

DARREN: By Christmas, you said. It's the first of December. (He puts it back.) You're early.

At the takeaway Lily Tang has the order bagged before he reaches the hatch.

LILY: Number nine. (She counts.) One. No charge, Mr Nowak.

If Ron is on the door, she counts two. She used to say that she remembered. She does not say it.

The gate. Stathers passes the chain through both leaves himself and closes the lock. A man from the Harbour Board wires a notice to the ironwork. The pen stands under its tin roof with nobody in it. At the gatepost, in front of a gate that will not open again, the man Frank put there is still standing.

THE MAN ON THE GATE: I'm stood here while you say different, Mr Nowak.

The tall man in black who marked the funeral on Monday has walked down behind the lorry at the same pace. He looks the gate over.

THE MAN IN BLACK: Five. No flowers, and done in the dark.

He turns and looks up the road, at the lit sign over the rank. Grace Rinaldi is on her step.

GRACE: And that one?

THE MAN IN BLACK (putting his hat on): I don't mark those.

Maureen Jensen has had a kitchen chair carried out to the cobbles to watch her gate chained. She sits on it in the fur collar with her bag on her knee. As Frank passes she does not turn her head.

THE WIDOW: Frankie.

Nobody has called him that before.

Ron. What is left is one of three, and in each of them Frank says a thing that stays said.

He went in. The Kingdom opens a door for Frank that it opens for nobody else: an officer on the remand wing, at lock-up, walks him down the landing and drops a hatch in a steel door. A man is sitting on a bed in a black overcoat, a second blanket folded beside him. He looks at the hatch.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own):

- "I kept the rank, Ron."
- "Say Frank. Once."
- "I should have gone up those steps."

To the first, and to the second:

RON: Boss.

To the third he looks at Frank for some time.

RON: Aye.

Nothing comes after either. The hatch is shut from the inside. Frank drives home down the dock road. The ice house goes by on the left, and the car is quiet.

He signed for it. Ron is on the door at Mickey's, as every night. Sheila has left a paper on the counter: the magistrates, the tenth. He has the Hook, and a date.

FRANK: Say Frank. Once.

RON (holding the door): Boss.

He walked, by a way that was bought. Whoever sold it is on the street tonight, holding it: Agar's notice wired to the yard gate, for Lady Day; or DC Wardell on the customers' bench, waiting for a cab he will not be charged for; or a plain car at the kerb with its engine running and a woman writing. Ron drives Frank back from the gate on the L-plates and names nothing.

FRANK: You can say them, Ron. I'd sooner you did.

If Frank came for him at the pen, and Mickey's watch is on his wrist, there is a long stretch of wall, and then:

RON: That's the ice house.

One building. He does not say it was coming, or that it has gone. Otherwise he drives.

The last thing, every way, is the telephone in the office, and Pauline answering it.

PAULINE: Mickey's. (She listens.) He's in.

She does not ask who is wanted.

## What the player can do

- Read the carbon and say the line, or leave it lying. Sign: Pauline lays it in front of him and waits. If he will not, she spikes it herself and the car stays on the rank.
- Walk down to the gate, or go to bed. Then it is chained without him, and the Widow says the name on Monday.
- Go to the remand wing, and say one of three things through a hatch. Or not go; then the last word between them is whatever was said at the visit.

## What the town remembers

- Everything; and from tonight nobody on the Hook tells him any of it to his face.
- The third column of the red book has a new name in it, and Sheila goes on writing it.

## Variants by path

- Danny was put back on the board under him. He is on the rank, third line, no arrow, eating. "Evening, Frankie." He is the second to say it.
- A friend still counts him: Ron, Sheila, Alison Sedman or Philip Danby. If the street also holds none of his night work as fact, and nobody was given to the police, one door on the walk is open when he passes.
- He is hunted for a killing. None of it is said to him. It is the same street with him not on it: Pauline to an empty counter, "He's not been in"; Lily with an order bagged and nobody to count it to; the Widow on her chair, to Stathers: "Frankie's away."
