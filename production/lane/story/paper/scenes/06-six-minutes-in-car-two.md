# 06. Six minutes in car two

- Where it sits: Act IIa, week two. Danny Cammack's visit.
- What wakes it: car three has been played (or Danny moved his own car and told it his way); Frank has answered Sheila's Sunday question, or the street has answered it for him; car two is standing on the rank with nobody in it; Frank is on foot on Quay Street as the lamps come on.
- Who must be present: Danny Cammack. Pauline on the air. Sheila at the board afterwards. Craig Fewson in the office doorway at the end. Fallback: if Danny cannot come (in hospital after week one), Fewson walks in with the offer, no car and no key, and says only, "Danny says half, and his name where it was." The board choice is the same.

Frank is trying to get Danny's men, his car and his nerve under Mickey's name without putting Danny's name over his own. He will give up half the nights, or the top of his board, or a quiet street.

## Staging

Quay Street at lighting-up time, about ten to six by the Harbour Board clock. The rain has stopped and the road holds the sky.

Car two comes up the street from the wrong end with its roof sign lit: MICKEY'S, yellow, on a magnet, its curly lead running in through the top of the driver's window to the lighter socket. It is a ten-year-old saloon that drives from the back wheels, washed every morning by a man who cannot drive it.

Danny is at the wheel with the seat run forward. In the ignition is a brass key on a leather fob gone black with handling. The car's own key is where it has been all day: on its hook on the board, where Pauline can see it.

On the dash: a meter with a flag, and the radio handset on its clip. In Frank's inside pocket, as on the day he came, is the typed envelope with Mickey's letter in it.

The camera rides in the back seat, between the two heads. As on every authored ride, the game clock slows to the pace of the talk. It is six minutes by Danny's count.

## The scene

The car stops beside Frank. Danny leans across and pushes the passenger door open.

DANNY: Cab for the nephew.

Frank does up the middle button of his jacket.

FRANK: Nowak. And it's my cab.

DANNY: Then you'll not mind getting in it.

If the player does not get in, Danny keeps pace at the kerb in first gear, says the same things through the window, and makes the offer at the office door. If he gets in:

Danny knocks the meter's flag down. It starts to tick.

DANNY: Flag's down. Six minutes, the whole Hook. Mickey took twenty and called it a round. (He drives quickly and well, one hand on the wheel.) That parade's worth thirty pound a week to a man who'd ask for it. I'm telling you what it's worth. I'm not telling you to do it.

The radio opens.

PAULINE: Car two. Car two, come in. Seventeen fifty-two. Your key's on my hook.

The handset is in reach of Frank's hand.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own, or leaves it):

- "Car two. It's Nowak. I'm in it."
- "Mr Cammack's driving, Pauline. On a key I've not seen before."

Danny shows him three doors. He slows for each.

DANNY (the dock gate, two men on it, a third with a list): Her gate. A fiver a car, a week. You're paying it and nobody's told you yet.

DANNY (a dark warehouse on the cold-store road, doors shut; he says it lightly): That door's mine. As your book knows.

DANNY (the landing, the overhang by the ticket window, nobody under it yet): Sixty a case, two cases a week, and an old man with a torch who thinks it's nineteen sixty.

PAULINE: Car two is not answering. Car one, if you see car two, tell it.

DANNY: She used to say that about me. I was answering. I just wasn't answering her.

Quay Street again, from the top. He does not stop at the rank. He takes the car on down past the chandler's, onto the coping stones, and eases the front wheels over the edge, one and then the other, until the sill touches stone without a sound. Handbrake. The yellow sign lights the wet quay round them.

DANNY: That's how you park it.

He flips the flag up and reads the meter.

DANNY: Four pound ten. That's the Hook, by Mickey's clock.

FRANK: Four pound ten. I'll have that off you before you get out.

Danny laughs, and stops laughing sooner than he meant to. He looks at Frank's jacket, where the envelope is.

DANNY: Nine year I drove this car. He left you a firm. He left Ron a door and Sheila a book. (Lightly; it costs him.) Did he say owt about me? In the letter.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own):

- "It's four lines and an address. He didn't say anybody."
- "Read it." Frank hands it over. Danny reads it by the light of the roof sign, twice, which does not take long. He folds it on its own creases and gives it back. "He's spelt you with a V." He looks at the water. "Well. He never wrote me a note for the milk."
- "He said you'd kept a key." It is not in the letter. Danny's whole face opens. "Did he." He touches the fob. He will never give it back now with a bad conscience, and one day he may find out what the letter says.

DANNY: Right. Here's me. Four lads who do as they're told and one who thinks. A motor, now your lot have done lifting it. And I'll go in any door in this town and come out smiling. Here's you. An office, a licence, a radio and Ron. Put them together and that's the Hook. Half.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own):

- "Half. On my board, under my name." "Then we'll go and write it up."
- "A third." Danny laughs. "A third. If my name goes back where it was." He means the top line.
- "No. I've seen how you park." "Then I'm the lad across the road." He puts the car in reverse; the back wheels take it off the edge as if it had been on a drive.
- "Friday. I'll tell you Friday." "Think quick. I've the ferry rank from Monday whether you've thought or not."

FRANK: And the key, Mr Cammack.

Danny works a key off his ring without looking and drops it in Frank's palm.

DANNY: There. Don't say I never give you owt.

At the office he walks in ahead of Frank and stops at the board. Under CAR 2 the paint holds the ghost of a name, rubbed and never washed: D. CAMMACK.

DANNY: You can still read me. He never washed it. He used his cuff.

He takes the chalk off the ledge and holds it out.

- The top line. Frank chalks D. CAMMACK above CAR 1. Danny looks at it and for once has nothing ready. "That'll do." Sheila, from her desk: "Cars keep their numbers, love. He can have the top of the board." The share is a third.
- The third line. Frank chalks his name after CAR 3. Danny takes the chalk back and adds a small arrow beside it, pointing up. "For now." Sheila: "That comes off in the morning." Danny: "It goes back on at night." The share is half.
- Nowhere. Frank puts the chalk down. Danny nods, goes out, and on the shutter of the empty unit three doors up writes CAMMACK in letters a foot high. "Mine doesn't rub off, nephew. I go over it."
- Frank pockets the chalk. "Think quick."

In the doorway Craig Fewson has come to drive Danny home. He reads the board, whatever it now says, before he looks at anybody.

Sheila takes the key from Frank and holds it to the light.

SHEILA: That's a Yale. That's somebody's front door.

She hangs it on the third hook all the same.

PAULINE: Out seventeen fifty-two, in eighteen-oh-three and a half. That's not six.

## What the player can do

- Get in, or walk. Sign: the lit sign on Mickey's own car. Walking, he hears less and is seen less.
- The handset. Hands: lift it and say one of Frank's lines, or leave it. If he says he is in it, the whole Hook, which overhears the radio, has the new owner riding with Cammack by his own word. If he says who is driving, Kevin comes on at once: "Has he." Nothing: Pauline says "Car two is not answering" three times in the ride. The player will hear those words once more, on the last day.
- The handbrake. Hands: pull it. The back steps out, Danny catches it, the car stops across the road. "Mickey used to do that. That's a no, then?" The offer is made there.
- The meter. Flip the flag up: "Then it's free. First thing you've give me."
- The letter: say what is in it, hand it over, or lie.
- The offer: four answers. The key: he asks, and gets a Yale. The chalk: four ways.

## What the town remembers

- Everybody with a radio heard car two called and not answering, or heard who answered. Ada saw it parked on the edge under its own sign. Next day, to Frank's face, Brenda: "I see Cammack's driving you about now"; or, if he answered the radio himself, Terry: "You want to mind whose car you're seen in. It's yours."
- Danny has what Frank told him about the letter. Read: he says to Fewson, that night, "A V. His own nephew." Told the lie: he tells the caff that Mickey meant him to keep the key, and for five weeks he half believes he is owed the car.
- The board holds where the player put him, to the end. On the third line the arrow comes back every night and Sheila rubs it off every morning; Danny asks twice more for the top. That is his grievance, and the player chalked it. On the top line there is no arrow, and it is Fewson who stops answering Danny.
- Fewson read the board from the doorway. With Danny on top of another firm's board he says, on the pavement, where Frank can hear: "So whose are we?" "Mine." "And whose are you?" Danny does not answer that.
- The Yale hangs on the third hook. In week three it is found to fit the side door of the empty unit three doors up, where Danny's men are by then.
- The brass key stays on Danny's ring. On the last afternoon it is in car two's ignition, on the quay's edge.
- Danny owes four pounds ten, and Sheila has written it under what he owed already.
- Ron comes on the door at six and hears it from Pauline. He goes out and looks at car two for some time, then opens the driver's door and runs the seat back to where it was.

## Variants by path

- Car three went into the dock. Danny has no motor to offer: "Three lads, and a hatchback with a crab in it."
- Frank kept the twenty in week one. "I've an account here already. Take the four pound ten out of it."
- He ended the arrangement on night one. Danny points at the empty overhang: "Nobody's stood there. Somebody will be."
