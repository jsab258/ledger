# 06. Six minutes in car two

- Where it sits: Act IIa, week two. Danny Cammack's visit.
- What wakes it: car three has been played (or Danny moved his own car and told it his way); Frank has answered Sheila's Sunday question, or the street has answered it for him; car two is standing on the rank with nobody in it; Frank is on foot on Quay Street as the lamps come on.
- Who must be present: Danny Cammack. Pauline Kitching on the air. Sheila at the board afterwards. Craig Fewson in the office doorway at the end. Fallback: if Danny cannot come (in hospital after week one), Fewson walks in with the offer, no car and no key, and says only, "Danny says half, and his name where it was." The board choice is the same.

Frank is trying to get Danny's men, his car and his nerve under Mickey's name without putting Danny's name over his own. He will give up half the nights, or the top of his board, or a quiet street.

## Staging

Quay Street at lighting-up time, about ten to five by the Harbour Board clock. The rain has stopped and the road holds the sky.

Car two comes up the street from the wrong end with its roof sign lit: MICKEY'S, yellow, on a magnet, its curly lead running in through the top of the driver's window to the lighter socket. It is a ten-year-old saloon that drives from the back wheels, washed every morning by a man who cannot drive it.

Danny is at the wheel with the seat run forward. In the ignition is a brass key on a leather fob gone black with handling. The car's own key is where it has been all day: on its hook on the board, where Pauline can see it.

On the dash: a meter with a flag, and the radio handset on its clip.

The camera rides in the back seat, between the two heads, for the whole drive. It looks where Danny points. The ride is about three real minutes of play, an hour and a half by the game's clock, and six minutes by Danny's.

## The scene

The car stops beside Frank. Danny leans across and pushes the passenger door open.

DANNY: Cab for Nowak.

If the player does not get in, Danny keeps pace with him at the kerb in first gear and gives the whole tour through the window, about Quay Street only, and makes the offer at the office door. If he gets in:

Danny knocks the meter's flag down. It starts to tick.

DANNY: Flag's down. Six minutes, the whole Hook. Mickey took twenty and called it a round.

He drives quickly and well, one hand on the wheel, pointing with the other. Every door gets a price. The player can speak at any time; Danny takes whatever is said into the patter and does not slow down.

DANNY: Fish. A fiver a week, and she'd throw a kipper after it. Launderette, a fiver. Takeaway, ten. I'm telling you what it's worth, I'm not telling you to do it. Caff: you'd get a slap and a tea. Her at the window's free. She's better than free, she's a camera.

The radio opens.

PAULINE: Car two? Car two, who's in car two? Your keys are on my hook.

The handset is in reach of Frank's hand. See what the player can do. Danny talks over it.

DANNY: Rita's. Nowt. Rita's is mine; you pay Rita. Ferry rank: thirty a week to whoever's stood on it. That's the Widow's lad stood on it. Give him a wave.

He sounds the horn. Mark Billany, on the ferry rank with his cap pushed back, watches Mickey's car go by with Cammack driving it and the new owner beside him.

DANNY: The gate. A fiver a car, a week, hers. You're paying it and nobody's told you yet. Cold-store road, that's Agar. You never see Agar. You get a letter.

The car slows for the first time, past a dark warehouse with its doors shut.

DANNY (lightly): And that door's mine. As your book knows.

He picks the speed up again.

DANNY: Landing. Sixty a case, two cases a week, and an old man with a torch who thinks it's nineteen sixty.

PAULINE: Car two is not answering. Kevin, if you see car two, tell it.

DANNY: She used to say that about me. I was answering. I just wasn't answering her.

Quay Street again, from the top. He does not stop at the rank. He takes the car on down past the chandler's, onto the coping stones, and eases the front wheels over the edge, one and then the other, until the sill touches stone without a sound. Handbrake. The yellow sign lights the wet quay round them.

DANNY: That's how you park it.

He flips the meter's flag up and reads it.

DANNY: Four pound ten. That's the Hook, by Mickey's clock. Now add up the doors. (He does not wait.) Hundred and forty a week before you touch the landing. He drove past it every day for thirty year with his flag down.

PAULINE: That was never six minutes.

DANNY (to the handset, without picking it up): Harbour Board clock's wrong.

He turns in the seat.

DANNY: Here's me. Four lads who do as they're told and one who thinks. A motor, now your lot have done lifting it. And I'll go in any door in this town and come out smiling. Here's you. An office, a licence, a radio and Ron. Put them together and that's the Hook. Half.

PLAYER (own words). The scene reads four kinds.

- Yes. "Then we'll go and write it up."
- A lower figure. Danny laughs. "A third. If my name goes back where it was." He means the top of the board, and says so if asked.
- No. "Then I'm the lad across the road, and you've seen how I park." He puts the car in reverse; the back wheels take it off the edge as if it had been on a drive.
- Not yet. "Think quick. I've the ferry rank from Monday whether you've thought or not."

If the player asks for the key, or holds out his hand for it, Danny works a key off his ring without looking and drops it in Frank's palm.

DANNY: There. Don't say I never give you owt.

At the office he walks in ahead of Frank and stops at the board. Under CAR 2 the paint holds the ghost of a name, rubbed and never washed: D. CAMMACK.

DANNY: You can still read me. He never washed it. He used his cuff.

He takes the chalk off the ledge and holds it out to Frank.

- The top line. Frank chalks D. CAMMACK above CAR 1. Danny looks at it and for once has nothing ready. "That'll do." Sheila, from her desk: "Then one's two and two's three, and I'm to tell Kevin he's been overtaken by a hatchback." The share is a third.
- The third line. Frank chalks his name after CAR 3. Danny takes the chalk back and adds a small arrow beside it, pointing up. "For now." Sheila: "I'll have that off in the morning." Danny: "I'll have it back on by night." The share is half.
- Nowhere. Frank puts the chalk down. Danny nods, goes out, and on the shutter of the empty unit three doors up writes CAMMACK in letters a foot high. "Mine doesn't rub off, nephew. I go over it."
- Frank pockets the chalk. "Think quick."

In the doorway Craig Fewson has come to drive Danny home. He reads the board, whatever it now says, before he looks at anybody.

If a key was handed over, Sheila takes it from Frank and holds it to the light.

SHEILA: That's a Yale, love. That's somebody's front door.

She hangs it on the third hook all the same.

## What the player can do

- Get in, or walk. Sign: the lit sign on Mickey's own car. Walking, he hears less and is seen less.
- The handset. Hands: lift it and speak, or leave it. "It's me" or the like: Pauline says, "Then tell whoever's driving I've had two calls for that car," and the whole Hook, which overhears the radio, has the new owner riding with Cammack by his own word. "Cammack's got it": Kevin, at once, "Has he." Danny leans in: "Evening, Pauline." Nothing: she says "Car two is not answering" three times in the ride. The player will hear those words once more, on the last day.
- The handbrake. Hands: pull it. Sign: it is by his knee. The back steps out, Danny catches it, the car stops across the road. "Mickey used to do that. That's a no, then?" The offer is made there, standing.
- The roof sign. Hands: pull the lead from the socket. The sign goes dark. "Suit yourself. They can still see it's his."
- The meter. Flip the flag up: "Then it's free. First thing you've give me."
- Ask for the key. Sign: the car's own key is on the board, and this one is in the ignition. What follows: a Yale.
- The chalk: four ways, as above.

## What the town remembers

- Everybody with a radio heard car two called and not answering, or heard who answered. Billany saw it pass. Ada saw it parked on the edge under its own sign. Next day, to Frank's face, Brenda: "I see Cammack's driving you about now"; or, if he answered the radio himself, Terry: "You want to mind whose car you're seen in. It's yours."
- Pauline holds the time and says it to anybody: "Six minutes. I had it at eleven and a half."
- The board holds where the player put him, to the end. On the third line the arrow comes back every night and Sheila rubs it off every morning; Danny asks twice more for the top. That is the grievance, and the player chalked it. On the top line there is no arrow, and it is Fewson who stops answering Danny.
- Fewson read the board from the doorway. With Danny on top of another firm's board he says, on the pavement, where Frank can hear: "So whose are we?" "Mine." "And whose are you?" Danny does not answer that.
- The Yale hangs on the third hook. In week three it is found to fit the side door of the empty unit three doors up, where Danny's men are by then. It is a way in, once, and Danny gave it him.
- The brass key stays on Danny's ring. Sheila, locking up: "He's still got it, then." On the last afternoon it is in car two's ignition, on the quay's edge.
- Ron comes on the door at six and hears it from Pauline. He goes out and looks at car two for some time, then opens the driver's door and runs the seat back to where it was.

## Variants by path

- Car three went into the dock. Danny has no motor to offer: "Three lads, and a hatchback with a crab in it." He opens at half and a new car on the firm's terms.
- Frank kept the twenty in week one. "I've an account here already. Seven goes. I'll take them in nights."
- He ended the arrangement on night one. The landing's price is not in the tour. Danny points at the empty overhang instead: "Nobody's stood there. Somebody will be."
