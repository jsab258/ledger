# 08. The gate at dawn

- Where it sits: Act IIa, week three: the fifth of the five mornings Eric Tharratt asked for.
- What wakes it: the firm has four cars and has met the packing shift on the minute four mornings this week; the Widow's people have had their first look at the firm; it is twelve minutes to six; Frank is driving the first car of four.
- Who must be present: Frank Stathers, the Widow's man on the gate. Ron Kirby, who is driving the fourth car on his L-plates because the firm is a driver short; he asked all six packers which of them held a licence, one did, and she is sat beside him because the book says somebody must be. The packers. The pen. Fallback for Stathers: Mark Billany, with the list and without the tally; then the offer to Ron is made a day later, at the office door, by the Widow's car.

Frank is trying to get four cars of packers to a clock by six. He will give up twenty pounds and his pride in front of the docks, or Ron's place among the men he stood with.

## Staging

The dock gate, in the dark. Sodium lamps, breath, frost on the rails.

The gate is two iron leaves on wheels across the dock road, with a small wicket beside it for people on foot. Four hundred yards beyond it the cold stores are lit, and over their door is a time clock that stamps a card black until six and red after. A red card is a quarter of an hour docked.

To the right of the road is the pen: a railed square, half roofed in tin. Fourteen men stand in it, apart, hands in pockets, waiting for a quarter to seven, when two ships' worth of them will be called and the rest will go home.

In the road stands a man in a white hard hat with a board under his arm. On his belt is a wire loop of brass tallies, each stamped with a number. This is Frank Stathers. He has had this gate for twenty years, and the pen calls him Frank.

In the back of the first car are three packers in headscarves and overalls, white wellingtons in carrier bags on their knees. They have been talking about the driver since Quay Street as if he were upholstery.

Three sets of headlights follow. The last is a long way back, and slow.

The camera is in the car until Frank gets out, then wide, so the gate, the pen and the cars are in one frame.

## The scene

PACKER (the youngest): Is that him? The nephew?

PACKER (the eldest): There's nowt of him.

PACKER (the middle one): Mickey had upholstery.

THE ELDEST: You could get hold of Mickey.

THE YOUNGEST: You did.

THE ELDEST: I never. I said you could.

The gate comes into the headlights, open. Two men in the road put their hands to the near leaf and walk it shut. It closes on its stop with a clang. Stathers stands in front of it and looks at his watch.

THE MIDDLE ONE: He's shut it.

THE ELDEST: It's twelve minutes to. If that card stamps red I'm quartered, and so are you, and I'll have it off him.

The player has Frank. He can get out, or sit. Behind, two cars pull up. The fourth arrives at eleven miles an hour, stops in three goes, and stalls. Ron gets out of it, puts his cap on, and comes forward into the lights. He looks at the pen, once, and then does not.

Stathers looks past Frank.

STATHERS: Now then, Ron.

RON: Frank.

It is the first time the player has heard Ron say that name. It was not said to him.

STATHERS: I hear you're driving.

RON: Learning.

STATHERS: At your age.

RON: Aye.

Frank does up his jacket button.

FRANK (his line, or the player's own words): Frank Nowak. They're my cars.

STATHERS: I know what you're called. There's one Frank on this gate, and he was here first. (He reads from the board.) Mr Nowack. Four cars. Five pound a car, the week. That's twenty pound, and it's Friday. You'll get a receipt.

Before Frank can answer he has turned back to Ron. He slides one tally off the wire loop and holds it out, across Frank, on the flat of his hand. The camera reads it: 39.

STATHERS: It's been on the hook since you handed your book in. There's a fruit boat on four berth wants sixteen, and I've eleven I'd pay. That's a day, Ron. And tomorrow's a day. (He tips his head at the pen, not unkindly.) You'd not stand in that. You'd walk through with me.

Fourteen men are listening to a man be offered what they are standing there for.

Ron takes the tally. He turns it to the lamp and reads the number. He puts it in his coat pocket. He does not move toward the gate, and he does not move back toward the car.

STATHERS: That's not an answer.

RON: No.

A packer winds her window down. It is nine minutes to six. The rest is the player's.

Pay. Frank counts twenty pounds onto Stathers's board. Stathers writes it up on a duplicate pad against his knee and tears off the top copy.

FRANK: And a receipt. Nowak. N, O, W, A, K.

STATHERS (looking at what he has written): That's what I've put.

The men walk the leaf open. Four cars go through with five minutes in hand, past a pen that watched the new owner pay.

Bluff. FRANK (his line, or the player's own words): "That's Mr Tharratt's packing shift you've shut out. It clocks on at six. Go and tell him why." It holds if it names something Stathers cannot check before six (Tharratt, the Harbour Board, whose shift this is) and if the packers will back it, which they will for any driver who has got them there four mornings running.

THE ELDEST (out of the window): Frank Stathers, I danced at your wedding. That's the packing shift you're stood in front of. Open that gate or I'll see your mother in chapel.

STATHERS (after a time): Today. (To Frank.) Friday it's twenty.

A bluff that is only a threat does not hold. Stathers says, "Then come and open it," and it is a push.

Push it himself. FRANK: "I'll open it myself." He walks to the leaf and puts his shoulder to it. The two gate men come at him, and it is a fight in the headlights, with fists, in front of the pen and three cars of women. Ron stands where he is with his hand in his pocket. Then the second man goes for Frank's back, and Ron is there: one shove, the man on the cobbles, and Ron's boot on the tail of his coat, holding him down without hitting him. He looks at nobody. Stathers does not lift a hand; he is watching Ron. If Frank wins, he walks the leaf open himself. If he loses, he is put against his own bonnet, and the packers get out and walk.

Send Ron. FRANK: "Ron. Open it." (Or anything in the player's own words that orders Ron to the gate.)

Ron looks at Frank. Then he walks to the gate. The two men look at Stathers.

STATHERS: You'll not put hands on him. I'll not have that said of this gate.

They step away. Ron sets both hands on the cold iron of the leaf and walks it open, the way he has watched it walked ten thousand mornings by the man whose job it was. Nobody stops him. Nobody speaks. In the pen one man turns round to face the dock wall. Then the man beside him. By the time the leaf is on its stop, fourteen backs are to the road.

Ron walks back to the fourth car and gets in.

Go round. Hands: back in the car, and reverse. The cold-store road and the fish dock, unlit, a long mile against the minutes. The fourth car cannot do it at eleven. Either its two packers are late, and Tharratt counts three cars; or they are squeezed into the others, six to a back seat, and Ron is left standing by an empty car with an L on it, between Stathers and the pen, with a tally in his pocket, until somebody comes back for him.

Let them walk. The packers get out, say what they think, and go through the wicket at a run, boots in bags. They may make the clock. The cars did not, and Tharratt was counting cars.

However it went, Stathers has one more thing for Ron, said to his back or his face.

STATHERS: It's on the hook or it's in your pocket, Ron. It can't be both for long.

Ron does not answer it.

## What the player can do

- Pay. Sign: the price is said aloud, with the day it falls due. What follows: the contract; £20 every Friday; a receipt made out to Mr Nowack, and a day later a second, in the Widow's own hand, spelt the same. That receipt is the name behind the gate entries on the fourth morning of the last week.
- Bluff. Sign: the eldest packer's window is already down. What follows: through today, and the twenty still owed on Friday.
- Push it himself. Sign: two men, squared up, and Ron's hand in his pocket. What follows: the gate, if he wins; the Widow moves against him a step; Ron has laid a hand on a gate man in front of the pen, and it was to keep him off Frank's back.
- Send Ron. Sign: Ron looks at him first, for as long as the player lets him. What follows: the gate opens without a blow, and the pen turns its back on Ron Kirby. It stays turned on Remembrance Sunday, and in week five.
- Go round, and decide what to do about the fourth car.
- Let them walk.
- Ask Ron about the tally, afterwards, in the car. He takes it out, looks at it, puts it back. "It's brass. It'll keep."

## What the town remembers

- The pen saw all of it and tells it by which thing Frank did. Paid: "Counted it onto Stathers's board like a man paying his subs." Pushed: "Went at the gate himself, I'll give him that." Sent Ron: nobody in the pen tells it at all, and that is how the street knows.
- The packers tell the cold stores before the hooter. "There's nowt of him" has gone round the packing floor by the first break; so has what he did at the gate. Eric Tharratt has it from them before he comes to the door with his clipboard: "Five mornings. On the minute. Now we'll talk." Or: "Four cars, I said."
- Stathers tells Maureen Jensen that Kirby took the tally and gave no answer. She asks which pocket.
- Ron has number 39 in his coat from this morning on. The player sees him turn it over in his fingers on the door, and once in the car. In week five Stathers calls him into the pen on it (scene 11). In one ending it hangs on a hook again.
- The name. Stathers says Mr Nowack to the end. The pen says "Mickey's lad" until the Landing. Ron goes on saying boss.

## Variants by path

- A pact with the Widow. The gate is open and Stathers waves the cars through with his board. He still stops Ron's. "Thirty-nine. It's yours whenever. She says so." Ron pockets it the same.
- Danny on the board. He drives the third car and gets out to watch, eating. To Stathers: "Morning, Frank. This is Frank. Have you two met?" It does not help.
- Haddock's dear ended with the Widow's favour asked. Stathers opens with that: "She's done you one turn already this month, Mr Nowack."
- A firm with no night money. The same morning and the same tax, paid out of fares.
