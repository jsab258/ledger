# 02. The ask on the rank

- Where it sits: Act I, week one, the Monday night (night one), about ten real minutes into play.
- What wakes it: dark has fallen on day one; Mickey's arrangement stands; Ron has found Frank. Ron brings it to the office and, if Frank is not there, goes looking. If he has not found him by one in the morning the ask passes unasked and comes again on Wednesday.
- Who must be present: Ron Kirby. Darren Milner is on the pavement if his routine has him there; if not, Pauline, through the open door, carries his part. Fallback carrier for the ask itself: the old firm's man walks up from the landing and says "Mickey's?" at the office door, and Ron explains it after.

Frank is trying to get told the size of what he has been left. He will give up the old man's easy opinion of him, on the first night, to say what he wants in its place.

## Staging

The rank outside Mickey's, twenty to ten. Fine rain has started. One lamp column makes a pool over the rank and the wet road gives it back; beyond the pool the street is dark as far as Rita's lit window and the phone box.

Car two stands on the rank. Car one is out. The office door is propped open with the bucket, and through it comes the desk lamp and Pauline's voice closing the evening: one fare given out, then the sound of a sheet torn off a pad along its perforation.

Ron comes out carrying two things. Over his arm, on a wire hanger, a plain dark coat that is nobody's, with a laundry ticket still pinned inside the collar. In his hand a brown window envelope gone soft as cloth. It has been turned inside out and re-stuck more than once, so an electricity board's red printing shows backwards through the paper. Behind the window is a strip of pink carbon paper with figures on it in blue: 2.40, 1.90, 3.10.

Sound: rain on the car roof, the radio, and from the water the ferry working up for its last crossing at twenty to eleven.

The camera comes in close for talk. It may read the window of the envelope. It may not read the laundry ticket until the coat is in Frank's hands.

## The scene

PAULINE (inside, on the radio): Car one, Terry, the boarding house for the cold-store road, he says he's late already. Two minutes. (A pause.) Real ones.

The pad tears. Ron steps out under the lamp and stops in front of Frank. He holds the envelope a little away from himself, as a man holds something that belongs to somebody else.

RON: Mickey took this down the ferry landing, regular. After ten, to the man who asks for Mickey's. They'll be expecting it. Say no and that's Mickey's arrangement finished, mind; your call.

He holds it out. Frank takes it and turns it to the light. The camera reads the window: the pink strip, the small sums.

RON: He never bought an envelope in his life. That one's done since Easter.

FRANK: What did he get for it?

Ron thinks about how to put it. The rain ticks on the roof of the car.

RON: Left alone.

FRANK: Thirty years, and that's the firm? An old man walking an envelope down to the water? Not for me. Every fare after dark, every door on this street, every quay with a rope on it: it goes through this office or it doesn't go. By Christmas, Ron. Mickey's runs the Hook. And when they say Mickey's, they'll mean me.

Ron looks at him for the length of it. Then he looks at the envelope in Frank's hand.

RON: Aye. Well. Envelope still wants carrying.

From the dark by the phone box, where he has been standing out of the rain with his collar up:

DARREN: By Christmas. (He comes into the edge of the light, pleased.) He never said which.

RON (not turning his head): Get home, Darren.

DARREN: I'm going. I'm only saying. It's October.

He goes, walking backwards for the first three steps so as not to miss anything. If Darren is not on the street, the line is Pauline's, called through the door without looking up from her pad: "By Christmas. I'll put it in the diary, shall I?"

Ron lifts the coat on its hanger.

RON: If you go tonight, boss, wear that. In the dark nobody looks twice at a coat.

The coat hint shows. From here Frank's words are the player's.

PLAYER (own words). What Ron holds, and will say if asked, each once:

- What is in it: "Never asked. Never looked. It's not mine to look."
- Who the man is: "Stands by the ticket window. He'll say Mickey's. You say nowt else to him and he'll say nowt to you."
- What "left alone" was: "Gate let his cars through. Licence come back signed. Nobody stood on that step he didn't want there. Thirty year."
- Whether Ron will carry it instead: "I'd carry it. They'd not take it. It has to be Mickey's, and that's you now."
- Whether Ron will come: "To the wall. Not past it. They see two, they think summat's changed."
- The coat: "It's just a coat." (If Frank has it in his hands, the camera can read the ticket pinned in the collar: a number, and KIRBY in the laundry's pencil.)

If the player says something that sounds like a no ("Tell them no", "I'm not doing it", "Count me out"):

RON: You want me to tell them no to the envelope, boss? That's Mickey's arrangement finished, for good. Say yes and I'll take your no down the landing.

Only a plain yes as the very next thing said ends it. Then:

RON: Right. (He takes the envelope back, puts it inside his own coat, and hangs the dark coat on the office door handle.) I'll walk it down. It's owed that.

He goes down Quay Street alone, in the rain, at his own pace, and does not look round. Anything else the player says leaves the ask standing:

RON: Then it stands, boss. I'll be on the door.

If the player takes it and goes, Ron walks at his shoulder as far as the quay wall, where the street lamp ends. He stops there with his hands in his pockets.

RON: I'll be here.

He is. When Frank comes back up from the landing, by whatever road and however late, Ron is at the wall in the rain, and falls in beside him without asking how it went.

## What the player can do

- Carry it, in the coat. Hands: one key puts the coat on. Sign: Ron's line. What follows: in the dark he is a coat, and whoever sees him on the quay holds a shape, not a man (scene 03).
- Carry it as he is. Sign: Ron glances at the funeral suit and says nothing. What follows: anybody out on the quay who saw him arrive this morning can say it was him.
- Tell Ron no, and say yes to his question. Sign: Ron's own words, twice, that it is for good. What follows: the arrangement ends tonight; Ron carries the answer down; every rival looks at the office a week sooner; the half line still goes in the book (scene 04).
- Say nothing, walk off, or go upstairs. The ask stands. If nobody has answered it by one, the man at the landing waited and says so tomorrow.
- Look inside. Hands: with the envelope held, one key untucks the flap, which is folded and not gummed. Sign: the window already shows the sums. What follows: Frank sees a whole carbon sheet of the night's fares, time, pick-up, drop and price, eleven lines; the last line has a time and a pick-up and nothing after. Nobody can tell it was opened. The player now knows before he gets there what the old firm buys.
- Ask Ron anything. He answers from the list above and from nothing else.

## What the town remembers

- Darren heard the whole speech. At the caff next morning, to the man from the landing, who is eating: "Mickey's runs the Hook, he says. By Christmas." By the second day it has reached the parade and comes back to Frank's face as a joke: Brenda Laverack, wrapping fish, "Morning. Does the Hook know yet?"; Kevin, on the radio, "Car one to the Kingdom, come in."
- The sentence stays in the town's mouth all game and changes its tone without changing a word. Danny says it in week one, sitting on the counter (scene 05). Garbutt says it in week four, as a fact. In the Kingdom's close a stranger says it to another stranger, and means him.
- Ada saw two men under the lamp and one of them walk to the wall with an envelope. It is no crime, so it is talk: "He was out late, the first night. With Ron."
- Pauline holds the time. She has written ten o'clock, Office, and R.K. in the column for whoever brought the job in, and left the rest of the line for somebody to finish. It is on tonight's carbon, in the envelope.
- Ron holds exactly what he said, and can say it again: where, when, what a no means. He tells nobody anything.

## Variants by path

- The ask passed unasked (Ron never found him): the speech has not been said. It is said on Wednesday, word for word, when the ask comes again.
- The fallback (the old firm's man at the door): he says "Mickey's?" and nothing else, and waits. Ron, from the doorway, gives his first line as written, and the scene runs from there with the man standing in the rain, hearing all of it. He hears "By Christmas". Darren's line is not needed; the man at the landing tells it himself.
