# 16. The Widow waits up

- Where it sits: Act IIb, week six, the same night as the yard, towards two in the morning. The rest after the heaviest fight, and the deed only Maureen Jensen would do.
- What wakes it: the yard has played; Frank comes back to Quay Street from casualty, or from Grace Rinaldi's kitchen; the Widow is alive and has not yet sat down at Garbutt's table.
- Who must be present: Maureen Jensen. Outside, in a car with its lights off, Frank Stathers. Whoever let her in: Ron on the door, or Julie on the night desk, or nobody. Fallback: if she cannot come, Stathers has left a covered plate on the counter with her card under it, and one line in her hand: "Twice in the oven. M.J."

Frank is trying to get to his bed without owing anybody for it. He will give up a dinner's worth of pride, because tonight he cannot cut his own meat.

## Staging

Quay Street, nearly two. Frost starting on the roofs of the cars. The front of Mickey's is dark. At the back of it one light is on, where no light should be: the door of Mickey's own office stands open, and the lamp on his desk is lit.

Frank's left arm is in a plaster back-slab to the elbow and a collar-and-cuff sling. In his coat pocket are two tablets in a paper envelope and a card for the fracture clinic. His bootlaces are undone, because nobody at casualty thought to do them.

A car is parked beyond the lamp column with a man in it, in a white hard hat he has not taken off.

In Mickey's office: the desk, cleared; the green lamp; the chair turned to face the door. In the chair, upright, in a dark coat with a fur collar, hat and gloves on the desk beside her bag, is a woman of fifty-seven with set hair and a good deal of gold. On the desk a brown teapot under a cosy, two cups, and a dinner plate turned upside down over another, standing on a pan.

The camera stays in the doorway with Frank for a long moment before it goes in.

## The scene

If Ron is on the door he says one thing as Frank passes.

RON: She's in the back. I've let nobody else.

If Julie is on the desk: "She said she'd wait. I didn't like to say. Nobody says, to her."

If there is nobody, the street door is unlocked, and that is the first thing she speaks of.

THE WIDOW: Your lock's a disgrace. Megginson's told you, and now I'm telling you.

She does not get up. She looks at the sling, and at the laces.

THE WIDOW: Sit down. It's been in the oven twice.

Frank's good hand goes to the middle button of his jacket. It cannot be done with one.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own): "What do you want, Mrs Jensen?" / "I can cut my own dinner." / nothing.

Nothing makes her hurry. If he stays standing she waits, and says it again exactly, and the second time lifts the top plate off.

It is haddock, done in milk, with mashed potato and peas. It has been cut up. Every piece on the plate is a size for a fork.

THE WIDOW: I was told the left. I'd have looked a fool if it had been the right; you'd have managed a knife.

She puts a fork by his good hand. She pours the tea, both cups, because he cannot hold the pot and the lid at once.

To his question, or to none:

THE WIDOW: I never ask anybody for anything, Mr Nowak. I find out what they're short of.

She looks round Mickey's office as a person looks round a room she knew well with other furniture in it.

THE WIDOW: A man with one arm is short of three things. His meat cut, his laces tied, and somebody stood on his door. I've seen to the first. The second you'll get the knack of. The third I can't do for you.

What she says about the door is the path's.

If Ron is inside:

THE WIDOW: There's a man does the teas on that remand wing who owes me a winter. Your Mr Kirby has had a second blanket since Monday, and the cell by the pipes. He didn't ask. I don't suppose he knows.

If Ron is on the door, and silent:

THE WIDOW: He stood my gate thirty-six years and your door six weeks, and he's not said a sentence to you since that Saturday. I know, because he's not said one to me either, and Frank Stathers asked him very civilly.

If he asks about the yard:

THE WIDOW: Not mine. Mine don't queue for it. Mine are paid Fridays.

If he asks about Mickey:

THE WIDOW: He sat where I'm sat and told me no, most politely, for thirty years. I miss it more than I'd have said.

If Frank called Ron out of the pen and the tally was hung up, she takes it out of her bag when he has eaten and lays it by his plate. The number is 39.

THE WIDOW: That's come back to me twice now. Once in nineteen seventy-two and once off your say-so. I don't keep what won't hang still. You give it him. From you he might take it.

If Ron kept it, there is nothing on the desk but the plates.

THE WIDOW: He's a number of mine in his pocket still, or the police are minding it for him. I shan't ask for it back. Ask him which pocket.

She puts her gloves on, a finger at a time.

THE WIDOW: Monday they bury that old man from the landing, and after it I shall sit across a cloth from you at Grace Rinaldi's and be very hard on you. I can't do that to a man who's falling off his chair. Eat. Take your tablets. Sleep on your right side.

She stands, and is smaller than she looked. At the door of Mickey's office she stops beside him. She looks down.

THE WIDOW: Put your foot up.

If he does, she bends, in the fur collar, and ties his bootlace. A double bow, pulled hard. Then the other.

FRANK (if the player wants it): I'd have managed. In a week.

THE WIDOW: You've not got a week.

THE WIDOW: That's a dinner you owe me, Mr Nowak, and a pair of laces. I'll not want it back in money.

She goes out through the dark office. The camera stays with Frank. Outside a car door opens and shuts, and an engine starts, and the street is quiet.

The pot is still warm. There is a second cup, poured, that nobody drank.

## What the player can do

- Sit and eat. Hands: one. Sign: the plate is cut. What follows: she talks, as above; she has seen him eat at her hand, and counts it. On Monday she opens harder for it, as she said, and she does not walk out.
- Stand, and hear her out. She says everything the same and leaves the plate covered.
- Send her away. FRANK: "Not in his chair, Mrs Jensen." (Or the player's own words.) She goes at once, without a word, putting her gloves on in the street. On the counter in the morning: the plate, cold, and under it a receipt for the gate tax to date, made out to Mr Nowack, with the dinner added as a line. "Haddock, 1. Laces, nil."
- Take the tally, if it is there, or leave it on the desk. Taken: it is his to give to Ron, or not, and Ron is asked a question by it either way. Left: Sheila finds it in the morning, looks at the number, and puts it in the tin.
- Put his foot up, or not. If not: "Then you'll go over on the stairs, and I shall have come for nothing." She leaves them undone.
- Ask her things. She will not speak of her husband, of Monday's terms, or of the fire. To the last: "Ask your priest. He'll not tell you either."

## What the town remembers

- Nobody saw Maureen Jensen walk up Quay Street but Ada, who had not gone back to bed. She tells nobody for two days and then tells Frank: "She had her hat on. She's not had her hat on for this street since his funeral, and she stopped at the back for that."
- Stathers sat outside for an hour and a half with his hat on. He tells the pen nothing. The pen knows where his car was.
- Ron, or Julie, let her in, and holds that Frank came out of that room with his laces tied.
- The Widow holds a dinner. On the fourth morning of the last week, when she is in the back of his cab at the dock gate and her signature is wanted, it is the first thing she prices: "There's the haddock."
- The second cup. Sheila washes two cups in the morning and asks nothing. Mickey's door is shut again by nine, and she has the key.
- The tally goes on from here: to Ron's hand, to a hook, or to the Widow's coat in one ending.

## Variants by path

- The arm was not lost. Somebody else's was, or a scalp; he has been at casualty with them, and has four stitches of his own. The dinner is cut up all the same. "I was told an arm. I'll have a word with who told me. You'll have to eat it like a man with two." She does not tie his laces; they are tied.
- He came from Grace's kitchen floor, not casualty. No plaster yet. "You want that looked at. Stathers'll run you up. No, he will. Finish that first."
- A pact with the Widow. She is not in Mickey's chair but in the customer's, at the side of the desk. "I'd not sit in his. Not while we've an understanding." The rest is the same, and Monday she will sit on his side of the cloth.
- A firm with no night money. "You've not a racket to your name and they've still broke your arm for you. Mickey would have laughed. I shan't."
