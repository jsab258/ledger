# 12. Lesson five

- Where it sits: Act IIb, week five, after the three goes at Frank's men and before the turn. The breach. The turn cannot wake until this has played.
- What wakes it: Ron has had four lessons (any the player skipped, Kevin gave him, complaining); this week the player has watched one leak happen with his own eyes (Danny in the phone box, a crewman at the caff window with a constable, Iveson at DS Ellis's car, or the old hand lifted off the landing); it is afternoon; Ron, on the door: "Lesson five, the book says. If you've the time. I'd as soon it were you sat there."
- Who must be present: Ron Kirby. Pauline at the back door for one line. Fallback: none. This one is his.

Frank is trying to get to the end of a driving lesson without being made to say what is in his own book. He will give up the truth, or the old man's trust in his word, and whichever it is he lets the brake off on it himself.

## Staging

The yard behind Mickey's, three in the afternoon. Dry and cold. The cloud has lifted an inch off the water and the light under it is yellow and flat.

Car two stands nose-in to the wall. It has an L on the bonnet tied with string and an L on the boot tied with a bootlace, since the string went to hold the lid down at the Landing. Whatever dents the lessons made are on it.

Ron is in the seat with his cap on the dash. Since week two the handbrake by Frank's knee has been Frank's.

The road is lesson one's: the yard gate, Quay Street, the fish dock corner, the dock road, the ice house, the four rails and the white board. Nothing has changed on it except that the timber ship has gone from the far wall, and the late boat is no longer on the timetable.

The camera is where it was in lesson one, low between the seats. For most of the scene the player has nothing to do with his hands, and the camera knows it: it watches Ron drive.

## The scene

RON: Door. Mirror. Belt.

He finds reverse the first time. He looks over the correct shoulder. Then he sounds the horn: three short.

RON: Going astern.

Car two backs across the yard toward the gate, straight, at a walk. The back door of the office opens.

PAULINE: Who's that hooting?

RON (looking backwards, steering): Car two. Going astern.

PAULINE: Well, go astern quieter. I've a caller on.

The door shuts.

RON: Coming up on the gatepost. (It passes, an inch off the wing.) That's the gatepost.

He swings her round in the street in one, finds first, puts his arm out and holds it there, and moves off without a jump.

It is a different drive from lesson one. He names the bollard, coming up, at, and leaving. He names Brenda's step. He names a fish lorry before it has begun to back. The needle goes past eleven and sits on fourteen. There is nothing for the player to stop. He can say the word, and Ron will stop dead and say "Aye," and go on; but nothing needs it. For the first time the man in the passenger seat is not wanted.

One line here is keyed to the leak the player saw this week. Ron says it in the same voice as the buildings.

- Passing the phone box across from the office: "That's the box. (A breath.) That's where he stood."
- Passing the caff: "That's Grace's. That's the window."
- Passing the scrap yard's end: "Leaving Iveson."
- Passing the head of the landing: "That's the landing. Nobody stood there."

Then the ice house, three times. Then the pen comes up on the right, with four men in it. See Variants for what he says there.

RON: Coming up on the railway. Stop, look, listen.

He stops at the white board. He has stopped at it in every lesson. Today he does all of it. Foot brake. Clutch. Then his left hand comes down between the seats, past Frank's knee, and takes hold of the handbrake, and draws it up, three clicks. He puts her out of gear. He switches the engine off.

He winds his window up. It is quiet in the car.

He looks left along the rails, into the grass. He looks right. He listens.

RON: Nowt coming.

He does not start her. His hands go back to the wheel, at ten to two, and he looks at the rails in front of the bonnet.

RON: That first night. What did you have her put in the book?

It is the first question he has asked since the morning Frank got off the boat.

PLAYER (own words). The Core holds the line that stands in the day book and reads the answer against it. The scene reads three kinds.

The truth, whatever the line is.

If the book has the envelope:

RON: And who's it say brought it?

Whatever the player says to that, since it is in the column:

RON: Aye. It would. (He nods, at the rails.) That's all right. It's what happened.

If the book has a fare:

RON: A fare. Landing and back. Waited. (He works it through.) And I'm down as booking it.

He thinks for a long moment.

RON: Then I booked a fare. I can say that. It's not a lie; I did say you were off the rank.

If the book has Sheila's line, and the player tells him so:

RON: She put it to Mickey.

He breathes out through his nose, once, which in another man would be a laugh.

RON: She would.

A lie: anything that is not what stands in the book. That there is no line. That it says something it does not. That his letters are not on it.

RON: Aye.

He takes it whole. He has never had cause to weigh a thing Frank told him, and he does not start now.

RON: Then I know what's there.

Nothing: silence, a turned question, "why?", "leave it".

Ron waits. He is good at it. A minute goes by on the dash clock.

RON (to himself): Stop, look, listen.

He waits a little more.

RON: Nowt coming.

If the player asks why he wants to know, he answers once, with the thing the player saw:

RON: They lifted that old lad off the landing with paper in his coat. (Or: Cammack stood in that box and give your car to the station. Or: Iveson's had his head in her car window. Or: your lad's been sat in Grace's window with a constable.) She'll be reading soon. It's my two letters at the end of that line. I've a right to know what's in front of them.

That is all he has to say. He turns the key. He winds the window down again and puts his arm out, straight, into the cold. He finds first. He lets the clutch up until the car leans against its brake and the bonnet lifts a little.

RON: Handbrake's yours, boss.

He put it on. He will not take it off. The car strains, quietly, and goes nowhere.

The player's hand is the only thing in the scene that can move it.

When he lets the brake off, car two goes forward over the four rails, one, two, three, four.

RON: Leaving the railway.

He turns in the mouth of the ferry road, by the book, and brings her back along the wall.

RON: Coming up on the ice house. That's the ice house. (The chained doors.) Leaving the ice house.

In the yard he stops in one go. It is the first time. He switches off and sits with his hands on the wheel.

RON: That's five. Book says a man can put in for his test after five. Sheila's getting the form.

He takes his cap off the dash.

RON: I'll want a morning off. When it comes.

He gets out, and goes round to the street, and stands on the door.

## What the player can do

- Say "Stop", or pull the handbrake, anywhere on the drive. Sign: Ron names everything today. What follows: he stops, says "Aye," goes on. Nothing is lost and nothing is needed.
- Answer him truly. Sign: the question is plain and he asks it once. What follows: Ron knows what is in front of his initials, and when the time comes he walks in to cover exactly that.
- Lie to him. Sign: he does not weigh it; the player can see him not weigh it. What follows: he walks in to cover a line that is not there. He hears the real one read out through the wall of a cell, in DS Ellis's voice, and covers that instead, and never says a word about the difference except once, on the visit or on the door: "It read different through a wall, boss."
- Say nothing. Sign: the dash clock. What follows: he walks in knowing only that his letters are on a line he has not been told. He covers everything, to be sure.
- Let the brake off. Hands: release. It is the only way the car moves, and whatever was said or not said is now what he drives away with.
- Get out and walk. Hands: the door. Ron may not drive her alone. He has done it once, on one night, for a reason, and will not today. He sits at the white board with the engine running until the light goes, and then switches off and walks home along the wall. Kevin fetches the car. "Left him sat at a railway. There's not been a train on it since I was courting."
- Ask why. Once.

## What the town remembers

- Colin Mastin, from his crane cab, saw Mickey's car stood at the old crossing for five minutes with two men in it and the engine off, and then go on. He says so at the caff. Nobody can make anything of it.
- Pauline holds that car two went astern on three hoots, and says it on the air for a week whenever Kevin reverses.
- Ron holds what he was told, word for word. It is the only thing in the game he takes on trust and then stakes himself on. In the station, two days on, it is what he sets his own statement against.
- The naming. The ice house on the way home was the last building Ron names for Frank before the night between. After that night, on every path, the car is quiet: he is inside; or he is on the door and has had the one thing he had to give taken off him; or he knows what was bought in his name. The player drives the dock road alone, and nobody tells him he is coming up on anything.
- The test. The form comes. It is on Sheila's desk through the last two weeks, filled in, in her hand, with a date on it he does not keep. In one ending there is still an L on the car.

## Variants by path

- The pen, by what Frank did in scene 11. He called Ron out, or stood beside him: "Coming up on the pen. That's the pen." It is the first time he has named it, and he does not say he is leaving it. He fought, or sent somebody, or did not come: Ron goes by it in silence, as in lesson one, and a hundred yards on says, "Leaving the gate."
- He fought at the gate. Ron's knuckles are split and taped, at ten to two.
- Danny on the top line, with Fewson gone quiet. The leak the player saw is Fewson's, at the box, and Ron's line is: "That's the box. That's where the other one stood."
- A bought Ellis. Ron does not know she is bought, and the question is the same. Nothing Frank can say in this car is "She's paid for", unless the player says it. Then Ron looks at him for the first time in the scene. "Paid." He turns back to the rails. "Handbrake's yours."
- The honest firm. The line still names the old arrangement, and the question is the same. Under it there is only the one night, and three more like it, and the brown pages.
