# 14. The night between

- Where it sits: week five, from the Friday morning in the cells to nine on the Saturday. The heaviest scene in the game.
- What wakes it: the search has played and Frank is in custody. Ron has walked into the station by himself, in the coat he wore to Mickey's funeral, asked for DS Ellis by name, and offered a statement nobody asked him for.
- Who must be present: Ron Kirby. DS Ellis. The custody sergeant. Through the night, by the player's choice: Simon Ombler at Agar's door, DC Wardell, Rita Welburn. Fallback: none. Ron is never gone; if a rival put him in hospital this week he has walked in with his arm strapped.

Frank is trying to get Ron out of a police station by nine without paying for it. He cannot. He will give up Ron, or his own name on a charge, or a piece of himself to whoever will sell him a third way.

## Staging

The cells first. A tiled room, a bench, a blanket, a hatch in a steel door. Through the wall on one side is the interview room; the wall is brick, painted cream, and it carries voices.

Then the front office: a counter with a flap, a bench under a noticeboard, a clock with a loud second hand. That is where Ron sits all night, by his own choice, cap on his knee, in a black overcoat that is tight across the back. He has not been arrested. He will not go home.

Then the night, which is the player's: the whole Hook after dark, wet, from about six in the evening until nine. Lit: Rita's window, with a watch in it. The Exchange, where one upstairs office has a lamp on. The back step of the station, where a detective constable smokes at the turn of each hour. A car in the station yard with its inside light on and a woman writing.

The camera in the cells does not move from Frank's bench. What comes through the wall, it only hears.

## The scene

The custody sergeant books him in. He has a form, a biro on a string, and all day.

SERGEANT: Name.

FRANK: Nowak. N, O, W, A, K.

SERGEANT (writing): Novak.

He looks at it. He crosses it out.

SERGEANT: Nowack. (He looks at that.) No. (He writes a third time, slowly, and turns the form round.) Is that it?

It says Knowak.

SERGEANT: Laces. Belt. Tie.

Frank's bootlaces go into a bag with a brown label. The sergeant gives him the gist of his rights, as approved, and the door shuts.

Time passes on the bench. Then there is a chair scraped on the other side of the cream wall, and a voice the player has heard every day for five weeks, pitched for a room and not for him.

RON (through the wall): That you, boss?

PLAYER (own words). Whatever he says:

RON: Say nowt.

A door. A second chair. Ellis's voice, level.

ELLIS: You understand nobody's asked you for this, Mr Kirby.

RON: Aye.

ELLIS: I'm going to read you the entry. Then you tell me what you want to say about it.

She reads the line. It is the third time the player has heard it today.

If Frank told Ron the truth in the car, at the white board:

RON: Aye. That's the one.

If he told him nothing:

RON: Read us all of it. The end and all.

She reads the last column. R.K.

RON: Aye.

If he lied to him, there is a silence on the other side of the wall. It is not long.

RON: Read that again.

She reads it again, the same.

RON: Aye. (A breath.) That's right. That's how it were.

Then, on every path, the same, steady, as he says the buildings:

RON: Mickey's arrangement were mine to carry. Thirty year. That envelope on that night were part of it, and I brought it in, like it says. Who had it off us at the water I'll not say, and you can write that I'll not.

ELLIS: And Mr Nowak?

RON: New. Didn't know what he had hold of. I've told you nowt that isn't so.

ELLIS: It'll be typed for nine. The duty solicitor sits in. It opens with a line you write yourself, in your own hand: I make this statement of my own free will. Go home, Mr Kirby.

RON: I'll stop here. If I go home, somebody'll come round and talk to me.

At about six the hatch drops and the door opens.

SERGEANT: You're released on bail without charge, while enquiries continue. Seems it's somebody else's entry. (He tips the laces out of the bag.) Sign there. However you spell it.

Ron is on the bench in the front office. He stands when Frank comes through the flap.

PLAYER (own words). To anything that is a refusal, a promise, or "I'll tell her it was me":

RON: Saying's nowt, boss. You've a firm to stand in. I've a door.

To what he has told her: "I've told her nowt that isn't so. I've just not told her you."

To a straight question, whether he would walk out if a way were found:

- If Frank came for him at the pen, or Mickey's watch has been redeemed: "If it's you that's found it. I'd hear you."
- If neither: "Not bought out. I never saw you at that gate."

He sits down again. The clock is loud. The night is the player's.

Let it stand. He can go home, or sit on the bench beside Ron, or drive. Nothing stops nine o'clock.

Sign first. At any hour he can come to the counter and say he wants to make a statement. Ellis is fetched. It is done in the interview room, under caution, in his own hand, and the player writes: the opening line she dictates, I make this statement of my own free will; and then the line from the book, a third time, and that it was his.

ELLIS: Three times the same. I'll say that for you, Mr Nowak.

She charges him. Ron is brought to the door of the room and told his statement is not wanted. He looks at the paper under Frank's hand.

RON: You've took the one thing I had to give.

He puts his cap on and goes out through the flap. He is on the door at Mickey's when Frank gets there, and says, "Boss," and that is all he says.

A cheaper way. There are three doors, and each is open only tonight.

- The Exchange. Simon Ombler answers the bell in his waistcoat; behind him Agar's lamp is on, and Agar does not come to the door. "Mr Agar can have a man at the station by half past eight. Mr Kirby would be a messenger, no more. There would be an understanding about the yard." There is nothing to pay tonight.
- The station's back step. Wardell, on the hour, with a cigarette. "There's a statement in that file that says which night. Files lose things. Two hundred. Once, and you never met me." It has to be in Frank's hand, in cash.
- The car in the yard. Ellis, alone, writing up. This door is there only if Frank has eight hundred pounds in cash on him. If he has not, it is a detective in a car and nothing can be said to her. If he has, she counts it on her knee, twice, and writes the sum and the date in a small notebook that is not the station's. "A hundred, Fridays. I'll write it down. I write everything down; that's what you're paying for. And you'll have lost me the best line I ever had."

Rita's window. If the watch is still in it, he can knock her up. She comes down in a wool coat over her nightdress with the ticket book. "Fourteen pound, and the fortnight's interest. I'll look at your money. Looking costs you nothing." If he pays and carries it to the bench, Ron takes it, winds it, and sets it by the station clock, five minutes on. "Mickey liked to be early." Then: "I'd hear you."

Nine o'clock. On every path Frank is asked one question, to his face. If he is at the station, Ellis asks it in the doorway of the interview room, with Ron at the table behind her and a pen in front of him. If he is anywhere else, a constable finds him at ten to nine and asks it for her.

ELLIS: Mr Nowak. Before he signs. Is there anything you want to tell me?

If a cheaper way was bought and Ron would hear it, there is nothing to sign. Ombler is in the room with a man in a wing collar; or the file is one statement short; or Ellis herself says there is no entry she can put to him.

ELLIS: Go home, Mr Kirby.

Ron gets up. He looks at who is standing in the room, and then at Frank, and he understands what was done. He says nothing at all. He walks out ahead of him.

If a cheaper way was bought and Ron would not hear it, the money is spent and he is still at the table.

RON: Not for me, boss. Not like that.

Otherwise the question stands, and the player answers it.

PLAYER (own words). "Yes", or that it was his:

ELLIS: Then you'll write it. Will you write it?

That is signing first, at the last minute, and it runs as above. Anything else, or nothing:

ELLIS: Mr Kirby.

Ron draws the paper to him. He holds the pen the way he holds a tally. He writes the first line himself, slowly, a docker's capitals. I MAKE THIS STATEMENT OF MY OWN FREE WILL. He signs each page.

He is cautioned, charged and kept. At the counter his pockets go into a bag: bootlaces, a comb, thirty-eight pounds in notes he has not broken, if Frank called him at the pen; a brass tally, if he kept it; the watch, if he has it.

RON: That's to go to him. Tell him it's five minutes fast.

SERGEANT: Name of the person it's to go to?

RON: Nowak. N, O, W, A, K.

The sergeant writes it. It is the first time anybody on the Hook has spelt it right without being told.

RON (at the cell passage, not turning): Boss.

Frank drives home down the dock road by himself. The chained doors of the ice house come up on the left, and pass, and nobody in the car says so.

## What the player can do

- Nothing. Sign: the clock, and Ron's own words. What follows: he is still asked at nine, and his silence is his answer. Ron goes in, to the end.
- Sign first. Sign: Ron has said that saying is nothing; only paper moves him. What follows: Frank is charged to the end. On Saturday Alan Sowerby's letter suspends the licence for a fortnight: every car off the rank, and the drivers go to Danny's unless they are paid in full from the tin. Eric Tharratt drops the contract. Ellis's case is on Frank. Ron is free, on the door, and silent.
- Agar's brief. What follows: Ron out as a messenger. In the last week the yard's lease is Agar's price, and the third morning is harder.
- Wardell. What follows: £200. He sells the fact to Danny within the week, and Danny says it on the quay in front of the wall.
- Ellis. Only with £800 in hand. What follows: £100 every Friday to the end, each written down; one missed and her notebook is the case.
- Ask Ron first whether he would walk. He tells the truth about it. This is the sign before any money is spent.
- Redeem the watch tonight. It is the one thing that can still change Ron's answer.
- Sit on the bench with him. He does not send Frank away. Asked nothing, he says nothing. Near four in the morning, once: "They do a tea at six. It's not Sheila's."

## What the town remembers

- The station knows that Mickey's doorman walked in off the street and would not go home. The sergeant tells it in the canteen; Wardell tells it to whoever pays.
- Father Walsh is in the front office at eight for another man, sees Ron's coat, and sits with him ten minutes. He is how Ron's messages come out afterwards.
- The Hook knows by dinner time who slept where. Ron inside: on the next Friday Lily Tang counts out one of number nine. "One." Pauline will not have the chair by the radio moved. The jacket stays on the hook.
- Frank signed: "He's signed for it himself. Charged. And Ron stood on the door like nowt's happened, only he doesn't speak." The drivers ask Sheila what the tin holds.
- A way was bought: the town does not know what was bought, only that both men walked. Whoever sold it knows, and Ron knows, and from this morning the car is quiet.
- The naming has stopped, on every path. The player hears it the first time he drives the dock road.
- If Frank lied in lesson five, Ron has that. He says it once, later: "It read different through a wall, boss."

## Variants by path

- Ellis was bought before the search. The car in the yard costs nothing more tonight. She has it ready: "I said it was Friday's business." The rest is the same, and so is Ron's understanding of it.
- The honest firm. There is no night money to find, and no £800. The doors are Agar's and his own.
- Danny on the top line, loyal. He is on the station steps at six with the hatchback. "I'd offer, nephew. They'd not believe it was mine. I've never written owt down in my life."
- The Widow allied. Stathers is on the bench beside Ron at midnight with a flask. He says nothing to Frank. In the morning the tally is in the property bag.
