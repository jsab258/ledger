# 23. Close: Burn Both

- Where it sits: the close, Saturday 1 December, from five until the evening boat has come in and emptied.
- What wakes it: when the books opened there was neither a firm that held nor a friend who still counted him. He left the line and a morning fell; or he bargained the book away across his own counter; or he put a name to it and lost both anyway.
- Who must be present: Shaun Hewson. A stranger off the evening boat. Sheila, for a minute, unless she left long ago. Ron, by path. Fallback: the stranger alone; he is always on the boat.

Frank is trying to find one door on Quay Street that is still his. He gave them up one at a time; the close counts them back to him.

## Staging

Quay Street under its lamps, and a fine rain, as on the first night.

There are cars on Mickey's rank. They are not Mickey's. Their roof signs are a different colour, and their drivers sit in them not looking at the office. Car two is among them, square to the kerb, dark, its key on its hook.

The board inside has been wiped with a sleeve. The radio set is on, hissing, with nobody at it.

Over the door the old paint says MICKEY'S and has been saying it for some time. A ladder leans beside it.

In Rita's window, lit, if it was never redeemed: a man's wristwatch on a card.

Down the dock road the gate is being chained, and nobody of his is there to see it.

The camera stands in the office doorway and looks out. It does not walk.

## The scene

Pauline is unplugging her headphones. She coils the lead round her hand, puts them in her shopping bag, and takes her cardigan off the back of the chair.

PAULINE: They're mine. I bought them. (At the door.) I'd have stopped, you know. If there'd been owt to call.

Sheila has her coat on and her handbag over her arm. She takes one thing from the counter, which is her pen. If the red book was never opened, it is in the bag still.

SHEILA: I'll have this home. It's the only one in here that was ever mine.

She does not say his name. She has reached the step when the evening boat sounds at the landing.

Shaun Hewson comes up the street with a pot and a brush, sets his ladder square under the sign, and looks up at it, and then at whoever is in the doorway.

SHAUN: Wants doing. What's it to say?

Nobody tells him. He waits a decent time. He goes up two rungs and starts rubbing down the M.

What can be seen from the door, in the order the street gives it.

The takeaway. Lily Tang has one order under the lamp, bagged, the top folded twice. She looks at the clock, and down the parade, and counts it.

LILY: One.

She waits. Then she takes it off the counter and puts it on the shelf behind her, where the orders go that nobody came for.

The boarding house step. Sylvia Rennison, in her slippers, if he ever said it to her:

SYLVIA: I'm not Mickey. That's what you said. (She goes in.) Well.

The gate. From the doorway it is only lamps at the end of the dock road, and the sound of a chain being drawn through iron. If Ron is free, he is one of the two men doing it. He is in a jacket that is not his own, with the Widow's mark on the back. When the lock is shut he takes a brass number out of the pocket and hangs it on the hook by the post, and stands back from it with his hands behind him, as a man stands a gate.

If Ron is inside, nobody at the gate ever spoke to Frank. On the remand wing the man who does the teas has been told to say there is a coat for Kirby when he wants it, and a number.

The landing. A man in a dark coat that is nobody's comes off the other firm's rank with something flat in his hand and walks down Quay Street toward the water, in the rain, at his own pace. Under the overhang by the ticket window somebody is waiting, out of the lamp.

THE MAN AT THE LANDING (it carries, on a quiet street): Cammack's?

Or the Widow's name, or Fewson's; whoever has the rank. The envelope is new. What is in it is pink.

Then the boat's passengers come up the brow. Most turn along the quay. One comes up Quay Street: a man of about thirty, in a suit that was bought for something else, with one suitcase and a typed envelope. Gordon Leckenby has told him the way and he has not listened. He finds the door by the ladder.

Sheila is on the step. She looks at the suitcase.

SHEILA: New management.

She goes down the street without waiting for an answer.

The man looks at the envelope in his hand, and then at Frank.

THE STRANGER: I've come about the office. I was to ask for a Mr Nowak. (He reads it off.) N, O, W, A, K.

It is spelt right on his letter. It is the last name anybody calls him.

## What the player can do

- Tell Shaun what the sign is to say. PLAYER (own words). Shaun listens, nods, and goes on rubbing down. "I'll wait till I'm told by whoever's paying."
- Answer the stranger, or not. "That's me" gets the letter: a month's notice on the lease, from a firm in the Exchange, courteous. Silence gets it put on the counter.
- Walk to the gate. The chain is on by the time he is there. If Ron is at the post he says, "Mr Nowak," and looks at the road.
- Start car two. Hands: the key off the hook, ten minutes, the handbrake on. If nobody has run her this week she does not turn. If he has, she starts, and runs, and he can sit in her, on another firm's rank, going nowhere.

## What the town remembers

- That there was a new owner for seven weeks. By the spring the parade has him down to one story, and it is the first: "Him that put Cammack's motor on the edge. What happened to him?"
- Rita winds the watch at closing, sets it five minutes on, and puts it back in the window.
- Sheila has the red book, and the pen, and nothing to write.
- The man at the landing has a new firm's sheet in his coat, and says of the old one only: "Somebody wrote him down."

## Variants by path

- A morning fell. Hebblewhite's long letter is on the mat, addressed correctly.
- The book was bargained away. The top line of the board is not wiped; it says D. CAMMACK, and under the sign Shaun has been told exactly what to paint. He asks Frank anyway. "I like to hear it from the door."
- He put Danny's name to it and lost both. The rank's cars are Fewson's. Terry Hodgson is driving one, and does not turn his head. Grace turns the card in her door to CLOSED as he looks at it.
- Sheila left weeks ago. Nobody takes the pen. The stranger says "New management?" himself, to the empty counter, as a question.
- He is hunted for a killing. The doorway is empty and the camera stands in it alone. Everything is said past it. Shaun asks the street what the sign is to say. The stranger reads the name off his letter to Pauline, who is leaving. "He's not here, love. He's not been here since he came."
