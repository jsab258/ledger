# 23. Close: Burn Both

- Where it sits: the close, Saturday 1 December, from five until the last boat, or until a man under an overhang has been answered.
- What wakes it: when the books opened there was neither a firm that held nor a friend who still counted him. He left the line and a morning fell; or he bargained the book away across his own counter; or he put a name to it and lost both anyway.
- Who must be present: whoever has the rank now (Danny Cammack; if not, Craig Fewson; if not, Frank Stathers for the Widow). Sheila, for a minute, unless she left long ago. Lily Tang. Ron, by path. Fallback: if none of the three will have him, the envelope is not offered, and there is only the boat.

Frank is trying to find one thing on Quay Street that is still his to do. He gave the rest up a door at a time. What is left is an errand, or a ticket.

## Staging

Quay Street under its lamps, and a fine rain, as on the first night.

There are cars on Mickey's rank, and they are not Mickey's. Their signs are a different colour. Car two is among them, square to the kerb, dark, its key on its hook.

Inside, the board has been wiped with a sleeve. On the counter lie two things. One is a long letter from Customs and Excise, addressed correctly. The other is a brown envelope, new, stiff, with a pink sheet in it. On the hook by the door, on its wire hanger, is the plain dark coat that was nobody's on the first night.

His suitcase is at the foot of the stairs.

In Rita's window, lit, if it was never redeemed: a man's wristwatch on a card. Down the dock road there are lamps at the gate.

The camera goes where he goes, a pace further back.

## The scene

Pauline is coiling the lead of her headphones round her hand.

PAULINE: They're mine, these. (At the door.) I'd have stopped, you know. If there'd been owt left to call.

The man who has the rank is sitting on the counter. If it is Danny, he has his heels on the customers' bench.

DANNY: You do days, I said. Only there's no days left, so here's an after. (He taps the new envelope.) Ten o'clock, the landing. He'll say Cammack's. You say nowt else. You know the way; you never had to ask it. Three pound a night. That's a fare.

Sheila has her coat on. She takes one thing from the counter, which is her pen. She looks at the man sitting where the book used to lie.

SHEILA: New management.

She does not say Frank's name. She goes.

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own, or only picks one thing up):

- "Three pound. I'll carry it." He takes the envelope, and the coat off its hanger.
- "Carry it yourself, Mr Cammack." He takes the suitcase. Danny shrugs. "Last boat's eight o'clock now. They took the late one off."

Outside, Shaun Hewson has set his ladder under the sign.

SHAUN: Wants doing. What's it to say?

Nobody tells him, unless Frank does. He waits a decent time, goes up the ladder a little way, and starts rubbing down the M.

Then it is a walk down Quay Street to the water, the same walk as the first night, and what is on it is the same whichever thing is in his hand.

The takeaway. Lily Tang has one order under the lamp, bagged, the top folded twice. She looks at the clock, and at him going by, and counts it.

LILY: One.

She waits. Then she puts it on the shelf behind her, where the orders go that nobody came for.

The boarding house step, if he ever said it to her:

SYLVIA: I'm not Mickey. That's what you said. (She goes in.) Well.

The wall at the end of the lamps, where a man once stood with his hands in his pockets and said he would be there. Nobody is.

The gate, along the dock road. A chain is being drawn through iron. If Ron is free he is one of the two men doing it, in a jacket with the Widow's mark on the back. When the lock is shut he takes a brass number out of the pocket and hangs it on the hook by the post.

FRANK (if the player wants it): Ron.

He does not turn round.

RON: Mr Nowak.

He stands with his hands behind him, facing the gate.

If Ron is inside, nobody at the gate ever spoke to Frank, and the chain goes on without a word.

Then the landing.

With the envelope. Under the overhang by the ticket window somebody is waiting, out of the lamp, a cigarette cupped in his hand.

THE MAN AT THE LANDING: Cammack's?

FRANK (the player picks one, or speaks his own, or holds it out):

- "Cammack's."
- "Frank Nowak."

To the second:

THE MAN AT THE LANDING: I didn't ask who you were. Cammack's?

He will ask three times. When he has the envelope he opens it under the timetable, reads the pink sheet by a pencil torch, and counts.

THE MAN AT THE LANDING: Eleven. (He puts the sheet inside his coat and drops the envelope in the water.) Right. Monday, same again.

That is the last thing anybody says to him. It is another man's name.

With the suitcase. The boat is at the stage with her lights on. At the ticket window Frank empties his pocket onto the ledge, and the player sees what six weeks came to. Karen Grummitt counts out the single fare and pushes the rest back.

At the head of the brow Gordon Leckenby looks at the case.

GORDON: You're Mickey's nephew. You came over in the back end. I told you the way.

FRANK: Frank Nowak.

GORDON: Aye. That were it. (He lifts the chain.) You'll want telling the way, the other side.

FRANK: I'll find it.

From the deck, going out, the Hook is a row of lamps. One sign is lit, too far off to read. At the end of the dock road two lamps stand where the gate is. And down Quay Street toward the water, at his own pace, a man in a dark coat is walking with something flat in his hand.

## What the player can do

- Take the envelope or the suitcase. Hands: pick it up. Sign: both are on show from the moment he comes downstairs. He cannot carry both.
- Tell Shaun what the sign is to say. He nods. "I'll wait till I'm told by whoever's paying."
- Say Ron's name at the gate, or walk by.
- Answer to another firm's name, or give his own three times and be sent away with the envelope still in his hand. Then there is not even the errand.

## What the town remembers

- By the spring the parade has him down to one story, the first: "Him that put Cammack's motor on the edge. What happened to him?"
- If he carried it: "He's on the landing for Cammack, nights. Three pound." Brenda does not make a joke of it.
- If he sailed: Gordon, to Karen, shutting the window: "Wouldn't be told the way. Either time."
- Sheila has the red book, and her pen, and nothing to write.

## Variants by path

- The book was bargained away. The top line of the board is not wiped; it says D. CAMMACK, and Shaun has been told what to paint. He asks Frank anyway. "I like to hear it from the door."
- He put Danny's name to it and lost both. The man on the counter is Fewson, who offers two pounds. Terry Hodgson is driving one of the cars on the rank and does not turn his head.
- The Widow has the rank. The word under the overhang is "Jensen's?"
- He is hunted for a killing. The camera makes the walk alone. Lily counts one to an empty street. At the gate Ron hangs the number up and says nothing. On the landing the man waits under the overhang until one, and nobody comes.
