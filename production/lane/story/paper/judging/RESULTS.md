# The key scenes judged blind: results

24 November 2026. Two checks, as the job asked: the checked scene judge as a screen, and fresh Opus readers who had not seen the frame. Marks from AI readers of AI writing are signs, not measurements (production/research/writing-approach/APPROACH-2026-11-24.md, "Judging without human readers").

## 1. The scene judge (screen)

Ten of the 23 scenes (the first hour's 01, 02, 04 and 05, and the heavy 10, 13, 14, 15, 19 and 20), each retold by a fresh Sonnet to the judge's format (330 to 370 words, disguised, judging/format/), paired by tools/ledger_pairs.py (seed 7) with 4 admired and 4 forgettable shelf scenes and 4 control pairs, both orders. Four fresh Opus and four fresh Sonnet judges, the judge brief word for word; each judge took one of the four groups for all ten scenes at once (so one judge read 60 pairs, never both orders of a pair). Raw output: SCREEN-RAW.txt; inputs and verdicts in screen/.

- The floor (beat all four forgettable scenes in both orders) is met by all ten scenes, for every model that was counted.
- Sonnet was not counted on six of the ten (01, 02, 04, 10, 13, 20): one control pair, the same one in all six because one seed built them, went wrong in both orders. Opus was counted on all ten.
- Against admired scenes (Opus; won, tied, lost of 4): 01 0-0-4; 02 1-0-3; 04 1-0-3; 05 2-0-2; 10 1-1-2; 13 2-1-1; 14 4-0-0; 15 3-1-0; 19 3-0-1; 20 3-1-0. Sonnet where counted: 05 1-1-2; 14 4-0-0; 15 1-2-1; 19 1-1-2; 20 1-2-1.
- Read: the heavy scenes hold their own against admired scenes in content; the opening scenes lose to them. The screen read the drafts before the revision round; it was not rerun after it.
- Limits: the judge was proven only at telling admired from forgettable, not great from good; one model family; a single seed, so the same shelf scenes sat in every scene's pairs; heavy compression (a 2,000-word branching scene to 350 words) favours plot over writing.

## 3. What the readers agree stands in the way (all six)

1. Frank. Everyone round him has a want, a deed and a voice; he has a button, a spelling and dry sums. The scenes players would retell belong to Ron, Sheila, the Widow and Danny. The revision gave him written lines at every decision and a habit; the readers still find him the quietest person in his story (P2 at half from four of six readers).
2. Small set pieces for a crime game: fists only, a midpoint that is a cage on a slope, the crime kept offstage, so menace seldom arrives (P8 at half or fail).
3. Danny's gains in the middle weeks are mostly hearsay (P5 at half from five of six).
4. The plan's machinery leaks into the scenes (rungs, Garbutt's weekly aims, the day of looking) and some things arrive unset (Mickey's watch, Lily's number nine) because the 23 scenes are a selection of a longer story.
5. Two voices alike in each reader's ear: Sheila and Pauline (first round), then Ellis and Hebblewhite, Garbutt and Hebblewhite, Stathers and the Widow.
6. The theme line "no column for who it was brought to" is said three times.

Recognised, by readers, as loose likenesses only: Get Carter, The Long Good Friday, On the Waterfront (the dock hiring), The Italian Job (the car over the edge), The Godfather (a sit-down; since rebuilt), GTA IV's cab depot and Mafia's cab start (premise). Nothing read as copied.

Plausibility points raised once each, not yet checked: remand for a man who confesses to false accounting is unlikely in 1990; a minicab could not lawfully use a street rank, roof sign and meter in 1990; why Customs and Excise inspects the firm is never said.
