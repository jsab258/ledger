## 3. The five weakest, and the one change each

1. **15, the yard.** Make the mutiny Kevin's own, built from the forty-two miles a week that Sheila exposed in week three. Lose the arm to that grievance, through a choice set up earlier, not to "two things in the yard cost the arm."
2. **17, the room at Grace's.** Tie the dead man to Frank's own hand. If the old man from the landing died because of a carbon Frank carried, the family's chair and the four pounds become guilt, not ceremony. As it stands nobody knows how he died.
3. **01, the ferry and the office.** Give the first minutes one deed that shows Frank's skill and costs him something, for example taking car two with Ron beside him and driving it well in front of the street. Then 07's claim that driving is "the one thing he is better at" has been earned.
4. **19, the quay at four.** Have Danny already walking the forty yards with the book when Frank comes out, so he must be stopped in the street in front of the wall. Cut the parked blackmail speech, which gives the opponent no reason to fight.
5. **13, write it again.** Let what the player writes decide who sits in the cells tonight. The same words twice: Ellis goes for R.K. Different words: Frank. Then the memory test has stakes the player can see.

## 4. Voices

Mostly yes. Ron ("Aye", "nowt", "I were", the naming), Sheila ("love", the book's grammar), Pauline (run-on chatter) and Danny (glib, "nephew") could be told apart with names hidden. The faults:

- **Garbutt and Hebblewhite** sound alike, and work alike: dry, exact officials who will not sit and who pay their way. "I'd not want it said anybody here gave me anything" (Garbutt) beside "I don't open a book early. It isn't fair to the book" (Hebblewhite).
- **Stathers and the Widow** share a formal, verdict-giving register: "I'll not have that said of this gate" beside "I don't keep what won't hang still."
- **Frank** has the weakest voice of the main cast: clipped quips ("I'll try not to go mad", "I'm in the wrong trade", "It's a start") that Sheila or Danny could equally say.
- Across the cast there is one authorial aphorism habit. "Somebody always asks what a man started with" (Sheila), "I find out what they're short of" (the Widow), "Somebody always saw. My trouble is finding the one who'll sign" (Ellis): many people speak in the same polished epigram.

## 6. World rules and confusions

No alcohol, gambling or children were found. "Peter" in Grace's kitchen (15) is unexplained; he reads as an adult, but say so. No first person. The dates check: 15 October 1990 was a Monday, 16 November a Friday, 1 December a Saturday, and the caution is the pre-1995 wording.

Plausibility:
- Ron remanded in custody (14, 18) for false accounting on one cab-book line. An elderly man with a fixed address would be bailed almost certainly.
- A minicab firm with a street rank, a lit roof sign and a meter (01, 06). In 1990 private hire could not stand on a rank; that is hackney-carriage work. The brief calls it a minicab office.
- Customs and Excise inspecting a small cab firm's day book holds only if the firm is VAT-registered, and nothing says so.

Confusing to a reader who has not seen the plan:
- What the envelope's carbon is for, and who "the old firm" is. Is it the Widow's, or separate from her? Why does a fare sheet send a van to a lodger?
- What the cases at the landing hold ("Sixty a case").
- How and why the old man from the landing died (16, 17). He is "lifted" in one leak path and buried in another file.
- Unexplained terms: "the second rung" and "the third rung", "the day of looking", "Haddock's dear", "Friday means Friday", "number nine" and Lily's "One", "Garbutt's truce", "Ellis offered it on Thursday", "a firm with no night money", "hunted for a killing", "Mickey's revolver", Iveson's eighty pounds, Alison Sedman and Philip Danby, and the Customs man's five mornings.
- In 10 Ron drives alone on L-plates. The text says so, but the constable never notices.

- The Long Good Friday (film, 1980): docks, and an owner's ambition to run the place by a date. Loose.
- Mafia: The City of Lost Heaven (game, 2002): a cab driver drawn into an organisation. Loose.

## 8. Distance from the bar

On the page this is close to the bar in craft. The naming device, taught in lesson one, broken at the pen, turned at the Landing, stopped by the handbrake in lesson five and silenced after the night between, is a structural idea as good as anything on the reference shelf. The week-one line coming back as the arrest is RDR2's Downes debt done in ink. Several scenes (03, 12, 14, 16, 20) would hold up in a fine shipped game. What keeps it from the top is the lead. Frank is a button he does up and a name he spells. He has no temper, his one claimed skill is never shown, and in every heavy scene the deed and the feeling belong to someone else: Ron walks into the station, Sheila turns the pen, the Widow ties the laces, Danny drives the car. Players remember Arthur because the cough, the choices and the dying are his. Here they will remember Ron. Until Frank does something in week one that only he would do, unprompted, with his own skill and at his own risk, and until his want and what it costs show in his own body and voice rather than in other men's sacrifice, this is a superb supporting cast round a polite absence. Second to that, the crime is kept so far offstage (a carbon, a van, unnamed cases) that a crime game's menace never quite arrives.
