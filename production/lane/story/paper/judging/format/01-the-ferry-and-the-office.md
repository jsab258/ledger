## Setting
A crossing at the harbour at ten past nine on a grey, damp morning, then a street running up from the water to a small cab office. An engine idles on the rank outside.

## People
Abel takes tickets at the boat and wants only to see the lead go by. Bram paints a sign from a ladder. Cass sits in a running car on the rank and asks nothing. Ada keeps the books and wants to show the lead round. Bess works the radio. The lead has just arrived with one suitcase, a typed letter and a small sum of money.

## What happens
The lead came off the boat and gave up his ticket. Abel said the lead would want telling which door; the lead said he would find it. Bram, on his ladder, called him the new owner and was given his full name. Cass watched from the car, lifted one finger,. Ada met the lead at the door and took him in. Bess, on the radio, asked how to say his name and told the drivers the new owner was in. Ada walked him round the office and the stairs to his flat. She counted his money onto the counter, wrote the amount in the cash book and gave it back. The lead went out to the rank, where Cass got out, wiped the door handle, and said he knew who the lead was.

## The lines that matter
Ada says she will write down that the money was there, not take it, because someone always asks what a man started with. Bess asks what to call him on the air and is told how to spell his name. Cass answers everything with the same words: he knows who the lead is. Ada says that on Sunday the lead will tell her what this firm is.

## What the player does
The lead walks up the street, listens to the walk-round or walks on, looks at the empty hook, tries the locked door, talks to anyone. He may pick up the money or leave it.
