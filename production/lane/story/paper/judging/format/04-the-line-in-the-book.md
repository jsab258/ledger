## Setting
The cab office at a quarter past eight the morning after, under a humming strip light, with wet daylight at the window. The street outside is being hosed down. The mood is plain and businesslike.

## People
Ada keeps the books; the lead knows she has told him a small debt falls due by Friday. Abel stands at the door and was on the rank all night. Bess works the radio and arrives last. The lead wants to take a paper and get out.

## What happens
Ada was sorting the night's takings into bags and ticking each line of the day book. She stopped at a half-written line: ten o'clock, booked by Abel, nothing else. She asked the lead what it was. If he gave her a fare, she wrote it up and charged him the fare out of his own pocket. If he told her it was the envelope, she wrote it as a packet carried by hand, no charge, and reopened an account that had been shut three weeks. If he said nothing, she finished the line herself, in a quick practised hand, and said it was what she always wrote. Then she initialled the page and put an older book on the counter, a list of people who owed money. Bess came in shaking her umbrella and asked who had finished her line.

## The lines that matter
Ada says she cannot carry half a line forward. She says she does not mind who the passenger was, only that he paid. She says the other book holds five names and none of them has paid. Bess says she writes only what she is told.

## What the player does
The lead answers in his own words, then says yes to her reading back. He may change the answer before the yes. He may read the page, ask about the old owner, ask her to rub it out, or keep away until noon, when she asks again.
