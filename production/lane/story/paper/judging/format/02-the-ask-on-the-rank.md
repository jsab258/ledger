## Setting
The rank outside a cab office at twenty to ten at night, in fine rain. One street lamp lights the wet road; beyond it the street is dark. A boat is getting ready for its last crossing. The mood is low and watchful.

## People
Abel is the old driver who stays on the door; he wants the arrangement kept going, and the lead knows him as a man who answers only what he is asked. Bram is a young man who hangs about the street and repeats what he hears. Ada works the radio inside. The lead has just inherited the business.

## What happens
Abel came out with a worn envelope and a plain dark coat. He told the lead the old owner had taken the envelope to the harbour every night after ten, to a man who asked for his name, and that in return the firm was left alone. Saying no would end the arrangement. The lead asked what it was worth, was told "left alone", and said the old way was not for him: by the end of the year every fare and door and wharf would go through this office. Abel said the envelope still wanted carrying. Bram, standing out of the rain, repeated the boast and was sent home. Abel offered the coat, saying nobody looks twice at a coat in the dark, and said he would walk with the lead as far as the wall and no further.

## The lines that matter
Abel says the envelope has to go in the old owner's name, and that is now the lead's. The lead says the whole street will answer to him by the end of the year. Abel says only that the envelope still wants carrying. Bram says the lead never said which year.

## What the player does
The lead may carry the envelope in the coat, carry it in his own clothes, or look inside. He may refuse; Abel asks once more, and a plain yes ends the arrangement for good. Or he may walk away, and the ask stands.
