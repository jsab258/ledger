## Setting
The cab office at seven in the morning, with the street lamp still on and the sky going grey. Two plain cars stand at the kerb. Police are emptying every drawer. The mood is orderly and quiet.

## People
Ada is a detective with a warrant, and wants the owner's own hand in the book. Bess keeps the books and sits with her handbag on her knee. Cora works the radio. Abel is the doorman and stands at the wall as every morning. The lead has kept one line in the book standing for five weeks.

## What happens
Ada read the warrant out and her officers bagged the keys and papers. A constable asked Bess for her bag; Ada said the warrant covered premises, not people. Bess offered Ada a mint from the bag, with red cloth showing under her knitting, and Ada declined without looking. Ada turned the day book back to the first Monday and read the line aloud. Then she turned the book to face the lead, held out a pen, and asked him to write it again underneath, the same words. The lead wrote. Ada compared the two and asked who the initials in the last column belonged to. She had him date and initial it, and had two copies certified. She arrested him on suspicion of false accounting. Cora stopped giving out fares. The hook by the door held a jacket, and Abel was gone.

## The lines that matter
Ada says she is asking the lead to remember it the same way twice. If the two lines differ she says that is two, and one of them is his. Bess says she writes what she is told, and that is why it is in hers. Ada says that in five weeks there had not been one line in the owner's hand until now.

## What the player does
The lead writes the line again from memory, writes something different on purpose, or refuses the pen. He may answer her question about the initials or ask for a solicitor. The arrest comes either way.
