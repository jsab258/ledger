## Setting
A police station through one night: a cell with a bench and a cream brick wall, a front office, then the dark town from six in the evening until nine the next morning. The mood is heavy.

## People
Abel is the custody sergeant, careless with names. Bram is the old doorman, who has carried the firm's secret thirty years; he has walked in alone to give a statement and will not go home. Ada is a detective who wants it signed. Cass is an officer who sells lost files. Dorran is a rival's clerk. The lead wants Bram out by nine.

## What happens
Abel booked the lead in and misspelt his name three times. Through the wall the lead heard Bram in the interview room, offering a statement nobody had asked for. Ada read him the entry from the day book. Bram said the arrangement had been his to carry, that he brought the envelope in, and that he would not say who took it. At six the lead was released on bail. Bram sat on the front bench and would not go home. The night was the lead's. He could sit beside Bram, sign a statement himself, or buy a cheaper way from Dorran, from Cass, or from Ada. At ten to nine Ada asked if he had anything to tell her. He said nothing. Bram wrote his statement in his own hand and was charged. He asked that his watch go to the lead, five minutes fast. Abel wrote the name down, and spelt it right.

## The lines that matter
Bram tells the lead to say nothing. He says saying is nothing, and the lead has a firm to stand in while Bram has a door. Ada asks whether the lead has anything to tell her. Bram says the lead has taken the one thing he had to give, if the lead signs.

## What the player does
The lead may do nothing, ask Bram whether he would walk, sit with him, or redeem the watch. He may sign first, or buy a cheaper way. Each has a price.
