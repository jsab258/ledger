## Setting
A quay at a quarter to four on the last afternoon, with the lamps coming on and the water high and still. A cab stands on the edge with its front wheels over, as another did in the first week. A crowd lines the wall.

## People
Ada keeps the books and has just lost the main book. Bess works the radio. Abel is a rival the lead once humbled; he has turned, and holds the book. Bram is his heavy, sitting on the wall. Cass is the doorman, loyal to the lead. The lead needs the book back within the hour.

## What happens
Ada told the lead the book was gone; she had turned round for the tin. Bess called the cab on the radio and got no answer. On the quay Abel sat in the cab with the book on his knee and a paper of food on the dash. He held the book out over the water by one cover and offered a deal: the lead would chalk his name at the top of the board, and he would carry the book up at five as a partner. The lead waited. Abel laughed, put the book on the dash and got out. Bram started off the wall; Abel told him to sit, and Bram said no, he wanted to see what the lead did. The two fought on the stones. Cass stood between them and the edge. The lead won. Abel sat against the wheel with his heels over the water. The lead took the book and the key. Bram drove Abel's car away.

## The lines that matter
Abel says the lead had the book, but Abel is the book. Ada says she turned round for the tin. Bess says the cab is not answering. Abel says to leave his mirror alone, and asks for a cab to the boat, on his account.

## What the player does
The lead may agree to the deal, rush him while the book hangs over the water, or wait and fight. He may hit Abel when he is down, then says what Abel is worth: a boat, the board, or the police.
