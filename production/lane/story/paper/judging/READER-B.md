**01 (6).** Strongest: Sheila counting his money and refusing it: "I'm not taking it. I'm writing down that it was there. Somebody always asks what a man started with." Weakest: the player walks and listens; he has nothing to do or risk. Only the mechanics are in his hands. The bare third hook is a quiet plant that most players will walk past.

**02 (6).** Strongest: Darren's "By Christmas. He never said which." It undercuts the boast and gives the town its joke. Weakest: the boast is stock gangster bravado, "And when they say Mickey's, they'll mean me." The refusal branch ("Say yes and I'll take your no down the landing") reads as a parser puzzle. Choosing the coat or opening the flap carries some meaning.

**03 (7).** Strongest: the torch stops on "Boarding house. Cold-store road. Twenty to ten" and the van goes. The player learns that his envelope sold a fare's whereabouts, and he did it himself. Weakest: the man at the landing is a type with no name or voice. The player's action carries meaning, but only on the "answer to Mickey's" branch.

**04 (6).** Strongest: one line is fixed in ink by the player's own words, "After the yes it is ink", and it drives the whole plot. Weakest: on the night it is a dialogue at a counter about columns, and only the £2.80 fare stings. The meaning is planted for later rather than felt now.

**05 (8).** Strongest: Ron walking backwards to the coping and beckoning, then "Whoa", then "Car three. Two pound forty to the quay. Sheila, put it in the book." It is a filmable deed only Frank would do, and Danny answers it. Weakest: an image very close to a famous film's ending (see section 7). The player's hands carry the meaning.

**06 (6).** Strongest: Danny parks Mickey's car on the edge, "That's how you park it", which mirrors car three. Weakest: the tour is a priced list of every door ("Fish. A fiver a week... Launderette, a fiver. Takeaway, ten."), exposition while the player sits. The chalk choice carries meaning, and the ride itself is mechanics.

**07 (8).** Strongest: the rule that teaches itself, "What Ron names, he has seen", and then Ron's silence at the pen: "I'd seen it. I just didn't say it." Weakest: the lorry and the bollard beats are filler round one real beat. The "Stop" key carries meaning, and Brenda's joke lands.

**08 (7).** Strongest: "Send Ron", and fourteen backs turn to the road. Weakest: there are six routes and most of them are cost tables. The packers' banter ("Mickey had upholstery") is good, but Frank himself has nothing to say. His action carries the meaning, and the price falls on Ron rather than on Frank.

**09 (6).** Strongest: the red book's third column, "R. Kirby. R. Kirby. R. Kirby." Weakest: the third counter scene with the camera in the same spot, and the mileage arithmetic ("the book will hide ten pounds and four fifths") buries the reveal. The player only looks. The meaning reaches him by reading, not by acting.

**10 (8).** Strongest: the L-plate car coming through the crowd at eleven miles an hour, "Coming up on the ticket window", and the gun shown, not drawn: "I'm showing you so you can't say you weren't shown." Weakest: the player's job is to hold a key down. The planning with tea things is charming but long. Meaning rides on Ron, not on Frank's hands.

**11 (8).** Strongest: the choice is a word, "Kirby" or "Mr Kirby" or "Ron", and the pen's rule gives each word its weight: "Go on, then. Mister Kirby." Weakest: the fight branch is generic. Standing in the rain is the best line the player can choose, and it is truly his. The action carries the meaning.

**12 (8).** Strongest: Ron sets the handbrake and says "Handbrake's yours, boss." Releasing it is the player's consent to whatever he just said. That is the best fusion of hands and meaning in the set. Weakest: the leak-keyed lines ("Leaving Iveson") depend on scenes we never see.

**13 (7).** Strongest: "I'm not asking you to remember everything, Mr Nowak. I'm asking you to remember it the same way twice." Weakest: the play is a memory test of a line typed five weeks earlier. Many players will fail it by forgetting rather than by choosing. Meaning is carried, but at risk of feeling like a quiz.

**14 (8).** Strongest: the name pays off: "Nowak. N, O, W, A, K." "It is the first time anybody on the Hook has spelt it right without being told." Also "You've took the one thing I had to give." Weakest: the night is a menu of three priced doors, with cash thresholds visible as rules (£800). Choosing to let it stand carries the meaning.

**15 (6).** Strongest: the man with the iron waits, and Ron's one word, "Mine." Weakest: the attackers are cast from the record, so each gets a slogan ("Board's coming down. He wants his name.") instead of a face. The yard reads like a branching chart. Taking the blow for Ron carries meaning, and the rest is a brawl.

**16 (8).** Strongest: the cut-up haddock and the bootlace: "I never ask anybody for anything, Mr Nowak. I find out what they're short of." It is a deed only she would do. Weakest: the player's part is sitting and lifting a foot, and the scene is close to a monologue. Putting his foot up is a small act with real meaning.

**17 (6).** Strongest: the coat rule with one arm. Who takes it off him is a choice with character in it: "Leave him. He can ask." Weakest: Agar appears for the first time in the penultimate week, and the scene is three sets of terms read out, with outcomes hidden behind fetch quests we never saw. Mostly mechanics.

**18 (8).** Strongest, in the free half: the player names the ice house himself and Ron answers "Aye. It is not the same, and both of them can hear that it is not." Inside: "Mind the bollard." Weakest: "Walked into a door. I know about doors" is a stock line. Naming the building is a deed that carries the meaning.

**19 (7).** Strongest: Fewson turns Danny's line back on him, "No. I want to see what you do." Weakest: the book held over the water by one cover and "I am the book" are stock villain staging, and the taunts during the fight ("That's for car three") are thin. The fight is mechanics. The choice of what Danny is worth carries meaning.

**20 (7).** Strongest: ruling the fourth column, then Hebblewhite: "It is a great deal more honest, and a great deal more expensive. I'm sorry." Weakest: the climax of a crime game is a man writing at a counter. It works only if the player has tracked four books' worth of rules. The action carries the meaning, but quietly.

**21 (7).** Strongest: the Widow's "Frankie." from a kitchen chair, and the hatch: "Boss." Weakest: the Man in Black's "Five. No flowers, and done in the dark" is opaque without the plan. The walk is a parade of callbacks. Reading the carbon and saying the line carries the meaning: he has become the man who sells fares.

**22 (8).** Strongest: "Coming up on the dock gate... That's the gate... Leaving the gate." Then "Where to, Frankie?" Weakest: there are many callbacks per minute (Lily, the Man in Black, Ada, Father Walsh), which tips into curtain-call. The player answers the last question, and that carries the meaning.

**23 (6).** Strongest: Lily puts the order "on the shelf behind her, where the orders go that nobody came for." Weakest: the stranger who repeats the opening ("New management") is the turn the reader sees coming from the first image of the ladder. The player mostly watches from a doorway.

## 3. The five weakest, and the one change for each

1. **15 The yard.** Cut the cast to two men, one of them Screeton, and give him a real exchange about the eighty pounds before the iron comes up. A face beats four slogans.
2. **17 The room at Grace's.** Put Agar on screen once before week six (for instance at the Exchange door in 14, rather than Ombler alone), so his "Mr Nowak" pays off something seen.
3. **06 Six minutes in car two.** Cut the price list to three doors that matter later (the gate, the cold-store door, the landing). Spend the time on Danny wanting something from Frank personally.
4. **23 Close: Burn Both.** Drop the stranger, or make him someone the player has met. The mirror arrival is the one turn every reader sees coming.
5. **02 The ask on the rank.** Rewrite Frank's speech so it is particular to him rather than a generic boast. "Every fare after dark, every door on this street, every quay with a rope on it" is anybody's line.

## 4. Voices

With the names hidden, most are distinct. Ron's clipped dock dialect and his naming ("Nowt coming", "Aye") stand out. Danny has patter and "nephew". The Widow is formal and future-tense ("I shall be very hard on you"). Hebblewhite is precise ("It isn't fair to the book").

Alike:
- **Sheila and Pauline.** Both say "love", both deliver dry deadpan from behind the counter. "I only write what I'm told, love" (Pauline, 04) and "I write what I'm told" (Sheila, 13) are almost the same line.
- **Stathers and Garbutt.** Both speak as men stating the rule of a room ("Man with a tally stands the call"; "Coat on the stand, Mr Nowak, or coat on your back").

The whole street also shares one house wit: "I've had faster trestles", "He's parked it better than you did". In places this flattens the walk-ons into one voice.

Frank has no voice to judge. After 02 nearly every line of his is "PLAYER (own words)".

## 6. Rule breaks and confusions

Rule breaks:
- 14 variant: "Stathers is on the bench beside Ron at midnight with a flask." A flask in a police station at midnight reads as drink to most readers. Make it tea, or say so.
- 02: Darren, sent off with "Get home, Darren" and walking backwards so as not to miss anything, reads as a boy. His age is never given. Make his adulthood plain.
- 07 says "the tax inspector walks round the car", but the inspector in 17 and 20 is from Customs and Excise. A small inconsistency.

Confusing without the plan:
- Names and terms used but never explained: "the second rung" / "the third rung", the "name ladder", "the Core", "The Ledger takes its first aim", "the day of looking", "Haddock's dear", "Friday means Friday", "number nine", "the first man", "the honest firm".
- Garbutt is spoken of from 10 onward but appears only in 17.
- People who appear only in conditions: Iveson, Julie, Alison and Philip Danby, Lee Drewery.
- The Man in Black's counting ("Five. No flowers"; "Seven. Half a firm") is unexplained.
- Rita's (the watch, the ticket book) is never set up as a pawnshop in these scenes.
- The dates check out against 1990 (Monday 15 October, Friday 16 November, Saturday 1 December, Remembrance Sunday on 11 November), and the caution wording in 13 is right for the period.

## 7. Recognition

- **The car on the quay edge, front wheels over the water** (05, 06, 19, 21): close to the hanging coach at the end of The Italian Job (1969). The image is near; the use is different.
- **Arrival by boat into a relative's minicab firm and a want to rise:** loosely like GTA IV's opening (a cousin's cab depot). Close in premise only.
- **A northern port, dying docks, an outsider come back for a dead man's affairs:** the tone of Get Carter.
- **A hard man wanting to run the docks:** The Long Good Friday, in outline.
- **Ron's "Coming up on... That's... Leaving..."** resembles a rally co-driver's calls. I know no game that uses it like this.
- I recognise no scene as lifted.

## 8. Distance from the bar

These are carefully made scenes. About half would sit well in a good shipped crime game (7 to 8), and the Ron thread (07, 11, 12, 14, 18, 22) and the early deed that returns (04, 13, 20) are the work of a writer who understands RDR2's method. None reaches 9. The losses are a man on remand and a voice gone quiet, not a death the player caused. The set pieces are small (a cage on a ramp, a fight in a yard, a man at a counter). The climax is an act of bookkeeping that pays off only for a player who has tracked four books. The single thing that stands most in the way is Frank himself. From his first night he speaks in the player's own words, so on the page he has no temper, no humour and no line anybody would quote. The heaviest scene in the game belongs to Ron. RDR2's best scenes are Arthur's, in his body and his voice. Until the lead is the loudest and most at risk in his own story, this stays a fine ensemble piece built round an empty chair.
