You are judging scenes from story-driven games. Each scene below is a retelling of one scene, in a fixed plain format of the same length, with names and places replaced. Every scene has been rewritten in the same flat voice, so do not judge the prose. Judge the scene underneath: the people, what happens, what is said, and what the player does.

For each pair, decide which scene players and critics would remember and praise more, if both were played in full in a finished game of the same quality. One must be chosen.

What tends to make a scene remembered (from studies of player reviews and of the best game stories):
1. People the player cares about, far more than plot or spectacle. A person with a want of their own, and one deed or line only they would do or say.
2. The lead wants something, acts, and pays for it. The heaviest moments happen to the lead or to someone close, and the lead had a hand in them.
3. A bond under strain: trust, a test, a break, a last word.
4. Stakes that are personal (a person, a body, freedom), not only money or a target.
5. A turn that is earned by what came before, not a surprise for its own sake. Pay-offs of things planted earlier.
6. The player's action matters to the meaning of the scene, not only to its mechanics. A choice with a cost.
7. Specific, lived detail, and comedy that comes from who people are.

What tends to make a scene forgettable: errands and set-ups that exist to move the player to the next job; people who only give orders or explain; danger without anyone the player cares about at risk; a plot turn that changes nothing for the people; action with no feeling attached; a scene any game could have.

Rules: judge each pair on its own; do not compare with other pairs. Do not try to guess which game a scene comes from, and do not let fame decide. Do not prefer a scene for being longer, louder or more violent. Read both scenes in full before choosing.

A run-down part of the city in daylight, a coast road running along the seafront, and a bus terminus at the far end of the route.

## People

## What happens

He waits at the bus stop, then follows the bus along the coast road in a car, staying close. Driving straight to the terminus fails the job. At the terminus the three men get off. They talk about wanting a car, notice the lead staring at them, and order him removed. The lead shoots all three in the bus bay. If any reach a car, the lead hunts them down. He then phones Bram to say it is done, and Bram is pleased.

## The lines that matter
Bram tells the lead the men are old associates who turned to crime, and that he wants them silenced. One of the men says they will buy a car once they have money. The lead tells Bram nobody will talk about him now. Bram says the lead is as hard as he is and promises to thank him at his civic dinner.

## What the player does
The lead keeps the bus in sight without losing it, then kills the targets at the end of the route. If the bus escapes or the men get away, Bram calls the lead useless and complains about his reputation.

## People

## What happens
Abel's car blocked the rank with the keys in it. He sat on the office counter eating breakfast and reading the day book, and told the lead he had come to be run. Cass walked beside the car with a hand on its roof, then ahead of it, and at the quay edge turned and beckoned the lead on with both palms. The front wheels went over the edge stones. Cass closed his fist, the lead braked, and the car hung over the water. The lead took the keys, hung them on the spare hook, and chalked a third car on the board. Ada charged a fare for the time stood on the rank. Abel laughed, left a note, and sent Bram for the car. Bram sat in it and spun the wheels in the air.

## The lines that matter
Abel says the last owner would have asked nicely for a fortnight. The lead says it was on his rank, so it goes on his board. Ada asks whether Abel wants a receipt. Abel says to call it an account, because he will be back on that rank.

## What the player does
The lead may talk and leave the car, drive it round the corner, or drive it to the edge on Cass's signal. If he drives through the fist the car goes in the water and Abel moves against him early. He may also take, change or refuse the note.
