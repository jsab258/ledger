# Frank's seven deeds

24 November 2026. A test on five key scenes, for the lane's lead. One deed for each game week: decided by Frank, done by him at his own risk, seen by the town and retold. Each can be declined, and the town says so.

## The thread: a driver who measures

Frank's skill is driving, and his temper comes out at the wheel. Crossed, he does not raise his voice. He goes formal, counts something aloud (seconds, bollards, feet and inches), and puts a car exactly where nobody on the Hook thinks a car can go, with people watching. The town already has pride from him and a button; the deeds give it a picture each week.

The seven are seven different pieces of driving, so they do not play alike: a dash against a clock, a theft with a chase, a shove, a mercy run, a crawl backwards in the dark, a blockade, a procession. What it costs him climbs: paint, then his liberty, then the firm's car and a bill, then the load, then his clean name, then the arm he changes gear with. The last deed is done with one hand, at walking pace.

His written lines in the deeds are sums and distances, because that is how he talks when he means it: "Eighty pence. The nine's in the book." "It's eleven minutes by the top road. I'll do it in four." "Eight foot four, less five foot six, less me."

Danny's gain is in view in every one: the white hatchback taking the boat's fares (week 1), his men loading on the warehouse row (2), the lit unit three doors up (3), his lads under the overhang by midnight (4), Fewson on Mickey's rank and on the brow (5), Fewson's roll at the yard gate (6), a young man saying "Cammack's?" at the ticket window (7).

## Week 1. Real ones

- Wakes when: the first morning. Pauline is promising a caller two minutes with car one at Fairview and car two on the rank under a man who cannot drive it. Mickey's minutes are the Hook's oldest joke. The caller is Julie Kettlewell, nine pounds in the debt book, with a week's trial across the water if she is on the 9.20 boat.
- What Frank does: ten minutes off the ferry, in his funeral suit, he tells Pauline to promise two minutes and to count them on the open channel. He takes car two off Ron, reaches Julie's step in the rows in under two, backs the whole length of the tenfoot because there is no room to turn, and has her at the brow with one rope off.
- Why only Frank: nobody else on the street can drive like it, and nobody else would mind the joke enough. He will not be told the way: Ron points with a flat hand, as a docker signals a crane.
- The risk: Mickey's own car, a hand's width from two walls, and his name. If he is late, the first thing every radio on the Hook hears about the new owner is "Same as ever, then." A scrape stays on the car to the end.
- What the town sees and says: Brenda, "Backwards. Down the tenfoot. With Ron Kirby sat in it holding his hat on." Kevin, "One fifty-two. She said it like the shipping forecast." Gordon, "Wouldn't be told the way. Found the boat all right."
- What a rival does next: Danny Cammack, who drove that car nine years, heard the count on a set. On Thursday his hatchback is across Mickey's rank and he is on the counter (S06, scene 05): he has come down to be run. Julie's two real minutes come back at the night desk (S44) and, in one close, in her own mouth (S60).
- Declined: Pauline says, "Set off walking, love." Julie misses the boat. Brenda: "New owner, same minutes."

## Week 2. Four pound ten

- Wakes when: Danny has taken car two for his six minutes and parked it on the quay's edge (S14), and owes four pounds ten by his own meter. Two nights running, coming home along the warehouse row, the player has seen Danny's van backed up to a dark unit and Fewson's lads carrying out crates of car radios under Norman Dent's torch. Danny has said he will have the ferry rank from Monday.
- What Frank does: he walks up to the van while they are inside, finds the keys in it, and drives it away loaded. The hatchback comes after him, and he loses it through the rows and the tenfoot he learned on Monday. At first light the van is locked in the first bay of the ferry rank, with 4.10 chalked across its windscreen, and Frank hands its keys to the Widow's taxman on that rank: "Mr Cammack's. He owes four pound ten on it."
- Why only Frank: Danny took his car to show him how to park it. Frank answers in kind and adds the bill. He does not keep the load; a thing moved on the Hook, so it is priced.
- The risk: liberty and skin. He is driving stolen radios past a night watchman and a beat constable. Caught on the row, it is a hiding from three men, and Danny's version is "caught the nephew thieving". Crash it, and the load is on the road with his prints on the wheel.
- What the town sees and says: Karen Grummitt, at her hatch, "There's a van on the cab stand with a price chalked on it like fish." Norman Dent, "Nothing moves on that row but me and the rats. And Thursday a van, with a white car after it." Danny, at the caff, "He's give my van to her lad to mind. I've had it valeted for less."
- What a rival does next: Maureen Jensen. Her man has been handed Danny's goods on her own rank in front of a boat queue, and she takes her first look at Mickey's the next dawn (S16): Stathers taxes Kevin at the gate. Fewson stands the ferry rank himself in that van (S15), and it is the van he puts Frank's man in three weeks later (S28). Dent's shape of a man is in DS Ellis's notebook at the search (S36).
- Declined: the van loads every night that week. Darren: "Forty radios a night off that row and nobody's said boo. I'd have thought you'd say boo."

## Week 3. The shell

- Wakes when: four of Tharratt's five mornings are met (S20). The Yale has been found to fit the side door of the unit three doors up, which is lit at night now, with Danny's men in it. On the Thursday dusk the player passes the warehouse row and watches two of them take the wheels and the radio off a car. At five on the fifth morning that car is across Mickey's yard gate, on bricks.
- What Frank does: the Hook's way is six men and a count. Frank puts the first car's bumper to the shell and shoves it on its drums thirty yards up the pavement in a tail of sparks, with every curtain on the parade opening, and plants it across Danny's shutter with his men inside. "Thirty yards. Four minutes. I've eleven." Then he is at the dock gate at twelve minutes to six.
- Why only Frank: he will not knock on six doors to ask for a lift on a street where none opened to him. He has a car.
- The risk: the firm's car and its clutch, on the morning the contract is decided. Danny's four come out by the side door at five in the morning, and he is alone. If it turns into a fight he is late, and Tharratt counts three cars.
- What the town sees and says: Ada, "Sparks. I thought the display had come a week early." Brenda, "He's posted it back through Cammack's door." Shaun, "Took the paint off my kerb, the pair of them."
- What a rival does next: Geoffrey Agar. The unit is let through his agents, and his second letter is not about the licence (S17, D16): a bill for a shutter and a footway, to Mr Nowek, and an inspection of the yard. Danny tells it as his own joke and keeps the shell where it is for a week.
- Declined: six men from the caff lift it clear at six. The cars are four minutes behind on the fifth morning. Brenda: "Had to be lifted out of his own yard."

## Week 4. The top road

- Wakes when: the Landing's night (S24, scene 10). The load is in car two, Ron is at the wheel, and the Widow's van driver has come up the brow with a revolver. The old man from the landing, who has stood under that overhang for thirty years, takes hold of his sleeve. The gun is fired, once, on every path, and the old man sits down against the ticket window with his arm shot through. The town looks up for a rocket.
- What Frank does: Ron is driving home at eleven, as he was told. Frank stops the car, goes back for the old man, lays him on the cases, puts Ron in the back to hold the wound shut with his cap, and takes the wheel. He drives the lip of the quay, where nobody walks, and the top road to the infirmary in four minutes, with four cases of tobacco under a shot man.
- Why only Frank: an ambulance is twenty minutes off, and nobody else on that quay can do eleven minutes in four. It is also the man who made him answer to "Mickey's?" on his first night.
- The risk: the load, and with it the landing; his liberty, at an infirmary door with a constable's bicycle against the wall; a wheel over the coping at speed.
- What the town sees and says: "Stopped that car dead and carried him. Then drove the edge of the quay like it had a white line on it." Ada, "That was Ron I heard. He was telling the buildings who they were." The constable writes down a cab with its boot tied with string.
- What a rival does next: Maureen Jensen sends the van driver across the water on the morning boat (with the Widow allied, Danny's lad fired, and Danny sends him inland), a man short, and counts it against Mickey's: her go at Ron in the pen opens harder (S30, scene 11). Danny's lads are under the empty overhang by midnight, and on Monday the caff says Cammack took the landing. At the infirmary the old man gives his next of kin as "Mickey's", which seats Frank in the family's chair in week seven (S46).
- Declined: Ron drives home at eleven with the load whole. Brenda: "Went by him at eleven mile an hour. You could have got out and walked back."

## Week 5. Astern, no lights

- Wakes when: the night between (S38, scene 14). Frank is bailed at six; Ron is on the bench with a statement to sign at nine, having told DS Ellis that Frank is new. The man Frank put on the landing will not go down for tonight's two cases: a plain car is sitting at the head of the brow. Fewson is on Mickey's rank in the hatchback, and Danny's lads will fetch the load for half.
- What Frank does: he takes car two through the dock gate alone, puts his lights out at the ice house, pulls the fuse for the lamps at the back, and drives the dock wall to the river side of the stage, counting nine bollards. He carries two cases up an iron ladder under the eyes of a police car, and then, with no room to turn and no brake pedal he dare touch, backs the length of the wall on the handbrake: "Nine. Eight. Seven."
- Why only Frank: Ron has just signed for a night that was the firm's. If a line goes in the book tonight, Frank wants his own letters at the end of it, and he is the one man who can take a car along that wall backwards and blind.
- The risk: all of his liberty. He is on bail. One touch of the brake lights the wall red, and he is back in the cell by eleven with two cases on the sergeant's counter. It also costs him what Ron was giving him: "I've told her you're new." "I was. Till ten o'clock."
- What the town sees and says: Norman Dent, "A car went backwards along that wall with no lights. I thought I'd dreamed it." Sheila, on Sunday, reading the week across the counter: "Friday, the landing. You." DC Wardell saw it from the plain car and wrote nothing down.
- What a rival does next: Wardell sells it to Danny Cammack, who has it on the quay on the last Saturday (S57, scene 19), beside the eleven lines with R.K. At the counter at five (S58) tonight's line already reads F.N. and wants nothing written beside it.
- Declined: at half past ten Fewson carries the cases up the brow past the plain car and Danny is on the kerb. Monday, at the caff: "Cammack's got the water."

## Week 6. The cork

- Wakes when: the yard (S44, scene 15). Kevin Needham has called a settle with the cars' engines running; Fewson is sitting on the hatchback twenty yards up the street with a roll, paying a tenner for every car of Mickey's that is brought to Danny.
- What Frank does: he says nothing, gets back into car two, and backs her into his own gateway along the left post, so that the gap left is too narrow for a car. He sits on her boot under the lamp with the tin and Sheila's sheet and settles by name. When they bounce her out of the gap by hand, he steps into it, between her wing and the brick post, and holds.
- Why only Frank: "Eight foot four, less five foot six, less me." He has put that car through that gate every night and knows it to the inch. And he would sooner be the thing in the way than ask anybody to stand there.
- The risk: his left forearm, which is the one he changes gear with. It breaks between his own car and his own gatepost. If he stands clear instead, the cars go to Danny.
- What the town sees and says: Ada, "He put Mickey's car in his own gateway like a cork in a bottle and sat on it. And when they went to lift it he stood in the hole." Terry, "All cars, she said. I've waited thirty year for somebody to say all cars." Frank himself, whom Pauline has put on the telephones by Friday: "Mickey's. He'll be two minutes. I don't know, I'm the owner."
- What a rival does next: Fewson takes the roll back to Danny whole, or lighter by a tenner a car, so on Monday Danny knows to the pound what Mickey's is worth and prices his terms by it (S46). The same night Maureen Jensen walks up Quay Street with a dinner cut for one hand (S45).
- Declined: he settles from the tin or refuses, as before. The cars the tin does not reach drive out past him, and Fewson counts tenners onto their bonnets. Brenda: "Paid who he could and waved the rest off."

## Week 7. Family car

- Wakes when: the Monday of the room at Grace's (S46, scene 17). The old man from the landing is buried with one hearse and no following car. Garbutt has spent a week getting the three firms' cars into an order behind it that none will call second.
- What Frank does: he puts car two in behind the hearse, ahead of all three, where the family's car goes, and nobody can say a word to that at a funeral. With the arm in plaster he cannot change gear and steer: he calls the gear and Ron's hand works the lever, or he reaches across himself with the wheel against his knee. He takes the coffin down Quay Street and past the ticket window, at a walk, with every door on the parade open and three firms following Mickey's.
- Why only Frank: the infirmary's label says the next of kin is Mickey's. And he is a stranger with nobody to follow him either.
- The risk: he walks into the tea having made all three heads follow a minicab with an L on it. Each opens harder. If he stalls her on the hill, it is in front of the whole town, and the Man in Black marks it.
- What the town sees and says: Brenda, "Agar's driver had to follow a minicab. With an L on it." The Man in Black, "Seven. Three firms, and one car of family." The young man Danny has put under the overhang says "Cammack's?" to a hearse, and Danny hears it back all week.
- What a rival does next: all three, across the cloth an hour later (S46). The dead man's torch comes to Frank in the infirmary's parcel, and whoever carries it out of the room has the claim to the landing; Agar, the Widow and Danny each reach for it before Frank has chosen. Danny takes the young man off the step, or does not, and that decides who is under the overhang on the last night.
- Declined: he walks with the rest. The Man in Black: "Six. Three firms and no family." Garbutt gives him the family's chair all the same, and it is a courtesy, not a place.
