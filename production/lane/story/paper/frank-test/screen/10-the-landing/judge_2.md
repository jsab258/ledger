You are judging scenes from story-driven games. Each scene below is a retelling of one scene, in a fixed plain format of the same length, with names and places replaced. Every scene has been rewritten in the same flat voice, so do not judge the prose. Judge the scene underneath: the people, what happens, what is said, and what the player does.

For each pair, decide which scene players and critics would remember and praise more, if both were played in full in a finished game of the same quality. One must be chosen.

What tends to make a scene remembered (from studies of player reviews and of the best game stories):
1. People the player cares about, far more than plot or spectacle. A person with a want of their own, and one deed or line only they would do or say.
2. The lead wants something, acts, and pays for it. The heaviest moments happen to the lead or to someone close, and the lead had a hand in them.
3. A bond under strain: trust, a test, a break, a last word.
4. Stakes that are personal (a person, a body, freedom), not only money or a target.
5. A turn that is earned by what came before, not a surprise for its own sake. Pay-offs of things planted earlier.
6. The player's action matters to the meaning of the scene, not only to its mechanics. A choice with a cost.
7. Specific, lived detail, and comedy that comes from who people are.

What tends to make a scene forgettable: errands and set-ups that exist to move the player to the next job; people who only give orders or explain; danger without anyone the player cares about at risk; a plot turn that changes nothing for the people; action with no feeling attached; a scene any game could have.

Rules: judge each pair on its own; do not compare with other pairs. Do not try to guess which game a scene comes from, and do not let fame decide. Do not prefer a scene for being longer, louder or more violent. Read both scenes in full before choosing.

## Setting
City streets, a ferry quay on the harbour, a walled hospital garden with an orangery, and the roads around it, in the run-up to a house-warming party.

## People
The lead is a man secretly paid by a rival firm to report on a gang. Abel is a member of the gang who is about to move in with his partner. Ada is his partner, who is exacting about small things. ## What happens
The lead picks up Ada and drives her to a shop, taking care not to anger her, since she notices every bump in the road. At the shop Abel phones the lead and asks him to find the launch carrying the party food and bring it to him. The lead runs the length of the quay, jumps onto the launch's stern, moves along its rail to the wheelhouse and keeps his balance.

He brings the food to a function room, then takes Ada on. She asks for a cutting of a pale rose from the hospital garden. The lead climbs the wall, talks his way past the gardeners, and gets close to the rose. The gardeners see through his story. The lead can fight or run to the car. A wardens' van chases him and he loses it, then drives Ada to her destination, which ends the job.

## The lines that matter
Abel asks the lead to find the launch and says the food must reach him safely. Ada tells the lead she wants only a particular rose. The lead tells a gardener he is there to check the drains. Ada tells the lead to hurry. Abel tells the lead she is nervous.

## What the player does
The player drives Ada carefully through the traffic, chases the launch, jumps onto it, brings the food to the function room, climbs the wall, talks to the gardeners, takes the cutting and loses the wardens.

## What the player does
The player takes the notice, follows each notebook to its place, fights the thieves at each, and takes the items. The player then returns to the owner for payment. No other person or event is met on the route.

A large house in a wealthy district, empty for years, then a sealed basement flat at dusk, fitted like an old family sitting room.

## People

The lead breaks in, puts down one guard, and forces the door. A friendly, crackling voice on an intercom mistakes the lead for Ada's staff and sends the lead below. A shed key starts the lift. The tapes show Bess refusing Ada's treatment, having seen what it did to the family dog. In the garage the lead finds the dog caged and wired to a drip, whining, and switches off the drip. Another tape shows Ada injecting Bess against her will. In the cellar the lead finds kidnapped people asleep in rows of beds. The voice plays Ada's log: after twenty-nine days Ada kept her mother alive in the sealed room, speaking only through the intercom. The voice offers Ada's location if the lead switches off the machines. The lead does, and Abel receives the location and a thank-you. Upstairs, Cora admits she went down earlier and could not do it.

## The lines that matter

- Ada, in her log: "My mother stays with me now."
- The voice: "I will tell you where she is, if you let me go."
- Cora: "Stop the clinic before thousands more suffer."

## What the player does
The lead picks locks, finds door codes, watches tapes and switches off the dog's drip. The only choice is the voice's bargain, which ends her.

A disused textile mill by the river, reached by a goods hoist. It is day, shortly after a meeting of the lead's team is broken up. Every floor is trapped.

## People
Abel is the lead's contact on the phone, who reads papers sent to him as photographs. Ada is the secret leader of a group the player knows has set warehouse fires; a film speaks for it. Bram is a security chief who wants power. Hired gunmen fill the final section.

A call from the arson group interrupts the team's meeting. The lead follows a courier's motorbike to the mill. The hoist needs a code, and a wrong entry sends armed guards. Abel says he cannot read the papers. Bram says he holds power in the city. Ada says the plan will tear down the order so it can be built again. Ada says she is the only member.

A disused set of tunnels under the old quarter, cold and half flooded, with brick halls, side passages and iron doors that open onto other parts of the system. Sluice gates stand open or shut in several rooms.

## People
Ada is a clever outsider who travels with the lead and wants a promised reward. Abel, a fixer, left a trail of recorded messages and is never met. A band of armoured hired men is on the same trail, led by Bram. The player already knows the lead is searching for a young woman who has gone missing.

Another points to the missing woman's car, and its number scratched in a pool opens a door. A huge watchman guards a gate, and Ada helps stun him. - Ada: "A sluice this old will drop us anywhere."
- Abel's recording: "She is the swift. Follow where they fly."

The player decides whether to use the sluice and whether to persuade Ada to stay, solves the clue rooms, and fights the dogs and Bram. At the end the player can follow Ada or leave her.
