## Setting
A harbour landing on a grey weekday morning at ten past nine, the stones dark from rain. A boat is due out at twenty past. A short street runs up to a cab office.

## People
Abel takes tickets at the landing. Bram paints a shopfront from a ladder. Ada keeps the office books and wants the new owner to say what the firm is. Bess works the radio and telephone. Cass drives the one car on the rank. Cora needs a lift to a new job before the boat goes. The lead is the late owner's nephew.

## What happens
The lead came up the ramp, and Abel said the office had the old owner's name over the door. Bram guessed who he was, and the lead gave his full name. Ada met him at the door. Inside, Bess was on the telephone to Cora, who would miss a job across the water unless she reached the boat. Bess asked what to call the lead on the air. He told her to say two minutes, took the car from Cass, and Cass moved to the passenger seat and pointed the way with a flat hand. The lead collected Cora from a back lane, reversed the whole narrow length of it and reached the landing as Abel held the rope. Cora paid eighty pence. Back at the office Ada wrote down the twenty-three pounds fifty he arrived with.

## The lines that matter
Bess says she has promised the town two minutes for nineteen years and none were real ones. The lead says these are, and she can start her clock when his door shuts. Cora says the old owner would have sent another driver and she would have swum. Ada says she is only writing down that the money was there, and that eighteen pounds is due by Friday.

## What the player does
The lead may say the two-minutes line or let the call go. He drives by Cass's hand signals, brakes at the end of the lane or not, and may scrape the walls. The fare is paid either way, and the town hears Bess count it.
