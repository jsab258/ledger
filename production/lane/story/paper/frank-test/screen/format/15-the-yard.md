## Setting
The yard behind a cab office, after eleven on a cold night. Brick walls and a double gate on the street. A storm lamp stands on a crate. Four cars wait nose to the gate with their engines running.

## People
Ada works the night desk and the radio. Abel is the day driver, who has the gate key and called the meeting. Bram owes the firm money and has none. Cass was taken on out of need. Dorran drives nights and wants his pay on Fridays. Ezra sits outside in a car with a roll of the rival's money. The lead wants to keep his cars and his people's keys for one more night.

## What happens
Ada called the lead on the radio to say men were queuing in his yard. Abel met him at the gate and said it was a settle: word was the firm would not last the week, so the drivers wanted their pay tonight or they would take their cars to the rival. The lead walked in, fetched the cash tin and set it by the lamp. Anyone paid hung his key on the board. The lead read each name off the wages sheet and counted. Bram put down one pound against what he owed. Dorran switched his engine off. The lead read Abel's name last. Abel said it was not the money, it was a man outside who knew what he was worth before he asked. The lead said he could keep the miles. Abel hung up the key himself.

## The lines that matter
Abel says he had six years on car one, and forty-two unpaid miles a week that the old boss knew about. Dorran says Fridays have always been Fridays. Bram says he is the only one in the yard who owes. The lead tells Abel to keep the miles, and to say it to his face next time, not through another man.

## What the player does
The lead chooses the order of names, and whoever the tin does not reach drives out or waits. He may answer Abel about the miles. If he refuses the settle, fists in the gateway follow.
