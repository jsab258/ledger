## Setting
A police station on the night after a search, from the morning in the cells to nine the next day. It is a tiled cell, a front office with a loud clock, and steps under a lamp, with the harbour wet and dark beyond.

## People
Abel is the desk sergeant. Bram is the lead's older doorman, who walked in alone. Ada is the detective, who wants a name. Cass is the man the lead set on the landing. The lead wants Bram out by nine without paying for it, and a load met tonight.

## What happens
The sergeant misspelled the lead's name three times while booking him. From the next cell Bram told the detective that the old boss's arrangement was his to carry, and that he would not say who took the envelope. She released him on bail, and he stayed on the front bench all night. The lead was released at six. Cass waited outside and said a load would come off the boat at ten, with a car of the rival's men watching the ramp. The lead took the car along the quay wall with its rear lights disconnected, used only the handbrake, climbed the ladder twice for two sacked cases, and reversed out. He went back to the front office with rust on his palms. At nine the detective asked if he had anything to tell her. He said no. Bram wrote his statement in capitals and was charged. He handed the lead his cap and asked him to hang it on his hook.

## The lines that matter
Bram says the lead should leave it lie, that one night is all she has and he told her the lead is new. The lead says he was new until ten o'clock. Bram says that one is the lead's, and this one is his. Bram spells the lead's name for the sergeant, correctly.

## What the player does
The lead may leave the load or fetch it, drive the wall without touching the foot brake, and may write the statement himself. Letting it stand, he takes the cap.
