## Setting
The afternoon of a funeral,. Chapel first, then a back room behind a cafe, with a gas fire, a white cloth, a brown pot and ham sandwiches. Tomorrow at nine a revenue inspector starts work on the lead's firm.

## People
Abel is the fixer who arranged the day. Bram, Ada and Cass head the three rival firms. Bess runs the cafe. Dorran is the undertaker's man. Ezra is the lead's doorman. The dead man stood on the landing for thirty years. The lead wants to leave still holding his licence and rank.

## What happens
Abel set the three firms' cars in order behind the hearse, by age. The lead brought his own car round and put it directly behind the hearse, where the family goes. No one could object. With his arm in plaster, Ezra changed gear for him. The hearse driver took the lead's signal and the procession went slowly down to the quay, past open doors. At the cafe Dorran gave his verdict: seven, and one car of family. The lead sat in the family chair. Bess handed over four pounds from the dead man's tin and a parcel from the hospital: a cap, a scarf and a torch. The lead stood the torch on the cloth. Ada said the shooting was never ordered. The lead laid a paper on the cloth, an option on a road, and passed it to Ada. She put it in her bag. Bram went pale. At the end the lead put the torch in his pocket.

## The lines that matter
Abel says nobody can mind age. Ada says the shooting was never ordered, and she will say so once, at the man's own tea. Bram says one would expect any tenancy to be written down. Abel says the revenue man has never once sat down for him.

## What the player does
The lead drives behind the hearse or walks. He puts a paper or a man on the cloth and says whose hand it goes to, which holds one head and makes an enemy of another. He decides what happens to the torch: keep it, give it or leave it.
