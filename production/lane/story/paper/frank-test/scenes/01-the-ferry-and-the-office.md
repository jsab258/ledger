# 01. The ferry and the office

- Where it sits: Act I, week one, the Monday, the first minutes of play.
- What wakes it: a new game. Nothing else has to be true.
- Who must be present: Sheila Dunn, Ron Kirby, Pauline Kitching. On the way up: Gordon Leckenby at the landing, Shaun Hewson on his ladder, Ada Vane at her window. On the telephone, and then on her step in the rows behind the parade: Julie Kettlewell. Kevin Needham on the air. Nobody can be absent this early, so there is no fallback carrier; if the player lets the telephone call go, the morning ends the same way, a fare short.

Frank is trying to get the town to take his name the first time he offers it. He will give up a quiet first morning, and risk Mickey's own car and the first thing the Hook says about him, to have it said about something he did.

## Staging

The ferry landing, ten past nine. Dry, grey and low, the stones still dark from the night's rain. The boat's ramp is down on the floating stage and Gordon Leckenby stands at the top of the brow taking tickets back. A board beside him gives her next sailing in chalk: 9.20.

In Frank's hands: one suitcase and a typed envelope. In his pocket: two ten-pound notes, three pound coins and a fifty.

From the stage the camera, behind his shoulder, can see the whole of what he has come to: the quay wall, three cranes with nothing hanging from them, a timber ship lying against the far wall with no lights on, and Quay Street running up from the water. Over one doorway at the quay end the paint says MICKEY'S and has been saying it for some time.

At the kerb by the head of the landing a white hatchback waits with its engine running and a green strip across the top of its windscreen. Two people off the boat with bags look up the street for a cab, see none, and get into it. It has no sign on its roof. It goes.

One sound does not belong to the water: a car engine idling up the street, and under it a woman's voice on a radio. It is how the player finds the office. Nobody points.

On the rank outside Mickey's stands one car with its engine running and a big man in the driving seat, both hands on the wheel, going nowhere.

Inside: a scuffed counter, the fare book, the radio set, a telephone, a wall clock. On the wall a painted board with two chalked lines, CAR 1 and CAR 2, and a row of three brass hooks. Two have had keys on them for years; the paint is worn round them. The third is worn the same, and bare.

## The scene

Frank comes up the brow with the case. Gordon takes the ticket and looks at the envelope.

GORDON: Quay Street's straight up from the wall. You'll want telling which door.

FRANK: I'll find it.

GORDON: Aye. They all do. It's got his name on it.

The player has the street. The engine and the radio get louder as he climbs. Under the ladder, Shaun Hewson looks down, brush across the pot.

SHAUN: You'll be Mickey's nephew, then.

Frank does up the middle button of his jacket.

FRANK: Frank Nowak. Both names are mine.

SHAUN: That's what I said.

Across the street a net curtain moves and is still. At the rank the man in the car watches Frank come in the wing mirror, and lifts one finger from the wheel.

Sheila Dunn is in the doorway before Frank reaches it: cardigan, glasses on their chain, a pencil.

SHEILA: New management. Sheila Dunn, I keep the books.

FRANK: Frank Nowak. It's the forty-first door up from the boat.

SHEILA: Is it. I've never counted.

Inside, Pauline Kitching has the telephone in one hand and the microphone in the other.

PAULINE (the telephone): Julie, I can't send what I've not got. Kevin's up Fairview and car two's being kept warm. (She listens.) I know when the boat goes, love. I can see the boat. (She covers the mouthpiece.) Is that him? What do I call him on the air?

FRANK: Nowak. N, O, W, A, K.

PAULINE: Board's for cars. Book's for fares. You've no column yet.

SHEILA (to Frank, flat, as she would read a line out): Julie Kettlewell, in the rows. Nine pound in the back of the book since the laundry finished her. She's a week's trial on the presses the other side if she's stood there at ten. Mickey stopped her cars in August.

The wall clock says sixteen minutes past nine. Through the window the engine on the rank runs on.

The player can let it lie. Or Frank can say one line.

FRANK: Tell her two minutes.

PAULINE: I've told this town two minutes for nineteen year. They've never once been real ones.

FRANK: These are. Start your clock when my door shuts.

He leaves the suitcase where it stands and opens the driver's door of car two.

FRANK: I'll have her, Ron.

Ron Kirby looks at him, and at the suit. He gets out, walks round the bonnet, gets in on the other side, and pulls the seat belt across.

RON: Where to.

FRANK: Kettlewell's. Don't tell me. Point.

The seat is set for a man a foot longer in the leg. Frank runs it forward. The door shuts.

PAULINE (radio): Car two, is that you, Ron?

RON (the handset, held like something hot): It's him.

PAULINE: Who's him?

RON: Boss.

PAULINE: Car one, be told. New owner's in. Nine sixteen. And he's out again. I'm counting.

KEVIN (radio, through traffic): What's he like?

PAULINE: He can spell. Ten seconds.

The player has the car. She is ten years old and pushes from the back wheels, and the setts are wet. Ron's left hand comes up off his knee, flat, the way a man on a quay tells a crane where he wants it: a flat hand to the left, a flat hand to the right, and held still for straight on. He does not speak. His right hand has hold of his cap.

Up Quay Street. Shaun takes his pot off the ladder's hook and holds it to his chest. At the top the flat hand goes right, into the rows, and the back of the car steps out on the wet and is caught.

PAULINE: Thirty. (To the telephone.) He's coming, Julie. I don't know. A suit.

Two turnings. Then Ron's hand points down a slot between two walls of back yards, with dustbins along it and washing across it, and a woman at the far end on a step with her coat on and a carrier bag.

PAULINE: One minute.

There is no room to open both doors. Julie Kettlewell gets in behind Ron.

JULIE: You're never Mickey's.

FRANK: Shut the door.

PAULINE: One forty. One forty-five.

FRANK (to the handset): She's in. One forty-eight.

PAULINE: One fifty-two, by mine. (A pause on the channel, which the whole Hook is listening to.) Well.

If the player was slow she gives the true figure, and adds, "Same as ever, then."

The boat sails in two minutes more, and the tenfoot is shut at this end. A man could turn a car here in nine goes. Frank puts his arm along the back of the seat, looks over his shoulder, and takes her out the way she came in, backwards, the whole length of it, at the speed he came in forwards. Bins go by a hand off each mirror. Ron watches the near-side wall come and not arrive.

At the mouth of the tenfoot, where it meets the street, Ron's flat hand closes.

RON: Whoa.

If the player brakes on the fist, a fish lorry goes across the mouth of the lane a yard behind the bumper. If he does not, the lorry stops for him, on its horn.

The fist opens. Out across the street backwards, the nose swung round in the width of it, and down Quay Street to the water. Gordon has the stern rope off the bollard and in his hands. He looks at the car, and at the clock on the ticket office, and holds the rope where it is.

Julie has her purse open before the car stops. She counts into Frank's palm in ten-pence pieces.

JULIE: That's eighty. The nine I've not got.

FRANK: Eighty pence. The nine's in the book, and it'll keep till Friday.

JULIE (already on the brow, over her shoulder): Mickey'd have sent Kevin, and I'd have swum.

The ramp comes up behind her. Ron lets go of his cap.

RON: She's not been off that kerb since he went.

In the office he takes the key from Frank's hand without asking and hangs it on its hook. Then he is at work: back to the wall beside the door, looking at the street.

Sheila has opened a narrow cash book at a clean page.

SHEILA: What did you come with?

FRANK: Twenty-three pound fifty.

He empties his pocket onto the counter: two tens, three pound coins, a fifty. Sheila moves each coin an inch with one finger as she counts it, the way a bank does. She writes. The camera can read it over her hand: the date, then New management, cash introduced, £23.50. She slides the money back to him.

SHEILA: I'm not taking it. I'm writing down that it was there. Somebody always asks what a man started with.

He puts Julie's eight coins down beside it. Those she takes, and Pauline writes the fare in the day book: 9.16, the rows to the landing, 80p.

From under the counter Sheila takes a buff rent book, limp with handling, and lays it on top of his money. Each week has a line and a collector's initials, D.P. For the last three weeks the amounts are in her own hand.

SHEILA: The tin's paid it three weeks. The tin's done. Eighteen pound by Friday, love, or the landlord has the keys. And Sunday you tell me what this firm is.

FRANK: That leaves five pound fifty. Five ninety. I drove the eighty pence, and half's the driver's.

SHEILA: It is. I'll not tell the landlord.

She walks him round then, a line a stop, and finishes each to his back if he walks on.

SHEILA (the board and its hooks): One driver by day, one by night. Car one's out with Kevin. Car two lives on that hook. That one's been empty a while.

SHEILA (a shut door behind the counter): That's Mickey's office. It's been locked since he died, and I've the key. Not today.

The AI notice shows, once. From here, wherever Frank speaks, the player can pick one of his lines or say his own.

What Ron will say on the door if he is asked, each once:

- About the engine: "She wants running or the battery goes. Ten minute. I've not missed a morning."
- About the tenfoot: "I've walked that forty year. I'd not have said it had a backwards."

## What the player can do

- Walk straight up, or wander. Sign: the engine and the radio. If he has not come in after a game hour, Sheila calls from the door, "In here when you've done looking lost." By then the boat has gone and Julie with it, on foot and late.
- Say the line, or not. Sign: the wall clock, the chalked 9.20 he walked past, and a car with its engine running. Not said, Pauline tells the telephone, "Set off walking, love," and the morning goes on to the cash book.
- Drive it. Hands: the wheel, and Ron's flat hand for the way. The clock is Pauline's and it is on an open channel. Beaten or not, the fare is paid and the fare is written.
- Brake on the fist, or not. Scrape her, or not: the tenfoot is a hand wider than the car each side, and paint left on a yard wall stays off the car to the end of the game.

## What the town remembers

- Every set on the Hook heard Pauline count. Kevin, at the evening change, to Terry Hodgson: "One fifty-two. She said it like the shipping forecast."
- Brenda Laverack, at her shop door by noon, to anybody: "Backwards. Down the tenfoot. With Ron Kirby sat in it holding his hat on." If Frank let the call go, she has the other one: "New owner, same minutes."
- Shaun, at the caff counter: "Gave me his whole name off the pavement. Both halves. Then he near had my ladder."
- Julie made her ten o'clock, or did not. She says which, on the kiosk step, for a week.
- Gordon, to Karen Grummitt at the ticket window: "Wouldn't be told the way. Found the boat all right."
- Somebody who drove that car for nine years had a set on. On Thursday a white hatchback stands across Mickey's rank.
- Sheila's entry, cash introduced, £23.50, is the first line of week one. At five on the last Saturday a man from Customs and Excise reads it out and asks whether that was all of it. "It's what was there."
- Frank's notebook, which the game calls the Ledger, keeps the week's aim in Sheila's words: eighteen pound by Friday; and Sunday you tell me what this firm is.

## Variants by path

None. Every game opens here.
