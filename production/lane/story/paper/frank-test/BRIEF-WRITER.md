# Brief for the writer: Frank's deeds, a test on key scenes

Fixed on 24 November 2026 by the lane's lead before the writer ran.

You are the writer for LEDGER, a third-person crime game set in the Hook, the old port of the town of Meridian, in Britain, October to December 1990. The lead is Frank Nowak, 32, who inherits his dead uncle Mickey's minicab office and the half-dead outfit behind it. The story on paper exists in full (folder production/lane/story/paper/ in /home/claude/ledger). Six blind readers score its scenes 7.0 of 10 (10 stands with Red Dead Redemption 2's best). All six say the same thing stands most in the way: Frank is the quietest person in his own story. Ron, Sheila, the Widow and Danny have the deeds players would retell; Frank has a button, a spelling and dry sums. Giving him written lines at every decision did not move the marks.

## The owner's decisions (binding)

1. Frank's voice: one loud deed of his own each week, decided by him, done by him at his own risk, seen by the town and retold; on top of the written lines he already has.
2. At the Landing (week 4) a gun is fired on every path, with a cost the town remembers. PEGI 18 within canon: guns stay rare and come after fists; light gore at most.
3. Seven game weeks are kept.

Also binding: the lead is Frank Nowak, name ladder the new owner, Nowak, Frank, Frankie (never Tom or Tommy); Danny Cammack is the opponent; Ron goes inside unless Frank pays to stop it, and Ron lives on every path; fights fully animated; light gore at most, no torture, drugs only off screen, no sexual content, no slurs; no alcohol or gambling shown or spoken of (pubs serve food; no betting); tobacco allowed; no children anywhere, nobody under 18; nobody asks about Frank's past. Canon (canon.md) and the owner's rulings (RULINGS.md) outrank everything; copies of the current ones are at /tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/b5ea6e5b-25aa-5ead-b1bd-0963f2d6d76f/scratchpad/canon-wip.md and RULINGS-wip.md (read the rulings on story, combat, the lead and suggested lines). Frank's lines are "mixed": written for hellos, goodbyes and decisions, the rest live talk; so a deed must be something he DOES, not a speech.

## What to write

1. production/lane/story/paper/frank-test/DEEDS.md: Frank's seven deeds, one for each game week. For each: the week; what wakes it (the town's state, in the cards' terms); what Frank does, in two or three sentences a player would retell; why only Frank would do it (his own skill, temper or want, not someone else's plan); the risk to him (body, name, money or liberty) and what happens if it goes wrong; what the town sees and how it is retold (two or three gossip lines in townspeople's voices); what a rival does next because of it (name the rival and the card or scene it changes). Each deed must be his own choice, never forced: the player can decline it, and declining has its own small cost (the town says so). Where it fits, a middle-week deed (weeks 2 to 6) puts the crime itself on stage in front of the player, and shows Danny's gains in play rather than reported (readers said: Danny's rise is hearsay; the crime is off stage; the set pieces are small). Vary them: not seven fights, not seven speeches. Give Frank one thread across the seven (a skill, a temper, an appetite) so the deeds feel like one man. His skill is driving (claimed in scene 07, never shown before; readers asked for it shown in week one). Keep guns rare: the only gun fired in the seven weeks before the climax is the one at the Landing. End DEEDS.md with seven deed cards in the same shape as SPINE-CARDS.md's cards, so they can be carried in if the test passes.

2. Five key scenes rewritten with the deeds in them, each a full replacement file in production/lane/story/paper/frank-test/scenes/ with the same file name as the original:
   - 01-the-ferry-and-the-office.md (week 1's deed, or its first sign; readers: the player only walks and listens; show his driving).
   - 17-the-room-at-graces.md (the funeral tea with the three heads; readers: the trades are abstract paper across a cloth, the dead man is not tied to Frank, the heads round the table are stock).
   - 15-the-yard.md (readers: fill the queue with people the player knows; give the arm one cause Frank chooses; drop the set-ups refused by somebody).
   - 10-the-landing.md (week 4; the gun is fired on every path, with a cost the town remembers; readers: the danger is thin, the gun shown is stock, the set piece is a held button at 11 mph).
   - 14-the-night-between.md (the middle scene where the readers found Frank quietest: three of them say the heaviest deed in the game is Ron's, walking into the station, and Frank only watches and prices escapes; keep Ron's act, and give Frank a deed of his own that night that costs him).
   Keep the same sections as the originals (header lines, Staging, The scene, What the player can do, What the town remembers, Variants by path) and about the same length (1,700 to 2,300 words each). Keep everything the readers praised in each (for example in 14: Ron spelling the name to the sergeant and "Hang that on my hook, boss"; in 10: the arm out of the window and Ron calling the ticket window; in 01: the cash book line). Keep facts consistent with the scenes you did not touch, the cast and the cards, so a reader of the ten scenes together sees no seam. Do not write notes, marks or revision comments inside the scene files: they will be read blind beside unchanged scenes, so nothing in them may say they are new.

3. production/lane/story/paper/frank-test/WRITER-NOTES.md: in at most 400 words, what you changed in each scene and what you kept, and any fact the cards or cast would need changed if the deeds are carried in.

## Style

Plain words, British spelling, no em dashes, no italics. Third person. The voices are those of CAST.md (Ron's clipped Yorkshire, Danny's "nephew", Sheila's "love" and sums). Give Frank a voice of his own in what he does and the few lines he is written: readers found his quips could be anyone's ("It's a start", "I'm in the wrong trade"). No stated theme; the theme line "no column for who it was brought to" is not repeated. Do not make him a stock hard man. Mafia is never a reference. Nothing copied from any game, film or book.

Work only in production/lane/story/paper/frank-test/. Do not commit or push; the lead does that. When done, reply with one paragraph: the seven deeds in one line each, and the files written.
