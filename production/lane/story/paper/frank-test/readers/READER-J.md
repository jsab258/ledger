**01.** Strongest: the open-channel countdown and the reverse down the tenfoot. "One fifty-two, by mine. (A pause on the channel, which the whole Hook is listening to.) Well." Weakest: nine named people in the first ten minutes, and mannered lines ("Both names are mine"; "It's the forty-first door up from the boat"). The action carries the meaning: the town's first word about him is earned at the wheel.

**02.** Strongest: the envelope as an object ("gone soft as cloth… red printing shows backwards") and Darren's puncture, "By Christmas. He never said which." Weakest: the scene is a stated-want speech, "Mickey's runs the Hook. And when they say Mickey's, they'll mean me." It is all talk, and the choice is a menu. Only the mechanics are in the hands. Looking inside the envelope is the one action that carries meaning.

**05.** Strongest: a deed the town retells, then the reversal: keys on the bare hook, "Car three. Two pound forty to the quay." Danny's real delight ("He's moved my motor") makes him a person. Weakest: Ron's beckoning hands guide the stunt again, so the deed is half his, and the fare sum is cute book-keeping. The action carries the meaning.

**08.** Strongest: Stathers offers tally 39 across Frank, and the pen turns its back: "By the time the leaf is on its stop, fourteen backs are to the road." Weakest: Frank is a bystander in his own scene ("There's one Frank on this gate, and he was here first"), and the packers' banter is stock ("Mickey had upholstery"). Under "Send Ron", the action carries the meaning.

**10.** Strongest: the gun under the fireworks, "Two hundred faces go up to the sky… Somebody says 'Ooh.' Nothing breaks", then Ron naming buildings from the back seat. Weakest: a crowded setup (Garbutt, the tea-things plan, three variants of the way in), and the masked gunshot is a known trope. "Stop" and the coping-stone drive carry the meaning.

**12.** Strongest: "Handbrake's yours, boss." He put it on and will not take it off. The player's release is the moral act. Weakest: it rests on "the line in the book", which a fresh reader cannot reconstruct ("If the book has Sheila's line"), and the naming device leans hard ("That's where he stood"). The action fully carries the meaning.

**14.** Strongest: Ron's unasked confession and the cap: "Hang that on my hook, boss." Also "You've took the one thing I had to give." Weakest: overloaded. A blind reverse drive, four buy-outs (Rita, Ombler, Wardell, Ellis) and a confession route dilute the night, and the lights-out reverse repeats scene 01's trick. Whether to take the cap carries the meaning.

**15.** Strongest: corking the gate with car two and sitting on it, then the arm: "Nobody will bounce a car onto a man." Weakest: Kevin's grievance ("forty-two mile a week… That were my rise") arrives unearned in these pages, and too many minor drivers queue for lines. Standing in the gap carries the meaning; the settle itself is a counting mechanic.

**17.** Strongest: the minicab with an L-plate puts itself behind the hearse, and the Man in Black's verdict: "Three firms, and one car of family." Also "Leave him. He can ask." Weakest: the middle is an item-trade menu (paper, man, load), each with three outcomes, and Agar is a voice with no deed. The hearse and the coat carry meaning; the cloth is mechanics.

**19.** Strongest: Fewson turns Danny's line back on him, "No. I want to see what you do", on the same edge as car three. Weakest: the climax is a fist fight over a ledger, and the mirror was seen coming from scene 05. Danny's threat is an abstraction ("Eleven nights with R.K."). The fight carries some meaning, the choice of what Danny is worth more.

## 2. The fifteen principles

- **P1 (1).** The want is loud and live on night one: "By Christmas, Ron. Mickey's runs the Hook." It drives the hands (fares, rank, gate). The break is the night between in week five. What Frank does differently afterwards is not shown. The scenes go on to the same contest for the town.
- **P2 (2).** Of the heaviest scenes (19, 14, 10, 15, 12), Frank acts at risk in four: the fist fight in 19, the dark wall in 14, the brow and the coping in 10, and his arm in the gap in 15. The week-one deed is answered: "He's moved my motor" (05). Caveat: he is less vivid than Ron and Danny, and his tic (the button) stands in for a temper.
- **P3 (2).** Ron has a beat in every scene. Strain comes in 08 (the tally) and 12 (the question, and the lie open to the player). The breach is 14, and Frank acts in it. Most scenes are shared drives. The last scene is implied in 19 ("Ron stands at the bonnet… between the fight and the edge").
- **P4 (1).** Ron, Danny and the Widow have wants and filmable deeds. Sheila's deed is writing ("cash introduced, £23.50"), not acting. The cast is far wider than five: Pauline, Kevin, Fewson, Garbutt, Stathers, Agar, Ellis, Wardell, Darren, Julie, Terry, Gordon, Brenda, Shaun, Ada and others all speak. This is the GTA IV blur.
- **P5 (2).** Danny is in week one, wants the same thing ("I've come down to be run"), and gains in view: the CABS card on the rank, "Cammack has the water", his lad under the overhang. He is faced in person in 19, and the fight can be won or lost. Weeks two and three cannot be checked.
- **P6 (2).** The rungs: eighteen pound by Friday, then the gate tax, then a shot man, then Ron's liberty, then Frank's arm, then the firm's book and Frank's liberty. "Somebody always asks what a man started with" builds toward the Customs reading.
- **P7 (2).** The first-night envelope, carried in week one (an ordinary errand), becomes the line with R.K. that jails Ron and arms Danny in the last act. Car three's keys and Fewson's seat also come back.
- **P8 (2).** Each scene has different ground: the ferry, a stand-off on the rank, the dock gate, fireworks on the brow, cells, the yard, a funeral, the quay. The Landing at the midpoint brings guns ("From tonight, the other side may bring one"). Lesson five has no danger to Frank, but the week holds 14.
- **P9 (1).** The aims are clear in week one ("eighteen pound by Friday"). Later ones need system talk: "Frank holds the third rung", "the three jobs Garbutt set", "a firm with no night money", and the unseen "line" whose wording matters.
- **P10 (1).** The climax uses planted things (the edge, the brass key, Fewson's mirror, "Leave my mirror alone"). Yet scene 19 is not one of the three heaviest; 14 is heavier. The ending choice, "the boat, the board, or the detective", is taught by smaller ones (Billany's cap, Fewson's keys). Scene 20 is not shown, so "answers week one" is implied, not seen.
- **P11 (1).** The question seems to be what a name is worth, and whose letters stand on the line. The book, the board and the town's tellings keep its score. Nobody quite says it, though "Somebody always asks what a man started with" comes near. The ending deed is unseen.
- **P12 (1).** Good unspoken loss: "three cranes with nothing hanging from them", "the timber ship has gone from the far wall, and the late boat is no longer on the timetable", the pen, Ron's tally. Period rules bite (L-plates, licence renewal, Customs, the tally hook). The ending's completed loss is not shown.
- **P13 (2).** Ron's loss (prison) has the whole game of shared drives behind it, and the player's answer at the white board shapes it. The old man's death is touched by "Stop", but he has little shared play behind him.
- **P14 (2).** There is a joke in every scene, and the running jokes pay off: the spelling of Nowak (the sergeant's "Knowak", then Ron's "N, O, W, A, K" in 14); "two minutes. Real ones"; "Leave my mirror alone" (05 then 19); "Don't stand him near the pot. He'll dissolve." The tone holds beside the gun.
- **P15 (1).** Many acts of the player's record return: the scraped paint, Billany's cap, Fewson's keys, the lie at the white board ("It read different through a wall, boss"), whether he hit a man who was down. Every job has routes, and failure moves on. Side stories are not visible, so (a) cannot be shown.

**Total: 23 / 30.**

## 3. The three weakest scenes, and the one change for each

1. **02 The ask on the rank.** Replace the speech with a deed. Have Frank open the envelope under the lamp in front of Ron and read the night's fares aloud, so his claim is made with his hands and the speech shrinks to one line. As written, "Every fare after dark goes in my book. Every door on this street rings my telephone. Every quay with a rope on it" is a trailer voiceover.
2. **19 The quay at four.** Make what is at stake Ron in body, not a column of initials. For example, Ron is at the plain car's window when the fight starts, and the book reaching Ellis puts him back inside in front of the player. That way the fight decides a person's fate. "Eleven nights with R.K." is book-keeping, and a climax should cost a face.
3. **17 The room at Grace's.** Cut the cloth to one item and one decision: the torch. The paper, the man and the load, with nine outcomes, turn a funeral into a trading screen. The hearse and the coat already carry the scene.

## 4. Voices

Most could be told apart with the names hidden.

- Ron: terse, nautical, flat ("Going astern"; "Nowt coming").
- Danny: chatty, mocking, warm ("I've had most things").
- The Widow: clipped and moral ("Leave him. He can ask").
- Garbutt: smooth and dry.

Two pairs blur. **Pauline and Sheila** share the same deadpan dryness ("He can spell" against "Is it. I've never counted"), and either could take the other's line. **Kevin and Terry** share the Hook's "year"/"were" grumble with no difference of temper ("Six year" against "I've waited thirty year"). Gordon's one line could be Ron's.

## 5. Clichés, stock lines and turns seen coming

- The nephew inherits the dead uncle's firm and swears to run the town by a deadline (01, 02). "And when they say Mickey's, they'll mean me."
- A heist plan laid out in tea things, with a sugar lump for a man (10).
- A gunshot masked by fireworks (10).
- The loyal old retainer takes the fall, and the boss is offered the chance to confess instead (14).
- Ron finally spelling "Nowak" right (14). This was telegraphed by every misspelling before it.
- The callbacks were all seen coming from scene 05: Fewson echoing "I want to see what you do", "Leave my mirror alone" coming back, and car two ending on the same edge as the hatchback (19).
- A fist-fight climax by the water with the street watching (19).
- Stock lines: "There's nowt of him" (08); "Say nowt" (14); "That's us straight" (10); "Mickey had upholstery" (08).
- The button tic is announced as a tell ("the player will learn what it means", 02). It is used as a beat in seven scenes and pays off by being impossible with the plaster (19), which is neat but expected.

## 6. Rule breaks and confusions

No breach of the content rules. Tea, peas and ham only, tobacco, no children ("the lad" reads as an adult). The dates check: Remembrance falls in week four, and 1 December 1990 was a Saturday.

Confusing to a reader without the plan:

- "Frank holds the third rung", "the three jobs Garbutt set", "a firm with no night money", "Frank signed first".
- What the line in the book actually says (12, 14, 19).
- "Somebody who drove that car for nine years had a set on" (01), and the brass key Danny "never gave back" (19). That Danny once drove car two is never stated here.
- "Mickey's watch has been redeemed" (14).
- "Lily Tang counts out one of number nine. 'One.'" (14) is opaque.
- "Mr Nowack (the second, if Kevin's was got in week two)" (08).
- "Since week two the handbrake by Frank's knee has been Frank's" (12): the lessons premise is not shown.
- "The Man in Black" is capitalised like a role title.

The production apparatus is often thicker than the scene, especially in 14 and 17.

## 7. Recognition

- Get Carter: a man returns to a northern port for a family funeral and takes on its firms. The setting and mood are moderately close; the plot is not.
- The Long Good Friday: the ascension-by-deadline speech. Loose.
- Mafia (2002): a cab driver drawn into an outfit, with driving as the core verb. Loose.
- The car on the quay edge has a faint echo of Reference 4's drive through the loading doors.

Nothing is copied closely.

## 8. Distance from the bar

At its best (05, 12, 14) this sits at a good shipped crime game's level, and in moments above it. The handbrake that only the player can release and "Hang that on my hook, boss" are real Red Dead moments of meaning carried by the hands. Overall it is about two marks short of the bar. The single thing most in the way is that the story's heaviest scenes belong to Ron, and the climax spends itself on a ledger rather than a person. Frank's deeds are mostly guided by Ron's hands (the flat hand in 01, the palms in 05, the lump plan in 10). His sacrifice is optional, and the last act is a fist fight over "eleven nights with R.K." rather than a reckoning that costs Frank someone or something he loves. Over all of it lies a cast too wide and a book-keeping mechanic too opaque for the player to feel the stakes without being told the count.

## 9. The people, by their own deeds

1. **Ron Kirby.** He walks into the station unasked, opens the gate under the pen's eyes, drives the L-plated car to the brow, and holds the handbrake for the player: the most retold deeds are his.
2. **Frank Nowak.** He reverses the tenfoot, puts Cammack's car on the edge, corks his own gate and stands in the gap, and puts a minicab behind the hearse. These are real deeds, but often steered by Ron's hands or chosen from a menu.
3. **Danny Cammack.** He parks across the rank, takes the day book to the quay edge, and parks car two "first go". He is the liveliest talker and the one whose own moves start trouble.
4. **Kevin Needham.** He calls the settle in Frank's yard and orders "Lift her out": one decisive act of revolt.
5. **Craig Fewson.** He refuses "Sit down, Craig" and drives off with the crew. It is a small part with one great deed.
6. **The Widow.** She is felt mostly through others (the tax, the gun, the van driver sent "across the water"). At the table, "Leave him. He can ask" and paying at the gate unasked are hers.
7. **Sheila Dunn.** Her deed is the record: "cash introduced, £23.50", and the rent book laid on his money. She is memorable, but she writes rather than acts.
