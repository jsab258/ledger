## 3. The three weakest scenes

1. **19 The quay at four.** It must be the biggest scene, and it is a scuffle over a ledger. Change: make Danny's price cost Ron in person, on the quay, so the climax is a choice between Danny's book and Ron, not a punch-up with a detective waiting for five o'clock.
2. **02 The ask on the rank.** Change: cut the speech to two lines and let Frank show the want with a deed at the envelope (open it in front of Ron, or walk it down himself), so Darren's mockery lands on a thing done rather than a thing declaimed.
3. **17 The room at Grace's.** Change: drop the three-token negotiation and keep one thing on the cloth, the torch, so the whole tea turns on who carries it out. The procession and the coat already carry the scene.

## 4. Voices

Ron is unmistakable ("Aye." "Nowt coming." "Coming up on the ticket window."). Danny is unmistakable ("I've had most things."). The packers, the Man in Black and Garbutt are clear. **Sheila and Pauline sound alike.** Both are dry, deadpan and number-minded ("Minimum fare, eighty pence. Twenty-one minutes stood on our rank..." against "Board's for cars. Book's for fares. You've no column yet."). With names hidden I could not split their lines reliably. Frank often slips into the same accountant's voice ("Eighty pence. The nine's in the book"; "It's five letters. You've found six twice"). The town chorus (Brenda, Ada, Shaun, Terry) is interchangeable: one wry northern line each.

## 6. Rule breaks and confusions

No breaks of alcohol, gambling or children found. The tea flask, chapel, caff and "danced at your wedding" are all clean. A town fireworks display with no children on the wall will look odd on screen.

What confuses a fresh reader:
- Frank Stathers and Frank Nowak in the same scene. The joke is meant, but it makes the prose hard to follow.
- What the envelope is. It holds carbons of the night's fares, so whose crime is it, and why does a fare line convict Ron of duty evasion?
- Scene 02 contradicts 01: "Not one opened", yet people met him at their doors.
- Unexplained terms: "the third rung", "Garbutt's truce", "Haddock's dear", "top line / third line / no arrow", "Mickey's watch redeemed", "Lily Tang counts out one of number nine".
- The brass key's history ("Nine year I've carried that") and the ghost name under CAR 2 are set up so faintly that 19's key payoff did not land.
- Who Peter Tang, Father Walsh and Norman Dent are.
- What "the books open at five" means (Customs, or Ellis?).

## 8. How far from the bar

These scenes stand at a good shipped crime game's level, around 7, with one scene (the Landing) near 8. Nothing reaches Red Dead Redemption 2's best. The texture is exceptional: period detail, a firm's arithmetic, a town that remembers, comedy from character. The single thing most in the way is that Frank is not the loudest person in his own story. Ron does every deed that hurts: he takes the tally, walks the gate, brings the car on four lessons, asks the question at the rails and walks into the station unasked. Frank's best moments are stunt driving in answer to other people's moves (Danny parks, Frank moves it; Kevin settles, Frank corks the gate; Garbutt orders the cars, Frank jumps the queue). The climax reflects this: with Ron gone, what is left is a fistfight over a book with Danny. Arthur's diagnosis happens to Arthur. Here the diagnosis happens to Ron, and Frank holds the handbrake.

## 9. The people, by their own deeds

1. **Ron Kirby.** He walks the gate open with the pen turning its back, drives the Landing alone on L-plates, and walks into the station unasked. Every retold moment is his.
2. **Danny Cammack.** He starts two key scenes himself (parking across the rank; taking the book to the quay) and pays Fewson's roll; he moves first and enjoys it.
3. **Frank Nowak.** He reverses down the tenfoot, puts Danny's car on the edge, corks the gate, steps into the gap and puts car two behind the hearse. These are retold stunts, but nearly all are replies to someone else's move.
4. **The old man from the landing.** One deed, a torch in a gunman's eyes ("Not on here"), and it costs him his life.
5. **Kevin Needham.** He calls the settle and drives the yard scene with a grievance of his own.
6. **Craig Fewson.** He refuses to sit down, takes Danny's crew, and drives off with the mirror tilted.
7. **Sheila Dunn.** One small deed, writing "cash introduced, £23.50", which echoes to the end. Otherwise she keeps the books.
8. **Pauline Kitching.** All voice, no deeds: a superb counter on the air.
9. **The Widow (Maureen Jensen).** Almost nothing on screen. Her deeds (the van driver sent away, the tax, the signature) happen offstage or are reported.
