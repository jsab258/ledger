**01.** Strongest: the backwards run down the tenfoot on Ron's flat hand, with Pauline counting on an open channel ("One fifty-two, by mine... Well."). Weakest: the cute name business piles up ("Both names are mine", "the forty-first door up from the boat"). The hands carry the meaning: the town learns who he is from how he drove.

**02.** Strongest: Darren's "By Christmas. He never said which." Weakest: Frank's ambition speech is a stock rise-to-power speech, with its list of "Every fare... Every door... Every quay" and "when they say Mickey's, they'll mean me." The player picks a line from a menu. The envelope choice matters later, but on the night it is only mechanics.

**05.** Strongest: keys on the bare third hook and "Car three" chalked, priced to the minute by Sheila. Danny is vivid ("So I've come down to be run"). Weakest: Sheila's sum is too neat, and five outcomes blur the one the town keeps. The hands carry the meaning: Ron's palms and fist give the player the stake as he drives.

**08.** Strongest: tally 39 held out across Frank, and fourteen backs turning on Ron. "Send Ron" is a real moral cost. Weakest: a second Frank (Stathers) muddies the script, and the five-way menu (pay, bluff, push, send, go round) reads like a choice wheel. The meaning lives in the choice, not in the hands.

**10.** Strongest: the L-plate car coming along the quay among the crowd, and "Coming up on the ticket window. That's the ticket window." Weakest: the gunshot taken for a firework ("Somebody says 'Ooh'") and the old man's "Not on here" are familiar turns. Stopping the car and driving the coping edge carry the meaning.

**12.** Strongest: "Handbrake's yours, boss." The only moving part in the scene is the player's hand, after the truth or a lie. Weakest: it hinges on a day-book line the player may not remember, and the three versions ("the envelope", "a fare", "Sheila's line") confuse. The action carries the meaning fully.

**14.** Strongest: Ron spelling "Nowak. N, O, W, A, K." to the sergeant, then "Hang that on my hook, boss." Weakest: it is overloaded. A lights-off smuggling run and four bribe vendors (Rita, Ombler, Wardell, Ellis) dilute the heaviest scene in the game. The statement, and taking the cap, carry the meaning.

**15.** Strongest: car two corked in the gateway with Frank sat on the boot, Darren's pound, and Terry's "Mine's Fridays." Weakest: the arm broken by men bouncing a car onto him is contrived staging. Kevin's grievance arrives cold ("forty-two mile a week"). Corking the gate and choosing who is called carry the meaning.

**17.** Strongest: one arm, a coat, three bosses watching ("Leave him. He can ask."), and the torch stood on the cloth. Weakest: handing out paper, a man and a load is a board-game turn, and Agar has no play behind him. Putting car two behind the hearse carries the meaning. The cloth choices are bookkeeping.

**19.** Strongest: the mirror of car three, with Fewson's "No. I want to see what you do." Weakest: the climax is a plain fist fight over a ledger. Danny's turn "from a cause the player made" is not shown, and the stake is paper. The callbacks land, but only the mechanics decide the fight.

## 2. The fifteen principles

- **P1. Want and need: 1.** The want is loud and live on night one: "By Christmas, Ron. Mickey's runs the Hook." It drives week one (the rent, car three) and the middle (gate contract, the Landing). It breaks in week five, at the night between. What he does differently after is not visible in play: "the car is quiet" is a change in Ron, not in Frank. The buried need (to be a name somebody stakes himself on?) is implied, never forced into his hands.
- **P2. Lead loudest: 2.** Four of the five heaviest scenes have Frank acting and at risk: the Landing (fists on the brow, a shot man), the night between (custody, the wall run), the yard (the arm), the quay (the fight). The funeral is the exception. The week-one deed is his alone and is answered: "He's moved my motor." Caveat: the deed at the core of the heaviest scene is Ron's ("I MAKE THIS STATEMENT OF MY OWN FREE WILL").
- **P3. The bond: 1.** Frank and Ron is the best thing here. Trust comes in weeks 1 to 2. Strain comes at week 3 (the tally, "Send Ron") and week 5 (the lie at the white board). The breach is week 5, and Frank acts in it. Most beats are drives (the lessons, the Landing). But no last scene between the two is shown, and in Act III Ron is "if he is free". On some paths the bond's last word falls to nobody.
- **P4. Few people: 1.** Ron (deed: walks into the station), Danny (deed: the hatchback across the rank) and Sheila (deed: "cash introduced, £23.50") are clean. The Widow's deeds are offstage ("The Widow has sent him across the water"). Kevin, Fewson, Stathers, Garbutt, Pauline and Darren all compete for carried weight. That is more than five.
- **P5. Opponent: 2.** Danny is in week one, with the hatchback already at the landing in scene 01. He gains in view: the rank, then the landing ("Cammack took the landing, and Danny lets it"), then Fewson taking fares, then the roll in the yard. He wants the same rank, and he is met in person on the quay.
- **P6. Stakes: 2.** The stakes climb: eighteen pounds rent, then standing at the gate, then a shot man, then Ron's liberty, then Frank's arm and charge, then the firm's book. Mid-game stakes are bodily ("There is a sound").
- **P7. Early deed returns: 2.** Carrying the envelope on night one (or refusing it) puts R.K. in the book. That returns as the search, Ron's charge, and Danny's "Eleven nights with R.K. at the end of the line."
- **P8. Middle that climbs: 1.** The places and dangers differ (gate, fireworks, cells, yard, funeral). But one move repeats as the set piece: reverse a car through a tight space or put one on an edge. That is the tenfoot backwards, the car on the coping, the wall astern with no lights, backing into the gateway, and car two on the edge again. The midpoint change ("the other side may bring one") is stated, not played.
- **P9. Clarity and pull: 1.** Some weekly aims are plain: "eighteen pound by Friday", "five mornings", "Whoever's stood there Sunday morning has the landing", get Ron out by nine. Others lean on unexplained counts: "Frank holds the third rung", "after the three goes at Frank's men", "Garbutt's three jobs", which line stands in the day book.
- **P10. Last act: 1.** What the climax uses is planted (the book, the brass key, the edge, Fewson's seat and mirror). It answers week one ("Sunday you tell me what this firm is"; £23.50 read out by Customs). Danny's worth (boat, board or detective) is taught by Kevin's and Fewson's earlier treatment. But the quay at four is not one of the three heaviest scenes; the night between, the Landing and the yard outweigh it.
- **P11. Theme: 1.** Implied question: what is a man's name worth, and who carries whose line? It is scored by the day book, the debt book and the hooks. The ending deed is unseen. Lines skirt saying it outright: "Somebody always asks what a man started with"; "Somebody has to be family."
- **P12. World ending: 2.** The loss is shown, not said: "three cranes with nothing hanging from them", the timber ship gone, "the late boat is no longer on the timetable", the pen shrinking from fourteen men to four, an old docker buried from his own tin. Period rules shape play: L-plates and the supervising passenger, brass tallies, the licence committee, Customs' jurisdiction.
- **P13. Losses: 2.** Ron's loss to the cells has five weeks of shared drives and lessons behind it. The player's truth or lie at the white board, and his statement, decide its shape. The old man's death is lighter, a figure more than a companion.
- **P14. Comedy: 2.** It comes from character and sits in the main line: the packers ("Mickey had upholstery"), "Not the stapler", "He can spell." Running jokes pay off late. The spelling of Nowak pays off when Ron spells it in the cells and when Agar says it right. "I want to see what you do" returns from Fewson. "Leave my mirror alone" returns too.
- **P15. Choices that return: 2.** Every scene has several routes, and failure moves on ("the morning ends the same way, a fare short"). Acts return many times (the car three telling, the tally, the lie heard "through a wall", Darren's pound). The ending is read from the book. No wrong deed is forced. Side stories can only be inferred (Julie, Darren, Lee).

**Total: 23 / 30.** Of the heavy seven, P3 and P8 are only half-met.

## 3. The three weakest scenes

1. **19 The quay at four.** Change: replace the fist fight with the handbrake. Danny in car two on the edge, front wheels over the water, and Frank's hand on the brake that lesson five made his, would turn the climax into a deed that only this lead, taught by this bond, would do.
2. **02 The ask on the rank.** Change: cut the speech to its last two sentences, and let one deed carry the want. For example, Frank writes his own name on the envelope's window, beside the carbon figures, before handing it back or carrying it.
3. **17 The room at Grace's.** Change: make one of the three things on the cloth cost a person at the table. For example, the man at the sink is Danny's lad who told on Ron. The tea then becomes a judgement on a face, not a share-out of goods.

## 4. Voices

Mostly distinct with names hidden:
- Ron: "Aye", "nowt", "Coming up on... That's the..."
- Danny: "nephew", bright mockery.
- Garbutt: careful "Mr" courtesy.
- Agar: "One would expect".
- The Widow: clipped, about owing: "I don't care to owe at a funeral."

Alike:
- **Pauline and Sheila.** Both are dry, deadpan women who count ("I've told this town two minutes for nineteen year" and "Minimum fare, eighty pence. Twenty-one minutes stood on our rank"). Swap their lines and few would notice.
- **Frank and Sheila.** Frank has taken on her arithmetic voice ("Eight foot four, less five foot six, less me"; "Eighty pence. The nine's in the book").
- **Kevin and Terry.** Both speak in the same Northern driver's idiom ("Six year", "Mine's Fridays").

## 6. Rule breaks and confusions

- **Rule breaks:** none found. No alcohol (Stathers brings "a flask of tea"), no gambling, no children seen, nobody asks Frank's past. "Peter Tang, he's woke. Fetch the car" (15) is unclear: if Peter is a child, it breaks the rule; he drives, so presumably not.
- **Two Franks.** Stathers is also called Frank (08). This is deliberate, but it confuses the script, and "Now then, Ron." / "Frank." takes a second read.
- **Unexplained system terms:** "the third rung", "the three goes", "Haddock's dear", "Frank signed first", "a firm with no night money", "Mickey's watch has been redeemed".
- **The brass key.** Danny "has the brass key he never gave back" (19) is never set up in these scenes. "Nine year I've carried that" and the ghost name under CAR 2 imply Danny once drove for Mickey, which is a large backstory left to a variant.
- **Opaque line.** "On the next Friday Lily Tang counts out one of number nine. 'One.'" (14) means nothing to a new reader.
- **The day-book line.** It is never shown verbatim, and lessons five and the night between both turn on it.
- **The Man in Black** reads as a stock title, not a person.

## 7. Recognition

- **On the Waterfront: close for 08.** The shape-up pen, brass hiring tallies, a docker offered work in front of men who are not.
- **The Godfather: loose for 17.** Heads of families at a table and a funeral as a power play. The loyal man taking the fall (14) recalls Pentangeli-type turns.
- **Get Carter and The Long Good Friday: tone and setting, loose.** A Northern port, and a man who would run the town by a date.
- **Mafia: The City of Lost Heaven: premise, loose.** A cab driver drawn into an outfit.
- **The driving lessons with the old man naming the buildings:** I do not recognise them, and they are the most original thing here.

## 8. Distance from the bar

The writing is well above most crime-game scripts. The objects carry the meaning (hooks, tally, torch, cap, handbrake, £23.50), the callbacks are earned, and the period detail is lived in. Two scenes (lesson five and the night between) come within reach of the bar. The set as a whole sits a clear step below Red Dead Redemption 2's best. The single thing most in the way is Frank. His inner life is a button, a spelling and a sum. Every feeling that lands belongs to Ron: "You've took the one thing I had to give", "Hang that on my hook, boss". Arthur's best scenes hit because Arthur feels and acts on it. Frank picks from menus of four or five outcomes, so no version of any scene has the weight of one authored deed by a man we know from the inside. Fix the lead's interior, give him one moment per act where he wants something for himself that costs him, and the bond scenes would carry the game.

## 9. The people, loudest first

1. **Ron Kirby.** He walks into the station unasked, drives alone through the fireworks crowd, walks the gate open with the pen's backs turning, and holds out his cap. The heaviest deeds are his.
2. **Frank Nowak.** He reverses down the tenfoot, puts Cammack's car on the edge, runs the wall with no lights, corks his own gate and puts car two behind the hearse. These are retellable stunts, but each is a response to someone else's move.
3. **Danny Cammack.** He parks across the rank, sits on the counter reading the book, and puts car two on the edge with the book. He starts the first and last confrontations.
4. **Kevin Needham.** He calls the settle in the yard and orders "Lift her out". That one deed breaks Frank's arm.
5. **Craig Fewson.** He sets Danny's seat to fit himself, refuses to sit ("No. I want to see what you do"), and drives off with the crew.
6. **The old man from the landing.** He puts a pencil torch in a gunman's eyes ("Not on here"), and his torch becomes the landing's claim.
7. **Sheila Dunn.** She writes the £23.50 and the rent book, and keeps the books that become evidence. She records more than she acts.
8. **Pauline Kitching.** She counts the clock on an open channel. A voice more than a deed.
9. **The Widow (Maureen Jensen).** Her deeds happen offstage: the tax, sending the driver across the water, the signature. On the page she mostly sits at a tea and says one hard line.
