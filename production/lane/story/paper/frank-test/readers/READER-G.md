**14.** Strongest: "Hang that on my hook, boss", and Ron spelling "Nowak" right, the payoff of a game-long joke. Weakest: bloated. **15.** Strongest: corking the gate with car two and sitting on the boot; Darren's pound ("I'm the only one stood in this yard who does"). Weakest: the broken arm comes from a bounce contraption that reads staged, and Kevin's grievance arrives in the same scene that pays it off. The hands mostly carry it.

**17.** Strongest: car two put in behind the hearse as family, and the coat Frank cannot take off with one arm ("Leave him. He can ask."). Weakest: a sit-down of three hand-it-to-whom menus, and Agar is a cipher ("One would expect any tenancy to be put in writing"). After the drive, it is mechanics with dialogue on top.

**19.** Strongest: the mirror of scene 05: "Sit down, Craig." / "No. I want to see what you do." Weakest: the climax is a fistfight with a jester, over a book, and Ron is optional ("if he is free"). The fight is the mechanics. The meaning is in a three-line menu at the end.

## 3. The three weakest, and the one change for each

1. **19, The quay at four.** Put Ron on the table. Danny's price should be Frank's choice between the firm's book and Ron's eleven lines, settled by Frank's own hand at the edge (the handbrake, the key, the car). As written, it is a pub brawl with a man the story has played for laughs. The climax has to be the bond's reckoning, not a sideshow beside it.
2. **17, The room at Grace's.** Cut the three hand-it-to-whom menus down to the torch alone. Give Agar one deed of his own on screen; the milk jug on the paper is the Widow's best moment, and he should get one too. The scene is a choice sheet in a tea room.
3. **02, The ask on the rank.** Cut the speech to its first and last lines and let the envelope do the talking. "Forty-one doors... Not one opened... By Christmas" is the lead saying the premise aloud to camera. Darren's mock already gives the town's answer; the want should come out through the carry, not the declaration.

## 4. Voices

Ron is unmistakable ("Aye", "Coming up on the... That's the..."). Danny is unmistakable (glib, "nephew"). The Widow is clipped and cold. Gordon, Stathers and Kevin are interchangeable working-Northern ("Aye. They all do."; "At your age."; "Six year").

Two pairs sound alike:
- **Sheila and Pauline.** Both dry, numerical and deadpan, both say "love". "I've told this town two minutes for nineteen year" could be Sheila's, and "I'm not taking it. I'm writing down that it was there" could be Pauline's.
- **Kevin and Terry.** "Mine's Fridays. It's always been Fridays" and "Six year. And forty-two mile a week" are the same man.

One house tic runs through many mouths: "never once". Pauline: "They've never once been real ones". Kevin: "You've never once asked me where her miles went". Garbutt: "I've never once got him to sit down". That is the writer's voice, not the people's.

## 5. Clichés, stock lines and turns seen coming

- 02: the "By Christmas, this town will be mine" declaration.
- 01 onward: the button tic, flagged as such ("the player will learn what it means"). It is used in every scene until it is mannered, and the plaster payoff in 19 is visible from the yard.
- 10: the old-timer who steps in front of the gun ("Thirty year. Not once. Not on here.") and is shot. The gunshot taken for a firework is a film staple.
- 10: "That's us straight" (the enemy who repays a courtesy).
- 14: the loyal retainer taking the fall. "You've took the one thing I had to give" is earned but familiar.
- 15: "It's not the eleven pound. It's that..." (not the money, the principle).
- 17: the crime bosses' funeral sit-down.
- 19: "I've wanted to since the hatchback." The "Sit down, Craig" reversal is neat, but the reader sees it coming from scene 05.
- 19: the climax as a one-on-one fistfight by water.
- 05 and 19: the car hanging over the quay edge, done twice. The second time is announced as an echo ("where the hatchback sat in week one").
- 01, 14: the reverse drive in a confined space, done three times in the set (with 15).

## 6. Rule breaks and confusions

There are no alcohol, gambling or children breaches. "A flask of tea" and "since I was courting" are fine, and nobody asks Frank's past. The dates hold: Saturday 10 November 1990 for the display, Saturday 1 December for the end.

Confusing to a reader who has not seen the plan:
- 01: "The AI notice shows, once" breaks the fiction on the page.
- 01: Ron sits in the driver's seat of car two with the engine running, and Pauline asks "Car two, is that you, Ron?". Only in scene 08 do we learn he cannot drive. Intended, but on first read it looks like a contradiction.
- 19: the brass key "he never gave back", "nine year", and the ghost name under CAR 2 imply Danny was once Mickey's driver. Nothing in these scenes says so.
- Unexplained terms: "the third rung", "Garbutt has said the week's aim", "Haddock's dear", "Mickey's watch", "Lily Tang counts out one of number nine", "the search", Wardell, the Man in Black's scoring ("Seven"), "Lady Day".
- Two Franks (Stathers) is a deliberate joke, but it muddles the scene headers ("Frank Stathers, the Widow's man... Frank is trying to...").
- Whether a "firm with no night money" path even has a story is unclear.

## 7. Recognition

No direct lift. The nearest:
- Get Carter (a northern port, a man arriving by ferry into a dead relative's criminal world). The mood is close; the plot is not.
- The Godfather and its many heirs for the funeral sit-down and the seating order (17). Fairly close in shape.
- The Italian Job's car over the edge (05, 19). Visual echo only.
- Boys from the Blackstuff and Our Friends in the North for the docks in decline and the dry northern register. Tonal.
- The firework that is a gunshot has been done in many crime films and series. Close as a device.

## 8. Distance from the bar

The middle is near the bar in places. Lesson five's handbrake and the Landing's "Mickey's. Quay Street." are moments a good shipped crime game would be proud of, and they make the player's hand carry the meaning, as the best of the reference shelf does. Overall these scenes sit at "good shipped crime game", around 6.5, not at RDR2's best.

The single thing most in the way is that the story's heart and its spine never meet. The weight is in Frank and Ron (the envelope, the lessons, the cell). The opposition and the climax belong to Danny, who is played as comic relief, and the climax is a fistfight in which Ron "does not lift a hand", or is absent altogether. The heaviest scene is in week five, and the last act asks nothing of the bond.

The habit of turning every peak into a four-to-six-way choice sheet ("pay, bluff, push, send, go round, walk") dilutes each scene's one true moment. RDR2's best scenes have one road and one meaning.

## 9. The people, by their own deeds

1. **Ron Kirby.** He drives the L-plate car through the crowd to the brow, walks the dock gate open while the pen turns its back, sets a handbrake he will not release, walks into the station unasked and hands over his cap. Every retold moment is his.
2. **Frank Nowak.** Backwards down the tenfoot, the car on the coping, car two corked in his own gateway, the lightless reverse along nine bollards. Loud, but half his deeds are menu options, and his heaviest moments are answers to Ron.
3. **Danny Cammack.** The hatchback across the rank and the book open on his knee at the quay edge. Big presence, few deeds: he mostly talks and turns up.
4. **The old man from the landing.** One deed, the torch in the gunman's eyes ("Not on here"), and it is the most retold in the middle.
5. **Kevin Needham.** He calls the settle in the yard and states the rule. One deed, well grounded in the forty-two miles.
6. **Frank Stathers.** He holds tally 39 out across Frank. A single sharp act.
7. **Craig Fewson.** "No. I want to see what you do." He turns once, and earns it with the seat notches.
8. **Pauline.** The count on the open channel in 01. A voice more than a doer.
9. **Sheila Dunn.** "Cash introduced, £23.50." She writes things down; she never acts for herself in these scenes.
10. **The Widow.** The milk jug on Agar's paper. Her only other deed (sending the van driver away) is off screen. Named as a head of the town, she barely moves the story here.
