# Frank's deeds: the test on five key scenes

24 November 2026. The owner's first decision of 24 November (one loud deed of Frank's own each week, tested on key scenes first) put to the test. Marks from AI readers of AI writing are signs, not measurements.

## What was made

- DEEDS.md: seven deeds, one a week, by the Fable writer (high effort), with seven deed cards F1 to F7 in the spine cards' shape. One thread: a driver who counts aloud and puts a car where nobody thinks one can go, at a cost that climbs until it takes the arm he changes gear with.
- Five scenes rewritten with the deeds in them (scenes/): 01 the ferry and the office, 10 the Landing (the gun fired on every path; it hits the old man from the landing, who dies later), 14 the night between (chosen as the middle scene where Frank was quietest: three readers named Ron's walk into the station as the heaviest deed in the game, with Frank only watching), 15 the yard, 17 the room at Grace's (the funeral tea, now the old man's funeral, tied to the Landing).
- WRITER-NOTES.md: what changed and what was kept.

## How it was judged

- Six fresh Opus readers (G to L), none of whom had seen any earlier version, read the five rewritten scenes mixed among five unchanged ones (02, 05, 08, 12, 19), all ten in story order under their usual names, with the same reader brief and reference scenes as phase 2 (readers/BRIEF-READERS.md: the same sections, the set cut to ten, the five weakest cut to three, and one added question 9 ranking the people by their own deeds). Their notes: readers/.
- "Before" marks are the three phase 2 readers who read the revised scenes (D, E, F, judging/), the version these scenes replaced.
- The scene judge (tools/ledger_pairs.py, seed 7, as in phase 2) as a screen: the five new scenes and the old scene 17 (never screened before) retold by fresh Sonnets to the judge's format (screen/format/), four fresh Opus judges for the five new scenes and four more for the old 17, so no judge saw both versions of 17. Raw verdicts in screen/, the scoring in screen/SCREEN-RESULT.txt.

## Is Frank still the quietest?

- No reader calls him the quietest. Asked to rank the people by their own deeds (question 9), one reader puts Frank first, four put him second (after Ron), one third (after Ron and Danny). The quietest is now the Widow, last or nearly last in all six lists.
- On principle 2 (the lead loudest), four readers give 2 and two give 1; in phase 2, five of six gave 1 (recounted from the six notes; judging/RESULTS.md says four), and two of the three who read the revision.
- So the note as written, Frank the quietest, has gone. A narrower one remains: four of six still name, as the thing most in the way, that the heaviest deeds belong to Ron (the gate, the Landing car, the handbrake, the station) and that Frank's deeds are feats of driving, often replies to someone else's move or steered by Ron's hands. Reader K: "He has more retellable deeds than anyone ... But they are feats more than choices with a cost." Reader L: "His inner life is a button, a spelling and a sum ... Frank picks from menus of four or five outcomes." Three or four readers say the four-to-six-way choices at every peak (mostly choices of route and outcome) dilute the one strong act in each scene. The other two name other things first (the heart of the story and its climax never meet; the spine is ink, not a body).
- New faults raised: the old man's death is thinly prepared (all six note it under principle 13 without lowering it to fail; three name it the Landing's weakest point); three reversing stunts and two cars on the coping repeat a trick; the Widow has no deed on screen.

## Verdict

The test is not passed as set. Its rule needs both a rise of about 0.7 and the quietness note gone. The second holds (no reader calls Frank the quietest); the first does not: the raw rise is 0.43. So the deeds are not carried into SPINE-CARDS.md or the other scenes in this run.

Against that, measured against five unchanged scenes read by the same readers, the rewritten scenes gained 0.87, above the bar the test set; the two weakest rewritten scenes gained most; and the screen moved the same way. The likeliest reading is that the deeds work and this panel was harsher, but the test was written on the raw rise, and that is what is reported.

What the deeds did not do is give Frank an inner life or a cost to someone he loves; four readers still hand the heaviest deeds and feelings to Ron.

Recommendation, as the job asks when the test fails: a fully written lead in the key scenes, keeping the deeds, tested again before anything is carried. This would replace the owner's ruling of 1 October (suggested lines "Mixed": written for hellos, goodbyes and decisions, else the small model) for Frank in the key scenes only. The evidence for it is thin and is said plainly: phase 2 already gave Frank written lines at every decision and the marks did not move, and the choices readers call a menu are mostly choices of route and outcome, not of his words. What a fully written lead adds is one authored line and one authored act at each peak, with the player's choice moved into the hands (as at lesson five's handbrake, the scene readers mark highest). What it would take:
1. Frank's lines at each peak in the 23 key scenes written in full, one line, not a list; the route and outcome choices cut to two or three where readers found them diluting.
2. Live talk stays for the street, the fares and small talk; the town's people keep live talk as now.
3. One want of his own that costs him in the middle weeks, set against Ron's (reader L: "give him one moment per act where he wants something for himself that costs him").
4. The seven deeds kept as written, to be carried into SPINE-CARDS.md (cards F1 to F7) and the scenes for weeks 2, 3 and 7 if the second test passes.
5. A second blind test of the same five scenes with Frank fully written, against the same five unchanged scenes, read by six fresh readers.
Cost: one writer pass on five scenes and one six-reader test, about the size of this run; then the other 18 scenes.

Owed whatever is chosen: the gun at the Landing is the owner's binding decision, so the unchanged scene 19's "guns have been fired" variant and card S32's wake must be re-keyed to a gun fired by Frank's side, since a gun is now fired on every path.

## Limits

- Six readers; one model family; marks of AI writing by AI readers.
- The rewritten scenes run 2,280 to 2,300 words, the unchanged ones 1,700 to 2,050; a reader could notice the longer ones. The old man's death reads as thinly prepared partly because the scenes between the Landing and his funeral were not in the selection.
- After the readers, the reviewer found small path seams in the five scenes and DEEDS.md, since fixed: who stands under the overhang; who fired on the path where the Widow is Frank's ally; Customs, not Ellis, charges Frank; who says the owner's line on the telephone. None changes what a reader read on the main path.
- The before marks came from readers of all 23 scenes; these readers saw ten.
- The deeds for weeks 2, 3 and 7 exist only in DEEDS.md; no scene for them was read.
- Two facts the writer used are not checked against the record (one fuse for a car's rear lamps; an infirmary sending a dead man's things to a firm named as next of kin).
