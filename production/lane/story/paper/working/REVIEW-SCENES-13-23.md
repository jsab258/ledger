Verdict: pass after must-fixes. The content rule, the owner's decisions, the 1990 calendar, the casting ages and the money hold across scenes 13 to 23. The faults are in path joins: who knows what, objects that are in two places at once, and end states that reach no close.

Reviewed: scenes 13 to 23 as one set, checked against scenes 01 to 12, the revised frame, PAPER-BRIEF sections 1 to 5 and 7 (with the lead's two later decisions), canon, the economy summary, CASTING.md and REGULARS.md.

Checked and sound, so not listed below: there are no em-dashes, italics, Tom or Tommy, Mafia references, GTA VI references or quotations of the owner. Nothing breaks the content rule (no drink, betting, children, sexual content, slurs or drugs on screen). The fights stay within PEGI 18 and canon: a blow on a man who is down, a boot, a nose and a mouth bleeding, an arm slammed in a door during a fight, no torture. Every date falls on the right day: Monday 15 October and Friday 16 November 1990, Remembrance Sunday on 11 November, the sit-down on Monday 26 November, the five mornings Tuesday 27 November to Saturday 1 December, the test on Friday 14 December and the magistrates on Monday 10 December. The money matches the economy and the earlier scenes: £23.50 to start, £18 rent, £2.40 fare, £5 a car, Ellis £800 then £100 on Fridays (cash in hand on the night), Wardell £200, the watch £14, £38 for a day at the pen, the £45 jacket and £90 coat, Darren's £22 and Iveson's £80. Ages and roles match casting (Agar 62, the Widow 57, Terry 52, Kevin 36 with six years on car one, Pauline 44 with nineteen years on the radio). Each close counts Ron and Sheila among the possible friends in some form (but see must-fix 2), and each pays off the envelope and the carbon (Kingdom: the spike and the empty soft envelope; Straight Life: the last pink sheet with R.K. put into Mickey's envelope; Burn Both: a new envelope, carried or left).

## Must fix

1. Scenes 21 to 23, "What wakes it": the three closes do not cover every end state at five. Writing it true with no friend left wakes nothing (scene 22 needs a friend, and scene 23 lists only "left the line and a morning fell", "bargained the book away" and "put a name to it and lost both"). Neither does leaving it with a morning fallen while a friend still counts him, or putting Danny's name to it while the firm survives. Add one line, shared by all three scenes, that maps every result (leave, true or Danny's name, crossed with the books held or a morning fallen, and a friend or none) to exactly one close and its better or plainer turn.
2. Scene 23, "What wakes it" ("nor a friend who still counted him"): the owner's decision is that every ending counts Ron and Sheila as friends, but Burn Both is the only close that never names them. Write "no friend who still counted him (Ron, Sheila, Alison Sedman or Philip Danby)", to match scenes 21 and 22.
3. Scene 13, Staging and "What the town remembers" (the keys of car one and car two and the Yale go into a police bag, and the Yale goes into Wardell's property book), against scene 15 (car one's key in the gate, and "If the Yale is still on the third hook"), scene 19 (car two's key on its hook) and scene 21 (the Yale on the third hook). Say where the keys come back, for example tipped out with Frank's laces at bail in scene 14, or handed back by Wardell once listed. Keep Wardell's knowledge of the unit as it is.
4. Scene 15, "What the town remembers" ("the second entry that book has had in three years"), and scene 19 ("the only debt in Mickey's book that is ever paid"): both contradict scenes 04 and 05 and scene 15 itself. Week one collects from five named debtors, Iveson pays £80 ("Mr Iveson paid that"), and Danny and possibly Frank are written in. Change them to, for example, "the first that book has had from anybody who wasn't made to" and "the only debt in that book paid from across the water".
5. Scene 19, the "A way was bought" line ("Ask him what got lost at the station, and who lost it"): only Wardell sells the fact to Danny (frame and scene 14). On Agar's brief or a bought Ellis, the town knows only that both men walked, so Danny cannot know. Limit this line to the Wardell path and give the other two a line built on what Danny can see (both men walked, and Frank is not charged).
6. Scene 14, "Or he can let it stand": if Frank goes home, the nine o'clock question comes at Mickey's door at ten to nine. After that, a "Yes" cannot "run as above" in time, and the cap cannot be handed to him "across the flap", so "Frank goes down the seven steps carrying another man's cap" cannot happen. Give the home branch its own ending: the constable brings the cap and the watch in the property bag, or a "Yes" at the door means Frank is driven to the station before nine.
7. Scene 20, "Leave it as it stands", Ellis's line ("Then the only signed account of that night is Mr Kirby's"): this is false on the cheaper-way paths (the statement lost, Agar's messenger, Ellis paid), and the bought-Ellis variant covers only one of them. Add a line for the Wardell and Agar paths (for example, "Then there's no signed account of that night at all, and I'll find out why").
8. Scene 22, two path-blind lines. Father Walsh's "A parishioner of mine was let out at eight" is false when Ron was never inside (Frank signed first, or a way was bought). Ellis's "The same way twice, Mr Nowak" is false if Frank wrote it differently at the search ("That's two") or refused the pen. Give both a variant, or say them only when they are true.
9. Scene 19, the "Nothing" branch after a win ("He is still sitting there when Ellis walks down at ten past five"), against scene 20, which has Ellis by the office door at five with her case. One woman cannot be in both places. Either move her walk down to after Hebblewhite leaves, and say so in scene 20, or have Wardell or a constable walk down instead.

## Should fix

1. Scene 13, "What the town remembers" ("a fourth is asked for"), and scene 22, Staging ("a page that now has four hands on it"): the page carries three hands (Pauline, Sheila and Frank twice), plus Sheila's initials. Say "four lines" or "four entries".
2. Scene 14, Ron's answer to "And Mr Nowak?" ("New. Didn't know what he had hold of."): the frame says Frank is not in the statement and not a word of it is false. If Frank told Ron the truth in lesson five, this line is untrue. Cut it to "New." or "Ask him." so that "I've told you nowt that isn't so" stays true.
3. Scene 13, Variants, "Ellis is already bought" ("What becomes of it is Friday's business. Have it with you."): this is said on a Friday morning, so a reader cannot tell whether it means tonight or next week. Since the cheaper way has to land before nine on Saturday, say "tonight's business".
4. Scene 01, "What the town remembers", line 161, has Hebblewhite read "cash introduced, £23.50" on the fifth morning. Scene 20 has him read it at five in the afternoon, and the frame has week one asked for in the morning. Pick one: the morning asks, and five o'clock reads.
5. Scene 18, Inside, the tally "in a bag at the station with his laces", which Frank can "sign for at the desk": once Ron is remanded, his property goes with him to the prison, and a third party needs his written authority to collect it. I am fairly sure of this for 1990. Have Ron sign a property release on the visit, with the prison holding the tally, or have Father Walsh carry the authority.
6. Scene 18, Staging, the remand visit through thick glass and a brass grille: in 1990, ordinary remand visits in English local prisons were normally open, at tables; glass "closed" visits were for named risks. I am fairly sure. Either give a reason (a fight on the wing, which also fits the split lip) or use a table with an officer watching. The Kingdom's hatch at lock-up is plainly a bought favour and can stand.
7. Scene 13, Wardell copying the page on a portable copier during the search: personal copiers existed in 1990, but taking one on a search would be unusual. PACE practice was to seize the book and copy it at the station. I am moderately sure. If the book must stay in the office, have Ellis seize it, copy it and return it the same afternoon, or have it photographed.
8. Scene 15, the opening ("It reaches Frank on the air"): the call depends on Julie being on the night desk and Ada watching. Give a fallback for a firm without the night desk or without Ada's window paid for, such as Terry on the handset or nobody (he finds it himself). The frame's "with nobody" outcome also needs its sign.
9. The third hook is overloaded. It holds car three's keys (scene 05), the Yale (06, 13, 15, 21), tally 39 if Sheila hangs it (16) and Danny's brass key (19, "on the third hook by five"). Give the brass key car two's hook, and keep the third for the Yale.
10. Scene 20, "Leave it", if Frank signed: "Then your own stands, Mr Nowak. Monday." Scene 21 then has a paper for "the magistrates, the tenth", so a reader cannot tell what Monday means. Say "the tenth" in both, or explain Monday.
11. Scene 23, Danny's opening ("You do days, I said"): he says "you'd be good on days" only in the no-night-money variant of scene 19. Make it "You'll do days" or tie it to that variant.
12. Scene 23, the gate: Ron "takes a brass number out of the pocket". On the paths where tally 39 was hung up by Frank (scene 18), kept by the Widow, or put on the third hook (scene 16), Ron does not have it. Make this conditional, or let the Widow hand him a new number.
13. Scene 18, Variants ("Thirty-eight pound... That's nineteen Fridays"): this makes two of number nine cost £2, which is cheap for a 1990 takeaway dish (more like £2 to £3 each). The price is not set in the economy. I am moderately sure. Either set number nine's price in the economy and keep the sum, or change the count of Fridays.
14. Scene 15, "Twenty-one to go, Darren": this works only if the reader remembers Darren's £22 debt from scene 02. Add it once in the staging or the line ("Twenty-one pound to go"), so a reader who is not tracking sums can follow.
15. Scene 20, "Put Danny's name to it": the sign is "Ellis offered it on Thursday". On the bought-Ellis path where every Friday was paid, she "offers no trade" (Variants). Say whether the third way is still open on that path, and in whose hands.
