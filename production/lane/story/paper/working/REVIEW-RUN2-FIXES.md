1. Ron cannot drive alone. Scene 06 calls him "a man who cannot drive" car two, scene 07 and S12 have him under L-plates with Frank beside him, and scene 18 has his driving test on 14 December. "Ron at the wheel of car four on the rank" and "Ron comes off the car" break that, and a learner alone at the wheel of a private hire car is a far worse offence than plying for hire. Fix: Ron stands by car four's front wing, as in scene 07, with a licensed driver at the wheel (Kevin Needham or Terry Hodgson). Sowerby can put his question to the driver and get Ron's silence instead.
2. "Fists ... the dial reads Shown." Shown means a gun shown (the dial goes Fists, Shown, Fired). A fist fight does not move it. As written it would also wake S32, whose condition is "a gun shown". Fix: drop "the dial reads Shown", or have Fewson show a gun and add S30b to S32's conditions.

### research/RECORD-CHECKS-2026-11-24.md

10. "Conspiring" is not what s.170(2) says. The story's charge is "conspiring to evade the duty", but s.170(2) is "knowingly concerned in any fraudulent evasion". Conspiracy is a separate charge under the Criminal Law Act 1977, s.1 (from the reviewer's memory, unverified). Also, s.145 governs proceedings "for an offence under the customs and excise Acts", so on the text just re-read it does not plainly cover a statutory conspiracy charge, and "a Customs officer must lay it" does not follow for conspiracy. Fix: add a line saying this. Keep the owner's ruled wording ("conspiracy to evade duty", CAST-CHANGES C3 cites his ruling of 24 November). Mark as inferred that Customs would bring a conspiracy to evade duty in practice, and soften "duty is Customs' to charge, not the police's" wherever it is stated as law (frame, scene 14).
11. The penalty is the 1979 one. "Imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years" is the 1979 text. To the reviewer's memory, the Finance Act 1988, s.12, raised the s.170 maximum to 7 years before 1990 (unverified). Say that the 2 years is as enacted, that the 1990 maximum is probably 7 years, and that it is not reached. The conclusion (imprisonable, so all the Bail Act grounds apply) stands either way.
12. Mickey's own rank is not shown to be lawful. The research honestly says "Nothing found forbids" it, but its own "So:" paragraph, S15 ("lawful only because it is the firm's door"), SUMMARY ("stays lawful") and KEEPER-LOG state it as fact. s.38 of the 1847 Act covers a car "standing ... for hire in any street", and canon's rank is outside on Quay Street. To the reviewer's memory, Rose v Welbeck Motors (1962) held that minicabs waiting in the street near their office could be plying for hire (unverified; read it before relying on it). Fix: mark it uncertain in all four places, for example "lawful as long as every job is booked first, though a council could argue that cars seen waiting in the street are plying for hire". Canon's rank stays. It is also a lever Agar or Sowerby can pull later.
