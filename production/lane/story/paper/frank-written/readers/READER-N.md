**01.** Strongest: the backwards run down the tenfoot with Pauline counting on an open channel, and Ron's "Nobody said which boat. I'd have walked down." Weakest: nine named people in the first minutes; the "three weeks late" grief is asserted, not felt. The action carries meaning: choosing the car over the grave is the want, made with the hands.

**02.** Strongest: Darren's "By Christmas. He never said which." and the coat with KIRBY in the collar. Weakest: the want arrives as a speech ("By Christmas, Ron. Mickey's runs the Hook"), the stock rise-to-power monologue. The player's action is a dialogue pick; only "Look inside" is a hand deed, and it is hidden.

**05.** Strongest: Danny's "Sit down, Craig. I want to see what he does," then the car on the edge on Ron's palms and "Car three" chalked. A scene players retell. Weakest: Sheila's fare sum is cute bookkeeping; the account/debt branch menu deflates the high. Action carries meaning: the edge or the dock is Frank's answer to Danny.

**08.** Strongest: Stathers holding tally 39 out "across Frank", and the pen turning its back, "fourteen backs are to the road." Weakest: Frank is a bystander in his own scene ("There's one Frank on this gate, and he was here first"); five option-branches read like a menu. Action carries meaning only in "Send Ron"; pay and bluff are mechanics.

**10.** Strongest: the shot taken for a rocket ("Somebody says 'Ooh.' Nothing breaks."), "Stop", and the next of kin: "Mickey's. Quay Street." / "Put me down as family." Weakest: the old man is met in two lines here; the bullet-mistaken-for-firework turn is visible once the display is staged. Action carries meaning: "Stop" costs two cases.

**12.** Strongest: Ron sets the handbrake and will not release it: "Handbrake's yours, boss." The player's hand carries the lie or truth across the rails. Weakest: it hangs on a day-book line and two initials a player must already understand; cold, it is opaque. Meaning, not mechanics: the brake is the answer.

**14.** Strongest: the pen between two men, the pawned watch ("He'd no flowers. There were nobody to send any."), "Hang that on my hook, boss," and "Coming up on." left unfinished. Weakest: overloaded: a lights-out reversing run, a bribe, a pawnshop and the spelling gag in one night. Meaning: taking or leaving the pen is the scene.

**15.** Strongest: car two corked in the gate, the settle "By name", Darren's pound, Terry's "Mine's Fridays." Weakest: the arm lost to four men bouncing a car is contrived, and Kevin's mileage grievance is explained, not played. Meaning partly: holding the brake is a refusal to ask for help, though the input is a held button.

**17.** Strongest: Frank, one-armed, asking the Widow: "Would you." / "That's one." and answering "Cammack's?" with "Mickey's." Weakest: the three-way torch auction is schematic, its bids ("Third line, no arrow") opaque; the Man in Black scoring funerals is whimsy. Meaning in the hearse drive; the torch choice is a trade screen.

**19.** Strongest: Fewson's "No. I want to see what you do." returning Danny's line, and the key drawn out so the sign goes dark. Weakest: the climax is a fist fight over a ledger with the detective waiting, mirrors so neat they show the plan. Mechanics mostly: fists, then a three-way verdict on Danny.

**Total: 21 / 30.** Of the heavy seven (P1, P2, P3, P5, P6, P8, P14), three half-pass.

## 3. The three weakest, and the one change

1. **19 The quay at four.** Make the price Ron, not a chalk line: Danny's book names Ron's eleven nights, and the only way to stop it costs Frank something he cannot get back (his own name on all eleven, or Ron's freedom). That turns a fist fight over a ledger into the story's question.
2. **17 The room at Grace's.** Cut the three bids to one. The Widow, who owns the shooting, offers the one thing Frank needs for tomorrow in exchange for the torch, face to face, and the player decides in one move with the old man's cap on the table.
3. **02 The ask on the rank.** Replace the "By Christmas" speech with a deed: Frank opens the envelope in front of Ron, or carries it himself in his funeral suit, and Darren's line answers the act, not the oration.

## 4. Voices

Mostly distinct. Ron is unmistakable (clipped, "Aye", "Nowt coming", naming the buildings). Pauline has the quickest tongue ("He can spell. Ten seconds."). Danny is fluent and pleased with himself ("I've had most things"). The Widow is terse and level ("Leave him. He can ask.").

Two who sound alike: **Kevin Needham and Terry Hodgson**, the same flat northern working-man register ("Six year." / "Mine's Fridays... It's always been Fridays."). **Stathers and Garbutt** also blur ("I'd not want it said..." / "I'll not have that said of this gate"). Frank himself slides into the town's diction ("I'll not be late twice on one street").

## 5. Clichés, stock lines, turns seen coming

- 02: the newcomer's "by Christmas I'll run this town" speech.
- 01, 02, 05: "Frank does up the middle button of his jacket... the player will learn what it means": a tic announced as a tic.
- 05: the car into the dock.
- 08: the old boss offering the loyal man his old job in front of the new boss.
- 10: a gunshot taken for a firework; the gruff old-timer hurt while saying his catchphrase ("Go home").
- 10: planning laid out in teapot and sugar lumps.
- 14: the loyal man takes the fall for the boss ("Hang that on my hook, boss"); the desk sergeant who cannot spell the name.
- 15: the hero who will not shout for help ("I've two").
- 19: the lieutenant usurping his boss; Fewson's returned line was visible from his seat-setting in 05.
- 19: the climax fist fight beside water, "It does not cut away from a blow."

## 6. Breaks of the world's rules, and confusions

No breaks seen: tea, ham and fruit loaf only; no betting; "the lad" always means an adult; nobody asks Frank's past (the old man: "I never asked who you were"). The dates hold: Saturday 1 December 1990 is a Saturday; the Landing falls the night before Remembrance Sunday (11 November).

Confusing to a cold reader:
- What is in the envelope, and why fare carbons are a crime. "Eleven lines; the last has a time and a pick-up and nothing after" is never explained.
- The day book, cash book, debt book and the "line" with R.K.: which book, which line?
- Undefined terms: "night money", "Frank signed first", "third line, no arrow", "the three goes at Frank's men", "Garbutt's truce", "Haddock's dear".
- Two Franks at the gate is deliberate but muddles the page.
- 14: Frank is on bail at six and runs a contraband load at ten the same night, which strains belief.
- Ron drives alone on L-plates (10); it is acknowledged, but the law's indifference is odd.
- "The Man in Black" reads as a mythic figure, not an undertaker's regular.

## 7. Recognition

- Premise: *Get Carter* (1971). A man comes north to a port town for a dead relative's funeral and walks into its underworld. The premise is close; the tone is far gentler.
- 08: *On the Waterfront*: the hiring pen, the brass tallies, the gate boss. Close in setting and device.
- Cab driver drawn into a crime firm: *Mafia* (2002). Loose.
- 05/19: a car balanced on an edge echoes *The Italian Job*'s ending. Faint.
- I could not match the scenes themselves to any published work.

## 8. Distance from the bar

The best moments are near the bar: the next-of-kin line in 10, the pen and the cap in 14, the brake at the rails in 12. In small scenes these pages are better than most shipped crime games. But RDR2's best scenes hang on a body, a bond and open country, and these hang on a ledger. What stands most in the way is the engine: the stakes run through a day book, a carbon and two initials that the player has to be taught before he can feel them. So the heaviest beats (the search, the statement, Danny's "Eleven nights with R.K.") are paperwork turns. They are delivered at walking pace, in set pieces that are mostly a car moving slowly near water. Until the thing at risk is something a player can see and grieve without a column explained to him, the scenes stay very good television, not RDR2.

## 9. The people, by their own deeds

1. **Frank**: backs Julie down the tenfoot, puts Danny's car on the edge, stops for the shot man, corks the gate and loses his arm, takes the pen; the player retells these as his own.
2. **Ron**: brings the L-plate car to the brow, opens the gate as the pen turns its back, walks into the station and signs for Frank.
3. **Danny**: parks across the rank, eats on the counter, takes the book onto the quay's edge; the town's best storyteller about himself.
4. **The old man**: puts the torch in a gunman's eyes ("Not on here"), names Mickey's as next of kin.
5. **Craig Fewson**: sets the seat to fit himself, then refuses to sit and drives off in Danny's car.
6. **Sheila**: writes "cash introduced, £23.50"; the line returns at the end, but her deeds are entries.
7. **Pauline**: counts the run on an open channel; voice, not deed.
8. **The Widow**: undoes a coat and disowns a shooting; quietest of the named heads for her weight in the plot.
