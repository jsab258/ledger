**01.** Strongest: the backwards run down the tenfoot on Pauline's open count, closed by "Nobody came down for mine." / "Nobody said which boat... I'd have walked down." Weakest: ten named people in the first minutes (Gordon, Shaun, Ada, Sheila, Pauline, Julie, Kevin, Brenda, Terry); the town crowds the lead. The hands carry meaning: choosing the car over the grave is the character.

**02.** Strongest: the envelope as object ("He never bought an envelope in his life"). Weakest: it is a speech scene; the want is declaimed ("By Christmas, Ron. Mickey's runs the Hook. And when they say Mickey's, they'll mean me.") and the player picks a line. Darren's "He never said which" is the best thing in it. Mechanics only: a dialogue menu.

**05.** Strongest: Ron walking the car to the coping, palms beckoning, then the fist; the hook and chalk "Car three". Weakest: the outcome is a stunt with arithmetic attached ("Twenty-one minutes stood on our rank, first five free"), and Danny's delight undercuts any danger. The player's hand carries the meaning: how far he drives is who he is.

**08.** Strongest: the tally 39 held out across Frank, and "Send Ron", where fourteen backs turn to the road. Weakest: the packers' banter is stock ("Mickey had upholstery"), and the two-Franks gag is laboured. Real meaning in the hands: each route costs money, pride, or Ron's standing, and the cost is shown, not told.

**10.** Strongest: the shot mistaken for a rocket ("Somebody says 'Ooh.' Nothing breaks."), then "Next of kin?" / "Mickey's. Quay Street." Weakest: the tea-things briefing is cute, and the old man is a device met in one word a night. The single word "Stop" and the edge-of-quay drive carry the meaning fully.

**12.** Strongest: Ron puts the handbrake on and will not take it off: "Handbrake's yours, boss." A whole moral choice in one release. Weakest: almost nothing happens for long stretches; the stakes ("what did you have her put in the book?") rest on a ledger line the player must remember. The hands carry meaning, elegantly.

**14.** Strongest: Ron's statement, the pen between two men, "You've took the one thing I had to give," and the cap held across the flap. Weakest: overloaded: bail, the cage run with no rear lamps, Rita's watch, Wardell's bribe, all in one night; the watch reveal is sentimental. The pen is the player's to take: meaning, not mechanics.

**15.** Strongest: Frank corking his own gate and holding the brake while the door closes on his arm: "That's the one I change gear with. Keys. On the board." Weakest: the setup (miles, roll, tenner over, settle by name) needs explaining; Kevin's grievance is told in exposition. The held brake carries the meaning.

**17.** Strongest: car two behind the hearse, "Thirty-nine. Forty. Forty-one," every door open, and "Cammack's?" answered "Mickey's." Weakest: the torch auction is abstract (licence, tax, "third line, no arrow") and the Man in Black's scoring is whimsy. Driving the hearse route carries meaning; the torch choice is a menu with good stakes.

**19.** Strongest: Fewson's "No. I want to see what you do", Danny's own line come back. Weakest: the climax is a fist fight over a book with a detective forty yards off; "Eleven nights with R.K." is bookkeeping, not danger, and "I've wanted to since the hatchback" is stock. Mostly mechanics: fists, then a verdict menu.

## 3. The three weakest, and the one change

1. **19 The quay at four.** Put a person on the edge, not a book. Let the price be Ron, or Fewson's men in the water, or the car going in with the book; at the moment the stake is ledger lines read to a detective.
2. **02 The ask on the rank.** Turn the speech into a deed. Cut "By Christmas... they'll mean me" to one line and make the claim something Frank does on the rank that night (the envelope carried in front of Darren, or refused to the man's face).
3. **01 The ferry and the office.** Cut the first-minutes cast to four names (Sheila, Pauline, Ron, Julie) so the drive and "I'd have walked down" land on a lead who is not yet crowded out.

## 4. Voices

Mostly yes. Ron is unmistakable ("Aye.", "Nowt coming.", "That's the gatepost."), so are Pauline, Danny, Agar and the Widow. Two pairs sound alike:

- **Ron and the old man** use the same clipped dock syntax: "Nobody's. That were the job." against "That were the chandler's."
- **Sheila and Pauline** share "love" and the same dry put-down ("He'll keep, love. He's nowhere to be." against "Not you, love.").

Frank also drifts into the town's dialect ("I'll not be late twice on one street").

## 5. Clichés and turns seen coming

- The lead's tics are mannered. "Frank does up the middle button of his jacket" appears in 01, 02, 05, 08, 15 and 19, and 02 announces it: "the player will learn what it means." Spelling out "N, O, W, A, K" appears in 01, 08, 10, 14 and twice more.
- 01: nobody meets the hero off the boat. The pay-off ("I'd have walked down") is good, but the setup is obvious.
- 02: the "By Christmas I'll run this town" vow.
- 05: the cocky gangster eating a sandwich on the counter.
- 08: the old union man tempting the sidekick back.
- 10: a gunshot masked by fireworks; a learner driver as the getaway car.
- 14: the loyal man takes the fall. The pawned watch for the funeral flowers is a sentimental reveal.
- 17: "Thirty-nine. Forty. Forty-one", doors open at the funeral. Seen coming from 02's "Forty-one doors".
- 19: the mentor's line turned back on the boss ("No. I want to see what you do"), signalled in 05. "I've wanted to since the hatchback". A fist fight at the water's edge.

## 6. Rule breaks and confusion

- No alcohol, gambling, children or questions about Frank's past that I could find. The tobacco smuggling, the pawnshop and the "flask of tea" all stay inside the rules.
- The sums and dates check out: £23.50 in coins, £2.40 rank fee, £17.60 credit, Darren's £21 to go, Saturday 1 December 1990, Remembrance weekend.
- Confusing without the plan:
  - "the half line still goes in the book" (02)
  - "Third line, no arrow" and "Top line. Half" (17)
  - "the brass key he never gave back" (19)
  - "Mr Nowack (the second, if Kevin's was got in week two)" and "Haddock's dear" (08)
  - "Garbutt's truce" (17)
  - "the man Frank put on the landing" (14)
  - "the Landing" used as an event name
- Two characters called Frank at the gate (08) is deliberate, but it muddies the scene.
- It is unclear why Frank, freshly bailed, can safely run the cage that night, and how "eleven nights" of R.K. squares with Ellis having "one".
- The Man in Black scoring funerals out of ten (17) is whimsy that sits oddly against a shooting.

## 7. Recognition

- **Get Carter:** the opening is moderately close: a man in a black suit arriving in a northern port for a relative's funeral and the business behind it.
- **The Italian Job:** car three's front wheels turning over the water echo its cliff-edge ending as an image.
- **Sleeping Dogs and GTA IV:** the dispatch office and the sidekick taking the fall are generic to the genre. Nothing is close enough to be called borrowed.

## 8. Distance from the bar

This is crafted, motif-rich writing that a good shipped crime game would be glad of in its best stretches (10, 14, 15). It is still a clear step below RDR2's best. The loss and danger there sit in bodies. Here the spine runs on paper: a day book, a line, two initials, eleven nights in a column, "third line, no arrow". The climax is a fight over a ledger with a detective waiting for five o'clock. The single thing most in the way is that the central threat is bookkeeping a player cannot feel. Next to it, the heaviest feeling in the story belongs to Ron, not Frank.

## 9. The people, by their own deeds

1. **Frank:** puts Danny's car on the edge, backs down the tenfoot, stops for the old man, corks the gate with his arm, takes the pen; the most deeds.
2. **Ron:** opens the gate before the pen and walks into the station in his funeral coat to sign for the line; the heaviest single deed.
3. **Danny:** the hatchback across the rank, the counter, the book carried to the quay edge; the opponent always moving.
4. **The old man:** takes the gunman's sleeve ("Not on here.") and names Mickey's as next of kin; two deeds the player will retell.
5. **Fewson:** sets the seat to fit himself, then "No. I want to see what you do," and drives off with the crew.
6. **Pauline:** the count on the open channel ("One fifty-two, by mine. Well."); a voice more than a deed.
7. **Kevin:** calls the settle in the yard and leaves his key in the gate.
8. **Sheila:** writes "cash introduced, £23.50"; a recorder, not a mover.
9. **The Widow:** mostly offstage power; on stage she undoes a coat and disowns the shooting.
