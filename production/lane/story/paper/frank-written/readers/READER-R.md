**01 (7).** Strongest: the choice of car over grave, and the quiet pay-off "Nobody said which boat. I'd have walked down." Weakest: a crowd of named voices in five minutes, and the backwards tenfoot run is a stunt laid on top of the feeling. Action carries meaning: picking the car door over the chapel yard is the character.

**02 (6).** Strongest: the envelope as an object ("He never bought an envelope in his life"), and "Left alone." Weakest: the want is declaimed, not done: "By Christmas, Ron. Mickey's runs the Hook. And when they say Mickey's, they'll mean me." It is a rise-of-the-boss speech. Action: carrying or refusing is mechanics. The meaning is in the speech.

**05 (7).** Strongest: Danny's delight ("He's moved my motor") and Fewson moving the seat and the mirror, a small humiliation planted for later. Weakest: the deed "only Frank would do" is steered entirely by Ron's palms. Frank drives where he is beckoned. Action carries meaning: chalking CAR 3 and the hook choice do.

**08 (7).** Strongest: the tally held out "across Frank", and "Send Ron", where fourteen backs turn. That is meaning carried by an order the player gives. Weakest: Frank is the spare man at his own gate ("There's one Frank on this gate, and he was here first"), and the packers' banter is a stock chorus. The action mostly carries meaning.

**10 (8).** Strongest: the shot under the fireworks ("Somebody says 'Ooh.' Nothing breaks"), the "Stop" word, and "Next of kin? / Mickey's. Quay Street." Weakest: the old man has had one word a night and no hours of play, so the loss is borrowed feeling, and "Put me down as family" is stock. The action (stop, lift the cases out, drive the coping) carries meaning.

**12 (7).** Strongest: the handbrake Ron sets and will not release ("Handbrake's yours, boss"), and his one question for himself. Weakest: it all turns on a ledger line, initials and column jargon a player must track, and for most of the scene the player watches. Action carries meaning: the release is the answer, and a lie is "taken whole".

**14 (8).** Strongest: the pen laid between two men, and "You've took the one thing I had to give." The watch ("five minutes fast") is earned. Weakest: overloaded, with cases, a fuse stunt, a pawn ticket, a bribe and the pen in one night, plus a laboured spelling gag ("It says Knowak"). The action carries meaning, since the pen is the choice.

**15 (7).** Strongest: corking the gate with car two ("Eight foot four, less five foot six, less me"), and Terry's "Mine's Fridays." Weakest: breaking his own arm by holding a handbrake is contrived martyrdom, and Kevin's forty-two miles is an abstract grievance. Action carries meaning: holding is refusing to ask for help, which is the flaw.

**17 (7).** Strongest: car two behind the hearse, "Cammack's? / Mickey's.", and asking the Widow to take his coat ("Would you."). Weakest: the torch auction turns the dead man into a bargaining chip, and the Man in Black's mark out of ten is whimsy. Action: hearse and coat carry meaning, but the torch is a shop menu.

**19 (6).** Strongest: Fewson turns Danny's own line back on him ("No. I want to see what you do."). Weakest: the climax is a fistfight over a ledger with a charming second-tier rival, and the mirror of car three is too neat and seen coming. The action is fists only. The meaning sits in the after-line menu.

## 2. The fifteen principles

- **P1 (1).** The want is loud and early ("Mickey's runs the Hook"), and the need is named ("Frank is trying to be met"). Car three and the gate move it. The break is week five's night between, but these scenes do not show what Frank does differently after it, beyond silence in the car ("Coming up on." He does not finish it).
- **P2 (1).** Frank acts and is at risk in 10, 15 and 19, and optionally in 14. The car-three deed is answered by Danny. But the heaviest moments are Ron's (the station, the gate, the pen left lying) or the old man's, and every danger to Frank can be declined: "The player can walk in through the gap on foot", "If he lets go and steps out".
- **P3 (1).** Frank and Ron is the best thing here: drives, lessons, strain at the gate (wk3) and at lesson five (wk5), a breach in which Frank acts ("If Frank takes the pen"). The last scene between them is not shown, and on one path Ron is inside for the last act ("Ron is inside. Nobody stands at the bonnet").
- **P4 (1).** Too many carried people: Ron, Danny, the Widow, Sheila, Pauline, Kevin, Fewson, Garbutt, Agar, Stathers, the old man, Darren, Terry. Ron, Danny, Fewson and the old man have filmable deeds. The Widow's deed is hanging a coat ("That's one"), and Sheila's is a ledger entry.
- **P5 (1).** Danny is in week one, wants the Hook and is faced in person in the last act. His gains are mostly reported, not played: "On Monday the caff says Cammack has the water."
- **P6 (2).** The ladder climbs from rent ("Eighteen pound by Friday") through a shooting, Ron's liberty and Frank's charge to Frank's arm and the firm itself.
- **P7 (2).** The night-one envelope goes into the book ("R.K. in the column"), becomes the line Ron goes to prison for, and becomes Danny's lever in the finale. The £23.50 is read back by Customs.
- **P8 (1).** Each week has a different shape (dawn gate, fireworks landing, cells, yard siege), and the Landing changes play ("From tonight, the other side may bring one"). But every set piece is car two on the same quay, edge or gate. The ground barely moves.
- **P9 (1).** Each scene opens with a clear aim ("Frank is trying to..."), but the lines, columns, initials, "third line, no arrow" and "top line" need explaining.
- **P10 (1).** The finale pays off car three, the key, the book and Fewson's seat, and choices are taught. But it is not among the three heaviest scenes (10, 14 and 15 are), and it ends on a ledger fistfight.
- **P11 (1).** The question is whose a man is ("I ask whose"), scored by names spelt right and the town's memory. It comes close to being said aloud: "And whose are you? ... Somebody must have you." The ending's answer is not shown.
- **P12 (1).** The cranes with "nothing hanging from them", "the timber ship has gone", "the late boat is no longer on the timetable", the pen. Period rules (L-plates, carbons, tallies) open and block. The ending scene is not shown complete.
- **P13 (2).** Losing Ron to the cells is prepared by five lessons and touched by the player's truth, lie or silence and by the pen. The old man's loss is thin by comparison.
- **P14 (2).** Pauline, Danny and the packers throughout. Running jokes pay off: "Two minutes... Real ones" (01, then Terry in 15), "Leave my mirror alone" (05, then 19), the spelling (Ron spells it in 14).
- **P15 (2).** Returns everywhere (Billany's cap, the treatment of Fewson, the tally, truth at the white board), several routes per job, and no forced wrong deed. The side stories cannot be seen here, so that part of the mark is implied.

**Total: 20 / 30.** Of the heavy seven (P1, P2, P3, P5, P6, P8, P14), only P6 and P14 pass.

## 3. The three weakest scenes

1. **19 The quay at four.** Make the last fight cost Frank his want, not his teeth. Let the only way to free Ron be for Frank to hand Ellis the book himself, giving up the Hook. The climax then answers night one's speech with a deed, instead of guarding a ledger with fists.
2. **02 The ask on the rank.** Replace the "By Christmas" speech with a deed under the lamp that says it. For example, Frank opens the envelope in front of Ron, or chalks his own name where Mickey's was. Darren's joke then answers an act, not a monologue.
3. **17 The room at Grace's.** Cut the torch auction. Make the choice a person: who stands under the overhang tonight (Frank himself, Ron, or Danny's lad). That way the old man's place is given to someone, not sold.

## 4. Voices

Mostly distinct with the names hidden. Ron is clipped and naming ("Coming up on... That's the..."), Danny is chatty and pleased with himself ("Nephew", "I've had most things"), Agar is a formal "One would expect", and Garbutt is mild and procedural. Two pairs sound alike:

- **Sheila and Pauline.** Both are dry Northern women with "love" ("He'll keep, love" / "I know when the boat goes, love"), and their sharp lines could swap.
- **Ron and the old man.** Both are laconic dock voices ("Nobody's. That were the job." could be Ron's; "Aye. Well." could be the old man's).

## 5. Clichés, stock lines and turns seen coming

- 02: the rise-to-power vow ("By Christmas... Mickey's runs the Hook").
- 01, 02, 05 and on: a signature tic announced as one ("the player will learn what it means"), the middle button.
- 01: a reckless reverse down a narrow lane, with a lorry missing the bumper by a yard.
- 08: the gossiping women's chorus ("There's nowt of him").
- 10: a gunshot masked by fireworks ("Somebody says 'Ooh'"); "Put me down as family. There's nobody else coming."
- 10, 17: the mentor-figure who always says "Go home", then dies.
- 14: the loyal man taking the fall in the cells, and the lead's refused attempt to swap ("I'd swap"); the pawned watch that turns out to have paid for the flowers.
- 15: enduring a crushing injury rather than ask for help, with a heroic line after ("That's the one I change gear with. Keys. On the board.").
- 17: the funeral cortège order as a power play; the hero joining the hearse as family.
- 19: the final fistfight at the water's edge; the henchman turning the boss's own line ("I want to see what you do"); "Leave my mirror alone" as a callback.
- Seen coming: car two on the edge where the hatchback sat; Ron going down once his initials were "on every one"; the old man shot the moment he takes the sleeve; Fewson taking the crew.

## 6. World-rule breaks and confusions

**Breaks:** none found. There is no alcohol (Stathers brings "a flask of tea"), no gambling and no children. Tobacco and smuggling are fine. The 1990 dates hold together: Saturday 10 November before Remembrance Sunday, and Saturday 1 December.

**Confusions for a reader who has not seen the plan:**

- "The book" means three things: the day book, the cash book, and the driving rules ("Book says a man can put in for his test").
- "Third line, no arrow" and "Top line. Half" are never explained, nor is "Sheila's line ... She put it to Mickey".
- "Haddock's dear", "Frank signed first", "taken on for his need / by a hook", Garbutt's office and why the heads defer to him, and the Man in Black are not explained.
- What the envelope buys stays vague ("Left alone") even after Ron explains it.
- Two Franks at the gate is deliberate but muddles the scene at first.

## 7. Recognition

Nothing is close enough to be lifted. Some echoes:

- **Get Carter** (a man in a dark suit arriving in a Northern port after a relative's death, among small firms): close in mood and set-up, not in plot.
- **The Italian Job** (a car balanced over the edge): a faint visual echo in 05 and 19.
- **The Godfather** (the order of cars at a funeral as a statement of power): faint.
- **Gran Torino / Driving Miss Daisy** (an older man and a younger one in a car, a bond made through driving): faint.

## 8. Distance from the bar

This is a well-made, observant, often moving script. Scenes 10 and 14 approach a good shipped crime game's best, and the Frank–Ron bond built through driving lessons is genuinely the stuff of Arthur on the ride. It is still a clear step below Red Dead Redemption 2's best. Those scenes put the lead's own body and choices at the centre of the heaviest moment and make the loss one the player caused. Here the heaviest moments belong to Ron (the station, the gate, the pen) and to the old man (the sleeve). Frank's dangers can almost all be declined, and the stakes are routed through ledger lines and initials that the reader has to track. The single thing most in the way: Frank does not cause the story's worst thing by a deed he cannot refuse. He watches loyal men pay for him, and the climax gives him a fistfight over a book instead of a choice that costs him his want.

## 9. The people, by their own deeds

1. **Frank.** The loudest deeds a player would retell are his: backwards down the tenfoot, Cammack's car on the edge, the coping drive with a shot man, his own car as a cork in the gate, the arm in the door. But most are cued by Ron's hands or can be declined.
2. **Ron.** He walks into a police station in his funeral coat to sign for Frank's line, opens the gate in front of the pen, and brings the car on L-plates to the brow. These are the deeds that carry meaning.
3. **Danny.** The hatchback across the rank, the twenty on the counter, taking the book and car two to the edge. He starts the action of 05 and 19.
4. **The old man.** He puts a torch in a gunman's eyes ("Not on here"). It is one deed, and it is the hinge of the midpoint.
5. **Fewson.** He moves Danny's seat and mirror, refuses to sit, and drives off with the crew. A small part, but it pays off.
6. **Sheila.** "Cash introduced, £23.50", the rent book, the arithmetic for car three. She shapes things through her pen, not her deeds.
7. **The Widow.** Offstage. She takes his coat off ("That's one") and ships the van driver away. Influence without a deed in view.
