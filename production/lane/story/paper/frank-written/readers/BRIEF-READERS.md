# Brief for the blind scene readers (written Frank test)

Fixed on 24 November 2026 by the lane's lead before any reader ran. Word for word the brief of the deeds test (frank-test/readers/BRIEF-READERS.md: the phase 2 reader brief with the set cut to ten and question 9 added), with one question added (section 10), so that who carries each scene's weight can be counted. Given word for word to each fresh Opus reader; the blind pack's folder is named in each reader's instructions.

---

You are a blind reader for LEDGER, a third-person crime game set in a British port town in 1990 (the Hook, the old port of the town of Meridian). The lead, Frank Nowak, 32, inherits his dead uncle Mickey's minicab office and the half-dead criminal outfit behind it, and sets out to make it run the Hook by Christmas. The town is simulated: people see, remember and gossip, and the story is a light written spine of scenes that wake on the state of the world, never a fixed mission line. Content rules you should not mark down: no alcohol or gambling exist in this world (pubs serve food), no children appear anywhere, nobody asks about Frank's past.

Read, in full:
1. (blind pack: principles, reference and the ten scenes copied to a folder outside the repository)/PRINCIPLES.md : fifteen principles of an exceptional game story, each with what the best game does.
2. (blind pack: principles, reference and the ten scenes copied to a folder outside the repository)/REFERENCE-ADMIRED.md : twenty admired scenes from shipped games, retold flat. They show the bar in content. Do not try to name them.
3. Ten key scenes, in story order: every file in (blind pack: principles, reference and the ten scenes copied to a folder outside the repository)/scenes/. They are a selection from a longer set of key scenes, so gaps between them are expected. They are written for production, with staging, branches and what the town remembers; judge the scene a player would play.

Read nothing else on the computer, and do not search the web.

The bar is Red Dead Redemption 2's best scenes (for example the Downes collection, Hosea's last job, Arthur's diagnosis and the rides that follow it, Arthur's last ride), as far as you know them, and the reference scenes above.

Write your report to the file named at the end of your instructions, with these sections:

1. Each scene: a mark from 1 to 10, where 10 would stand with Red Dead Redemption 2's best scenes, 7 is a scene a good shipped crime game would be glad of, 5 is competent and forgettable, 3 or less is a scene players would skip. Then, in at most 60 words: its strongest thing, its weakest thing, and whether the player's action carries the meaning or only the mechanics. A table with the ten marks first (scene number, mark), then the notes.
2. The fifteen principles, 0 to 2 each (2 met, 1 half, 0 failed), judged on what these scenes show of the story; where the scenes cannot show a principle, say so and mark what they imply. Total out of 30.
3. The three weakest scenes, in order, and for each the one change that would gain most.
4. Voices: could you tell who speaks each line with the names hidden? Name any two people who sound alike.
5. Clichés, stock lines and turns you saw coming: list them with the scene.
6. Anything that breaks the world's rules above, or that confuses a reader who has not seen the plan.
7. Do you recognise any of it from a published game, film or book? Name it and how close.
8. One paragraph: how far is this from the bar, and what single thing stands most in the way.
9. The people: rank Frank, Ron, Danny, Sheila, the Widow and any other person you choose by how much each one drives these scenes by their own deeds (things a player would retell afterwards), loudest first, with one line each saying why.

10. The weight: for each of the ten scenes, name the one person whose deed or feeling carries the heaviest moment of that scene, in a table (scene number, name, five words why).

Be exact and severe; quote the scene's line that shows each point. Plain words, British spelling.
