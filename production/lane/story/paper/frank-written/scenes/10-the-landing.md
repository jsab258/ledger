# 10. The Landing

- Where it sits: the midpoint, week four: the Saturday of the town's display on the quay. The night before Remembrance Sunday.
- What wakes it: Garbutt has said the week's aim, or a rival has reached a threat; Ron has had four lessons; it is dark.
- Who must be present: Ron Kirby. Two of the Widow's men on the stage: Mark Billany, if he was put down on the ferry rank in week two, and the man who drives her van. Gordon Leckenby on the boat. The old man from the landing, under the overhang by the ticket window, where he has stood every boat night for thirty years. The town on the quay wall, and one constable on foot. Fallback: with fewer than four lessons Ron cannot bring the car; whoever Frank took on first brings it, at forty, on his horn.

Frank is trying to get the Widow's load off the landing stage and a man of his stood there by morning. Then the one man on the Hook who has never called him anything but Mickey's is shot on those stones and tells him to go home, and Frank has to say, in cases, what it is worth to him to go back.

## Staging

First, the Monday, at the counter. Keith Garbutt, in a car coat he never unbuttons, has a cup of Sheila's tea, standing up.

GARBUTT: Her load comes in on Saturday's last boat. Whoever's stood there Sunday morning has the landing. (He lays a pound by the saucer.) I'd not want it said anybody here gave me anything.

Then the Saturday afternoon, the same counter. The plan is laid out in tea things: the teapot is the boat, a saucer the stage, two spoons the brow, the sugar bowl the ticket window. Frank reaches for the stapler.

SHEILA: Not the stapler.

FRANK: It's the van.

SHEILA: It's my stapler. Use the milk.

A sugar lump goes down by the tea caddy, which is the chandler's corner.

RON: Is that me?

PAULINE (not turning from the radio): Don't stand him near the pot. He'll dissolve.

The lump's orders: when the last rocket goes, if Frank is not back at the car inside five minutes, bring her to the top of the brow. Then home, at eleven, and stop for nobody.

Then the night. Twenty to eight. The Harbour Board fires its display from a barge moored off the quay, and the whole Hook is along the wall in coats, faces up. The last boat is standing off in the basin with a launch across her bows showing a red lamp. The tide board says low water, half past eight; the brow is steep and wet.

On the stage two men wait under the lamp. At the kerb above stands the van. Under the overhang, out of the lamp, a cigarette is cupped in a hand. Nobody has told the old man it is not his landing now, and he has not asked. He has said one word to Frank every night Frank has come down here, and he says it now.

THE OLD MAN: Mickey's?

FRANK: You say that every night. You know my name.

THE OLD MAN: I never asked who you were, Nowak. I knew that the first night. I ask whose. Brow's greasy at low water. Keep the rail on your left hand. Mickey would never be told.

FRANK: Nor will I.

THE OLD MAN: No. (A pencil torch goes back into his top pocket.) Go home after. It's all I ever tell any of you.

## The scene

The last rocket breaks at eight and comes down gold. The red lamp goes out, the boat comes in, and the town turns from the wall and starts to walk home along the quay.

The deck hand runs a wire parcels cage down onto the stage: cartons on top, and under them, sacked, four cases of tobacco that no duty has been paid on. The Widow's two start it up the brow, bent, a yard at a time.

Frank meets them halfway, with the rail on his left hand. If the nearer man is Billany, and Frank handed him his cap in week two, Billany takes his hands off the cage, says, "That's us straight," and walks away with a stitched eyebrow. Otherwise it is fists, on a wet slope over the river, and it is short. The van driver goes down it on his back and is a while coming up.

Then the cage is in Frank's arms, and one man cannot push it up that brow. The player holds.

At the chandler's corner a pair of headlights comes on, and between them a white square with a red L. He is alone in the car. The book says he may not be. A right arm comes out of the driver's window, straight, palm forward.

The car comes along the quay at the pace of the people, among them. In the quiet after the fireworks a voice carries, level, to nobody.

RON: Coming up on the ticket window. (People turn their heads.) That's the ticket window.

Ron stops at the top of the brow in three goes, comes down the slope sideways in his boots, and puts his hands on the wire beside Frank's.

RON: On mine. Hup.

At the top the cases go in, two in the boot, two on the back seat; the lid will not shut, and Ron ties it down with the string off the back L-plate.

The van driver has got up the brow. He has blood on his teeth, and a short revolver held down along his leg where the crowd cannot see it.

THE VAN DRIVER: Them's hers. Open that boot.

Ron stands in front of the string. He does not say anything.

Out of the dark by the shutter comes the pencil torch, lit, straight into the man's eyes, and behind it a raincoat and a scarf.

THE OLD MAN: Not on here. (He has hold of the man's sleeve.) Thirty year. Not once. Not on here.

The gun goes off.

Along the quay two hundred faces go up to the sky to see where it will break. Somebody says "Ooh." Nothing breaks.

The old man sits down against the shutter of the ticket window, slowly, like a man who has been asked to wait. His left sleeve is dark from the elbow and getting darker. The torch is still lit, on the stones, pointing at the water.

The van driver looks at the crowd, which is walking again. He puts the gun inside his coat and drives the van away with no lights.

Frank takes a step toward the shutter. The old man lifts his good hand, flat, as he would to a car.

THE OLD MAN: You've done your bit. Go home.

Along the wall, against the crowd, a constable's helmet is coming.

Ron has done what the lump was told to do. He is in the driving seat. Frank has the other door.

RON: Door. Mirror. Belt.

Car two moves off at eleven miles an hour. "Faster, Ron," Frank says, and Ron hears it. It is not a word he was taught.

In the mirror the town is walking round a pair of boots.

The player has what he has had since lesson one: the handbrake, and one word.

If he says "Stop", the car stops dead, and Ron's cap goes off the dash.

RON: Load's in, boss. He said go home.

FRANK: He's sent me home every night for four weeks. Look in your mirror. Two hundred of them, and they're walking round him. I've been walked round.

He gets out and goes back. The sleeve is wet through. He gets the old man up over his shoulder, and it is on his jacket then, and on his neck. Gordon Leckenby has come up the brow at a run.

GORDON: I've rung. They're at Fairview with the display. Twenty minutes, she says.

FRANK: It's eleven minutes by the top road. I'll do it in four.

There are two cases on the back seat, where a man has to lie. Frank lifts them out himself and stands them on the stones, in the lamp. At the kerb two of Danny Cammack's lads have stopped walking.

FRANK: That's half the night, Ron. Put it down to me. Now out. In the back. Hold that shut with your cap.

Ron looks at the wheel, and at him. He gets in behind, takes the old man's head on his knee, and presses his cap to the arm with both hands.

The crowd fills the road. On the river side there is a strip of coping stones the width of a car and no wider, where nobody walks in the dark.

The player has the wheel. Along the lip of the quay with the off-side wheels a hand from the drop; left at the chandler's, the back stepping out; over the top road to the infirmary with the needle past sixty. From the back seat a voice goes on, a little behind the car.

RON: Coming up on the chandler's. (It has gone.) That were the chandler's.

The old man speaks once.

THE OLD MAN: Mickey's?

FRANK: Mickey's. And whose are you? Thirty years stood there. Somebody must have you.

THE OLD MAN: Nobody's. That were the job. (His eyes stay shut.) He never got me anywhere this quick. He were never in a hurry to.

Under the infirmary's porch a constable's bicycle leans on the wall, and there are two cases in a boot tied with string. Frank does not look at either. A sister comes out with a chair, and he carries him in.

THE SISTER: Name?

Frank does not know it. Nor does Ron. Nobody on the Hook ever has.

THE SISTER: Next of kin?

THE OLD MAN: Mickey's. Quay Street.

THE SISTER (writing it): And which of you is that?

FRANK: Me. Nowak. N, O, W, A, K. Put me down as family. There's nobody else coming.

In the yard Frank switches off, and does not take his hands from the wheel. They are dark to the cuff. Ron has come round to the front seat. His hands are on his knees, at ten to two, and one of them still has the cap in it.

RON: Leaving the landing.

FRANK: Every night for four weeks I've given that man my uncle's name. I never asked him his.

RON: Nobody asked. He'd not have said.

FRANK: He said Mickey's. To a nurse. With me stood there.

RON: Aye. (He turns the cap over, and puts it on as it is.) You stopped, boss. I'd not have. I'd been told eleven and home.

Then there is Ron's night to pay for, and the player's hand does it: a cut counted out of the tin onto the dash, as for any man, or the tin left shut.

- A cut. Ron looks at the notes. "Mickey paid me Fridays." He folds them into the pocket with the tally. "Then I'm on the tin. Same as the lad."
- The tin shut. "Aye." He looks at the driver's seat. "She pulls left. You'll have felt it."

## What the player can do

- Hold the cage. Hands: one held input, until Ron comes. Let go and it runs: four cases go into the basin, and the gun comes up the brow all the same.
- Say "Stop", or not. Sign: the mirror. Said, it costs two cases on the stones, and then the wheel is his: the narrowest driving in the game, with a shot man in the back. Not said, Ron drives home at eleven with the load whole and says nothing; the old man is twenty-five minutes reaching a bed.

## What the town remembers

- Half the Hook heard a gun and looked up for a rocket. They learn on Sunday what it was. From tonight, the other side may bring one.
- The old man is back under the overhang on Wednesday with his arm strapped across his chest, having signed himself out. The infirmary's form says next of kin, Mickey's.
- Who went back. "Stopped that car dead and carried him. Then drove the edge of the quay like it had a white line on it." Or, from Brenda: "Went by him at eleven mile an hour."
- Two cases stood on the quay in the lamp. Danny's lads had them up the cold-store road before the constable reached the landing. Danny, at the caff on Monday: "He left me a tip."
- Ada, after chapel: "That was Ron I heard. He was telling the buildings who they were."
- The constable on foot writes down a cab with its boot tied with string. It is the first line on Frank in a police notebook.
- The Widow has the van driver on the morning boat with a bag, and counts it.
- If nobody of Frank's is stood under the overhang at first light, Danny's two lads are. On Monday the caff says Cammack took the landing, and Danny lets it.

## Variants by path

- Danny allied. He keeps the kerb and does not come down. "I don't do lifting, nephew. I do after." With the Widow allied, the load is Danny's, and it is Danny's lad who fires; Danny sends him inland.
- A firm with no night money. There is no cage. The cars carry the crowd home past her taxmen, and the gun is pulled to stop the last of them. The old man has hold of the sleeve the same.
