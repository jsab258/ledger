# 01. The ferry and the office

- Where it sits: Act I, week one, the Monday, the first minutes of play.
- What wakes it: a new game. Nothing else has to be true.
- Who must be present: Sheila Dunn, Ron Kirby, Pauline Kitching. On the way up: Gordon Leckenby at the landing, Shaun Hewson on his ladder, Ada Vane at her window. On the telephone, then on her step in the rows: Julie Kettlewell. Kevin Needham on the air. Nobody can be absent this early, so there is no fallback; if the player lets the call go, the morning ends the same way, a fare short.

Frank is trying to be met. Nobody came down to the boat for him, and he is three weeks late for the one funeral that was his to stand at. He will give up the hour he came for, at a grave with no stone on it, and risk Mickey's own car and the first thing the Hook says about him, to be the man who turns up when somebody rings.

## Staging

The ferry landing, ten past nine. Dry, grey and low, the stones still dark from the night's rain. Gordon Leckenby stands at the top of the brow taking tickets back. A board beside him gives the next sailing in chalk: 9.20.

In Frank's hands: one suitcase and a typed envelope. In his pocket: two ten-pound notes, three pound coins and a fifty. He is in a black suit and a black tie, and the suit is new.

Behind his shoulder the camera sees what he has come to: the quay wall, three cranes with nothing hanging from them, a timber ship against the far wall with no lights on, and Quay Street running up from the water. Over one doorway at the quay end the paint says MICKEY'S and has been saying it for some time.

Other people off the boat are met. A woman has her bag taken from her. A man is asked what kept him. Frank stands at the top of the brow a moment longer than he needs to, looking along the landing, and then stops looking.

At the kerb a white hatchback with no sign on its roof takes two people off the boat who looked up the street for a cab and saw none.

One sound does not belong to the water: a car engine idling up the street, and under it a woman's voice on a radio. It is how the player finds the office. On the rank outside Mickey's stands one car with its engine running and a big man in the driving seat, both hands on the wheel, going nowhere.

Inside: a scuffed counter, the fare book, the radio set, a telephone, a wall clock. A painted board with two chalked lines, CAR 1 and CAR 2, and three brass hooks. Two have had keys on them for years. The third is worn the same, and bare. Over the board hangs a framed photograph of a man in a flat cap with a moustache, squinting, on this quay.

## The scene

Gordon takes the ticket and looks at the envelope.

GORDON: Quay Street's straight up from the wall. You'll want telling which door.

FRANK: I'll find it.

GORDON: Aye. They all do. It's got his name on it.

The player has the street. Under the ladder, Shaun Hewson looks down, brush across the pot.

SHAUN: You'll be Mickey's nephew, then.

Frank does up the middle button of his jacket.

FRANK: Frank Nowak.

SHAUN: That's what I said.

Across the street a net curtain moves and is still. No door opens, on either side, the whole way up. At the rank the man in the car watches Frank come in the wing mirror, and lifts one finger from the wheel.

The office door is propped open with a bucket. Nobody comes to it. Inside, Pauline Kitching has the telephone in one hand and the microphone in the other. Behind the counter a woman in a cardigan writes to the end of her line before she looks up.

SHEILA: New management. Sheila Dunn, I keep the books.

FRANK: Frank Nowak.

He has put the case down. He is looking past her, at the photograph.

FRANK: Is that him?

SHEILA: That's him.

FRANK: I'd have walked past him on the boat.

She looks at the suit, and the tie.

SHEILA: You're three weeks late for that, love. He's in the chapel yard, the new end. There's no stone yet, so you'd not find him. Ron'll walk you up. It's quiet till ten.

PAULINE (the telephone, without a breath): Julie, I can't send what I've not got, Kevin's up Fairview and car two's being kept warm by a man who can't drive her. (She listens.) I know when the boat goes, love, I can see the boat. (She covers the mouthpiece.) Is that him? What do I call him on the air?

FRANK: Nowak. N, O, W, A, K.

PAULINE: Board's for cars and the book's for fares, and you've no column yet. (The telephone.) Not you, love.

SHEILA: Julie Kettlewell. Nine pound in the back of the book. Mickey stopped her cars in August.

PAULINE: And a week's trial on the presses over the water, if she's stood there at ten. Which she'll not be.

The wall clock says sixteen minutes past nine.

There are two things outside that door for him: the walk to the chapel yard, which is what the suit is for, and the driver's door of car two. The player's hand chooses. If it goes to the car, Frank says it over his shoulder.

FRANK: Tell her two minutes.

PAULINE: I've told this town two minutes for nineteen year, and they've not been real ones yet.

FRANK: I was three weeks late for him. I'll not be late twice on one street. Start your clock when my door shuts.

He leaves the suitcase where it stands and opens the driver's door.

FRANK: I'll have her, Ron.

RON: She's Mickey's.

FRANK: He's not coming for her, Ron. I am.

Ron Kirby looks at him, and at the suit. He gets out, walks round the bonnet, gets in on the other side, and pulls the seat belt across.

RON: Where to.

FRANK: Kettlewell's. Don't tell me. Point.

He runs the seat forward a foot. The door shuts.

PAULINE (radio): Car two, is that you, Ron?

RON (the handset, held like something hot): It's him.

PAULINE: Who's him?

RON: Boss.

PAULINE: Car one, be told. New owner's in. Nine sixteen. And he's out again. I'm counting.

KEVIN (radio): What's he like?

PAULINE: He can spell. Ten seconds.

The player has the car. She is ten years old and pushes from the back wheels, and the setts are wet. Ron's left hand comes up off his knee, flat, the way a man on a quay tells a crane where he wants it: left, right, held still for straight on. He does not speak. His right hand has hold of his cap.

Up Quay Street. At the top the flat hand goes right, into the rows, and the back of the car steps out on the wet and is caught.

PAULINE: Thirty. (To the telephone.) He's coming, Julie. I don't know. A black suit.

Two turnings. Then the hand points down a slot between two walls of back yards, dustbins along it and washing across it, and at the far end a woman on a step with her coat on and a carrier bag. She has been stood there since nine, in case.

PAULINE: One minute.

There is no room to open both doors. Julie Kettlewell gets in behind Ron.

JULIE: You're never Mickey's.

FRANK: I'm what he left. Shut the door.

PAULINE: One forty. One forty-five.

FRANK (to the handset): She's in. One forty-eight.

PAULINE: One fifty-two, by mine. (A pause on the channel, which the whole Hook is listening to.) Well.

If the player was slow she gives the true figure, and adds, "Same as ever, then."

The boat sails in two minutes more, and the tenfoot is shut at this end. Ron's hand comes down onto his knee. He has no sign for this.

Frank puts his arm along the back of the seat, looks over his shoulder, and takes her out the way she came in, backwards, the whole length of it, at the speed he came in forwards. Bins go by a hand off each mirror. Ron watches the near-side wall come and not arrive.

At the mouth of the tenfoot Ron's hand closes.

RON: Whoa.

If the player brakes on the fist, a fish lorry crosses the mouth of the lane a yard behind the bumper. If he does not, it stops for him, on its horn.

Out across the street, the nose swung round in the width of it, and down to the water. Gordon has the stern rope off the bollard and in his hands. He looks at the car, and at the clock on the ticket office, and holds the rope where it is.

Julie counts into Frank's palm in ten-pence pieces.

JULIE: That's eighty. The nine I've not got.

FRANK: The nine's in the book. It'll keep till Friday.

JULIE (already on the brow, over her shoulder): Mickey'd have sent Kevin, and I'd have swum.

The ramp comes up behind her. Frank does not start the car. He is looking at the top of the brow, where he stood with a case twenty minutes ago.

FRANK: Nobody came down for mine.

Ron lets go of his cap.

RON: Nobody said which boat. (He looks through the windscreen.) I'd have walked down.

In the office he takes the key from Frank's hand without asking and hangs it on its hook. Then he is at work: back to the wall beside the door.

Sheila has opened a narrow cash book at a clean page.

SHEILA: What did you come with?

FRANK: Twenty-three pound fifty.

He empties his pocket onto the counter. Sheila moves each coin an inch with one finger as she counts it, the way a bank does. She writes, and the camera reads it over her hand: New management, cash introduced, £23.50. She slides the money back to him.

SHEILA: I'm not taking it. I'm writing down that it was there. Somebody always asks what a man started with.

He puts Julie's eight coins down beside it. Those she takes, and Pauline writes the fare in the day book: 9.16, the rows to the landing, 80p. The telephone rings under her hand, and again as she puts it down.

PAULINE: Boarding house for the Exchange. And Brenda wants one for her sister. Nineteen year I've begged this town to ring, and now it's heard she moves.

Sheila lays a buff rent book, limp with handling, on top of his money. For the last three weeks the amounts are in her own hand.

SHEILA: The tin's paid it three weeks. The tin's done. Eighteen pound by Friday, love, or the landlord has the keys. And Sunday you tell me what this firm is.

He looks at the two fares on Pauline's pad, and at the clock, and up the street toward the rows.

FRANK: The chapel yard'll have to keep.

SHEILA: He'll keep, love. He's nowhere to be.

Ron, on the door, if he is asked about the chapel yard: "Say when. I know where he is."

## What the player can do

- Walk straight up, or wander. Sign: the engine and the radio. After a game hour Sheila calls from the door, "In here when you've done looking lost," and Julie has gone for the boat on foot.
- The car door, or the chapel yard. Sign: the wall clock, the chalked 9.20, a car with its engine running. If he takes the walk, Pauline tells the telephone, "Set off walking, love." Ron stands at the yard gate with his cap off and points, with a flat hand, at a strip of turned earth and a jam jar.
- Drive it. Hands: the wheel, and Ron's flat hand for the way in. The way out is his own. The clock is Pauline's, on an open channel. A scrape on a yard wall stays on the car to the end of the game.
- Go up to the chapel yard later, any afternoon the telephone lets him. It never rings less than it did this morning.
- Talk. Once the cash book is shut the street is his to speak to, in Frank's lines or the player's own words.

## What the town remembers

- Every set on the Hook heard Pauline count. Kevin, at the evening change, to Terry Hodgson: "One fifty-two. She said it like the shipping forecast."
- Brenda Laverack, at her shop door by noon, to anybody: "Backwards. Down the tenfoot. With Ron Kirby sat in it holding his hat on." If Frank took the walk, she has the other one: "New owner, same minutes."
- Ada, at her window: "He came up in black. I thought, somebody's told him late."
- Shaun, at the caff counter: "Gave me his whole name off the pavement. Both halves."
- Somebody who drove that car for nine years had a set on. On Thursday a white hatchback stands across Mickey's rank.
- Sheila's entry, cash introduced, £23.50, is the first line of week one. At five on the last Saturday a man from Customs and Excise reads it out and asks whether that was all of it. "It's what was there."

## Variants by path

None. Every game opens here.
