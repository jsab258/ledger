# 15. The yard

- Where it sits: Act IIb, week six, a night in the middle of the week. It breaks into the three jobs Garbutt set.
- What wakes it: at least two and a half game days have passed since the night between; the Hook has decided Mickey's will not see Friday; Frank is out in car two after dark. Somebody with a key to the yard gate has called a settle.
- Who must be present: Kevin Needham, the day driver, who called it and has the key. Craig Fewson, in the street outside, with Danny Cammack's roll. Everybody Frank pays: Terry Hodgson, Lee Drewery or whoever he took on first, Darren Milner, Julie Kettlewell on the night desk, Ron if he is free. Ada at her window. Fallback: if Kevin was sacked over car one's miles, Fewson has called it himself, from inside the yard.

Frank is trying to keep his cars in his yard and his people's keys on his board for one more night. They are the only people on the Hook who are his, and he will not ask one of them to stand beside him. He will give up the tin to its last pound, or the use of the arm he changes gear with.

## Staging

The yard behind Mickey's, after eleven, dry and cold.

Brick on three sides. In the street wall the double gate, eight foot four between its posts. The gate is on the latch, and a key is in its lock on the outside, on car one's leather fob.

The lamp over the office's back door is out; its bulb lies on the step, whole. They have brought their own light: a storm lamp on an upturned fish box in the middle of the yard.

Four cars are drawn up nose to the gate with their engines running and their drivers in them. Along the wall, in the lamp's edge, stand the people who are owed and do not drive. It looks like the pen at a quarter to seven.

Pinned to the back door is Sheila's wages sheet for the week, a name and a sum to each line. On the hook inside hangs a donkey jacket and, if Ron is in gaol, a cap.

In the street, twenty yards up, the white hatchback is parked with its sidelights on. Fewson is sitting on its wing with a roll of notes in his fist and a rubber band round it.

In a cab office, a settle is what happens every night at the counter: the driver and the firm square up. This one is in the yard, at midnight, and nobody from the firm called it.

## The scene

It reaches Frank on the air, in car two, wherever he is.

JULIE (radio): It's the night desk. Ada's rung. She says there's men in your yard, and you're not one of them. (A beat.) She says they're queueing.

He stops in the street, short of his own gate. Kevin Needham comes to the gap in his anorak, with car one's log book under his arm.

KEVIN: It's a settle. That's all it is. Word is you'll not see Friday, so we'll have what's ours tonight, in the hand. Or we'll have it in motors. (He tips his head up the street.) Cammack's paying a tenner over for every car that's brought him, and the half every night, not Fridays.

FRANK: Six years you've had car one, Mr Needham.

KEVIN: Six year. And forty-two mile a week out of her that nobody paid for, and Mickey knew, and had it out with me every Friday, and never stopped it. That were my rise. You've had that book five weeks and not a word. (He is not angry.) I don't know if you can't count or you don't care. He counts. He's sent a man to sit outside and do it.

Frank does up the middle button of his jacket. He says nothing. The player can walk in through the gap on foot, to the tin and the sheet. Or he has the car.

Frank puts his arm along the back of the seat, swings her nose out across the street, and backs car two into his own gateway. She is five foot six across the wings. He lays her along the left post with a hand's breadth to spare, so the gap that is left on the right is too narrow for a car. Then the handbrake.

He gets out into the gap, gets up on her boot, and sits there with his feet on the bumper, in the lamp, in his own gate.

FRANK: Eight foot four, less five foot six, less me. Nobody's driving out of this yard tonight. Six weeks back I'd not a door on this street to knock at. I've this gate. Fetch me the tin and the sheet. We'll settle. By name.

Nobody moves. Then Ron, if he is free, comes through from the office with the tin under his arm and the sheet in his teeth, and holds the storm lamp up so Frank can read. He has not said a sentence to Frank since the station. If Ron is inside, it is Darren who brings them, in his socks.

The order is the player's. The tin holds what it holds. He reads a surname, and counts.

FRANK: Drewery.

Lee comes to the lamp. If he was taken on for his need, he looks at the notes and does not pick them up. "I'll wait. I've waited longer for worse." If he was taken on by a hook, he counts it twice and cannot look up.

A man gets out of the back of the spare car with a coat round him. He has not been called.

DARREN: Am I on that sheet? I'm not on it. I owe him. (He finds a pound coin and puts it on the fish box, in the light.) There. I'm the only one stood in this yard who does.

FRANK: Twenty-one pound to go, Darren.

DARREN: I know what it is.

Headlights swing into the street. Julie has had the microphone open: "All cars. The yard." A fare is still in the back of the car that answers it. Terry Hodgson gets out and looks at car two across the gate, and at the owner sat on it.

TERRY: Now then. (To the fare.) He'll be two minutes.

FRANK: Hodgson.

TERRY: Mine's Fridays. (He reaches in and turns his engine off.) It's always been Fridays.

Then the one line on the sheet with no sum against it.

FRANK: Kirby.

If Ron is holding the lamp he says one word, the first since the station that was not "Boss".

RON: Fridays.

If Ron is inside, nobody answers, and the yard hears Frank read it. He looks through the open door at the cap on the hook.

FRANK: Kirby's is Fridays. He's sat in a cell tonight so there'd be a Friday. Any man that takes a car up that street can tell him what he got for it. A tenner.

The last name the player reads, whenever he reads it, is Needham.

Kevin does not come to the lamp. If the tin has his money, he looks at it lying there.

KEVIN: It's not the eleven pound. It's that there's a lad sat out there with a roll who knew what I was worth before I'd asked him.

FRANK: Forty-two miles a week. I've read it every Sunday. I let it lie because it was Mickey's to give you, and I'd no business with what he gave. That was wrong. It's mine now. Keep them, Mr Needham. And tell me to my face next time, not Cammack's lad.

If Frank asked him where those miles went in any of the five weeks, Kevin breathes out. He does not smile. He hangs car one's key up himself. If this is the first word he has had on it, or the tin ran dry with drivers unpaid:

KEVIN: Five weeks to say that, and a lad with a roll to make you. Lift her out.

One of Fewson's two comes down the street, throws car two's driver's door open against the gatepost, and leans in for the handbrake. Frank is off the boot. He has the lad out by the collar, without a blow, and stands in the open door himself, in the hole, with his left hand down on the lever. It has been his through five lessons.

The Hook has never needed a brake off to shift a car. Four men get their hands under her back bumper and bounce her, on her springs, and at the top of each bounce walk her a foot toward the road.

TERRY: Do you want a hand in there?

FRANK: I've two.

The player holds the brake. With every foot she goes forward, the door that stands against the post is pushed a foot nearer shut, and he is standing in it. The men at the bumper cannot see that.

- If he lets go and steps out: three more and she is in the road. The cars go out past him one at a time, slowly. Fewson counts tenners onto each bonnet by his sidelights. Kevin goes last. The yard is left with car two, the storm lamp, and a pound coin.
- If he holds: on the fourth the post shuts the door. He has got his body out of it, and not the arm. There is a sound.

Eight hands come off the bumper at once. Nobody will bounce a car onto a man. The door is fast on the post, and the arm hangs, and the fingers will not close.

KEVIN (white): Get him out of there. Terry. Get him out.

FRANK: Leave it. (He is looking at the hand.) That's the one I change gear with. Keys. On the board. Then you can get me out.

They go past him into the office one at a time, and each hook takes its key. No car leaves. Fewson stands in the road with the roll for a while, then puts the band back round it and walks to the hatchback.

TERRY: You'd only to shout. I'd said Fridays. (He opens the back door of his cab, where the fare still is.) Shove up, love. We're going round by the infirmary.

FRANK: Two minutes, Terry.

TERRY: Real ones.

However it ended, Kevin has one thing more, from the gate, with the log under his arm.

KEVIN: I'll have my cards Friday. You never asked me where her miles went till there was a roll sat outside. (Or, if he hung his key up: I'll be in at seven.)

He goes out by the gate and leaves his key in it.

## What the player can do

- Walk in, or cork the gate. Sign: Kevin says the rule aloud, and a man with a roll is sitting twenty yards off. Hands: reverse, the mirrors, the left post. On foot he settles the same, and any driver the tin does not reach drives out past him.
- Choose who is called first. Hands: read a name, count the money. A man the tin cannot pay waits, or lifts.
- Hold the brake, or let it go. Hands: one held input, as on the brow at the Landing. Sign: the door, the post, and the width between them closing on every bounce.
- Hit the lad at the door instead of pulling him out. Then it is fists in his own gateway, six to one, and a man who is put down may be hit again. He comes round on Grace Rinaldi's kitchen floor with a folded tea towel under his head. "Don't get up. You've been carried."

## What the town remembers

- Who said Fridays. Terry, at the caff: "All cars, she said. I've waited thirty year for somebody to say all cars."
- The gate. Ada, to the fish shop: "He put Mickey's car in his own gateway like a cork in a bottle and sat on it." If he held: "And when they went to lift it he stood in the hole with his hand on the brake. He'd not shout for one of them."
- Darren's pound. Sheila finds it on the fish box in the morning and writes it in the debt book: "D. Milner, £1, received." It is the first that book has had from anybody who was not made to.
- Fewson's roll. He takes it back to Danny whole, or lighter by a tenner for every car he brought. On Monday, across a cloth, Danny knows to the pound what Mickey's is still worth.
- Kevin has gone, with car one's log, or is in at seven. If he went, a man from Customs and Excise asks for that log in the last week.
- The arm, if it went: plaster to the elbow and a sling. It is the left, and the gear stick is on his left. For days he cannot drive, cannot fight with it, cannot tie a lace or do up a button. Pauline has put him on the telephones by Friday, and he answers them himself: "Mickey's. He'll be two minutes. I don't know, I'm the owner."
- The night is not over. See scene 16.

## Variants by path

- Frank signed first. The cars have been off the rank all week and the drivers are owed for it. The sums on the sheet are bigger, and Kevin says so.
