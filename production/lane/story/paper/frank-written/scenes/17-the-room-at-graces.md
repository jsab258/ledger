# 17. The room at Grace's

- Where it sits: the close of Act IIb: the Monday that Garbutt named a week ago, in the afternoon. Tomorrow at nine the man from Customs and Excise starts.
- What wakes it: Garbutt's day has come. It is the day the old man from the landing is buried, and the tea is in Grace's back room. Frank has fetched one, two, three or none of what the three heads want: a man of Danny's, a paper of Agar's, a load of the Widow's.
- Who must be present: Keith Garbutt. Geoffrey Agar, seen before, if at all, only at the head of his own stairs. Maureen Jensen. Danny Cammack. Grace Rinaldi at the hatch. The Man in Black. Ron, if he is free. Fallback: a head who is dead or gone is sat for by his second (Simon Ombler, Frank Stathers, Craig Fewson), who can take what is handed him and can promise nothing. A head in a pact with Frank sits beside him.

Frank is trying to bury, as family, a man whose name he never learned, and to leave the tea still holding his rank, his licence and his way through the dock gate. The dead man's torch is the one thing anybody on the Hook has left him on purpose. Three people will pay him well to part with it, and tomorrow he needs what they would pay.

## Staging

First, the chapel door, a quarter to two, a thin rain. One hearse. The old man had a tin under his bed with FUNERAL on the lid, and it ran to a coffin and a ham and no following car.

He was shot through the arm on the night of the display, signed himself out of the infirmary, and stood three more nights under the overhang with the arm strapped across his chest and his cigarette in the wrong hand. Each night he said the two things he always said to Frank: "Mickey's?" and "Go home." Then it went to his chest.

Three cars wait behind the hearse with their engines running, and Garbutt is on the pavement in his car coat, getting them into an order that none of the three will call second: a long dark saloon with a driver, for Mr Agar; the Widow's, with Stathers at the wheel in a black tie; a white hatchback, washed. In front of the hearse, hat in hand, stands a tall man in black who walks behind every old docker's coffin on the Hook and marks the funeral out of ten.

Frank is on the far pavement in the black suit. It has been to no funeral yet.

Then the back room at the caff, three o'clock, a gas fire. The good white cloth; the big brown pot, cups, ham sandwiches, a cut fruit loaf. On the sideboard a black-edged card with no name on it, because nobody at the caff ever knew one; Grace has written "Our friend from the landing". There are five chairs. The one at the foot of the table is where the family sits.

Wet coats hang on a stand by the door: a camel overcoat, a fur collar, a leather jacket. At a tea, a person who is stopping takes his coat off.

Frank's left arm, if the yard took it, is in plaster under the good overcoat, which is buttoned over the sling because that is the only way it will go on.

## The scene

GARBUTT (at the chapel door): Mr Agar, then Mrs Jensen, then Mr Cammack. By age. Nobody can mind age.

The player can stand on the pavement with the rest. Or he can cross the road.

FRANK: I bought this suit for a funeral and got here three weeks after it, Mr Garbutt. I'll not walk behind this one with the firms.

He gets into car two, brings her round in the width of the street and puts her in behind the hearse, in front of the long dark saloon, close enough that nothing will go between. It is where the family's car goes. Garbutt takes one step off the kerb and stops; there is nothing a man can say to that at a funeral.

With the plaster, Frank cannot reach the gear stick and hold the wheel. If Ron is free he has got in beside him without being asked. He names nothing. But his hand is on the lever.

FRANK: First.

Ron puts her in first. If Ron is inside, Frank says it all the same, to the empty seat, and then reaches across himself with his right hand, and holds the wheel with his knee while he does it.

The hearse driver looks in his mirror for his orders, as he would to any family car. Garbutt's road is the top road, which is nobody's ground. Frank flashes his lamps once, and the hearse turns down Quay Street.

It is the slowest the player has driven in the game. The Man in Black walks ahead with his hat against his chest. On the parade people come to their doors: Brenda, Shaun down off his ladder, Ada on her step and not at her window.

FRANK (under his breath, as they pass): Thirty-nine. Forty. Forty-one.

Every one is open. They are not open for him, and he drives past them at a walk, with three firms following Mickey's.

On the quay the hearse stops at the ticket window, because the car behind it has stopped. Under the overhang a young man in a new cap, who has stood there for Danny since the old man took to his bed, does not know where to look. He says what he has been told to say.

THE YOUNG MAN: Cammack's?

Frank winds his window down. It is the old man's question, in the wrong name, and he answers it for him.

FRANK: Mickey's.

Two cars back, a window that was down is wound up.

At the caff door the Man in Black gives it to the room, as a verdict.

THE MAN IN BLACK: Seven. The ham was good. Nobody knew the second hymn. Three firms, and one car of family. (If Frank stalled her on the hill: It stalled.)

If Frank walked, it is the old verdict: "Six. Three firms and no family." He puts his hat on and goes.

GRACE (through the hatch): I've had one funeral in here today. If there's to be another I want notice.

GARBUTT (mildly): You'll stop for a cup, Mr Nowak? Then you'll want that off. That's your chair. Somebody has to be family, and it seems you've seen to it.

With two arms, the coat is a matter of two seconds. With one, he cannot get out of it alone, and three people watch him find that out.

DANNY (up at once): Here, nephew. I'll have that off you. I've had most things.

THE WIDOW (not moving): Leave him. He can ask.

FRANK: I'd an arm that wouldn't ask, Mrs Jensen. It's in plaster. (A breath.) Would you.

She gets up, undoes it as she would a parcel, and hangs it by its loop.

THE WIDOW: That's one.

He sits in the family's chair. Grace reaches through the hatch and puts down by his saucer four pound coins and a brown paper parcel with an infirmary label.

GRACE: Four pound over, from his tin. And they sent his things to his next of kin. (She turns the label to him.) It says Mickey's. Thirty years he had my window table, and I never had a name to put on the slip.

In the parcel: a cap, a scarf, a pencil torch.

FRANK: Four pound and a torch. I came here with twenty-three fifty and nobody. He put me on a form as his family, and I never knew what to call him.

He stands the torch on the cloth, on its end. All three of them look at it. It is thirty years of standing under that overhang, and whoever carries it out of this room has a claim to stand there next.

THE WIDOW (to the torch, not to Frank): That was never ordered. I'll say so once, at his own tea, and not again. The man who did it is across the water and he'll not be back.

DANNY: She's sent him to the seaside. (To the room.) My lad's been stood there since Sunday week. He says it lovely.

AGAR (soft, to Garbutt): The Harbour Board lets that landing. One would expect any tenancy to be put in writing.

THE WIDOW: You'd put a tenancy on a wet step, Geoffrey.

Then each of them bids for it, and what Frank fetched this week sets the bid. A head he has brought something for makes an offer. A head he has brought nothing for names the same thing as a threat.

Agar takes a letter from his inside pocket, typed, to Mr Nowek. He unscrews a fountain pen, strikes out the e, writes an a above it, and initials the change. Then, for the first time, he looks at Frank.

AGAR: Mr Nowak. The licence, renewed. The yard as it stands. (With no paper of his in Frank's pocket: Mr Nowak. The yard reverts to the freeholder at Lady Day. Unless.)

Before the letter is across the cloth the Widow sets the milk jug down on it.

THE WIDOW: No tax on your cars from tonight, and my name behind your gate entries on Friday. I don't ask, Mr Nowak. I'm telling you what you're short of. (With no load of hers come home: Five pound a car is five pound a car. And there's the haddock.)

DANNY: Third line, no arrow, and my lad comes off your step. I'll even learn him to say Mickey's. (With no man of his at Grace's sink: Top line. Half. I'm not greedy, I'm just first.)

When the pot is empty Garbutt stands, and so the tea is over. The torch is still on the cloth. The player's hand takes it up, and it goes one of three ways.

- Into his pocket. FRANK: "It came to his family." Nobody at the table says no to a parcel with a label on it. All three have been refused at a funeral, and each remembers it in the five mornings that come.
- Across the cloth, to one of them. That head has the landing, and pays what was bid, and the other two saw. Grace takes the black-edged card off the sideboard and lays it face down.
- Onto the sideboard, by the card. Nobody has it, and nobody pays. Danny's lad stays where he is.

They take their coats: Mr Agar first. The Widow, passing the foot of the table, looks at Frank's boots. The laces are tied.

Garbutt is last. He puts a pound on the cloth for his tea.

GARBUTT: Nine tomorrow you've a man from Customs and Excise. I'm no help to you there. I've known him eleven years and I've never once got him to sit down.

## What the player can do

- Put car two behind the hearse, or walk. Sign: three cars being put in order, and nothing in front of them. Hands: the wheel, and a gear called to Ron or reached for across himself.
- The torch: keep it, give it, or leave it. Kept, he goes into the last week with nothing bought. Given, he has what he needs most, for the one thing the old man left him.
- Ask for Garbutt's truce, if it has not been spent. "One, for one of them, a fortnight. Which?"

## What the town remembers

- The street saw Mickey's car go down Quay Street behind a coffin with three firms after it. Brenda: "Agar's driver had to follow a minicab. With an L on it."
- The young man under the overhang said "Cammack's?" to a hearse, and was answered. Danny hears it back all week.
- Grace tells the caff at breakfast the coat, and then the torch. "He'd one arm and three of them sat watching, and he asked her." Kept: "It went home with the family." Given: "He sold it before the pot was cold."
- Agar has corrected a name in his own hand, at a table. Simon Ombler types "Nowak" from Tuesday.
- The Widow holds a coat, with a dinner and a pair of laces. She prices all three at the gate on Friday.

## Variants by path

- "I'm not stopping." Frank stands behind the family's chair in his coat. Each head says his settled thing, and the torch stays in its parcel. Going out, Danny: "It's a nice coat. I'd have kept it on."
- The arm was not lost. He changes his own gears, and nobody has to be asked. The Widow still looks at his boots.
- Frank holds the landing. His own man is under the overhang with his cap off, and asks it right. The answer is the same.
- He drove past the old man on the night of the display. The label says Mickey's all the same. The Widow, when the torch goes on the cloth: "You went by him at eleven mile an hour, I'm told. So did my conscience."
- The Widow allied. She turns the shooting on Danny: "The lad who did it was never mine. Ask who sent him inland."
- Frank signed first. Agar, to Garbutt: "One is not certain the committee can renew for a proprietor under charge."
