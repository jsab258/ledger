# 14. The night between

- Where it sits: week five, from the Friday morning in the cells to nine on the Saturday.
- What wakes it: the search has played and Frank is in custody. Ron has walked into the station by himself, in the coat he wore to Mickey's funeral, asked for DS Ellis by name, and offered a statement nobody asked him for.
- Who must be present: Ron Kirby. DS Ellis. The custody sergeant. Through the night: the man Frank put on the landing (if Danny's lads hold it, Fewson stands in his place), Rita Welburn, DC Wardell. Fallback: none. Ron is never gone; if he was put in hospital this week he has walked in with his arm strapped.

Since the boat, Frank has wanted somebody on the Hook to come down for him. Tonight somebody has: into a police station, in a funeral coat, to sign for a line that was Frank's. He has until nine to take what he wanted, at that price, or hand it back, which is the one thing Ron has to give.

## Staging

A tiled cell: a bench, a blanket, a hatch in a steel door. The interview room is being painted, so they have sat Ron at a table in the next cell with its door open, and every word comes down the passage.

The front office: a counter with a flap, a clock with a loud second hand, and a bench. Ron sits on it all night, by his own choice, cap on his knee, in a black overcoat tight across the back.

Outside, seven stone steps under a blue lamp, and the Hook after dark, from six until nine. On the landing stage, at ten, a parcels cage with a padlock on it; at the head of the brow, a plain car with two men in it and no lights. Inside the dock gate the wall runs behind the sheds to the river side of the stage: a strip of coping a car wide, nine iron bollards along it, and no room to turn.

Lit, besides: the station's back step, and Rita Welburn's pawnshop window, with a man's wristwatch in it on a card.

## The scene

The custody sergeant has a form, a biro on a string, and all day.

SERGEANT: Name.

FRANK: Nowak. N, O, W, A, K.

SERGEANT (writing): Novak. (He crosses it out.) Nowack. No. (A third time, slowly. He turns the form round.) Is that it?

It says Knowak.

FRANK: It's five letters. You've found six twice.

SERGEANT: Laces. Belt. Tie.

Then a chair scrapes in the next cell, and a voice comes down the passage.

RON: That you, boss?

FRANK: It's me. Go home, Ron. Whatever you've come to say, it's mine to say.

RON: Say nowt.

ELLIS: Nobody's asked you for this, Mr Kirby. I'm going to read you the entry. Then you tell me what you want to say about it.

She reads the line. If Frank told Ron the truth at the white board in lesson five: "Aye. That's the one." If he told him nothing: "Read us all of it. The end and all." If he lied, there is a silence in the next cell, not a long one. "Read that again. Aye. That's how it were."

RON: Mickey's arrangement were mine to carry. Thirty year. That envelope on that night were part of it, and I brought it in, like it says. Who had it off us at the water I'll not say, and you can write that I'll not.

ELLIS: And Mr Nowak?

RON: New. I've told you nowt that isn't so.

In his own cell Frank is on his feet, with the flat of his hand on the steel.

FRANK: Sergeant. She's got the wrong man. Fetch her back.

SERGEANT (not coming): Sit down, Mr Knowak.

ELLIS: It'll be typed for nine. Go home, Mr Kirby.

RON: I'll stop here. If I go home, somebody'll come round and talk to me.

At six the door opens.

SERGEANT: Released on bail without charge, while enquiries continue. Sign there. However you spell it.

Ron is on the bench. Frank stands over him with his boots open and his tie in his fist.

FRANK: What have you told her?

RON: Nowt that isn't so. I've just not told her you.

FRANK: Then I'll tell her me. I carried it. You stood at a wall.

RON: Saying's nowt, boss. You've a firm to stand in. I've a door.

Frank sits down on the bench beside him. For once there is no wheel in front of either of them.

FRANK: Nobody came down to that boat for me, Ron. Five weeks I've wanted somebody to. I never wanted it to be this.

RON: I said I'd have walked down. (He looks at the counter.) This time I knew which door.

FRANK: If I found another way. Would you walk?

If Frank came for him at the pen: "If it's you that's found it. I'd hear you." If he did not: "Not bought out. I never saw you at that gate."

Under the blue lamp the man Frank put on the landing is waiting with his collar up.

THE MAN: Tonight's, Mr Nowak. Two cases, ten o'clock, in the cage. I'm not going down. There's a car sat at the top of that brow with two in it. (He holds out a padlock key.) Cammack's lad says they'll fetch it. For half.

Up the street a white hatchback stands on Mickey's rank with a card in its windscreen that says CABS, and Fewson at the wheel, taking a fare that should have rung Mickey's telephone. Frank goes back in with the key in his hand, and Ron sees it.

RON: Leave it lie. One night's all she's got. I've told her you're new. Don't give her two.

FRANK: Eleven nights in that book, and your letters on every one. I want one with mine on it before you sign for any of them.

The player can leave it lie, or fetch it himself.

He takes car two through the dock gate. Past the ice house he puts his lights out, reaches under the dash and pulls one fuse: the lamps at the back. The foot brake is dead to him then; a red light on that wall can be seen from the brow. He has the handbrake, and what the water gives back.

FRANK (as the bollards come out of the dark): One. Two. Three.

At the ninth he stops her on the handbrake over the stage's iron ladder. Down it. The key. Two sacked cases, up the ladder one-handed, twice, with a cigarette brightening behind a windscreen forty yards off. There is nowhere to turn. He puts his arm along the back of the empty seat and takes her out astern, the length of the wall, with no lamp showing at either end of her.

FRANK: Nine. Eight. Seven.

If the player touches the brake, the wall goes red behind him, and he is in the cell again by eleven with two cases on the sergeant's counter. Ellis, in the passage: "That's two nights, and two of you." At "one" he is at the gate, and through it.

He walks back up the seven steps with rust from the ladder on both palms. Ron looks at his hands.

RON: You've been down the water.

FRANK: Two cases. Nine bollards. Nobody sat beside me.

RON: I've told her you're new.

FRANK: I was. Till ten o'clock.

RON: Then that one's yours. (He turns his cap round on his knee.) This one's still mine.

At two, if the player knocks, Rita comes to her shop door in her coat and does not ask why.

RITA: Fourteen pound and the fortnight. I'll look at your money. Looking costs you nothing.

She turns the ticket over to reckon the interest, and the camera reads the name she will not say: KIRBY R., and a date.

Frank lays the watch on the bench, on Ron's side.

FRANK: Your name's on her ticket. The morning he was buried.

RON (to the floor): He'd no flowers. There were nobody to send any.

FRANK: There was me. Three weeks off, buying a suit. They were mine to send, Ron. That's fourteen pound I owed you before I'd met you.

Ron winds it, and sets it by the station clock, five minutes on.

RON: Mickey liked to be early. (He closes his hand on it.) I'd hear you. If you found owt.

What can be found tonight is a hook. On the station's back step, on the hour, DC Wardell is smoking.

WARDELL: There's a statement in that file says which night. Files lose things. Two hundred. You never met me.

If he pays it, Frank has to walk back in and say so to the bench. Ron looks at him until he knows what was done. If he said he would hear it, he gets up, and says nothing, then or after. If not: "Not for me, boss. Not like that." The money is spent, and he stays where he is.

At nine, if nothing was bought, Ellis comes through the flap with two typed sheets. She lays them on the counter, and her pen across them, and stands back.

ELLIS: Mr Nowak. Before he signs. Is there anything you want to tell me?

The pen lies between two men. Ron has got up. The player's hand can reach it first.

If Frank takes the pen:

FRANK: Frank Nowak. N, O, W, A, K. That line's mine. I'll write it.

It is done under caution, in his own hand: the line from the book, a third time. A Customs officer Ellis has rung at home comes in with his coat over his pyjama collar and charges him. Ron is told his own is not wanted. He looks at the paper under Frank's hand.

RON: You've took the one thing I had to give.

He puts his cap on and goes out through the flap. He is on the door at Mickey's when Frank gets there, and says "Boss," and nothing else.

If Frank leaves it lying, Ron's hand closes on it. He holds the pen the way he holds a tally. He writes the first line himself, in a docker's capitals. I MAKE THIS STATEMENT OF MY OWN FREE WILL. The Customs officer charges him with conspiring to evade the duty on that landing's loads, and he is kept for the magistrates on Monday, because he will not name the men.

At the counter his pockets go into a bag: laces, a comb, a brass tally, if he kept it; the watch, if he has it.

RON: That's to go to him. It's five minutes fast.

SERGEANT: Name of the person it's to go to?

RON: Nowak. N, O, W, A, K.

Nobody on the Hook has spelt it right before without being told.

Then Ron turns round. He holds his cap out across the flap.

RON: Hang that on my hook, boss.

The player has to take it, or leave a man standing there with his arm out. Frank takes it in both hands, like something that might spill.

FRANK: By Christmas, I told you. The Hook. I'll have you out by Christmas, Ron. I'd swap.

RON: Aye. Well. Hook still wants running.

Frank drives home by the dock road with the cap on the dash, where it sat through five lessons. The chained doors of the ice house come up on the left.

FRANK: Coming up on.

He does not finish it. The doors pass, and nobody in the car says so.

## What the player can do

- Fetch tonight's himself, against Ron's word, or leave it lie. Fetched, it is the first night of the firm's that Ron's name is nowhere near.
- Redeem the watch, if he has fourteen pounds. It is the one thing that can change Ron's answer to a bought way.
- Buy the way out. Whoever sold it holds it over Frank to the end, and Ron knows what was bought in his name.
- At nine, take the pen or leave it. Taken: Frank is charged to the end, the licence is suspended a fortnight, the cold stores drop the contract, and Ron is free, on the door, and silent. Left: Ron is inside, and his cap is in Frank's hands.

## What the town remembers

- Left to lie: at half past ten Fewson carries two cases up the brow past the plain car. On Monday the caff says Cammack has the water.
- Fetched: the night watchman on the warehouse row saw a car go backwards along the dock wall with no lights and thought he had dreamed it. Wardell saw it and wrote nothing down; on the last Saturday Danny has it. On Sunday Sheila reads the week across the counter: "Friday, the landing. You."
- Rita tells nobody who pawned that watch. By Monday the parade knows who paid for the uncle's flowers, and at what hour.
- Quay Street sees the owner of Mickey's hang a cap on a hook beside a donkey jacket.
- From this morning Ron names nothing, on every path.

## Variants by path

- Other sellers. For a paper about the yard, signed on Geoffrey Agar's hall table, his clerk fetches Ron out at eight. For eight hundred pounds in cash, Ellis counts it twice in her car: "A hundred, Fridays. I write everything down; that's what you're paying for."
- The Widow allied. Stathers is on the bench beside Ron at midnight with a flask of tea, and pours him the lid.
