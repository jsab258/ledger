## Setting
A harbour quay at night during a fireworks display. The tide is low, the slope to the landing stage is wet, and the town stands along the wall with faces turned up. A last boat waits in the basin.

## People
The lead wants a rival's load taken off the landing stage and his own man standing there by morning. Abel, a go-between, set the aim. Bram is the driver the lead has taught for four weeks. Cass is an old man who has stood under the ticket-window overhang every boat night for thirty years. Ada is the rival boss. Dorran and Ezra work for her.

## What happens
The last rocket broke and the boat came in. Dorran and Ezra pushed a wire cage of untaxed goods up the slope. The lead fought them halfway, and the cage was then too heavy for one man. Bram drove along the quay through the crowd, calling landmarks aloud, and helped him load the cases. Ezra, bloodied, came up with a short gun and demanded the boot. Cass took hold of his sleeve and the gun went off. The crowd looked up for a rocket. Cass sat down with his arm bleeding and told the lead to go home. A constable was walking along the wall. Bram drove off slowly. The lead stopped him, went back, unloaded two cases onto the stones and put Cass in the back seat, with Bram pressing a cap to the wound. He drove the edge of the quay to the hospital. A nurse asked for next of kin.

## The lines that matter
Cass, each night: "Whose man are you?"
Cass, before the shot: "Not here. Thirty years, never once."
Cass, after: "You have done enough. Go home."
Cass, to the nurse: "Next of kin is the dead man's firm."
The lead, to the nurse: "Put me down as family. Nobody else is coming."

## What the player does
The player holds the cage until Bram arrives. He decides whether to say "Stop" and go back for Cass, which costs two cases, then drives the narrowest road in the game. Afterwards he pays Bram a cut or leaves the tin shut.
