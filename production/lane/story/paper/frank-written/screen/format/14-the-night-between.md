## Setting
A police station on a harbour city's docks, from a Friday morning to nine on Saturday. Tiled cells, a front counter, a loud clock.

## People
The lead is held after a search. Abel is his driver, who walked in alone in his funeral coat to take the blame for a line in the books. Ada is the detective. Bram is the custody sergeant. Cass is the man the lead left on the landing. Bess is a pawnbroker. Dorran is a constable who sells favours. The lead wants to be met, but not at this price.

## What happens
Next door Abel told Ada the arrangement was his, from the old boss, and that he would name nobody. The lead called out that he was the one who carried it. At six he was released on bail and sat beside Abel on the bench. Abel said he had told the detective nothing untrue, only not mentioned the lead. Cass brought word of that night's load, two cases and a watched brow. Abel said leave it. The lead drove a car along a dock wall with no lights and fetched the cases alone, reversing past nine bollards. At two Bess showed him a watch Abel had pawned for the old boss's funeral flowers. The lead redeemed it, and Abel set it by the station clock. Dorran offered to lose a file. At nine Ada laid out a statement and a pen, and the lead took the pen.

## The lines that matter
Bram, writing the lead's name wrongly: "Is that it?"
Abel: "I told her nothing untrue. I only left you out."
Abel: "Leave it. She has one night on you. Do not give her two."
The lead: "Nobody came to meet my boat. I never wanted it to be this."
Abel: "You have taken the one thing I had to give."

## What the player does
The player decides whether to fetch the load, redeem the watch, buy a way out or take the pen. Taking the pen charges him and frees Abel. Leaving it jails Abel and puts his cap in the lead's hands.
