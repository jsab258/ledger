You are judging scenes from story-driven games. Each scene below is a retelling of one scene, in a fixed plain format of the same length, with names and places replaced. Every scene has been rewritten in the same flat voice, so do not judge the prose. Judge the scene underneath: the people, what happens, what is said, and what the player does.

For each pair, decide which scene players and critics would remember and praise more, if both were played in full in a finished game of the same quality. One must be chosen.

What tends to make a scene remembered (from studies of player reviews and of the best game stories):
1. People the player cares about, far more than plot or spectacle. A person with a want of their own, and one deed or line only they would do or say.
2. The lead wants something, acts, and pays for it. The heaviest moments happen to the lead or to someone close, and the lead had a hand in them.
3. A bond under strain: trust, a test, a break, a last word.
4. Stakes that are personal (a person, a body, freedom), not only money or a target.
5. A turn that is earned by what came before, not a surprise for its own sake. Pay-offs of things planted earlier.
6. The player's action matters to the meaning of the scene, not only to its mechanics. A choice with a cost.
7. Specific, lived detail, and comedy that comes from who people are.

What tends to make a scene forgettable: errands and set-ups that exist to move the player to the next job; people who only give orders or explain; danger without anyone the player cares about at risk; a plot turn that changes nothing for the people; action with no feeling attached; a scene any game could have.

Rules: judge each pair on its own; do not compare with other pairs. Do not try to guess which game a scene comes from, and do not let fame decide. Do not prefer a scene for being longer, louder or more violent. Read both scenes in full before choosing.

## Setting
A disused set of tunnels under the old quarter, cold and half flooded, with brick halls, side passages and iron doors that open onto other parts of the system. Sluice gates stand open or shut in several rooms.

## People
Ada is a clever outsider who travels with the lead and wants a promised reward. Abel, a fixer, left a trail of recorded messages and is never met. A band of armoured hired men is on the same trail, led by Bram. The player already knows the lead is searching for a young woman who has gone missing.

## What happens
Ada tells the lead that Abel said he was hiding in the tunnels. They find the hired men on the same trail. The lead asks Ada to open a sluice to catch up, but it jams and splits them apart. He finds her near nests of rats and destroys them. She wants to leave because something blocks her radio, and he can talk her into staying. Recordings left by Abel give clues. One uses a codename that means swift, so the lead follows painted birds through a fume-filled passage full of hanging coats. Another points to the missing woman's car, and its number scratched in a pool opens a door. A huge watchman guards a gate, and Ada helps stun him. In a cold store Bram opens kennel gates that release guard dogs, and the lead guards Ada while she shuts them. Bram then fights the lead. Afterwards the lead finds tablets, burned notes and a book. A last recording shows the missing woman's enemy was three partners, not one. At a junction Ada leaves to seek a ring she was promised.

## The lines that matter
- Ada: "A sluice this old will drop us anywhere."
- Abel's recording: "She is the swift. Follow where they fly."
- Ada, at the junction: "I am going after what I was promised. Come, or do not."

## What the player does
The player decides whether to use the sluice and whether to persuade Ada to stay, solves the clue rooms, and fights the dogs and Bram. At the end the player can follow Ada or leave her.

## Setting
A wet afternoon in a harbour city. A chapel door and a hearse, then the back room of a cafe at three, with a white cloth, a pot of tea, sandwiches and a gas fire. A tax inspector starts on his firm tomorrow.

## People
The lead is burying an old dock man whose name he never learned, and wants to keep his licence and his place. Abel arranged the day. Bram is a lawyerly boss, Ada a widow who runs a rival gang, Cass a rough boss. Bess owns the cafe. Dorran is the mourner who scores funerals. Ezra stands under the overhang for Cass. Fenn drives for the lead.

## What happens
Abel put the three bosses' cars in a fixed order behind the hearse. The lead drove his own car in front of them, directly behind the hearse, where family goes. Fenn sat beside him and worked the gear lever for his injured arm. They went slowly through the street while people came to their doors. At the quay Ezra asked the hearse whose man it was, and the lead answered for the dead man. In the cafe Dorran scored the funeral. Bess gave the lead the dead man's things, including a pencil torch. The lead could not remove his coat with one arm. Ada undid it after he asked. He stood the torch on the cloth, and each boss bid for the landing it stood for. Bram offered a renewed licence, Ada no tax and cover at the gate, Cass a place in the queue. Abel left a coin and mentioned the inspector.

## The lines that matter
The lead: "I will not walk behind this one with the firms."
Ezra: "Whose man?"
The lead: "The old firm's."
Ada: "Leave him. He can ask."
Bess: "He had my window table thirty years and I never had a name to put on the slip."

## What the player does
The player drives behind the hearse or walks. He asks for help with his coat. He decides the torch: pocket it, hand it to one boss, or set it on the sideboard so nobody has it. The choice decides who holds the landing.

A tall private surgery on the edge of the city, with a generator room in the basement, a service lift in the next building, an operating suite and a last chamber at the top.

A message from Ada's former driver tells the lead where Ada is. The lead enters with a stolen pass and takes control of the generator room. ## The lines that matter
Bess tells the lead to stop the procedure before it finishes. Ada pleads with the lead to leave the equipment alone. Ada says that whatever the lead decides, neither she nor Bess can change it, and the choice is the lead's. Bess, if the procedure is allowed to finish, swears at the lead and ends the call.

## What the player does
The lead takes the generator room, cuts the two feeds and fights through guards. The player then makes a single choice: end Ada or spare her.
