You are judging scenes from story-driven games. Each scene below is a retelling of one scene, in a fixed plain format of the same length, with names and places replaced. Every scene has been rewritten in the same flat voice, so do not judge the prose. Judge the scene underneath: the people, what happens, what is said, and what the player does.

For each pair, decide which scene players and critics would remember and praise more, if both were played in full in a finished game of the same quality. One must be chosen.

What tends to make a scene remembered (from studies of player reviews and of the best game stories):
1. People the player cares about, far more than plot or spectacle. A person with a want of their own, and one deed or line only they would do or say.
2. The lead wants something, acts, and pays for it. The heaviest moments happen to the lead or to someone close, and the lead had a hand in them.
3. A bond under strain: trust, a test, a break, a last word.
4. Stakes that are personal (a person, a body, freedom), not only money or a target.
5. A turn that is earned by what came before, not a surprise for its own sake. Pay-offs of things planted earlier.
6. The player's action matters to the meaning of the scene, not only to its mechanics. A choice with a cost.
7. Specific, lived detail, and comedy that comes from who people are.

What tends to make a scene forgettable: errands and set-ups that exist to move the player to the next job; people who only give orders or explain; danger without anyone the player cares about at risk; a plot turn that changes nothing for the people; action with no feeling attached; a scene any game could have.

Rules: judge each pair on its own; do not compare with other pairs. Do not try to guess which game a scene comes from, and do not let fame decide. Do not prefer a scene for being longer, louder or more violent. Read both scenes in full before choosing.

--- SCENE 1
## Setting
The yard behind a taxi office, after eleven, dry and cold. Brick walls and a double gate on the street. A lamp stands on a crate. Four cars wait with engines running.

## People
The lead wants to keep his cars in the yard and his drivers' keys on the board for one more night. Abel is the day driver who called the meeting and holds the gate key. Bram waits in the street for a rival firm with a roll of notes. Ada works the night radio. Cass, Dorran, Ezra and Fenn are the lead's staff, all owed money.

## What happens
Ada radioed that men had filled the yard. Abel told the lead the firm would not last the week, so the drivers wanted pay now or their cars gone to the rival. The lead backed his car into his own gateway, left no room for a car to pass and sat on the boot. He asked for the money tin and the wages sheet and paid by name. Ezra, who owed money, put down a coin. Cass said his pay could wait until Friday. Fenn, holding the lamp, said the same. The lead told Abel he was wrong to let the missing miles slide. One of Bram's lads opened the car door and reached for the handbrake. The lead pulled him out and stood in the doorway with his hand on the lever. Four men bounced the car forward. On the fourth the post shut the door on his arm. The men stopped, hung up their keys and no car left.

## The lines that matter
Abel: "It is a settle. We want what is ours tonight, in hand or in cars."
The lead: "Nobody drives out tonight. Fetch the tin and the sheet. We settle by name."
Cass: "Mine is Fridays. It has always been Fridays."
The lead, of his hand: "That is the one I change gear with. Keys on the board first."

## What the player does
The player chooses to walk in or block the gate, picks who is paid first and holds the handbrake or lets go. Holding costs him his left arm. Letting go lets the cars leave.

--- SCENE 2
## Setting
A large house in a wealthy district, empty for years, then a sealed basement flat at dusk, fitted like an old family sitting room.

## People
Ada is the absent owner, who runs a private clinic keeping dying patients on machines against their wishes. Abel is a voice on the lead's phone. Bess is Ada's mother, who is old and weak. Cora is an ally who wants Ada stopped. The player knows Ada has hurt many people.

## What happens
The lead breaks in, puts down one guard, and forces the door. A friendly, crackling voice on an intercom mistakes the lead for Ada's staff and sends the lead below. A shed key starts the lift. The voice sends the lead to watch tapes in the sitting room, and a watch in a drawer has the door code engraved on its back. The tapes show Bess refusing Ada's treatment, having seen what it did to the family dog. In the garage the lead finds the dog caged and wired to a drip, whining, and switches off the drip. Another tape shows Ada injecting Bess against her will. In the cellar the lead finds kidnapped people asleep in rows of beds. The voice plays Ada's log: after twenty-nine days Ada kept her mother alive in the sealed room, speaking only through the intercom. The voice offers Ada's location if the lead switches off the machines. The lead does, and Abel receives the location and a thank-you. Upstairs, Cora admits she went down earlier and could not do it.

## The lines that matter
- Bess, on a tape: "This is not my home, and I do not agree to it."
- Ada, in her log: "My mother stays with me now."
- The voice: "I will tell you where she is, if you let me go."
- Cora: "Stop the clinic before thousands more suffer."

## What the player does
The lead picks locks, finds door codes, watches tapes and switches off the dog's drip. The only choice is the voice's bargain, which ends her.

## The lines that matter
Bess tells the lead to stop the procedure before it finishes. Ada pleads with the lead to leave the equipment alone. Ada says that whatever the lead decides, neither she nor Bess can change it, and the choice is the lead's. Bess, if the procedure is allowed to finish, swears at the lead and ends the call.

A run-down part of the city in daylight, a coast road running along the seafront, and a bus terminus at the far end of the route.

## People
Abel is a photographer for a local paper and has just shown Bram the pictures from a profile. Bram is a former gang boss trying to look respectable, and wants men who mocked him silenced. The lead knows Bram as a man who hands him jobs. Three men ride the bus and are the targets.

## What happens
Bram looks at the pictures of himself and says they make him look soft. Abel tells him he will be well known. Bram says old associates talk behind his back. The lead asks who they are. Bram calls the lead his oldest friend and asks him to silence the men on the bus, which runs from one district to another, and promises a large favour. The lead agrees. He waits at the bus stop, then follows the bus along the coast road in a car, staying close. Driving straight to the terminus fails the job. At the terminus the three men get off. ## What the player does
