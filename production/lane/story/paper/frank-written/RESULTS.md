# A written Frank in the five key scenes: the test

24 November 2026. The owner's decision of 24 November (late afternoon): test a fully written Frank in the key scenes only, keeping the deeds; his ruling of 1 October (Frank's lines mixed) is not changed by this test. Marks from AI readers of AI writing are signs, not measurements.

## What was made

- scenes/: the same five scenes as the deeds test (01 the ferry and the office, 10 the Landing, 14 the night between, 15 the yard, 17 the room at Grace's), rewritten by the Fable writer (high effort, one pass) from the deeds versions, with Frank fully written: one want across the five (to be somebody's own on the Hook, the man who turns up when sent for, with one person who would come down for him), one written line at each peak, the menus at the peaks cut, the old man planted before the Landing. 2,269 to 2,288 words each. Brief: readers/BRIEF-READERS.md, the deeds test's brief word for word plus one question (10: whose deed or feeling carries the heaviest moment of each scene). Reference scenes: judging/REFERENCE-ADMIRED.md. Notes: readers/.

## Verdict

The bar is not met. The rise is +0.33 raw and +0.43 against the unchanged scenes, against about +0.5 asked; and the weight sits with Frank in two of the five scenes, not in most readers' notes (Ron keeps the opening and the night between, the old man the Landing). Everything moved the right way (every reader marks the five higher than the controls, the screen won three more pairs, Frank is now the loudest by deeds for five of six), but by less than the test asked, and the readers' main complaint is no longer mainly about Frank: it is the story's engine (stakes in a book) and the climax, which written lines do not reach.

Recommendation: keep mixed lines (his ruling of 1 October stands). The written versions stay on file as this test. What the readers now name (stakes a player can feel without being taught a column; a climax where the bond and the opponent meet; the heaviest act of the story done by Frank, not watched) is story structure, a matter for the frame and the cards.

What this recommendation costs, said plainly: under it the story keeps the deeds versions, whose readers still named Frank and Ron's weight first (four of six); the move of the complaint to the story's engine was seen in readers of the written scenes, not of the deeds versions; and the written versions did better with every one of the six readers and on the screen. The case for keeping mixed lines rests on the bar being missed on both counts and on the next gains lying in structure, not on the Frank problem being solved in the story; it is not. Adopting a written Frank would also mean the same pass on the other eighteen key scenes, and one more test.
