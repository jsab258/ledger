# The story on paper: the lead's brief (phase 2)

Written 24 November 2026 by the lane's lead, before any helper ran, from the advisor's job of the same day and the owner's decisions on the frame. Every writer, card-maker, reader and reviewer in phase 2 works from this sheet. Canon and RULINGS outrank it; the owner's decisions below change canon and bind everybody.

## 1. The owner's decisions of 24 November (binding)

1. The lead is FRANK NOWAK: Polish father, a post-war settler; British mother, Mickey's sister; Mickey is Michael Suddaby. The town's name ladder: the new owner, Nowak, Frank, Frankie. Nobody ever says Tom or Tommy, in any line, anywhere. Frank everywhere you write; the internal id may stay "tom".
2. The main opponent is Danny Cammack. Canon gains: he once drove Mickey's own car (car two) and never gave the key back.
3. The big loss: Ron goes inside unless Frank pays to stop it. Ron never dies. The loss must land harder (section 3 below).
4. The ending counts Ron and Sheila as friends too (beside Alison and Danby). Mickey's envelope holds a carbon copy of the night's fares.
5. Structure stays: a living town and a crime empire with a light written spine on top, never a fixed mission line. Cast in three layers: core about six, supporting 20 to 30, residents 200 to 300 (residents added after the first loops; name none of them now beyond those already written).
6. A game day is 48 real minutes (a game hour is 2 real minutes; a game week about 5.6 real hours; about 7 game weeks). Scenes are keyed to world state and game time, never to real time.
7. PEGI 18 within canon: fights fully animated, including a blow on a man who is down; light gore at most; no torture; drugs only off screen; no sexual content (seaside-postcard innuendo is fine); no slurs.
8. No alcohol or gambling shown or spoken of: no drink, no betting, no raffles, no "sure things"; pubs serve food only. Tobacco allowed. No children anywhere, in image or in any line.
9. The economy is decided (production/lane/blueprint/economy/SUMMARY.md): a Hook fare is about £3; drivers keep half; more cars on terms (£90 down, £22 a week); protection £5 to £15 a shop; smuggled tobacco £60 a case; DS Ellis CAN be bought, at about £800, then £100 a week, never twice, a missed week and her case jumps and she remembers (this replaces the frame's "cannot be bought"); an honest firm stops at rung 2 (so the wound-down path never reaches rung 3, and its midpoint wakes on the week-four fail-safe).

## 2. What the readers said is still weak (from the six readers of the frame)

- The loss reads soft: a careful player loses nobody; the heaviest moment belongs to Ron and a pen.
- The aim in weeks five and six blurs: the engine (posts, ladders, the sit-down's fail-safe) is invisible, so the player cannot say what he is trying to do.
- Side stories are tied by number, only five by name.
- The line in the book must play as ordinary paperwork, never flagged as a test; but the player must recognise it when it returns (Sheila reads his words back in her own; in week three he sees the line again in the red book, and R. Kirby in the column).
- The turn must never feel like a timer: whatever tip reaches Ellis, the player has seen the leak happen in the week before.
- Danny's turn must come from causes the player made and saw (his grievance chalked on the board from week two); if no grievance was planted, Craig Fewson takes Danny's key and is the man on the quay, and Danny stands beside Frank or is the price of the fight. Fewson must be met beside Danny in week one or two.
- The yard's arm: lost only for a choice made in the yard (taking the hardest man himself, going for the tin alone); a player who lets his people carry it pays a different cost (a man hurt, a post).
- A lost quay fight changes the terms at five (Danny holds the book; Frank bargains at the counter), never removes the choice.
- The last act must be louder than the middle: two of the five mornings are set pieces with their own danger (Kevin's chase by night with Danny's men also after the log; the break-in at Agar's with a constable on the beat).

## 3. The loss, made harder (the lead's design; Ron lives on every path)

On every path something is lost that stays lost, and the player feels it in his hands, not only in a scene.

- What Ron loses if he goes in: his liberty to the end (remand, 2.6.4); his door, the one thing he wanted (to be a man who is sent for); his place at the pen, whose men turn their backs on Mickey's man who signed; and his body: at the visit he has a split lip and a bandaged hand he will not explain ("Walked into a door. I know about doors."). The hurt is off screen.
- What the firm loses: its door. Within two game days of Ron going in, Danny's car stands on Mickey's rank and nobody moves it (Danny's week-six gain); car one stands in the yard with nobody to drive it; the old hand at the landing will deal only with Ron; the drivers ask for their half in cash, daily.
- What the town loses: Lily Tang counts out one of number nine on Fridays, not two; the chair by the radio stays empty and Pauline will not move it; Ron's coat stays on its hook.
- What the player feels with his hands: the silence in the car. Since lesson one Ron has named the buildings as they pass ("Coming up on the ice house. That's the ice house. Leaving the ice house."). After the night between, on EVERY path, the naming stops: Ron is inside; or Ron is free but shamed, because Frank refused the one thing he had to give; or Ron knows Frank bought a statement or a copper, which is near enough to grassing for his code. Frank now drives himself past the ice house and nobody says it. The naming comes back once, in the last drive of the Straight Life (and, if earned, of the Kingdom's better turn), as the last word.
- What Frank has to give, by path:
  - Let it stand: Ron, all of the above.
  - Sign first: Frank is charged to the end; the licence is suspended a fortnight (every car off the rank, and the drivers go to Danny unless paid full from the tin); Eric Tharratt drops the contract (no charged firm on the packing shift); Ellis's case sits on Frank, which closes the Kingdom's better turn. And Ron's door is taken from him by the man he offered it to: "You've took the one thing I had to give." He stays, silent.
  - A cheaper way, each a hook somebody holds over Frank to the end: Agar's brief (Agar's price comes due in Act III: the yard's lease, and the third morning's invoice harder); Wardell losing the statement (£200, once; Wardell sells the fact to Danny, who uses it on the quay); a bought Ellis losing it (her best line, once; she is then owed £100 every week to the end, and she writes every envelope down in her own book). Ron accepts a cheaper way in his name only if Frank came for him at the gate or redeemed Mickey's watch, and the naming still stops.
- The breach is Frank's own act, in lesson five: Ron stops the car and asks what is in the book. Frank's answer (the truth, a lie, or nothing) is what Ron walks into the station to cover. If Frank lied to Ron, Ron hears the true line read through the wall, and covers it anyway.

## 4. One aim a week, said by a person (every path)

Each week has one aim, said aloud by a named person in his own words and kept in the Ledger; the aim for the rung sits beneath it in Frank's words.

1. Week 1, Sheila: "Eighteen pound by Friday, love, or the landlord has the keys. And Sunday you tell me what this firm is."
2. Week 2, Ron: "Get a third car on that board and stand on the ferry rank till they stop asking whose it is."
3. Week 3, Eric Tharratt: "Five mornings, on the minute, four cars. Then we'll talk."
4. Week 4, Garbutt (or Danny, if allied): "Her load comes in on Saturday's last boat. Whoever's stood there Sunday morning has the landing."
5. Week 5, Garbutt's true read, the Monday after the Landing: "They'll each have a go at one of your men this week. Get to Sunday with your three men stood where you put them." The turn breaks into it: then Sheila, the night before the search: "Clear it by morning. Leave the book."
6. Week 6, Garbutt: "Monday week, the back room at Grace's. You go in with something each of them wants, or you go in with your coat on." The week is three jobs to fetch what each head wants (a turned man, a paper, a load), each its own kind of play; the yard breaks into it.
7. Week 7, the inspector, Derek Hebblewhite: five things by five mornings; then Pauline: car two is not answering.

## 7. Rules for every writer

Plain words, British spelling, no em-dashes, no italics. Never quote the owner. No GTA VI story spoiler anywhere. Mafia is never a reference. Nothing in Frank's past is said (canon: nobody asks, he never tells). Frank can be hurt, arrested and held; he cannot die. Fists knock a man out and never kill alone; a fall after a blow can kill, rarely. Cars can knock a person down, never kill. Police are corruptible as individuals, never as a thesis. Mickey's people never go to the police about one of their own; Ron's statement informs on nobody but himself. Racism may exist as a fact about a character, never voiced. Religion never mocked. Everyone an adult. Every heavy scene is Frank's own deed, at his own risk. In key scenes nobody says what they feel outright. Each scene opens with one line: what Frank is trying to get, and what he will give up for it. Each scene's central incident starts from a real-feeling period specific (a price, a procedure, a job's routine of 1990 Britain), never the first idea; write down the first idea for the turn and do not use it.
