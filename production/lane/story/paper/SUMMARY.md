# The story on paper: one page

24 November 2026. The whole story now exists on paper: the frame revised to your decisions, every beat written as a card the game can choose from as the town's state allows, 25 side stories tied to the gangs, and 23 key scenes written in full. AI readers' marks are signs, not measurements.

## Shortfalls first

- No scene reaches the bar. Six blind AI readers put the scenes at 7 out of 10 on average, where 7 is a scene a good shipped crime game would be glad of and 10 stands with Red Dead Redemption 2's best. The best, Ron's fifth driving lesson, gets 8.7 from three readers.
- All six say the same thing stands most in the way: Frank is the quietest person in his own story. Ron, Sheila, the Widow and Danny carry the scenes people would retell. One revision gave Frank written lines at every decision and a habit; the marks did not move (7.0 from the first three readers, 7.0 from three new ones; principles 22.7, then 23.7 of 30), so I stopped revising, as the method says.
- The middle is small for a crime game: fists in nearly every fight, crime mostly off stage, Danny's gains in the middle weeks mostly heard about, not seen. Four readers find the climax too much like bookkeeping.
- The weakest scenes, even rebuilt: the yard (the night men queue in his yard to be paid or take a car) and the funeral tea where Frank meets the three gang heads.
- Checked against the record since (24 November, second run): Ron could be kept on remand in 1990, but it is not certain, and it is a Customs charge, so a Customs officer now lays it; he comes back before the magistrates every eight days, once in the last week. A minicab may not lawfully stand on a public taxi rank: Mickey's own rank at the firm's door is a grey place in law that nobody tested while Mickey was alive, and winning the ferry rank is now plainly a crime that can cost the licence. The one card left over (Danny's go at Ron on the ferry rank when the Widow is your ally) is written, and uses that law.
- The length does not fit your 15 to 20 hours: seven game weeks at 48 minutes a day is about 39 hours of clock.

## The story, as you would tell a friend

Frank Nowak gets off the ferry with £23.50 and a dead uncle's cab office, on a street where nobody opens a door to him. The first night he tells Ron that by Christmas every night fare and every quay on the Hook goes through Mickey's, and Mickey's will mean him. He builds it car by car: a third car on the board, the ferry rank, the dawn run for the cold stores, then the Widow's load off the last boat on the night of the fireworks. Danny Cammack, who drove Mickey's car and kept the key, wants the same thing and is never far away. On Frank's first morning Sheila had him finish a line in the fare book; five weeks later the police read it back. Ron walks in to take the blame. Frank can let him go inside, sign for it himself, or buy a way out that somebody then holds over him. Ron lives whatever happens, but he stops naming the buildings as they drive. The last week is five things the VAT man wants, then Danny on the quay with the book in Mickey's car, and at five o'clock Frank decides whose name the line carries.
