## The story, as you would tell a friend

Frank Nowak gets off the ferry with £23.50 and a dead uncle's cab office, on a street where nobody opens a door to him. The first night he tells Ron that by Christmas every night fare and every quay on the Hook goes through Mickey's, and Mickey's will mean him. He builds it car by car: a third car on the board, the ferry rank, the dawn run for the cold stores, then the Widow's load off the last boat on the night of the fireworks. Danny Cammack, who drove Mickey's car and kept the key, wants the same thing and is never far away. On Frank's first morning Sheila had him finish a line in the fare book; five weeks later the police read it back. Ron walks in to take the blame. Frank can let him go inside, sign for it himself, or buy a way out that somebody then holds over him. Ron lives whatever happens, but he stops naming the buildings as they drive. The last week is five things the VAT man wants, then Danny on the quay with the book in Mickey's car, and at five o'clock Frank decides whose name the line carries.
