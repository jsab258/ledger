# LEDGER story bible v1: a player's review

## 1. Scores

Overall: 7

1. Tom: 6. The suitcase under the bed and the name ladder are clever ways to make a silent man somebody, and the five phone calls are a lovely echo of the coins. But his want ("a place where his name is said") is abstract, he has no joke, no habit and no friend who is his rather than a judge of him, so for long stretches he is a pair of hands the town reacts to.
2. The cast: 8. Sheila, Ada, Reese, Cyril and Pauline are people I would remember and quote. It is also far too many: by my count over thirty named faces in a game of fifteen to twenty hours, and several (Kevin, Terry, Darren, Garbutt) exist mainly to carry a plot point.
3. The antagonists: 6. The Widow is excellent: she prices, never threatens, and her history with Ada gives her weight. Cammack is the loud local lad with a dead-dad grudge that a convenient page disproves, and Agar is a sequence of letters who cannot be touched and whose final move is a phone call.
4. The central mystery: 5. The truth (Mickey drove the fire-setters, the word was Ada's) is real and it does pay off at the climax through the fare book. But I would have it guessed by the Wednesday of week one, because the letter, the fare book rule, "they'll send a man" and Alison's "a cab was seen at the landing" all point the same way inside the first hour.
5. The opening and its retold scene: 8. The ferry, the empty envelopes on the plate, the carry with three routes and the constable who might turn round is a strong first hour. The failed carry ending with "Nowak, T. £82" in the debt book is the scene I would tell a friend about.
6. Structure and pace: 7. Three acts with calendar days (Bonfire Night, Remembrance Sunday, the anniversary, the hearing) pulling a slow player forward is smart. The rival peaks in turn give the middle a shape on paper, but Act III crams too much into two or three hours.
7. The middle: 6. The Fifth and Remembrance Sunday are standout, but weeks two to four on the keeper's road are described as "a first front, a first round, the cold-store contract", which is a loop, not a story. The straight road claims "the same number of scenes" but most of its extra content is forms and committees.
8. Surprise against cliché: 5. The watcher at the window being the firm is a good idea, but a crime game's sweet old lady who invites you to tea and "doesn't ask twice" is the first person a player like me suspects. Uncle's letter, inherited business, local thug, developer with a marina: each piece is familiar, and the bible has not yet put one turn in that I did not see coming.
9. The endings: 8. Every ending has a real last image in the fare book, and "Ferry landing. One. No charge." is chilling. The Kingdom's "Night, Tom" and the Straight Life's "Nowak, T. Round the Hook. 80p. Paid." are earned and quiet in the RDR2 way.
10. The side stories: 8. The watch, Norman Dent and Karen's wardrobe have proper turns and an end the town keeps. The camcorder with two halves is the best crossing of side and main story in the bible.
11. Tone and humour: 7. Pauline on the radio and Reese's legal-but-alarming ride-along are genuinely funny, and the wedding car to the funeral tea is a proper set piece. Some of the jokes on the page (the haddock line, Sheila's "untidy") are mild, and the second half after the Fifth has little light in it.
12. Story and play feeding each other: 8. The carry, the Friday round, the night of the book and the five nights behind five mornings are story told through hands, and nothing is a game over. The cost is that both of the hardest rivals and the climactic scenes (two widows, Reese, Ellis's trade) are talk with no hands at all.

## 2. The five weakest points

1. The mystery is solved by the player before the story solves it. What is wrong: every clue about the fire arrives in the first hour and all of them point one way. Where: section 7 (Sheila's walk-round, the letter, Alison on Wednesday) and section 3. Why a player feels it: I spend ten hours waiting for the game to catch up with me, and when Reese reads the line on the Thursday I already knew it was there. The bar names "an ending guessed in the first hour" as the commonest punished thing. Fix: the bible already has a better secret sitting unused. Cyril gave Ada his tally of what Alan stole the week before Alan drowned, and Maureen did not push him. Make the truth underneath the fire the truth of Alan's death: Ada had her own son put in the dock, and Maureen has carried the suspicion for four years. Plant it in Cyril's ride on night seven (he goes quiet at one name), Gordon Leckenby's "what he saw", and the tape. Let it land in the two widows' cab ride, where the player who has found it can say it once. That changes what the first tea, the window and the Widow's coldness meant, which the fire alone does not.

2. Tom has no Henry and Hans. What is wrong: every strong relationship is someone judging him (Sheila, Ada, June, Ellis, Alison) and the "someone" is a pick between Alison and Philip with little written for either. Where: sections 4, 5 and 8 scene 4. Why a player feels it: I care about Sheila but she will never be my mate. Nobody laughs with Tom. Fix: make Ron the friendship and write it for hands. Ron teaches the round, drives with him, has running bits (the Tangs' two of number nine, his old gang's nicknames), and the watch is between them. Give them three small shared jobs in Act II that are about nothing but the two of them. Then the Widow taking Ron in scene 10 hurts like a death, and "Ron has the gate or the door" in the endings means something.

3. The middle sags on both roads. What is wrong: between Agar's first letter and the Fifth, and between the hearing and the sit-down, the keeper's road is a rising number (£164 a week) and generic rackets; the straight road is forms. Where: section 8, the paragraphs after scene 8 and "The straight road through the second half". Why a player feels it: the bar's own warning, a loop by hour ten; and the honest player gets paperwork while the crook gets break-ins. Fix: give each road three named, hands-first set pieces in those gaps. Keeper: a front that goes wrong in public, a far-bank payer who refuses and must be dealt with, Darren bought by Cammack and caught. Straight: the honest round robbed and Tom walking it himself at night, a cold-store job where he has to keep his drivers out of the Widow's stoppage, and Ada quietly paying the forty-one around him so he must find where her money comes from.

4. Two of the three rivals have no teeth Tom can feel. What is wrong: Agar and the Widow are untouchable by his line, Agar never acts with anything but paper, and Cammack's arc rests on a page that conveniently proves his grudge false. Where: section 6. Why a player feels it: the GTA V complaint exactly, antagonists thinner than the lead, and with only Danny to fight the "different ways of coming at him" narrow to talk. Fix: give Agar hands he pays for: bailiffs or a security firm clearing the yard and the mooring at night, which Tom can fight, outwit or film. Give Cammack a turn the page does not settle: Frank knew Mickey paid him and Danny learns his father lied to him for six years, so the page makes Danny angrier at his own house, not grateful.

5. Act III is overloaded and leaves threads hanging. What is wrong: in two or three hours the player gets Reese, the missing sheets, the ride-along, the two widows, Ellis's trade, Alison's proof, the boarding-house break-in, Les Coultas, Garbutt's favour, Ada's demand for the book and five phone calls. Where: section 9. Why a player feels it: the hurried last act. Les Coultas arrives as "the only living man who can say who paid him" and nothing is written for him; Garbutt's favour is never named. Fix: plant Les at the landing in Act II (the tape, Darren's sightings) so he is not new; give him the Thursday night as one clear scene; cut Garbutt's last-week favour or make it Les. Move Ada's demand for the book into the week-before so the five nights are one job each.

## 3. Would I single this story out

No, not yet, though it is close. I would tell a friend about Sheila, about the night I dropped the envelope and found my own name in the debt book, about the car burning under Ada's window with two people standing at it, and about two widows in the back of my cab with the meter running. Those are real scenes and better than most of what I have played. But I would not tell them to play it for the story the way I would RDR2, because I saw the fire coming in the first hour, the lead is a careful outline more than a man, and the middle on paper is a rising sum. Fix the mystery and give Tom one friend and it becomes a yes.

## 4. What must not be lost

1. The fare book as alibi and noose, and its last images: every fare goes in the book, Reese's "Somebody drove a long way for nothing, Mr Nowak", and the endings closing on a line in Sheila's hand, including "No charge".
2. The Fifth: the burned car, Terry pulled out, the guest seeing it, and Ada at the window with the tallyman beside her, revealed without a word, then "You'd better come in, Tom."
3. Sheila and the names: "Mr Nowak" with the real book, "Tom" once for a kindness she finds in the count, set inside the name ladder that runs from "Mickey's?" to "Nowak's?" to "Nowak!".
