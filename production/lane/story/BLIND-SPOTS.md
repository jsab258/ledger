## 3. Blind spots and partial ones, most important first

1. Story and characters worth caring about (KCD2 32.2%; a thin or predictable story punished in 8.3%). Blind spot. Cheapest real check: write a one-page story bar from the review study's story praise and punishment, have a fresh reviewer judge the approved outline, the nine first-week designs, the cast sheets and the Act II proposal against it, and put the story on his page as a story to read and judge, not only as decisions; that needs his word on the 8 Nov ruling that story drafts are judged built. When: before the Unreal build, now; then each act as it is written during the prototype loops.
2. A living world that feels lived in (24.5%). Partly. Check: record the same two minutes of Quay Street at morning, dusk and night in the packaged build beside KCD2 town footage; a fresh reviewer counts people moving, idle acts, distinct sound sources and reactions against a bar written from the footage, and crowd variety (5.1.5) gets its standard at the same time. When: art stage, before the friends' evening and before the crowd is multiplied.
3. Faces while talking (inside looks, 17.8%). Partly. Check: a 30-second talk clip per principal added to the gate, judged against KCD2 talk footage by a fresh reviewer and then his look, as whole people already are. When: art stage, before more faces are made.
4. Side quests as whole stories (13.7%). Partly. Check: the same story reviewer reads the 32 drafts against "a person with a want, a turn and an end the town keeps, not an errand", and he reads the best three. When: during the prototype loops, before they are built.
5. Period detail beyond the visuals (10.0%). Partly. Check: a fresh reviewer with 1990 British sources reads a sample of lines, prices, police steps and routines, and each owed 1990 figure gets a date. When: during the prototype loops.
6. Price, length and value (punished 8.1%, praised 7.1%). Partly. Check: count authored content per hour (jobs, side stories, named people, lines) against 15 to 20 hours with CENSUS's per-hour figures, and answer the player study's open question on putting price and length on the Monday risk list. When: before the Unreal build; measured again at round 33.
7. Runs well on players' PCs (7.8%; stutter, crashes and loads 4.6%). Partly. Check: measure the presets below High on his PC now; then one packaged run (first launch, shader compile, frame times at each preset) on a weaker PC or a rented machine, at a cost that is his to approve. When: presets now; the weaker PC at the art stage, before release.
8. The same loop across Act II (6.8%). Partly. Check: the playtester plays game weeks 2 to 5 at speed and logs repeats per hour of activities, fare wrinkles, lines and set pieces; the panel reads week 5, not week 1. When: prototype loops, stage C.
9. Difficulty after week one (5.9%). Partly. Check: the scripted fighter and the playtester play the same mix of fights and jobs at rungs 1, 3 and 4; failures per hour by act give a curve to hold inside a band. When: prototype loops, stage C.
10. Music and sound design (4.7%). Music is a blind spot; sound is partly. Check: for music, decide its route and amount (scope, his) and put one listening page of period-mood references against a bar; for sound, his overdue ear on the street ambience (P13), and fight sound, car sound and the mix judged by ear against reference footage in the gate, as accents already are. When: the music decision before the Unreal build; sound at the art stage.
11. Humour and a mix of tones (4.7%). Blind spot. Check: the story reviewer scores a sample of street lines, side stories and live replies against canon's tone line, with a few written examples of the target. When: during the prototype loops, where the text already is.
12. Voice performances (3.8%). Partly. Check: a listening page with the same three short scenes in the current voices, prepared and live, beside an acted reference; his ear judges the delivery, not just the casting. When: art stage, before the friends' evening, where live talk is part of the Meridian Test.
13. The few staged scenes (3.4%). Partly. Check: the ferry arrival and Alison's back-seat question judged as scenes (camera, timing, performance) by a fresh reviewer, then on his page. When: art stage.
14. Endings that land (1.7%). Partly. Check: the story reviewer reads each ending's close and the signs before it; one question added to the panel after the playthrough run: did the ending feel earned? When: the writing before the Unreal build; the play at stage D.

Just below the line in KCD2, for completeness: stiff faces, repeated models and voices (2.4%) are covered only if checks 2, 3 and 12 run; choices that change nothing (2.1%) are covered by the consequence checker; the studio's AI use (1.4%) is answered by the AI notice, the report key and the Steam disclosure, all designed (ER-TALK-07); the love story (1.3%) shares row 1's gap, since "someone worth being honest for" (3.2.5) has no judge; an ending that landed (1.2%) is item 14.

## Notes

- Shares are KCD2's only, as the owner ruled at 14:30 on 20 Nov: Mafia was picked to study how Unreal is used and is not a reference game. The references are KCD2, GTA V and RDR2.
- GTA V and RDR2 review data is not yet in the players' study. It will be added, and may reorder this list or bring back a dropped theme such as driving.
- The sample is not random: it holds about as many negative reviews as positive ones, so a share ranks themes against each other and is not the share of all players. The script's tags are rough within a factor of about one and a half (PLAYERS.md, method).
- Verdicts. Covered: someone checks its quality against a bar before it ships. Partly: designed, but nobody judges the quality. Blind spot: no design target for what players praise or punish, and nobody checks it. A part existing, or a structural test passing, is not counted as a check of a story, look, sound or voice theme.
- Testers as the files name them: B the clumsy-player bot, S scripted tests, H the AI hands-on playtester, the panel of eight reviewer cards that reads H's record (DESIGN 19; BUILD-ORDER's measures M1 to M5), the consequence checker and the publish gate (ONE-SHOT-LOG), the art gate (CLAUDE.md), the feel studies starting next week, and his page.
