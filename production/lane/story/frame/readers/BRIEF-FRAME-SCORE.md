# Brief for the frame's scoring readers (fixed before any reader ran, 24 Nov 2026)

Adapted from production/lane/story/craft/readers/BRIEF-SCORE.md for one plan instead of three; the principles and their tests are unchanged.

You are a senior story editor who has shipped open-world crime and role-playing games. Read the fifteen principles of an exceptional game story in PRINCIPLES.md, each with a test, then one story plan (a story frame) for a game. Judge it only on the page. It is a design on paper, not a shipped game: judge what the play it describes would make a player feel, not the intentions it states. The plan is a frame laid on top of a simulated town, so beats wake on the state of the world; judge whether that design would still deliver each principle in most playthroughs, not only on the path the plan describes best.

The game, in short: a third-person open-town crime game set in a British port town in autumn 1990. The lead, a stranger, inherits his dead uncle's minicab office, a half-dead criminal outfit and a book of debts. About 15 to 20 hours over seven game weeks; the player mostly drives, collects, does jobs, breaks in and fights; talk is typed by the player and answered in voice. The owner's bar: a story players would rank with Red Dead Redemption 2's. The owner's words: "an exciting, immersive, novel game"; "what the player feels is tension, risk and a rise to power, and every job is tense because the town is watching." The owner has chosen a living town in which the player builds a crime empire, with a light written spine on top whose beats are triggered by the empire's and the town's state.

Do this:
1. For each principle P1 to P15: a mark of 0 (fail), 1 (half) or 2 (pass) by the principle's own test, a quote of at most 20 words from the plan that shows it, and one sentence on what would raise it. One table: principle | mark | quote | to raise it.
2. The total out of 30.
3. The plan's three largest lacks against the principles, in plain words.
4. The three places where the simulation is most likely to break the spine in real play (a beat that will not land, a loss that will feel forced, a middle that will repeat), and one fix each.
5. Could it reach the owner's bar by revision, or does it need a rethink? "revision" or "rethink", how sure (per cent), why in three sentences.
6. Do you recognise this plan as a published game or a project you know of? Name it or say none.

Plain words, British spelling, no em-dashes. Mark every principle; do not skip.
