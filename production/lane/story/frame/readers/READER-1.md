## 3. The three largest lacks

1. The big loss is Ron's choice, not the player's fault. The plan says outright that nothing changes what Ron means to do; the player's first-morning line only decides whom Ron shields. RDR2's Downes debt works because the cough comes from Arthur's own collection. Here the player will feel the loss was coming whatever he did, and after it the bond spends two weeks behind a hatch as talk.
2. Too many systems for the player to hold. Rungs, a four-step dial, a five-step ladder per rival, pacts, truces, paid holds, Ellis's four stages, posts lost after three days, the inspector's five graded mornings. Each is sound; together they rebuild the "dense, intricate and hard to follow" complaint, and the last week is the worst of it.
3. The theme's price side is invisible, and the ending leans on a menu. The name ladder makes the rise felt, but nothing shows the player what the name is costing until the search. The ending is then chosen from three options at a counter, with the record only flavouring the voices. Side stories are named by number, without their tie to the question.

## 4. Where the simulation will most likely break the spine

1. The turn will fire at the wrong time. It wakes when Ellis reaches investigating and somebody close names the night. A careful player who leaves no witnesses never reaches it, so the fallback pushes the search, the loss and Ron's going inside into Act III, piled on the inspector and the quay. A reckless player can reach it in week three, before the bond has had its hours. Fix: give the turn a window (end of week four to end of week five) and a guaranteed talker from the director inside that window, and do not let it fire before a minimum of Ron's beats has played.
2. The holding middle will repeat. "A post needs a man every day, and one empty three game days is lost" across three posts is a defend-the-turf chore loop that will fill weeks five and six. Fix: replace the generic upkeep with one authored attack per post, each with its own place, people and danger, and let posts held quietly stay held.
3. The forced losses will feel forced. The yard is "a fight he can shape and cannot win" in a sandbox where the player may by then have armed men and a full board; and Danny's turn "at the latest on the Customs letter" is a fail-safe the player will see as the script overriding an alliance that was working. Fix: let the yard be winnable at a real cost, with the arm broken by the hardest fight in it, and make Danny's turn come from a visible cause the player made (a man of his paid short, Tom passing the third rung) with the fail-safe hidden behind a planted grievance.

## 5. Revision or rethink

Revision, 70 per cent sure. The spine is sound by the principles' own tests: a loud early want, a close opponent who wants the same thing, a bond built in drives, an early ordinary deed that returns, a midpoint that changes play and a last act that pays off. What holds it below the bar is the player's ownership of the loss, the system load and the last week's crowding, which are revisions to the turn, the holding and Act III rather than to the frame. The 30 per cent doubt is whether a state-woken spine can deliver an RDR2-grade loss at all, since the plan's own cures (fallbacks, fail-safes, chairs) are the parts most likely to feel forced.
