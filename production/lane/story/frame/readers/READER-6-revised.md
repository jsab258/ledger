# Reader 6, the revised frame: scored as a player would feel it

Read: the brief, PRINCIPLES.md and STORY-FRAME-2026-11-26.md only. Read as somebody who has spent hundreds of hours in GTA, Mafia, Sleeping Dogs and Red Dead, asking of every line: what would my hands be doing, and would I feel it?

## 2. Total

27 out of 30.

## 3. The three largest lacks

1. The loss is soft and avoidable. Ron does not die, and the plan hands the player a way to stop him going inside. Players who love these games save the companion when they can, so for most of them the big loss becomes Tom's licence for a fortnight and a post. The scene that should hit like Hosea's death becomes a transaction.
2. The turn and the climax are paperwork. The early deed is a typed sentence, the turn is a sentence written twice, the ending is a name written at a counter. The idea is clever and true to the book at the heart of the firm, but for a crime-game player the loudest choices are made with a pen. The quay fight is the only loud thing in the last afternoon, and it can be lost outright.
3. Clarity under a hidden engine. Rungs, ladders, the dial, pacts, fail-safes and the director all wake beats the player cannot see the cause of. Weeks five and six split into several aims or none, and a cast of thirty names around five carried people asks a lot of a player who is mostly driving.

## 4. Where the simulation will most likely break the spine

1. The turn arrives on a timer. "By the end of week five a tip reaches her regardless." A careful player who left no witnesses and kept his men paid will be searched anyway, and will feel the game cheated. Fix: the fail-safe source is always something shown in play in the week before (the old firm's man taken on the night quay with the carbon, seen by the player), so even a clean player watched the leak happen.
2. The yard injury is forced. "The hardest fight in it costs him the arm either way." Players who prepared, brought Ron, Darren and the night desk, and fought well will lose the arm anyway, and it will read as a cutscene override. Fix: the arm is lost for a choice made in the yard (going for the tin alone, taking the hardest man himself), with a different cost (a post, a man hurt) for a player who lets his people carry it.
3. Danny turns on schedule. Under a long pact, "If nothing else has turned him by the Customs letter, that does." A player who treated Danny well all game will see an ally betray him because the spine needs a man on the quay. Fix: if no grievance was planted, Craig Fewson takes Danny's key and car two and is the man on the quay, and Danny stands beside Tom or is the price of the fight.

## 5. Revision or rethink

Revision, 70 per cent sure. The bones are right for the bar: a loud want on night one, a close opponent who gains every week in view, a bond built in drives and jobs, and an early deed that comes back. What keeps it short of RDR2 is feeling, not structure: the loss can be bought off, the turn and the end are made with a pen, and the hidden engine leaves weeks five and six unclear. Each of those can be fixed inside this frame without changing its shape.

## 6. Recognition

None as a published game or project. The nearest echo, which the plan itself names, is Mafia (2002): a cab driver called Tommy drawn into a firm who ends by giving a detective his account. The plan keeps its ending on the firm's own book, which holds it apart.
