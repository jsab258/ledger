# Story frame: the structure (Opus lead, 24 Nov 2026)

The decisions the frame is written from. The writer turns this into STORY-FRAME-2026-11-26.md. Where this says "decided", the writer keeps it; where it says "the writer's", the writer invents within the rules. Everything here sits inside canon.md, RULINGS.md and the blueprint; where it reaches past them it is marked PROPOSED or OPEN.

## 0. The shape in one breath

A on top of C (his decision, 24 Nov 09:04). The town is the game: Tom builds a crime empire out of a dead man's cab office, against three rival firms, the law, the docks, the council and the press, in a town of people who remember, gossip, keep loyalty and have nerve. On top runs a light spine: beats that wake on the state of his empire and the town, never on a mission list. The spine has one question, one opponent close to Tom, one bond, one early deed that comes back, and a last week that reads back what he did.

## 1. The empire goal (decided)

- Tom's loud want, said aloud in the first hour (to Ron, on the rank, the first night, after the outfit's first ask): every night fare, every door and every quay on the Hook goes through Mickey's. Short form the town repeats: "Mickey's runs the Hook." The writer's line for it, in Tom's voice.
- A little wrong: he wants to be somebody in a town that has never heard of him, and he wants it fast (class, not greed: canon's register). The buried need: a place where somebody waits up for him (Ron, Sheila); the empire keeps costing him exactly that.
- The question the game scores and the ending answers, never said aloud: what is a name on the Hook worth, and who pays for it? (The name ladder is the score: the new owner, Nowak, Tom, Tommy.)
- Progress the player feels week by week, all in position, never numbers (ruling 30 Oct): cars on the board and on ranks; men at posts (dock gate, cold store, landing); fronts and rounds; the name people use; doors that open (inner office, Harbour Board counter, solicitor, the sit-down); who steps aside on the pavement; what Pauline's radio carries; the clothes. Blueprint weekly rule: every game week one rival rung, one firm change, one return of a deed.
- The four rungs are the empire's stages: the nephew, Nowak, Tom, the port's man (blueprint 2.12). Each rung change is a felt event: a named person says it to his face, a door opens, a new kind of night job appears.

## 2. The town's powers (decided, from canon and blueprint; relations PROPOSED)

Canon fixes three rival organisations: the old-money machine (Geoffrey Agar: paper, lawyers, the council), the dockside syndicate (Maureen Jensen, the Widow: the dock gate, muscle, smuggling), the new crew (Danny Cammack: loud, flash, reckless, the lock-ups on the north road). Other forces: the police (DS Carol Ellis, who cannot be bought; two bought officers; the beat constable), the docks as a place and a workforce (the Harbour Board counter, the cold stores and their shift manager, the pen of men who once had the register), the council (the licensing committee; the licence hearing), the press (Alison Sedman and the paper twice a week, which prints only what she knows), Customs and Excise (Act III), and Keith Garbutt, the Fixer, who brokers between all three rivals.

Story-level relations (PROPOSED, the frame's main new system):
- Before Tom: Mickey's arrangement kept a cold peace. Each rival paid or tolerated Mickey for the night landing and the rank; none could take the Hook without the other two uniting against it. Mickey's death breaks the peace. That is why all three are looking at the office in week one.
- Each faction wants one thing and fears one thing. The writer gives each a want and a fear in one line each. Agar wants the cold-store road clean and bought before the docks are redeveloped; the Widow wants the dock gate and the men who stand at it; Danny wants to be taken seriously by the two old firms and the street (he wants what Tom wants). Police: Ellis wants one case that joins the warehouse fire to the street's crimes. Press: Alison wants the fire's truth. Council: a quiet licence list. Docks: work. Customs: books that match.
- Alliance: Tom can make a pact with one rival against another, at a sit-down or through Garbutt. A pact has terms (which posts each holds, a joint hit, who keeps out of whose ground). It holds while the ally gains by it. It breaks when Tom grows past what the ally can live with (rung 4 makes every ally a rival), when Tom's men do a deed against the ally's, when the ally is offered more, or when Tom breaks it. Betrayal is a beat the director may wake when a pact's conditions turn.
- War: the blueprint's escalation dial (0 Fists, 1 Shown, 2 Fired, 3 War) and the rival ladders (noticed, a visit, pressure, a threat, a sit-down). Truce: Garbutt's one truce a game, or a pact.
- Recruiting rivals' men: a rival's man whose loyalty to his head is low (paid short, humiliated, his head lost a sit-down, Tom spared him or helped his family) can be turned by meeting his need or spending a hook. He brings his head's habits (a true read), but his old head may learn and send for him: a returning-man beat.

## 3. The core cast (decided: six)

1. Tom Nowak, 32: want above; flaw: pride, the stranger's need to be seen to win, and a temper that shows when someone treats him as Mickey's boy; the one deed only he would do: the writer's (it must happen in week one, be answered by another person, and be a risk to his own body or standing). Tom can be hurt, arrested and jailed for hours; he cannot die.
2. Ron Kirby, 58, the bond. Docker until the scheme ended in 1989, kept on by Mickey for the door and the rank. Rides every job Tom lets him. Docker's code (you do not grass, you do not leave a man on the quay, you turn up). Comedy: Ron learning to drive at 58 (the driving lessons, kept from The Knowledge), building names a street late. Bond beats in nearly every week: trust, small strains, a breach, a last word. Arc variants by path (see the loss).
3. Sheila Dunn, 53, the book. At Mickey's since 22, keeps the fare book and knows the real one. Withholds his name until she trusts him (canon). She writes what Tom tells her to write: this is where the early deed comes from (section 5). Arc: she can become the firm's brain, or the one who leaves, or the one who keeps the true book to save him from himself.
4. Danny Cammack, 30 (RECOMMENDED as the main opponent; OPEN choice 1): head of the new crew. PROPOSED canon detail: he drove car two for Mickey before he went on his own, so he knows the office, the yard, the landing and the book. He wants what Tom wants (to be the Hook's name) and thinks it should have been his. Comes to Tom in week two or three with an offer: his lads and his nerve for a share of the night. Close, funny, generous, faster than Tom. If allied he becomes Tom's best earner; his betrayal ripens on states (section 6). If refused he is the loudest rival. Faced in person at the end, on the quay.
5. Maureen Jensen, the Widow, about 60: the dock gate's power. Kindness as a weapon ("I find out what they're short of", kept from The Arrested Ship). Courts Ron for his old standing with the men. Can be Tom's ally against Danny, his landlord at the gate, or the wall he breaks. Protected by Tom's line (RT2-05: she cannot be killed by Tom).
6. DS Carol Ellis: the law closing in, never bought. Four stages (nothing, asking round, investigating, manhunt). Wants one case. She reads the book in the turn. Offers the trade in Act III (give her a rival; the street learns who talks).

Each core person: want, flaw, the one deed only they would do, bond with Tom, arc by path. The writer invents the deeds and the lines.

## 5. The early deed that comes back (decided)

Night one, the outfit's first ask: Ron brings the envelope for the ferry landing. Whatever Tom does with it (carries it, refuses it, opens it), Sheila asks him in the morning what goes in the book for that night. Tom dictates the line. The player chooses the words: a cover fare (a lie), the truth, or "leave it blank". The line is stored word for word.
- It comes back at the turn (Act IIb), by state: when Ellis reaches "investigating" and someone close to the office talks (Danny if allied and turning; else a poached crewman, or Iveson), Ellis knows which night to look at. The office is searched; the book is read.
- A lie: it is the first false entry and the thread every later wash hangs from (it also seeds Act III's VAT grading). The truth: it is a written record of the landing run, with Ron's name beside it. Blank: Sheila wrote something herself to cover him, and she is the one exposed. Every path bites, differently. The writer shows all three.

## 6. The spine (acts as world-state conditions)

Canon's three acts, with Act II split by its midpoint. The calendar is canon's (15 Oct to about 2 Dec 1990; 60 s a game hour; Act I one week, Act II about five, Act III one). Beats wake on states; dates appear only when a person names them (Sheila's Sunday, the Customs letter). Fail-safes keep the length.

- Act I, The Inheritance (opens on arrival; closes on Sheila's Sunday question). Most authored. Beats: arrival; the outfit's first ask; the line in the book; first collections; each rival's first look (the Widow's men tax Kevin at the gate; Agar's letter about the licence; Danny parks outside and comes in like he owns the place); Ada's tea; Alison's fare to the warehouse row; Sunday: wind it down, take it over, or refuse to say. Stakes: money (rent of 18 pounds on Friday, 23.50 in his pocket, the debt book).
- Act IIa, The Rise (opens: rung 2). Taking ground. Beats: first recruit; Danny's offer (pact or rivalry); the cold-store contract; first front (Rita's back room); first round; Garbutt; rivals' first visits; the real book on Sheila's trust; Ron's driving lessons; first fight on the rank. Closes at the midpoint.
- The midpoint, "the Landing" (the writer may rename): wakes when Tom holds rung 3, or a rival reaches "threat", or by fail-safe at the end of game week four. A big night job to take a post the Widow holds (or Danny's, if the Widow is the ally), with Ron and, if allied, Danny. It turns the game from taking ground to holding it: posts need a man every day, rivals answer, Ellis moves to "asking round", the dial may reach Shown. How you play changes: you now defend and keep men, not only grab.
- Act IIb, The Squeeze (opens after the midpoint). Stakes move to a person. Beats: a crewman poached or hurt; the guaranteed collision (day life and night life at one door); the first gun; Ron in danger; Danny's betrayal ripens (pact conditions: Tom past rung 3, Danny's man taken or paid short, Danny offered more by the Widow or Agar); the turn (section 5: the book read; the office searched); the loss (OPEN choice 2); an attack on Tom's own body (an ambush in the yard or on the quay: hurt for days, an arm in a sling changes play); the sit-down (blueprint fail-safe four game days before the act's end). Stakes reach Tom's body and freedom: Ellis's manhunt is possible from here.
- Act III, The Ledger Comes Due (opens on the Customs letter, after the sit-down or by end of week six). Canon's VAT inspection: five mornings, one item each, graded from the books; Sheila says where the lie holds if she trusts him; Ellis's trade; the successor's offer; Tom faces the opponent in person (on the quay; fists unless the dial is at Fired or War); the last day's three calls; the final choice; the books open at five.

The final choice (decided shape; feeds the built ending rule RT2-04, which is unchanged): keep the firm (and whoever took the fall stays inside); give Ellis the true book to clear the one who took the fall (the business does not survive the books); or give Ellis the opponent (removes a charge; the street learns Tom talks; Kingdom's best turn is closed). Variants by state.

For each act the writer gives: the opening conditions, the beats each condition can wake, the variants by path (allied with Danny, allied with the Widow, alone, wind-it-down), and what the act closes so a player who stops still had an arc.

## 7. The story director (decided; the frame describes it, SYSTEMS lists it as missing)

- A small scheduler over a pool of authored beats. Each beat has: conditions (world state), a priority, a weight class (light, middle, heavy), a cooldown, a shape card (opening, turn, ending, action, place, company, mood), one or more people who can carry it and a fallback role if the person is dead, gone or jailed, and a sign shown before it lands.
- Pacing: something every 30 real minutes after Act I (WQ-01); Act II in at least four stages of at most 4 real hours, each ending in a rung change (WQ-02); at most one heavy beat a game week; a rest or funny beat after each heavy one; the weekly rule (one rival rung, one firm change, one return).
- Escalation and cooling: it reads the dial, the rival ladders and Ellis's stage; it may not move them itself (the Core moves them on deeds). It can only choose which eligible beat plays and when. It cools by offering rest beats when the dial or a ladder rose twice in a day.
- No repeat: neighbouring beats differ on at least three of the seven shape-card fields; a beat never repeats; a side story or happening fills a gap before a filler job does.
- The next want: the Ledger always holds one named next thing, said by a person (who, where, what for), plus Tom's own goal for the rung; no markers (ruling 23 Nov).
- Late or absent: every main beat has a second way in (a person brings it to Tom; another person carries it if the first is gone). No beat can fail the run. The simulation may take away only who is alive, who knows what, who is where, who will talk, and how the law stands (the rule recorded in v3).

## 8. Memory, gossip and talk carrying the story (decided)

- Said to his face: every deed with a named witness reaches Tom as a named person's remark within a game day (the blueprint already does this for rival rungs; extend it to every deed of weight).
- Returning people: anyone Tom hurt, humiliated, spared, paid off, poached or let down goes into a pool; the director casts from it into beats (the man spared on the rank drives the van at the midpoint; the debtor squeezed in week one is the witness in week five). The writer gives three worked examples.
- Versions: gossip carries told versions, never above what was seen; the town's version of Tom is something the player can hear, correct or exploit.
- Live talk: anyone can be spoken to; they say only what the simulation knows (claim check). Key characters bring the spine's facts to talk (Ron about the book line, Sheila about the name).
- The name ladder is the town's verdict, spoken.

## 9. The ending, assembled (decided)

Three endings as built (Kingdom, Straight Life, Burn Both, each with a better and plainer turn; RT2-04 unchanged). Assembled from: (1) the final choice; (2) the empire's state when the books open (rung, posts, licence, books, who is still in the crew); (3) what the town remembers: the close is spoken by named people who remember Tom's own deeds, drawn from the record (up to about five lines, as The Arrested Ship's log was praised for), and the last name Tom is called. Signs before every cost (3.1.5).

## 11. Rules the writing keeps

Canon's content rule: no alcohol or gambling shown or spoken of (pubs are places, serving food or boarded), tobacco fine, no children anywhere (the school stands closed), no sexual content (seaside-postcard innuendo fine), no slurs, drugs only as other people's off-screen trade, no torture. Tom cannot die. A car hurts and never kills. Guns rare and late, through the dial. Killing rare, permanent, remembered forever. Police corruptible as individuals, never as a thesis. The player chooses; the story never forces a wrong act. Mickey died of his heart; nobody thinks otherwise. The warehouse fire's truth is never the street's. Third-person game, but the frame is prose about play. Plain words, British spelling, no em-dashes, no italics. Mafia is never a reference. No GTA VI story detail.
