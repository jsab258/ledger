# LEDGER story bible, phase 2 version 5: a script editor's note

## 1. Scores

Overall: 7 out of 10. The spine would sit in good British television: a bookkeeper who has never said a false thing, two lines in a fare book, a docker learning to drive so one night never happens again. Around it: a last act that talks too much, justice that does not add up, and a middle and second road still sketched.

1. Tom: 7. The flaw is played rather than described, through the take-or-pay offers and the standing question, and the suitcase is a clean, cheap image of fear. His want is thin on the page and his change lives almost entirely in a verb the player must find, so a player who never asks gets a Tom with no arc at all.
2. The cast: 8. Sheila, Ron, Ada, Pauline, Terry and Reese each have a voice; Ivy is a gem. There are too many names for sixteen hours, and Kevin, Darren and Julie are functions more than people.
3. The antagonists: 7. The Widow alone is a 9: lawful, rule-bound, dangerous because she gives people what they want. Danny is the familiar flash rival saved late by his father; Agar is a calendar and a speech.
4. The central mystery: 8. Fair, layered and found with a steering wheel and a mileometer, which is rare. The shape (the most trusted person did it) is one an experienced viewer knows, and the crime underneath, who drove a car, is small.
5. The opening and its retold scene: 7. The carry and the first night still work, and the first lesson is a good second. Twelve people, rules and introductions in two hours is a lot of talk for a hands-first game.
6. Structure and pace: 7. The act shape is sound: friend gained, friend lost at the midpoint, the turn at scene 15, the bill in Act III. Act III then confirms what scene 15 already showed and spends its time in speeches.
7. The middle: 6. The scenes are good, but the weeks between them are a table of one-liners. This is exactly where the reference reviews punish repetition, and the bible has not yet earned its claim that no week repeats.
8. Surprise against cliché: 7. The two turns that are fresh (the car went and came back; Ron was not bought) are carried by play. The final turn is fair but leans on one pronoun and a deduction the narrator makes for the player.
9. The endings: 7. Every ending has a last scene, a fare-book line and a Ledger line, and several are lovely ("Ron drove"; "Ferry landing. One. No charge."). The best ending's justice is lopsided, and the Straight Life's mechanics are muddled.
10. The side stories: 7. Ivy and her daughter, each protecting the other from a thing both know, is a whole story and feeds the climax. The watch is good; One way is a sketch; the umbrella needs its second beat to cost something.
11. Tone and humour: 7. Pauline on the radio, Terry's fifth word, Shiner under orders not to speak and the crabs are genuinely funny. The set-piece list reads as scheduled rather than arising, and the middle movement's humour is thin.
12. Story and play feeding each other: 8. The seat, the dial, the handbrake, the offers and the bench are story carried by systems. Typed answers that the Widow "remembers" and prices on are a promise the systems cannot keep, and Act III leans on reading and listening.

## 2. The five weakest points

1. The law in the last act does not add up. Where: section 8, Thursday and Friday; section 9, the Kingdom's better turn and the Straight Life. Three men broke into a bonded store, yet only the person who signs is charged; Tapper and Shiner sign a Customs paper that names them, for nothing; Reese claims a power over registration no VAT officer has; Ellis "charges the owner with what she can" when there is nothing to charge. Why it matters: the best ending has Ron, who was inside the building, riding free while the woman who went back for him is in the dock. A television audience would write in. Fix: Thursday's papers are statements from the men themselves, and Ellis charges everyone named. In the better turn Ron and Sheila are bailed from the counter together to 14 January, which makes "I can fetch them as'll sit" stronger, not weaker. In the Straight Life, Ellis charges Tom with nothing; the council, not Customs, refuses an operator's licence to a proprietor who will not answer the police.

2. Act III talks, and reveals twice. Where: section 8, Agar's fare, Reese on Monday and Wednesday, Sheila's kitchen. Scene 15 has already given the truth through a seat and a road; the kitchen gives it again as a monologue, and Agar and Reese get paragraphs each. Why it matters: LEDGER is not story-first, and the bar punishes long scenes and too much talk; the climax should be the drive, not the confession. Fix: cut Agar to three lines and the hat. Merge Reese's Monday and Wednesday. In the kitchen Sheila says at most four sentences: the two at the landing, the bollard, the Monday. Let the Friday drive tell the rest, as the route itself.

3. The middle and the second road are still outlines. Where: section 7, the week table; section 11. The Seat movement is four and a half hours with seven scenes; between them is "days are fares, dusk is a job, night is the count". The wind-down road gets six lines for nine hours of play. Why it matters: the bar names the middle and the road of the player who refuses the crime as the places a story is found thin. Fix: give every dusk job a twist and a consequence that returns in a later count or a later conversation. Write the wind-down road's six weeks as six scenes of equal weight to the crime road's, starting with Edith Stockill's finger on the eleven miles, which deserves to be a set piece.

4. Choices at the hinge change too little. Where: scene 15's last question, the "asked first" branch in the Fifth and the Empty Seat. "Come back" puts Shiner off the night gate "with a name in his mouth", and that name never surfaces in Act III. The asked-first branch removes scene 14 (Ron is not foreman) and the setup of scene 15 (Ron is not at the gate) and replaces neither. Why it matters: choices that change nothing are the second thing the bar punishes, and this is the choice the whole friendship builds to. Fix: if Ron comes back, Shiner talks in the Tangs' queue in the week of Agar's letter, and Reese arrives already knowing a woman's name; if Ron stays, he can lend Tapper and Shiner on Thursday without being asked. Write the asked-first branch's scenes 14 and 15 properly: Ron tells the third lesson's truth from the passenger seat, not the driver's.

5. The key clue arrives by coincidence, and its physics are off. Where: scene 10. The wing "wants doing again" for no reason, the garage happens to take the seat out, and the Bishop's tally is under the driver, not the burned man. Nobody says who struck the repair off the bill. Why it matters: a turn praised as fair has its central object turn up because the plot needed it. Fix: let the player cause the find. Kevin's knees are a running joke; make freeing the seat an offer, Shaun's favour to take or pay, or let Danny's lads kick the wing in on the Fifth. Put the Bishop across the back seat with his gloved hand down behind the driver's seat into the runner. Say that Sheila struck the repair off, because a seat that moved would be a question she could not answer.

## 3. Logic holes and period faults

1. Sheila's licence "kept up every three years since" 1961: from 1976 a licence ran to the age of seventy. Fix: a paper licence she has never surrendered, kept in its wallet for thirty years.
2. The Widow's husband "ran the dock gate", and she has hired no Cammack since 1984: under the dock labour scheme registered dockers were not picked at a gate. Fix: the blacklist starts in July 1989, when the gate became hers.
3. Shiner has done "a month on the night gate", but Ron only took the coat on the Friday before the Fifth. Fix: "a fortnight".
4. Tapper and Shiner incriminate themselves on paper for no reason given. Fix: they sign because Ron asks, and are charged with him.
5. A VAT officer cannot hold up a registration over an open night. Fix: the council's licensing sub-committee does it.
6. Ellis charges Tom "with what she can". Fix: she cannot charge him, and says so.
7. The tally and glove are in the driver's seat runner. Fix: the Bishop lay across the back, his hand behind the driver's seat.
8. Who struck the seat repair off, twice? Fix: Sheila, named in section 10.
9. "Not many women it could be" ignores June, Julie and the Widow. Fix: Shaun says on finding the tally that the seat is set for somebody no bigger than Sheila.
10. Agar's watchman swore he was on his rounds, yet told Agar he saw a cab; Agar's silence has no cost to Agar. Fix: it was the watchman's heater, so telling would have sunk Agar's own insurance; that is why he waited until the bank left him nothing to lose.
11. The week table lists the eleven-mile drive as a dusk job Danny sets off; section 16 says nothing asks for it. Fix: drop it from the table.
12. The hours do not reconcile (16 against 16.5), and section 0 skips items 8 and 9. Fix: reconcile and renumber.

## 4. Would I single this story out in a review?

No, not yet. I would single out three scenes: the bench of dockers, Ron braking at the mouth of the row, and Sheila driving the eleven miles with Ron saying "You come back, though." But a reviewer plays the middle, and the middle is a table; the last act is people explaining themselves at a counter; and the best ending lets a burglar off and charges his rescuer. Fix the law, cut the talk and write the weeks, and this is within reach of a yes.

## 5. What must not be lost

1. Sheila, and the fare book as alibi and noose: a woman whose every line reads true twice, who wrote what the car did and could not write who. "Nobody reads a fare book" is the line of the game.
2. The car as evidence and the handbrake as theme: the seat, the dial, the painted bollard, four miles found missing by driving. The last input being the right one to leave alone is the best idea in the document.
3. Ron and Tom, written for the hands: the passenger seat, buildings named by what they were, the lessons, and a man asking for something for the first time to keep a name out of a queue. Which seat Ron is in is the verdict; keep that rule absolute.
