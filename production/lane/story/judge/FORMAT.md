# Scene format and disguise recipe

Written 23 November 2026, 22:25 Swiss time, before any scene was rewritten. Every scene on the shelf, and later every LEDGER key scene put before the judge, goes through this same recipe, so both sides carry the same marks.

## The format (330 to 370 words, all five headings, in this order)

1. Setting (about 40 words): where and when in the story, the mood of the place. No real place names.
2. People (about 60 words): who is present, what each wants in this scene, and the one thing the player already knows about them.
3. What happens (about 150 words): the scene beat by beat, in plain past tense, flat register. Report what happens; do not praise or dramatise it.
4. The lines that matter (3 to 5 lines, about 80 words): the key spoken lines, paraphrased so no famous line appears word for word, but the sense, tone and who says it kept.
5. What the player does (about 40 words): the player's actions and choices in the scene, and what follows from them.

## Register

The same flat, plain voice for every scene, admired or forgettable. No adjectives of judgement (moving, iconic, tense, dull). The writer must not try to make a scene better or worse than its source; the source's own content carries it.

## Disguise recipe

1. Every person gets a name from the fixed list below, in order of first appearance in that scene: men Abel, Bram, Cass, Dorran, Ezra, Fenn, Gil, Hale; women Ada, Bess, Cora, Della, Edie, Faye. The player's character is always "the lead".
2. Places: no real or in-game place names. Use a kind only: "the city", "the hill country", "the harbour", "the camp", "the castle town".
3. Period: kept vague. Weapons are "a gun", "a blade", "a bow"; transport is "a horse", "a car", "a boat" (kept, as kind only). No brands, no dates, no real organisations, no named gangs, factions or companies (use "the gang", "the police", "the lord's men", "the company").
4. Famous lines paraphrased; signature props, songs, catchphrases and named set pieces reworded so a player who knows the game would have to think hard.
5. LEDGER's content rule applies to the rewrite: no alcohol and no gambling (a drinking scene becomes tea, coffee, tobacco or a meal, a card game becomes some other pastime; a drunken night out becomes a night out); no children (a child becomes an adult, or the scene is not used).
6. Nothing outside the scene: no studio, title, sequel, award or reception.

## Recipe version 2 (23 November, about 22:40, after the first recognition screen)

Version 1 failed: a fresh Opus named the game of all 40 scenes, and the mission of most, from the setting's kind (frontier, castle, monster), the set piece and the mechanics. Version 2 adds these steps on top of version 1, for every scene:

7. One shared world. Every scene moves to the same unnamed place: a coastal city with docks, an old quarter, estates, a hospital, a police force, gangs and businesses, and farmland and woods beyond it. No period markers: ordinary cars, phones, guns; no horses, castles, swords, hacking, drones or computers doing the work.
8. Kinds of people kept by role, not by trade or period: a lord becomes a landowner or boss, a knight a hired man, a sorceress a clever outsider, a monster or spirit a human threat with the same effect on the story (a killer, a sickness, a debt, a lie).
9. The set piece swapped for one of the same weight and shape: a robbery stays a robbery but the route, target and escape change; a wedding becomes another family gathering; a chase by plane becomes a chase by some other means. The mechanic the player uses is described in general terms.
10. What must survive: who the people are to each other, what each wants, the turn, who causes it, who pays, the weight of the loss or gain, the tone (comic stays comic), the order of beats, what the player does and what follows. If a scene cannot survive the move, it is dropped.
11. Recognised, for the screen: the fresh model names the right game with confidence 50 or more, or names the right mission. Such a scene is dropped. Right-game guesses under 50 with the wrong mission are counted and reported (an eight-game field gives about one in eight by chance) but kept.
