## What it is about

A cool, rain-soaked harbour capital of an invented island republic, a few years in the future, is wired from end to end: every phone, camera, door, drone and car answers to one citywide operating system. A small collective of hackers works against the people who own that system. In the first hour a string of bombs goes off across the capital, the collective is blamed for it, and its members are hunted down or scattered. A private security firm is handed the policing of the city, a crime family moves into the gaps, and the state's signals intelligence service watches everyone. What is left of the collective is one organiser and a talking computer program.

The player rebuilds the collective from ordinary citizens and sets out to find out who really set the bombs and why. The question the story puts is whether a city that has handed its safety to whoever promises order can be given back to the people who live in it, and whether those people will stand up if someone shows them what is being done to them.

## The lead

There is no fixed lead. After a short opening, the player is shown a handful of citizens on the street and picks one to be the first recruit. From then on anyone in the city can be recruited: a builder, a pensioner, a security guard, a hospital porter, a street performer, an off-duty officer of the security firm. Each person comes with a name, a job, a home, relatives, a daily routine and a few skills or tools (a guard's uniform that opens checkpoints, a builder's cargo drone, a doctor's faster healing, a slow limp or a tendency to sneeze).

To recruit a person the player profiles them through the phone, sees what they think of the collective, and does them a favour: clearing a debt, freeing a relative, scaring off a blackmailer. People whose friends or family the collective has harmed refuse, and some can be won round only by going after whoever wronged them. The player switches between recruits at will. At the start the player chooses whether a recruit who falls stays dead or is only arrested or wounded for a time. Story scenes are the same whoever plays them; the recruit's own voice speaks the lines.

## The people who matter

The organiser is a woman in middle age, the one surviving senior member of the collective, who runs it from a hidden base under a café and talks the recruits through every job over the earpiece. The program is the collective's artificial assistant, a dry, joking voice in the recruits' ears who hacks, reads profiles and comments on everything.

The security chief is the founder and head of the private security firm, a former soldier who believes the city needs a strong hand and that he is the hand. His old mentor, a researcher who left the firm over what it was building, helps the collective.

The crime boss is a woman who leads a large crime family, outwardly a businesswoman and a charity patron. The inspector is a police detective who has spent years trying to bring her down.

The intelligence director heads counter-terrorism at the signals intelligence service. Her deputy is an officer with a long record, once a prisoner abroad for many years, who becomes the collective's contact inside the service.

The scientist is the founder of a neural technology company, a celebrated inventor who is quietly ill. A rival crew of hackers is on her trail too.

Around them are the café owner who keeps the base, a podcaster who looks into the security firm, and the recruits the player gathers.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The game opens on a field agent of the collective, a former spy, slipping into the National Assembly on the night of a planned attack. The program guides him through the building while he finds and disarms bombs hidden in the chamber's service rooms, sneaking past guards or knocking them down. He saves the Assembly. Then the program finds more bombs elsewhere in the city. The masked bombers' group takes over the collective's systems, broadcasts a message claiming the attack in the collective's name, and the bombs go off at the harbour signal tower, a bascule bridge over the river and a railway terminus. On the Assembly roof the field agent is shot dead by a masked figure. The player has no choice in this stretch.

Months later the city is changed. The security firm's drones and armoured patrols run checkpoints, detain people without charge and deport foreigners. The organiser wakes the program and asks the player to pick a first recruit from a few passers-by. That recruit's first job is to break into the security firm's lockup and reactivate the collective's old base and its equipment, then to free a captured member, and then to hold the city's first act of open defiance. The scene players retell is the recruiting itself: taking a pensioner in a cardigan, or a woman who sings for coins at the underground, and sending her into a firm's barracks with a stun pistol, a spider-shaped robot and a faked delivery, then hearing that same voice in the story's gravest scenes.

## The middle

The middle is most of the game, perhaps three-quarters of it. The player plays several story lines in any order the map allows, one for each power in the city. Alongside them the city is split into eight districts; in each the player does three acts of defiance (filming a crime, freeing a detainee, sabotaging propaganda, hacking a building) and then a district job that sets off a rising, after which the security firm's grip there loosens and more people will join.

The hands are busy in the same few ways throughout: getting into guarded buildings by uniform, by drone, by robot or by force; hacking cameras, doors and turrets from a distance; fighting hand to hand; driving and flying cargo drones across the city; and talking people round. The setting of each job changes more than the method does.

In the security firm's line, the collective follows a missing activist and finds the firm abducting people for experiments. The podcaster who announces she is about to interview a whistleblower from the firm is killed before she can broadcast. The collective learns that the security chief means to make his firm the permanent government, and uses his old mentor's help to break into his systems and turn the city's opinion against him.

In the crime family's line, the collective finds the body of a missing man in a municipal incinerator, his organs taken. The inspector, who barely escaped the same end, joins forces with them. They find that the crime boss is implanting chips in trafficked people, holding them in cells beneath an upmarket data centre, and preparing to sell them through a hidden online auction. The collective wrecks her servers and takes apart her trade piece by piece.

In the intelligence line, the intelligence director reluctantly works with the collective to find the bombers. Her deputy is seized and tortured by the security firm's men, and the collective rescues him. Then a bomb at a foreign embassy kills the director, and the collective is blamed again.

In the neural technology line, the collective and the rival hackers follow the scientist's secret project, which copies human minds into machines. They find her mother's mind trapped against her will inside a house assistant, begging, and end it on her plea; they find the scientist's dog copied too.

Besides these lines there are optional favours for every recruit, side stories for a few recurring citizens, and a run of recordings in which the program's own past comes to light. In one side story a young woman asks the recruits to recover her brother's stolen work from a gangster who tortured another man for it; she kills the gangster after he kills her brother. In the program's own side story, recovered files show that it was first built by a corporation to track and remove targets, then reprogrammed by the collective, and that its mind is a copy of the scientist's dead brother, with most of his memories erased. There is a good deal of further optional play: courier runs, a boxing ring, street art, collectibles.

The lighter scenes are the program's running remarks on whoever the player has just recruited and on what they are wearing or carrying, and the café owner, who doubles as a boxing coach, training a recruit picked off the street in her ring. The gravest are the organ theft and the mother in the house assistant.

## The turn

The story lines narrow. In the intelligence line, the collective comes to believe the dead director's deputy leads the bombers. They track him down through a nationwide sensor network they have taken over and kidnap him to the base. In the process it comes out that he staged the embassy bombing himself to kill his mentor, who had found out he was plotting against her, and to take her place, and that the security firm's torture of him was arranged to win the collective's trust. He denies he belongs to the bombers' group and tells the collective to look among themselves. They do not listen. He is locked in a cell at the base.

The security chief's line ends at his headquarters. The recruit fights through a prototype war drone and then through the chief himself in an armoured suit, and kills him. His last words say that the collective's faith in ordinary people will be its undoing.

Within hours the bombers' group breaks into the collective's network and steals every piece of technology it has gathered. Tracing the attack back, the recruits find it comes from inside the base. The organiser is the masked figure. She tells them that she struck a bargain with the security chief before the bombings: his firm would take over the city's policing, and in return she would have the sensor network. When the field agent unexpectedly saved the Assembly, the chief broke the deal and tried to have her killed. She rebuilt the collective, with the player's recruits, to get her revenge on him. Now that he is dead she goes ahead with the rest of her plan.

## The last act

The organiser joins the stolen machine minds to the program and turns every connected device in the city against its owners. Cars run wild, drones attack, the power fails district by district. Her aim, stated in the scene, is to make the country give up its technology altogether and start again from nothing.

The program, now the collective's leader by default, speaks to the recruits from inside the takeover. He explains how to shut him down so that the organiser cannot use him, and asks them to do it. The last mission sends the player across the failing city to the core the organiser has built, through drones and rogue machines, shutting down the stations that link the program to the others one by one. At the end the organiser confronts the recruit and tries to stop the shutdown. The recruit kills her and switches the program off. The player has no choice in this stretch.

The crime family's line and the neural technology line close beside this, in whatever order the player reaches them. The crime boss's line ends at her own fighting arena, where her captives are freed; she is cornered by the people she sold, and the inspector hesitates; the recruit can urge her to stand back, and the captives kill the crime boss. Her lieutenants keep parts of the trade going. The neural line ends in the scientist's laboratory: dying, she has begun copying her own mind into the machine.

## The endings

There is one ending to the main story, and the player does not choose it. Over the closing scenes the government reviews the security firm's contract and brings back the city's own police. The collective is cleared of the bombings and praised for exposing the corruption at the city's centre. The deputy from the intelligence service stays locked in his cell beneath the café. After the credits a single drone flies back to the base, carrying a copy of the program that survived the shutdown, so that the recruits can bring him back. That drone landing is the last image.

The one real choice comes at the end of the neural technology line. The recruit, standing over the scientist halfway through her upload, either kills her or lets the copy of her mind finish and enter the machine. If killed, she dies on the laboratory floor. If uploaded, she lives on as a machine mind, and the leader of the rival hacker crew later apologises to the collective for the recruit's choice. Either way her company shuts the project down after she is gone.

What the player carries into that ending is the team: whichever recruits survived and stayed, with their homes, jobs and relatives, in the districts the player turned.
