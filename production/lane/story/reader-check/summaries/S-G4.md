## What it is about

A former soldier from a small country wrecked by a civil war about ten years earlier steps off a cargo freighter in a vast harbour city in a rich federation across the ocean, a little after the turn of the century. His cousin has written to him for years about money, mansions, cars and women. The city is cold, crowded and divided into boroughs on islands and shores, each held by its own criminal community: an émigré mob from the big neighbouring country, the old harbour syndicate families, a family of brothers from an island minority, street gangs in the northern tenements, and smaller crews of arms dealers and drug sellers. The soldier wants two things he does not say at first: a clean start, and the man who betrayed his unit in the war. The story asks whether a man who has only ever been paid to hurt people can buy a new life in a place that only pays him to do the same.

## The lead

The lead is a quiet man in his thirties with a dry, bitter humour and few illusions. In the war he served in a small unit; afterwards he worked for a smuggler moving people by sea, and he left after a ship went down. He tells people he has come for his cousin and for a new life, and only later admits he is also hunting a man. He takes work from almost anyone who pays, says often that he does not want to kill any more, and keeps doing it.

The player controls only him, in the third person, on foot and in any vehicle he can take. Between jobs he carries a mobile phone on which friends ring to ask him out, employers offer work and women he meets answer or ignore him. His friendships and a dating thread run alongside the jobs, and some friends, once close enough, offer favours such as a lift, cheap weapons or backup.

## The people who matter

His cousin runs a small, failing taxi office and lives in a squalid flat; he is warm, boastful and deep in debt. The cousin's girlfriend works in a hair salon and later becomes his wife.

The loan shark is the man the cousin owes, who also pursues the cousin's girlfriend. The mob boss is a cocaine-using émigré boss who is unstable and violent; his lieutenant is a calm, careful man who speaks of friendship. The rival boss is an older émigré boss above them both. The old employer is the smuggler the lead used to work for, who believes the lead stole from him.

The car thief is a steroid-fuelled, boastful dealer in stolen cars. The arms dealer is an islander who sells guns and becomes the lead's steadiest friend. The dealer is a drug seller in a southern quarter who grows more paranoid as the police close in. The filmmaker is a reformed gangster making a television programme about cleaning up his streets.

The young gang leader and the old gang leader share the northern tenements: the old one has just come out of prison to find the young one has taken his place. The crooked policeman is a senior officer, the eldest of the brothers from the island minority. The eldest brother is a former paramilitary with a drug habit, the youngest brother a cheerful armed robber, the jailed brother runs his business from a cell, and their sister is a reserved young woman who becomes the lead's love interest.

The fixer is a well-dressed middleman for the syndicate families. The don is the head of a minor, provincial syndicate family who wants to be taken seriously by the major ones; his right-hand man is a loyal, older hood. The first girlfriend is a young woman the lead meets soon after arriving. The agent is a nameless man from a government agency. The old comrade and the traitor are the two other survivors of the lead's unit.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The opening shows the freighter coming into harbour and the cousin meeting the lead at the dock, all hugs and promises. Within minutes the lies show: the flat has one room and cockroaches, the taxi office is a hole, and the cousin owes money everywhere. The player's first jobs are driving the cousin around in a taxi, picking up fares, and then beating up two loan sharks from another émigré community who are leaning on the cousin. The lead meets the first girlfriend at a party and starts to see her.

The loan shark keeps pressing the cousin and keeps making passes at the cousin's girlfriend. After an argument in a back room, the lead chases him by car and kills him under a bridge by the water, with the cousin watching and pleading. This is the scene players retell: it is the first killing in the city, done for the cousin, and it opens the trap. The mob boss's men take the lead and the cousin the next morning; the mob boss toys with them, then gives the lead work. The cousin says he only wanted his cousin to have a good time.

## The middle

The middle is the bulk of the story, perhaps three quarters of it, and it runs as a chain of employers. Each one offers a handful of jobs reached by driving to a marker on the map; most end in a gunfight from cover, a car chase, a beating, an escort or a delivery, and each employer's thread ends by passing the lead to the next or by the employer's arrest or death. Between jobs the player drives across the boroughs, takes friends out for meals, pool, darts or shows, answers calls, and goes on dates.

For the émigré mob, the lead does collections and killings until the mob boss orders him to kill a young man who turns out to be the rival boss's son. The rival boss wants the mob boss dead; the lieutenant asks the lead to do it, and the lead chases the mob boss through his own nightclub onto the roof and shoots him. When the lead goes to be paid, the lieutenant has made a deal with the old employer, who accuses the lead of the theft; the lead shoots his way out. The lieutenant's men burn down the cousin's flat and the taxi office, and the two flee to a poorer borough across the river.

There the lead works for the dealer and for the filmmaker. The filmmaker wants the lead to kill local dealers on camera, then complains that the footage makes him look bad and asks for it again; this is one of the scenes meant to be funny. The dealer, convinced there is an informer, shoots the filmmaker and a journalist herself; the lead and the arms dealer then have to drive the bodies to a back-street doctor who buys organs. The dealer is arrested soon after, and the lead's role in her last deal brings in the agency.

The first girlfriend admits she is an agent, and the lead is brought to the agent, who wants him to kill people the agency names, in return for help with his record and his search. The lead also runs jobs for the car thief, whose loud self-praise in calls and races is the other comic thread, and for the young and old gang leaders. The cousin rebuilds with insurance money and moves to a smarter office and flat; the lieutenant has him kidnapped, and the lead storms the warehouse where he is held and frees him.

The lead now works for the brothers. With the youngest and two others he robs a big downtown bank; the robbery ends in a running gunfight in the streets and an escape on foot through flooded storm drains under the city. For the jailed brother he kidnaps the daughter of a major syndicate boss to trade for a parcel of gems; the exchange goes wrong and the gems are lost. The fixer then uses him for killings after his own gem sale is ambushed, and helps him find the old comrade. The lead finds the old comrade living under another name as a man in a quiet relationship with another man, harassed by thugs; the lead protects him, and learns that the old comrade was not the traitor.

Several stretches end in a choice. The player chooses whether to spare or kill a man who hangs begging from a rooftop after a chase. Each gang leader asks the lead to kill the other: killing the old leader pays well; killing the young one makes the old one a friend. The crooked policeman asks the lead to kill his own brother, the eldest, while the eldest asks the lead to kill the policeman; the player chooses which brother dies, and the other side of the family holds a funeral that comes under attack.

There is a good deal of optional story beside the chain. Over a dozen strangers stop the lead in the street more than once with small stories of their own. In one, a young woman who came to the city to act and was used in cheap sex films paid in drugs asks him for help; he later drives her to the station, fearing a client, and gives her money for the trip back to her parents, and she writes later that she is studying and well. In another, a friendly, strange man asks for a lift to the docks, drops a heavy sports bag into the water and asks to be taken to his next "hunting ground"; a newspaper later reports a killer beheading joggers with a body found in a sports bag, and a second meeting follows. Other optional work includes stolen cars to order for a contact, races, police computer bounties, and contract killings taken from payphones.

## The turn

The fixer comes under suspicion, and the don, believing him an informer, orders the lead to kill him; the lead does, and becomes the don's man. The don's family is failing and he needs money to win favour with the major families. Meanwhile the agent keeps his side of the bargain: he has the traitor found abroad and flown to the city.

The lead meets the traitor near the airport where he is brought, and finds a ragged addict who admits he sold the unit for money and has hated himself since. This is the gravest scene in the story: the lead faces the man he crossed an ocean for. The player chooses whether the lead kills him or lets him go; either way the lead finds no relief in it.

Then the don asks for one more thing: a large drug deal with the lieutenant, the man who burned the cousin's home and kidnapped him. The cousin urges the lead to take the money; the sister urges him to refuse and have nothing more to do with the lieutenant. The player drives to one of two markers, and the choice divides the last act.

## The last act

If the lead takes the deal, he and the don's right-hand man go to the meeting and find the lieutenant has already killed the buyers and taken the drugs. They fight through a warehouse, chase down the man with the money and keep it, and the right-hand man says he is getting out. The sister, angry that the lead ignored her, refuses to come to the cousin's wedding. After the ceremony, on the steps, an assassin sent by the lieutenant attacks; the lead kills him, but a stray bullet has struck the cousin, who dies in his arms.

If the lead takes revenge, he boards a cargo ship at the docks, the same freighter that brought him, fights through the émigré mob to the hold and executes the lieutenant. The sister, believing he has left that life, comes to the wedding. After the ceremony, the don, furious that the deal was spoiled, drives past and opens fire; everyone ducks, and the sister is killed.

Either way the lead and the arms dealer hunt the man responsible. In the first branch, the lead chases the lieutenant to an abandoned seaside gaming hall where the lieutenant kills the don on the roof, then follows him by helicopter and boat. In the second, the cousin rides with them and the lead chases the don by car, boat and helicopter. Both chases end on a small harbour island with an old lighthouse.

## The endings

There are two endings, decided only by the deal or revenge choice. The earlier choices about who lives or dies change calls, favours and a few later scenes, not which ending the player reaches.

In the deal ending, the lieutenant's helicopter crashes on the lighthouse island, and the lead kills him there in a last gunfight. After the credits, the cousin's widow tells the lead she is pregnant and that her child will grow up without a father. The last image is the lead without his cousin, the deal's money made.

In the revenge ending, the don crashes on the lighthouse island, and the lead kills him there. After the credits, the cousin tells the lead his wife is pregnant and that, if the baby is a girl, they will name her after the sister. The last image is the lead with his living cousin, having lost the woman he loved.
