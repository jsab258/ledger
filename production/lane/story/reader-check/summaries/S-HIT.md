## What it is about

A secret bureau sells killings to the very rich and works out of no fixed country. Its finest hired killer is a bald, silent man with no past he can remember, raised in a closed laboratory and trained to take any face and any uniform. He works through one handler, a cool woman who speaks in his ear and chooses his work. Over a long run of contracts in invented cities across the world, someone outside the bureau begins to arrange which names reach his list. The story asks who is steering the killer, who steered him before he could remember, and what he owes, if anything, to the people who made him.

## The lead

The lead is a cloned assassin in late middle age, the product of a laboratory in a cold mountain country where he and others were bred and conditioned for killing. He has no family, no friends and no record of most of his own life. He is patient, precise and almost wordless, and he follows the bureau's rules: kill only the paid target, leave no trace, never ask why. His only steady bond is with his handler, whom he has worked with for twenty years. Through the story he is pulled between that obedience and a growing wish to know what was taken from him.

## The people who matter

The handler is the bureau's senior agent officer, who recruited the lead and directs every contract by radio. She is loyal to him in her own guarded way and keeps her private history to herself. The hidden client is a former soldier who runs a small private army and hires the bureau through cut-outs, so that its contracts serve his ends. The go-between is an elegant, soft-spoken man who speaks for a secret circle of old money that has shaped governments for generations. The circle is led by three partners: an old industrialist, a banker and a woman who heads a landed family. A young freelance hacker joins late. Each contract also brings its own targets: arms dealers, a spy-ring couple, a scientist, a rock singer, a banker, a general, a militia leader, a traitor inside the bureau, a cartel family and others.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The story opens twenty years before the main events, in the bureau's training facility in a snowbound northern valley. The lead arrives as a nameless applicant. The handler watches him through glass while he runs staged tests on mock-ups: a yacht party where he must kill a playboy dealer in stolen technology, and a mock air base where he must kill a defecting chess master under the eye of a suspicious instructor. The player learns the work here: changing into a guard's or waiter's clothes, slipping poison into food, rigging accidents, hiding bodies in cupboards, walking past people who have no reason to stop him. The bureau's director doubts him; the handler signs him on.

The scene players retell comes next, twenty years later, at a fashion show in a palace in the capital of an invented continental republic. The targets are a designer couple who secretly run a ring that sells state secrets to the highest bidder. The lead can dress as a lighting man, a security guard, or a famous male model and walk the catwalk himself, then reach the couple in their private offices during the secret auction upstairs, or drop a lighting rig onto one of them on the runway. The player picks the route; the targets, not the method, are fixed.

## The middle

The middle is most of the story, perhaps four fifths of it. It is a chain of single contracts, each in a large self-contained location in a new country, each with one or more named targets, many guards and staff, and many ways in. In every one the player's hands do the same kinds of work: watching routines, stealing uniforms, listening to talk, picking locks, poisoning food, pushing people off ledges, rigging gas pipes and electrical faults, and hiding bodies. Between contracts, short scenes advance the plot in the background.

The first stretch runs: a coastal villa where a scientist works on a virus that kills one chosen person by their genetic code, and the lead must destroy it; a desert city on the eve of a coup, where a disgraced banker hides in a consulate and a general plans to seize power; a riverside hotel where a rock singer is suspected of killing his girlfriend and his lawyer has covered it up; a mountain farm where a militia of four plans attacks for the hidden client; and a hospital in the mountains of a far eastern island, where a bureau director who betrayed the lead's colleagues waits for a heart transplant, which the lead can sabotage by switching the donor heart. Slowly the handler sees that these targets are agents of the circle, and that the bureau has been tricked into doing the hidden client's work.

The second stretch sends the bureau after the hidden client's backers and allies: a beach house where one of his lieutenants lives, a motor race on a coastal circuit where a father and daughter build armed drones and arm his men, a cartel family in a mountain village that funds him, and a slum and film studio where a gang lord linked to him rules. Then the targets become the circle's own: a quiet suburban street where an old member of the circle lives under a false name, and a private island where the circle's twin heiresses host a secret society's gala. Partway through, the lead meets the hidden client face to face and learns that they shared the laboratory as young men and once tried to escape together. The lead's memory of this was erased. The hidden client asks him to join a war on the circle that made them both, and the lead agrees.

Optional story sits mostly inside each location rather than around it. One side story is a scripted murder enquiry inside the landed family's manor, where the lead can pose as a private detective, question the family and staff, read the clues and name the culprit, or name the wrong one, before reaching his target. A second is a short extra chain in which a cult spreads a disease through several of the earlier locations, and the lead must find and kill the carriers before the outbreak spreads. Beyond these, every location carries dozens of small stories that hand the player a route to the target.

The funny scenes are mostly in disguises. At the motor race the lead can walk round as the race's giant mascot, a costumed fox, and kill a target in it. On the suburban street he can dress as a lawn sprinkler repairman or a party clown and wander between neat lawns and nosy neighbours. The gravest scene in the first stretch is at the riverside hotel, where the lead finds the lawyer's proof of how the girl's death was covered up, and can kill the singer in the suite where she died.

## The turn

At the close of the first stretch, the go-between sits down with the handler and offers her a trade: the circle will give her the hidden client's identity in exchange for the bureau's help, and with it the lead's own past. She looks at a file that shows the hidden client and the lead as young men in the same laboratory.

The second turn comes when the lead captures the go-between on the private island. To save himself, the go-between names the three partners. The handler, the lead and the hidden client then break with the bureau, which they now know was founded with the circle's money, and set out to kill the partners.

## The last act

The last act opens in the world's tallest tower, in an invented gulf emirate, where the old industrialist and the banker meet in a penthouse amid a lavish opening. The lead parachutes onto the roof, kills both, and slips out in a falling storm. The woman partner has fled after discovering the go-between was stealing the circle's funds. While the lead works, the go-between escapes from the hidden client's custody and calls the handler, promising to build a new circle.

The lead tracks the woman partner to her family's manor in the moors of an invented northern kingdom, where her brother has been found dead. She has hired a detective to prove it was not suicide. The lead takes the detective's place or works around him, kills her, and takes the circle's file on the go-between. As he leaves, the go-between's mercenaries close in on the hidden client's hideout. The lead walks out disguised as one of them. The hidden client, pinned down, shoots himself rather than be captured and expose the lead. The go-between's men take the handler from her office. The file proves useless.

Alone with the hacker, the lead reaches a riverside capital in an invented eastern federation, where twelve bureau agents hunt the hacker. In a huge night club, the lead must find and kill five of them among the dancers, each one an agent with the lead's training. Then he enters the bureau's data centre, buried in a rain-soaked megacity in an invented southern republic, kills its two supervisors to pass a biometric lock, and publishes the bureau's contracts to the public. The bureau falls. He is now a hunted man without an employer.

The handler returns. The go-between has given her a file proving the lead killed her parents years ago, a car bombing ordered by the circle while the lead was still conditioned. He has offered her his own seat in exchange for proof of faith. She calls the lead to a plantation estate in an invented southern republic, a circle gathering, and tells him to kill two rivals there: a grand old planter and a woman broker of secrets. He does. At sunset in the olive grove she has him surrounded, freezes him with a nerve agent, tells him why, and hands him to the guards. The player has no choice here.

The lead wakes inside a dream of the poison. The handler accuses him; a figure of the hidden client leads him through a graveyard of his own victims and tells him the handler has every right to her anger. He wakes in a locked compartment on the go-between's armoured private train climbing through snowbound mountains towards the laboratory. The go-between plans to wipe his mind and make him a tool again. The handler's poisoning was a ruse; she has made him a weapon delivered to the one man no one could reach. The lead prises the door open with a crowbar and fights or sneaks forward, carriage by carriage, through the circle's guards and lesser members, to the last car. The go-between is waiting there with the serum that wipes memory.

## The endings

There are two endings, decided in the last carriage. The player picks up the syringe of serum.

If the player uses it on the go-between at once, the lead injects him. The go-between, who has boasted that he will at least die knowing who he is, loses his memory and is left, empty, as the lead stops the train and walks away into the snow at sunset. The handler takes the go-between's place at the head of the circle and takes it apart from within: its members resign, its banks give up their holdings, and she burns the order for her parents' killing. A year later the lead, on a rooftop in a hot city, takes a call from her on a satellite phone. He tells her he will stay a killer, now of the powerful people who believe they cannot be touched, and they resume work as killer and handler, freelance and on their own terms. His last line is that he is glad to be back.

If the player holds the syringe and waits, the go-between speaks to him about the freedom of having no choice and no memory, and offers him that freedom. A new option appears: the lead injects himself. He falls. The go-between promises to take care of him. The last scene is a padded white cell behind an iron door, the lead waking with nothing, and the go-between's voice over a speaker telling him to wake up, his friend. Nothing more is shown; the handler's story is left unresolved, and the lead has gone back to where he began.
