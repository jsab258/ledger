## What it is about

Porto Velasco is a crowded, humid harbour city of some seven million on the Atlantic coast of an invented South American republic, a former colonial port built over an island and a strip of mainland, with steep streets, night markets, container docks and towers. Around 2010 its streets are shared between the police and the Brotherhood of the Lantern, an old sworn society with oaths, ranks, district bosses and a chairman, and a rival syndicate from across the border that is pushing into Lantern ground.

The lead is a police officer who grew up in Porto Velasco, left as a boy for a city on another continent, served in its police, and has now been sent home to go undercover inside the Brotherhood. Two senior officers run him: a handler who meets him in safe flats and a superintendent with ambitions who wants the Brotherhood's leaders in prison. He gets in through a childhood friend, wins a place in one district gang, and is soon trusted by men he likes and is meant to destroy.

The question the story puts is whether the lead can stay a policeman while living as a sworn brother: whose side he is on when the two demand different things, and what his loyalty to each costs him and the people round him.

## The lead

The lead is in his late twenties, a fighter trained in a hand-to-hand art, quick to anger and proud. He left the city as a teenager with his mother and his younger sister. The sister fell in with a Lantern man who got her using heroin and sold her body, and she later died of an overdose. Abroad, the lead's violence after her death is part of why his old force was glad to lend him out. He carries that history into the job and keeps it from the gang.

In play the player is the lead throughout. The hands are busy fighting unarmed with counters, grapples and holds, and with throws into the scenery; chasing on foot over rooftops and through markets; driving cars and motorbikes, often leaping from one moving vehicle to another; shooting in slowed-down moments; and doing small police jobs such as picking locks, planting bugs and cracking phones. Two scores run beside the story. A police score falls when he harms civilians or wrecks property during a police mission. A gang score rises when he fights hard and with style. Each unlocks its own skills.

## The people who matter

The handler is a careful senior officer who worries the lead is getting too close to the gang and tells him so. The superintendent runs the operation from above, wants results he can show, and speaks of the lead as a tool.

The childhood friend is a low-ranked hanger-on in the Brotherhood who wants to be seen as important, introduces the lead to the gang, and over time grows tired of the life and wants a quiet one with his girlfriend.

The district boss runs one of the Brotherhood's gangs from a family restaurant, takes the lead in, and treats him as a brother. He is about to marry. His mother owns the restaurant, speaks only the old local dialect, distrusts the lead at first and is fiercely devoted to her son.

The chairman is an old, ill man who holds the Brotherhood together. Under him stand rival district bosses. One, a cheerful and ruthless boss from the northern districts known by his grin, wants the chair. Another, a lean, loud boss from the bay district, is at war with the lead's own boss and is the man who hooked the lead's sister. The chairman's protégée, a calm woman who rose through the ranks, is the grinning boss's main rival for the chair.

There are also a woman police inspector who works ordinary crime and lets the lead help with her cases, a torturer who works for the grinning boss, and a gunman with a rodent's nickname who does dirty jobs for hire.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The game opens at night on the container docks. A drug deal goes wrong, police arrive, and the lead runs with the dealers through the stacks and over the water before he is caught and beaten into a van. In the holding cells he meets his childhood friend, who is pleased to see him and offers to introduce him to his gang once they are out. Only then does the player learn the arrest was staged: the handler and the superintendent take the lead out for a quiet talk, set out his task, and send him back in.

Once released, the lead is taken to the restaurant to meet the district boss, who puts him to work at once. The first jobs teach the hands: a market brawl, collecting protection money from stallholders, a foot chase through a night market after a man who owes the gang, and driving.

The scene players retell comes soon after: the initiation. The lead and his friend are brought before the Brotherhood's elders in a candlelit room, swear an oath in which a traitor is promised a death that lasts for days, alone and unseen, and are then sent to kill a man to prove themselves. The target is a former drug dealer who sold to schoolchildren. The lead fights him; while they struggle, the friend shoots the man in the back of the head. The friend is sick about it afterwards. The lead, who came to arrest such men, now stands beside a killing he took part in, and the handler hears of it.

## The middle

The middle runs for roughly half the story and is filled by the district boss's war with the bay district's boss. Missions repeat a pattern: the boss names a job, the lead drives across the city, fights his way through a club, a building site, a fish market or a boat, and wins back a piece of ground or punishes a rival. Some jobs are robberies, such as a heist on the docks that ends with the friend arrested; the lead asks the superintendent to have him released, and the superintendent does it to keep the lead's cover. Between gang jobs the handler sends the lead on police work: he plants bugs, cracks phones and picks locks on gang ground and passes on what he finds, so that raids hurt the rivals without exposing him. The handler warns him more than once that he is enjoying the gang too much. What changes over the stretch is the lead's standing: the boss trusts him more, the boss's mother comes round to him, and he meets the chairman and the other district bosses. The bay district's boss sneers at him at their first meeting and speaks crudely of a girl who sounds like the lead's dead sister; the lead keeps his temper.

Beside the story the city is open, and there is a good deal of optional story: favours for strangers, street races, fight clubs, collecting shrines and lock boxes, singing contests, and a run of dates.

The first side story is the inspector's cases. Through her, the lead takes on drug busts: he beats the dealers' lookouts in a district, hacks the street cameras, watches the footage to spot the real dealer, and calls in the arrest. Her longer cases run as investigations; in one, a series of murders with organs removed, she suspects a surgeon working for the cross-border syndicate, and the lead's legwork proves her right.

The second side story is the dates. The lead takes several women out, each in a short scene or two: among them a foreign tourist he shows round the city. Each one, once dated, marks something on his map. One of them, a sharp young woman, later finds out he has been seeing others and confronts him; she breaks it off, and only after that can he date the next woman on the list.

One scene meant to be funny comes during a sneaking mission at a building site: three fighting roosters kept there turn on the lead, take an absurd amount of punishment and chase him off the ground. Another comes when the lead, needing to get past a worker, simply tells him the whole plan out loud and is waved through.

## The turn

The district boss marries in a rooftop banquet at a hillside hotel. The lead is there as a trusted brother, the chairman is there, the boss's mother is there. Gunmen dressed as members of the cross-border syndicate storm the reception. They shoot the bride and the groom in front of the guests and wound the chairman. The lead cannot save the couple. He gets the chairman out under fire and takes him for treatment.

This is one of the gravest scenes in the game, and it changes the lead's place. The chairman, grateful, makes the lead the new boss of the dead man's district gang. The superintendent sees a chance to finish the operation and wants him out; the lead refuses, partly for revenge and partly because he fears what the grinning boss will do with the Brotherhood if he leaves.

## The last act

The lead hunts the killers. He finds the gunman with the rodent's nickname, beats him, and learns the attack was not the cross-border syndicate's work: the bay district's boss hired the gunmen and dressed them as foreigners, with the grinning boss behind him. The lead tracks the bay boss down, beats him nearly to death, and delivers him to the restaurant. There the dead groom's mother forces him to eat part of the gunman's body, which she has cooked, and then kills him with her butcher's knife. This is the other gravest scene, and the lead lets it happen. The man who ruined his sister is gone, and the lead has handed him to the Brotherhood's justice rather than the court's.

The chairman dies in hospital. Now the chair is open, and the lead backs the chairman's protégée against the grinning boss in the coming election. He fights the grinning boss's men across the city, and the handler's warnings grow sharper. The superintendent, wanting the operation closed his own way, leaks the lead's real identity to the grinning boss, who plans to expose him at the election and use him to sink the protégée.

The friend, who wants out, tells the lead he will explain himself to the elders at the election and ask to leave. The lead offers to go with him; the friend says no. Later the friend sends him a text saying he has found something to show him. The lead drives to an alley and finds his friend hanging from scaffolding, cut open. As he tries to cut him down, someone strikes him from behind. He wakes strapped to a chair in front of the grinning boss's torturer, who works on him with pliers and a soldering iron while the vote goes ahead elsewhere. The lead breaks his straps, kills the torturer's men and then the torturer, and takes the torturer's car.

He drives to the grinning boss's compound, fights through the guards with a rifle, kills the boss's long-haired bodyguard, and reaches the boss's office in a restaurant above the water. The boss shoots him in the shoulder and runs for a speedboat. The lead leaps aboard, they fight for the wheel, and the boat runs into a fuel barge and burns. Both men are thrown into the water and come ashore at a fish wharf, where the boss tells him that the superintendent gave him away. They fight hand to hand; the wounded lead must turn the boss's blows until he can take him down, and he kills him by forcing him into a fish-processing grinder. The police then arrive and arrest the lead, as they did on the first night.

## The endings

There is one ending, and nothing the player does in the story changes it. After the grinning boss's death the lead is praised for taking down so many senior Brotherhood men, but the handler tells him the superintendent has been moved beyond his reach.

Then a packet arrives from the protégée, now chairman. Inside is a recording of the superintendent with the old chairman the day before he died. In it, the old chairman has been handing the superintendent three senior Brotherhood figures to build his career; to keep this hidden, the superintendent tampered with the old man's life support and killed him. The lead brings the recording forward, and the superintendent is arrested.

In an interview room the superintendent defends himself and expects to talk his way out. The lead tells him people get what they deserve, and lets him know that he has already told the Brotherhood men in prison what the superintendent did. The superintendent understands what waits for him inside.

In a car across the city, the new chairman talks with two of her elders. They know the lead was a policeman. She says his loyalty is to her and to the Brotherhood, and tells them to let him live.

The last scene is quiet. The lead sits with the inspector in a park and thanks her for letting him work her cases, which he says kept him sane. He tells her he may stay in Porto Velasco for a while. He returns to the police, still alive, with the Brotherhood's new chairman choosing to leave him be.
