## What it is about

Three criminals share a large city on the warm coast of an invented southern republic and the dry country inland. One is a retired bank robber living under a false name in a big house with a family that no longer respects him. One is a young repo man from the poor west of the city who wants out of street crime. One is a violent drug maker in a desert caravan who has not forgotten the robber's old crew. A planner who works from his flat, two government agents and a billionaire push the three together and into a run of robberies that builds towards one enormous job on a store of gold. The story is a satire of the republic's money, fame and law enforcement, and puts one question: can three men with a buried past and no reason to trust each other pull off the last score and walk away.

## The lead

There are three leads, and the player is each of them in turn. The retired robber is a middle-aged father. His wife has an affair, his daughter chases fame, his son lies on a sofa playing games, and he sees a therapist he cannot be honest with. The sources give his want as boredom with retirement: he craves the thrill of crime, wants to be a major player again, and wants his family to respect him, though his behaviour works against that. The young repo man works with his loud friend for a crooked car dealer. He wants to leave the gang life he sees as destructive and to be a better kind of criminal, with wealth and status from organised crime in place of street work. The old partner runs a drug and gun business from the desert. The sources give his want as chaos and destruction for their own sake, with money a bonus, an ambition to expand his business, and a deep grudge against the retired robber.

The player controls one at a time and can change to another at nearly any moment outside missions. Many missions use two or three of them together, and in those the player can switch mid-action, for example to fly the helicopter as one man and shoot from it as another. Each has a power that works when a meter fills. The retired robber steadies his aim in a gunfight, the young repo man sharpens his reflexes at the wheel, and the old partner's rage makes him hit harder and take less harm.

## The people who matter

The retired robber's wife leaves him after he attacks her fitness instructor, and she returns later. His daughter wants to be on a television dance contest, and his son gets into trouble and is later kidnapped.

The young repo man's friend is a loud gang member who keeps pulling him back to the street. The car dealer who employs them is a scammer.

The old partner has a nervous companion in his caravan and a biker gang who are first his rivals and then his victims.

The planner is a bitter, housebound gadget and heist expert who organises every big job. The first government agent helped fake the retired robber's death, takes a bribe and then calls in favours. The second is a loud, ambitious officer who forces jobs on the leads to damage a rival agency. A billionaire who co-owns a private security army and has a film studio uses the crew for car thefts and does not pay. The drug lord demands money from the retired robber.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The game opens with a bank robbery in an ice-bound mining town in the far south, ten years before the present. The player is, in turn, the retired robber and the old partner, a crew of three plus a driver who dies at once. The player drives, shoots and holds off police in the sleet. The job goes wrong. A sniper hits the retired robber and the third crewman, and the old partner flees across the mine tailings while the other two are left behind.

The retired robber, officially dead, is carried off under a sheet, and the sniper signs the coroner's papers. The story jumps to the present, where the retired robber lives in the city under a changed name on a deal with the first agent.

The young repo man repossesses vehicles, and comes for the son's motorbike while the retired robber waits in the garage. The retired robber has him ride the bike through the dealer's showroom window and beats the dealer, and the jobless young man asks to be taught. They fall in together after thieves drive off with the family's motor caravan with the son aboard, and the two chase it and pull him off. The robber then finds his wife with her swimming coach and, with the young man, drives a digger through the house the coach is hiding in, which belongs to the drug lord's mistress. The drug lord demands two and a half million. The planner organises a jewellery store robbery to pay it. The old partner sees the robbery on the news, realises his friend is alive, and wipes out a biker gang and a family of drug rivals before going to the city.

## The middle

The middle is most of the game and is built from a repeating cycle. A heist is planned, its set-up missions are played one after another, the heist itself is played, and the money comes in. Between them come work forced on the leads, family scenes, the old partner's desert business and optional missions. The main list runs to about seventy entries. Optional missions for odd strangers run to nearly sixty more for the three leads, in chains of one to eight.

The heists run in order of size, the jewellery store job first. The player picks one of two approaches, quiet with sleeping gas or loud and violent, and a driver, a gunman and a computer expert, each taking a share. Better crew cost more and make fewer mistakes. One source says a cheap crew member who survives rises in skill and is better by the last heist, while the cheapest gunman here cannot be used again. The second is a raid on a container port, by boat or by lorry, to steal a secret device from the private army. It pays nothing, and the leads return the device. The third is a small-town bank robbery with no choice of approach, where a cheap gunman means a companion dies though the player still pays him. The fourth is a break-in at the headquarters of the government bureau, by abseiling from the roof or disguised as an ambulance crew. The fifth, saved for the end, is the gold store. In these sources the choices change the money and the difficulty, and nothing in the story afterwards.

The agents force much of the work. One mission puts the old partner in a cold store with a prisoner the agents believe is a spy. The player picks the methods in any order, from a hammer to an ice bath to a cattle prod, and revives the man with an injection if his heart stops. Each session gives a clue to a man the retired robber then finds at a rooftop wedding party and kills. The agent orders the prisoner killed. The old partner lets him go, tells him to leave the country for good, and says that pain buys only the answers the torturer wants. The young repo man is sent to assassinate businessmen so the billionaire can profit on the stock market. In a street deal the old partner finds the drugs are bags of flour, just before the gang attacks. In the fitness scene the retired robber, arguing with his wife, charges the instructor and ends in the garden fountain, and she announces the family is leaving.

Two optional stories show the rest. In one, the retired robber answers a self-help movement's leaflet, then pays larger and larger donations, delivers cars, walks the desert with a metal detector collecting trinkets, buys white tunics, sleeps in them and walks barefoot across the salt flats. At the end the leader asks for a final large donation, and the player can hand it over and get a rusted motor launch, or keep about two million and receive angry calls. In the other, the old partner takes jobs from an odd pair who steal pop stars' belongings, which leads to a kidnapped singer. In the last mission he drives the man in the boot to a level crossing, and the player decides whether to let him out for a little cash or park on the line and let a train hit the car. A third chain has the young repo man ride a photographer's scooter beside a car and film a politician and his mistress at a spa, until the couple crash chasing them.

The family scenes run through all of it. The retired robber reunites the family and rescues his daughter from a stalker and his son from kidnappers. The old partner kidnaps the drug lord's wife and takes her into the desert, where the two form a bond.

## The turn

The old partner goes to the far south and learns that the grave of the retired robber holds the body of the crewman he believed to be in prison. The heist was not an accident. The retired robber had made a deal with the first agent to be shot, to vanish with a new name and pay the agent a bribe, and it cost the crewman his life.

The two men draw guns on each other. Gangsters who mistake the retired robber for the old partner's lover attack, and the old partner sabotages his friend's car and later refuses to help. The robber is taken, and the young repo man frees him in a warehouse. Later both bureaus and the private army attack the two older men together, and in that fight they make an uneasy peace.

## The last act

After the break-in at the government bureau, the billionaire cancels the film for the insurance, plans to turn the studio into flats, and sends the private army after the retired robber's family. The player fights through the retired robber's house to free them. A lawyer holds the only copy of the film, and the retired robber chases her to the cargo docks, where a falling container crushes her. The film is saved, and the premiere follows. The second agent tells the young repo man to kill the old partner, who is a liability. The crew then plans the big job.

The final heist is the gold store. The player picks a quiet approach, cutting the power and driving armoured vans out through a storm drain, or a loud one, with a cargo train and a gunship, and picks the crew accordingly. The take is close to two hundred million. The player plays each stage, switching between the three, and it succeeds.

The three are rich and briefly free. The billionaire and the second agent each go to the young repo man. The billionaire wants the retired robber dead, because the robber is the cause of his losses, and the agent wants the old partner dead. The player is the young repo man at the decision.

## The endings

There are three endings, decided by the young repo man's choice, which is permanent. For the third he must call the planner before the mission marker appears.

In the first, he kills the old partner, on the billionaire's request. He lures him to a meeting and draws a gun, and the partner flees in a truck. The retired robber rams it over the lip of a flooded quarry. The old partner, on its roof, calls them traitors and tells the young man to get it done, and one of them shoots him and the truck sinks with him. The last image is the two surviving men parting, the retired robber needing time away, and the player then controls the two and shares out the dead man's cut. Both the billionaire and the agent live.

In the second, he kills the retired robber, on the agent's request. The old partner refuses to help him. The young man meets the retired robber, who takes a call from his wife about their daughter starting college, and chases him to a dam. After a fight he pushes the robber off the spillway wall, then grabs him, and the player is asked to pull him up or let go. The robber dies either way. The young man then phones his friend to make amends. The old partner disowns him, the dead man's wife curses him, the son rings both men, and the player then controls the young man and the old partner, with the dead man's cut going to his family.

In the third, he refuses both and calls the planner, who lures the agents and the private army into an ambush at a disused shipyard with a rumour about the gold. The three survive and split up to hunt their enemies. The old partner kills the second agent, the retired robber kills the gang member who betrayed the young man's friend, and the young man kills a crime boss who threatened them. The old partner knocks the billionaire out at his mansion and puts him in a car boot, and the three drop the car from a dock crane into the deep harbour. All three live and keep their shares. The last scene is the three retired from crime, still friends, awkwardly. The planner sends each of them his untouched cut.
