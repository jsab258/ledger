# Fifteen principles of an exceptional game story

23 November 2026. Merged from the five anatomies in anatomy/ (RDR2, KCD2, GTA V, GTA IV, Sleeping Dogs), the craft theory in THEORY.md and the players' own words in the Steam story study (production/research/lane/STORY-PATTERNS-STEAM-2026-11-20.md). Every claim points to the file holding its sources; nothing here is new evidence. Players name most: characters (46% of coded story reviews), a weak or forgettable story (20%, the largest complaint), writing (18%), moments that hit (9%), missions on rails (8%), weak endings (6%), humour (6%), endings that land (5%), turns (5%).

Each principle gives what it asks, what the best game does, and a test for a written plan. A test is passed, half passed or failed, and the reader quotes the plan's line that shows it. Where a test gives no half mark, half means all conditions but one are met. "Week" means the plan's own unit if it uses another. A plan can state what a shipped game must earn, so the reader looks for the play that makes the player feel a thing, not a sentence saying he will.

## The lead

### P1. A lead the player wants to be, with a loud want and a buried need

Asks: in the first hour the lead wants something plain, active and a little wrong, that the player can say in one sentence and chase with his hands; underneath is a need the lead does not see, and the story forces the two apart.

Best: RDR2. Arthur wants the gang to hold and Dutch to be right; he needs a way to be good without Dutch; the Downes debt, Dutch's drift and the diagnosis force it in steps, and the player's conduct feeds his journal (anatomy/RDR2.md, sections 2 and 6). GTA IV's Niko wants the man who betrayed his unit beneath a claimed fresh start (anatomy/GTAIV.md, section 2). KCD2 gives Henry a job somebody else set and lets events take it away (anatomy/KCD2.md, sections 2 and 10).

Test: (a) the want stated in one sentence a player would say, live by the end of the first hour; (b) it drives what the hands do in the first third, and a middle-week job moves it forward (GTA IV lets hours pass with no progress; anatomy/GTAIV.md, section 10); (c) a named event, at a named week, breaks it; (d) what the lead does differently after, in play. Pass all four; half three; fail two or fewer. Fail outright if the lead's want is to avoid something (to owe nobody, to be nobody's passenger) rather than to get something.

### P2. The lead is the loudest person in his own story

Asks: the heaviest scenes happen to the lead and are caused by him, not watched by him. He has a temper, a skill, a habit people laugh at, and he takes risks with his own body, liberty and standing.

Best: RDR2 (Arthur's illness is in his body, his choices decide which death); GTA V (three leads who each start trouble: Michael's debt to Madrazo, Trevor's appetite for chaos, Franklin's wish to rise; anatomy/GTAV.md, section 2). A Sleeping Dogs player praises a lead who "is not an idiot" (the Steam study, Sleeping Dogs DE). The v3 readers marked our leads down for this: "withdrawn by design", "less vivid than the people round him" (v3/measure/RESULT-P2.md).

Test: take the plan's five heaviest scenes (climax first, then deaths, betrayals, arrests, injuries) and count those where the lead acts and is himself at risk. Pass at four or five; half at three; fail at two or fewer. A pass also needs one deed in week one that only he would do and that another person in the plan answers; without it, drop one grade.

## The bond and the people

### P3. One bond at the centre, built in the hands, through trust, strain, breach and a last word

Asks: one relationship carries the story to the last scene, with a beat in nearly every chapter, through trust, strain in small betrayals, a breach the player feels and a settled last scene. The player comes to love the other person by doing things with him (jobs, rides and drives with talk, rescues, small optional moments); cutscenes are kept for turns.

Best: RDR2's Arthur and Dutch, loyalty (chapters 1 to 2), doubt (3), strain (4), breach (5 and 6), each beat at a named mission, built on rides "talking all the while" and camp (anatomy/RDR2.md, sections 3, 4 and 10; THEORY.md, Part B). GTA IV's Niko and Roman, every plot orbiting it, the depot's burning the first fall, Roman's possible death the last choice's price; its calls tired players when they interrupted (anatomy/GTAIV.md, sections 3, 4 and 10). KCD2's Henry and Hans, in trouble together on every leg, rescuing and rescued (anatomy/KCD2.md, section 10). Against: GTA V's Michael and Trevor, strain told more than played (anatomy/GTAV.md, section 3).
Test: list the bond's scenes week by week, each marked shared action, shared drive, standing talk or cutscene. (a) A beat in at least five weeks of seven; (b) at least two strain beats at named weeks before the breach, and the lead acts in the breach rather than watching it; (c) a last scene between the two; (d) at least two thirds of the scenes are shared action or drives, and no invitation the player turns down costs him the bond. Pass all four; half three; fail two or fewer. Fail outright if the central person dies or leaves before the last act with nothing taking up the bond's last word.

### P4. Few people, each wanting his own thing and doing one deed only he would do

Asks: three or four carried people, each with his own want, one unforgettable deed and a voice of his own; the rest light. Too many blur.

Best: RDR2's gang (Hosea, Sadie, Charles each with a turn of their own; anatomy/RDR2.md, section 3) and GTA V's cast; players praise the people in 83%, 73% and 72% of GTA V, RDR2 and GTA IV story reviews (the Steam study). GTA IV players complain of too many characters and a story that drops the early ones (anatomy/GTAIV.md, section 9).

Test: name each carried person's want for himself and his one deed; pass if there are three to five carried people, the plan names a scene where each one's want pulls against the lead's, and each deed is a single act that could be filmed (who does what, where), not a trait ("loyal", "funny"). Half if one person misses; fail if two miss or more than five people are carried.

## Opposition and stakes

### P5. An opponent close to the lead, who gains ground in view and is faced in person

Asks: the opponent wants what the lead wants or protects, sits inside or beside his circle, wins visibly, and is met face to face at the end.

Best: RDR2's Micah, Arthur's partner on early jobs, rising in camp, replacing him, exposed and fought (anatomy/RDR2.md, section 3). Sleeping Dogs' police handler, the lead's own side as the threat, though players saw the betrayal coming (anatomy/SLEEPING-DOGS.md, sections 3 and 10). Against: GTA V's FIB, arriving hours in (anatomy/GTAV.md, section 10). Truby: the best opponent competes for the same goal (THEORY.md, Part A).

Test: (a) the opponent is in the first week; (b) the plan names, for each week, one gain of his that the player sees in play; (c) the opponent wants the same thing the lead wants; (d) a meeting in person in the last act that the lead can win or lose. Pass all four; half three; fail two or fewer.

### P6. Stakes climb in clear steps, up to the lead's own body, liberty and soul

Asks: what can be lost rises act by act: money, then safety, then a person, then a home or a way of life, then the lead's body or freedom, then what he is.

Best: RDR2's ladder from cash and shelter through Jack, Hosea, the gang and Arthur's body to his soul (anatomy/RDR2.md, section 5); GTA IV's from Roman's debt to the burned depot to a death at the wedding (anatomy/GTAIV.md, section 5). The v3 readers: "small stakes with no danger a player feels" held all three pages under 8 (v3/measure/RESULT-P2.md).

Test: write the ladder by week. Pass if each act sits a rung higher than the last, at least one rung threatens the lead's body or liberty in a playable scene, and something the player has built (the office, the crew, a standing) can be taken from him. Fail if the largest stake in the middle weeks is a sum of money or a mark.

### P7. The lead's own early deed comes back as the turn

Asks: something the player did with his own hands early, often a routine job, returns later as the cause of the worst thing. The story's question becomes something the lead carries.

Best: RDR2's Downes debt: a collection job in chapter 2, a cough in the face, tuberculosis in chapter 6, unavoidable even for a gentle player (anatomy/RDR2.md, sections 2 and 7). GTA V's 2004 betrayal, planted in the first hour and paid off slowly (anatomy/GTAV.md, sections 1 and 10).
Test: name the early deed, its week, the player's hands in it, and the later scene it causes. Pass if the deed is ordinary play (a job, a collection, a fare) in the first two weeks and its return changes the lead's goal, body or standing for the rest of the game; half if the return only costs money or a scene.

## Shape and momentum

### P8. A middle that climbs: every chapter a different shape, each with a loud set piece

Asks: no repeating weekly loop; each chapter changes the ground (place, kind of job, danger) and has a big scene the player retells. The midpoint changes what play is.

Best: RDR2 moves camp each chapter; its chapter 4 bank job (Hosea's death) changes the game; Guarma strips the gang (anatomy/RDR2.md, section 1). GTA V's heists give the middle a rhythm of set-up and execution (anatomy/GTAV.md, section 5). Against: GTA IV's slow middle (5 of 25 story reviews; anatomy/GTAIV.md, section 9). Baldur's Gate 3's writer made act two deliberately more contained (THEORY.md, Part B). The v3 readers: "a quiet repeating week" in all three pages (v3/measure/RESULT-P2.md).

Test: give each week's shape in one line: its place, its kind of job, its main danger. Pass if no two weeks match on all three, each week has a named set piece with danger to the lead, and an event between 40 and 60 per cent of the way through changes what the player does afterwards. Fail if the plan describes the middle as a cycle (a weekly funeral, a nightly fare, a daily round).

### P9. The player always knows what he wants next, and the story makes him want it

Asks: clarity and pull. At any hour the player can say what he is trying to do and why it matters; information is withheld so he wants the next beat, never so he is lost.

Best: Sasko's rule from The Witcher 3 (withhold to create want; cut recap; key information in focused moments; THEORY.md, Part B); Druckmann: every section moves the story to its end (THEORY.md, Part B). The v3 readers called The Knowledge "dense, intricate and hard to follow" (v3/measure/RESULT-P2.md).

Test: write one sentence per week: the player's aim and what he loses if he fails. Pass if the reader can write every sentence from that week's part of the plan alone, without first explaining a count, tally or rule, and each aim follows from the last; half if one week fails; fail if two or more do.

### P10. A last act as strong as the middle, paying off what was planted

Asks: the last act is the loudest stretch: a reckoning with the opponent and the bond, an irreversible change answering the story's first question, nothing the player was not shown earlier. An ending choice is taught by smaller choices first.

Best: RDR2's last chapter holds the diagnosis, Micah's exposure and the money-or-John choice, honour deciding which death (anatomy/RDR2.md, sections 1 and 5); an ending that landed, 5.2% of its script-coded reviews against GTA V's 1.6% (section 9). Against: GTA V's unwarned closing choice and anticlimax (anatomy/GTAV.md, sections 6 and 10); Sleeping Dogs' "second half falls apart" (anatomy/SLEEPING-DOGS.md, section 10). Craft: the climax is the irreversible change answering the inciting incident's question; a payoff is only as good as its setup (THEORY.md, Part A).

Test: (a) the last act holds one of the plan's three heaviest scenes; (b) the climax changes something for good and answers a question raised in week one; (c) every person, object or skill the climax uses appears two weeks earlier or more; (d) an ending choice has two earlier choices of its kind. Pass all four; half three; fail two or fewer.

## Theme and world

### P11. One question, asked by the systems, answered by the ending, never said aloud

Asks: the theme is a question; the play keeps score of the answer (honour, loyalty bars, what is owed); the ending answers it with a deed; no speech explains it.

Best: RDR2's honour as a running tally feeding the journal, the last dream and Micah's fight (anatomy/RDR2.md, section 6); Sleeping Dogs' cop and triad bars, which name the theme but "barely push back" (anatomy/SLEEPING-DOGS.md, section 10); Disco Elysium's mechanics that speak about poverty (THEORY.md, Part B).

Test: (a) the question in one line; (b) the system that keeps its score, which the player can see change; (c) the ending scene that answers it by a deed; (d) no character states the theme. Pass all four.

### P12. A world that is ending, shown in play

Asks: the setting is losing something real and the player sees it go (places close, people leave, the law closes in), and the period's daily rules block or open the lead's way.

Best: RDR2's camp moved again and again, the law closing, a city with trams, a tribe being moved (anatomy/RDR2.md, section 6); KCD2's period rules (clothes, reading, reputation, the law) deciding which doors open (anatomy/KCD2.md, section 6); GTA IV's immigrant dream against the city (anatomy/GTAIV.md, section 6).

Test: name, week by week, what the player sees ending without anyone saying it, and one way the period's rules change what the player can do. Pass if every week has one and the ending scene shows that loss complete; half if one or two weeks lack it.

## Feeling

### P13. Losses prepared for hours, touched by the player's hands

Asks: the moments players remember are losses of people they spent hours with, prepared by errands and jobs, tied to something the player did or chose.

Best: RDR2's Hosea and Arthur (moments that hit: 8 of 26 RDR2 story reviews, no other game above 2); Sleeping Dogs' wedding built from errands the player ran for the man who dies (anatomy/SLEEPING-DOGS.md, section 10); GTA IV's last choice costing Roman or Kate (anatomy/GTAIV.md, section 1).

Test: for each loss, the hours of shared play before it, the player's part in it, and who is changed for good after. Pass if the main loss has at least a third of the game's hours of shared play behind it and the player's choices touch it.

### P14. Comedy from who people are, in the main line, every chapter, steady beside the violence

Asks: humour from character that recurs and pays off, in the main missions, not only side ones; tone held so jokes and brutality do not read as accidents (anatomy/SLEEPING-DOGS.md, section 10).

Best: GTA V (humour in 4 of 18 story reviews; anatomy/GTAV.md, sections 8 and 9); Houser: comedy needs narrative and heart under it (THEORY.md, Part B); RDR2's camp bickering and Arthur's dry replies on the ride (anatomy/RDR2.md, section 8); KCD2's self-deprecating period humour (anatomy/KCD2.md, section 8). LEDGER's canon asks for dry British wit with seaside-postcard smut, sparingly.

Test: count main-line scenes per week whose plan line names the joke, and running jokes with a named pay-off. Pass at one or more such scenes in every week's main line and at least two running jokes that pay off in the last act; half if one week lacks a comic scene or only one running joke pays off.

## Play and consequence

### P15. Side stories and choices that come back and change the ending

Asks: side stories answer the lead's question in small ways and leave something behind in the world; choices return later as different scenes, and the ending is read from the player's record, not a last button. And the story never forces the wrong deed: it tempts.

Best: KCD2 for choices and side stories (choices that change what happens 7.1% of its reviews against RDR2's 1.7%; side stories 13.7% against 4.1%; anatomy/KCD2.md, section 9); The Witcher 3's Bloody Baron, a side story that mirrors the lead (THEORY.md, Part B); RDR2's strangers whose thanks Arthur cannot take (anatomy/RDR2.md, section 7). Against: missions on rails (8% of coded reviews; KCD2 5 of 43, RDR2 3 of 26); KCD2's answer is several routes per quest and failure that carries on (anatomy/KCD2.md, section 10).

Test: (a) each side story names its tie to the lead's question and what it leaves behind; (b) at least eight acts of the player's own record return as scenes; (c) the ending is read from the record; (d) no wrong deed is forced; (e) every main job names at least two routes, and failing it moves the story on rather than restarting it. Pass all five; half four; fail three or fewer.

## How to use these

A reader marks each principle 0 (fail), 1 (half) or 2 (pass), quoting the line that shows it, with one sentence on what would raise it. The sum out of 30 is a lead, not a measurement. P1, P2, P3, P5, P6, P8 and P14 weigh most with players and the v3 readers' complaints; a plan failing three of them is not near the bar whatever its total.

Two thresholds in the tests (a third of the hours behind the main loss in P13; eight returning acts in P15) are the team's own settings, not evidence from the games; read them as bars to argue with.
