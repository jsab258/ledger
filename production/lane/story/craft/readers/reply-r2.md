# Story review: three plans for the port-town minicab game

Marks follow each principle's own test. Quotes are verbatim from the plans.

---

## Plan A: *The Burial Club*

| P | Mark | Quote | To raise it |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 0 | "Tom Nowak steps off the ferry wanting one plain thing: to owe nobody." | Give him a want to *get* something (to make Mickey's office pay, to take the Hook) that the club then blocks. |
| P2 | 0 | "Tom's talk is the player's, so his authored lines are few, flat and short" | Put Tom's body and liberty at the centre of at least four of the heaviest scenes; on the default road he is at another graveside when Ron falls. |
| P3 | 1 | "He changes once. In week four, in the cab, he asks for something. He does not live to change again." | Give the bond a real breach between the two men, and a last scene with Ron alive, not his coffin. |
| P4 | 2 | "He rehearses his father's funeral." | Cut the eight light named people back so the four carried ones stand out. |
| P5 | 2 | "A man in a pale raincoat at the back of Lord Ash's funeral counts the turnout on his fingers." | Make Danny's gains threaten Tom's office and safety, not only the turnout. |
| P6 | 1 | "It is the only score in the game and it is a man's voice." | Replace the middle weeks' top stake (a mark out of ten) with Tom's liberty or body in a playable scene. |
| P7 | 2 | "It is the smallest verb in the game and the turn rests on it." | Tie the stamp, or the week-one collection, to Ron's death as well as to the turn. |
| P8 | 0 | "One funeral a week is the spine, and no two ask the same thing of the hands." | Break the weekly funeral and give each week a different place, job and danger, with a loud set piece. |
| P9 | 1 | "Whether, and how, week three is put right, which can only be done by a deed" | Give week four a single plain aim that follows from week three without explaining the marks and stamps. |
| P10 | 2 | "He's paid up. I checked. Every week since fifty-three, and never a black one. You owe him six men." | Raise the gate scene's danger so the last act is louder than the middle, not only sadder. |
| P11 | 2 | "The one question is the game's own: what does Tom owe the dead man and the living?" | Keep Danny's "spending money on the dead" line from edging into saying the theme. |
| P12 | 2 | "The last ship of the year sails without a registered man having touched her." | Already strong; make one period rule block a crime job, not only a funeral. |
| P13 | 2 | "On every road Ron Kirby dies that Thursday." | Make sure the commonest road puts Tom beside Ron, not paying out a strap. |
| P14 | 2 | "You're still too tall. Get Ada to do the other side." | Set some comedy beside real violence, so the tone is tested rather than kept gentle throughout. |
| P15 | 1 | "Twelve acts of the player's own record, each read in the open on the last Thursday" | Name, for each side story, its tie to the question of what is owed. |

**Total: 20 / 30**

**Three largest lacks**
1. Tom is a passenger in his own story. He wants to owe nobody, speaks in flat lines, is dressed and led by others, and is usually away when the worst thing happens.
2. The middle is a weekly funeral loop scored by an old man's mark. The test names this exact cycle as a fail.
3. There is almost no tension, risk or rise to power. Tom's body and liberty are seldom in danger, and the crime is a side errand to paying for stones.

**Verdict: rethink, 80% sure.** It fails three of the seven heavy principles (P1, P2, P8), which the guide says puts it far from the bar whatever its total. Its premise is a gentle elegy about keeping a promise to the dead. The owner asked for tension, risk and a rise to power, and that does not come from a burial club. Revision could sharpen Tom, but the funeral spine is the structure, and replacing it is a rethink.

**Total: 24 / 30**

**Three largest lacks**
1. Tom is still quieter than the people round him. His risks to body and liberty come late, and the early weeks hold little danger.
2. The loss is a light character with only a few scenes behind him, so it will land less hard than the plan hopes.
3. The middle leans on a nightly fare and deliveries. The crime and the rise to power are small: he is called the Admiral but never really holds the town.

**Verdict: revision, 60% sure.** Its spine works: the opponent sits inside the circle, the lead's early deed returns as the turn, and the stakes climb with a fall the player causes. These are the RDR2 bones the principles reward. The fixes are within its premise: a louder Tom, more danger in weeks one to three, more shared play with Juris, and a nightly fare that is one thread rather than the loop. My doubt is the owner's "rise to power" line: the ship gives Tom standing rather than power, so the crime side would need to grow without breaking the story.

**Total: 20 / 30**

**Three largest lacks**
1. Tom's want is an avoidance, which the test names as a fail.
2. The heaviest scene happens behind a shut door while the player stands outside with nothing to do.
3. It is hard to follow. Kerbs against pen, receipts, badge numbers and a radio audit trail all need explaining, which is the very complaint the v3 readers made of *The Knowledge*.

**Verdict: rethink, 70% sure.** Its bond and its jokes are the warmest of the three, and its endings land. But a story about a man learning to drive and a stranger learning streets is a gentle drama. It does not deliver a crime game of tension, risk and a rise to power, and its central loop of lessons and a morning ride cannot carry that. Fixing P1, P2 and P9 means changing the want, the loss and the structure, which goes past revision.

## A sketch for this game instead (for Plans A and C)

**Want.** Tom wants the Hook. He means to turn Mickey's dying office into the firm that runs the night, so that every fare, every load off the dock and every word on the air passes through his radio. **Need.** To stop treating people as routes and assets, and to learn that power held by fear is owed to nobody.

**Central relationship.** Ron Kirby, laid-off docker, Tom's muscle and partner on every job. Ron wants the old dock code kept: you never sell a man to the law. They rise together, in rides and fights.

**Opponent.** Maureen Jensen, the Widow, Mickey's oldest partner. She backs Tom's rise from week one because she means to own him, and she wants the same thing he wants: the night.

**Shape.**
- **Weeks 1 and 2:** collections, the first runs off the dock, and taking Cammack's rank. Each job is watched by a town that talks.
- **Week 3:** a raid on a bonded warehouse. Tom squeezes an informant for it.
- **Week 4, the midpoint:** Tom takes the Widow's night gate in one heist. Play changes from doing jobs to running crews from the office, and the town now answers to his radio.
- **Week 5:** strain. Tom starts selling rivals to DS Ellis to clear his way, and Ron sees it.
- **Week 6, the loss:** the man Tom squeezed in week one gives Ellis Ron, not Tom. Ron is taken, keeps the code, and says nothing. The Widow's first gun appears.
- **Week 7, the last act:** a night at the dock and the Widow faced in person. Tom can keep his empire by giving Ellis what she wants, or free Ron by handing over the office and his own liberty.

The town's record decides who stands with him at the gate.

## Recognition

None. I do not recognise any of the three as a published game or a known outside project. They read as successive internal drafts from one studio: they share a cast and setting, and Plan C reuses the title *The Knowledge* that the v3 readers already reviewed.
