# Reading of Plans A, B and C

One thing first, because it shapes every mark below. All three plans share two habits.

- **The same twist.** In each, the player's suspicion turns out to be a hidden kindness. Ozols sold the brass to feed his crew. Ron wore the coat to get debtors work. B.W. stood for "black weeks". Every road makes the player wrong and the other person good.
- **The same arc.** Each ends with Tom giving something up: the ship, his pride, the club. None of them is the owner's "rise to power".

P8's fail clause names "a weekly funeral, a nightly fare, a daily round". Those match Plan C, Plan A and Plan B exactly.

**Three largest lacks**
1. Tom is the quietest person in his own story. Ozols, the Widow and even Ron are louder, and Tom rarely risks his own skin.
2. The middle is a nightly loop on one dock. The ship never moves until the last night.
3. The loss falls on a light character the player has only ridden with a few times.

**Revision or rethink: revision, 60% sure.** The bones are RDR2-shaped: a want that is a little wrong, a close opponent who wants the same thing, an early routine deed that returns as the turn, and a bond with a settled last scene. Its faults are fixable inside the premise. Make Tom louder and in danger. Make the "Admiral" weeks a real rise, with the ship's hold carrying the outfit's trade. Break the nightly loop. Move the loss onto Ron. The doubt is the owner's "rise to power": this story is at heart a renunciation, and revision has to make the rise in weeks one to four big enough that giving it up costs something.

**Three largest lacks**
1. Tom's want is to avoid something. The plan builds him as lost, passive and led, which is the opposite of a lead the player wants to be.

**Revision or rethink: rethink, 70% sure.** The bond is lovely, but the premise depends on Tom being a passenger: lost, unable to find his own office, saved by a doorman. P1 fails outright on that, and no polish turns it into tension and a rise to power. The structure is a daily drive and a weekly lesson, building to a driving test. That is gentle where the owner asked for risk. You could keep Ron and the hands, but the story around them has to change at its root.

**Total: 19 / 30**

**Three largest lacks**
1. The want is an avoidance and the lead is never in danger. Tom is a pallbearer, not a man taking risks.
2. The structure is a weekly funeral with a score. That is the named fail, and it never climbs.
3. The tone is elegy and service. It is not tension or a rise, and the central person dies before the last act.

It fails four of the weighted principles (P1, P2, P6 and P8). By the principles' own rule, that puts it far from the bar whatever its total.

**Revision or rethink: rethink, 85% sure.** The writing, comedy and record system are the best of the three, but they serve a different game. This is a gentle, funny elegy about burying the old, and it runs on a weekly funeral loop with no rising danger. The owner asked for "tension, risk and a rise to power, and every job is tense because the town is watching", and nothing here can be revised into that. Keep the stamp, the Man in Black's voice and the turnout as the town's record, but build a new story around them.

## Sketch: what the story should be instead (B and C)

**Want:** to turn Mickey's dead outfit into the firm that runs the Hook's nights by Christmas. **Need:** to learn that what the watching town remembers is a kept word, not fear.

**Central bond:** Ron Kirby, doorman and ex-docker, Tom's partner on every collection, carry and break-in. The bond grows in rides and jobs, and Ron's dry talk carries it.

**Opponent:** Danny Cammack, who once drove car two for Mickey. He walks in during week one asking to be taken on and becomes Tom's best earner. He wants the same thing: the Hook's night. He rises inside the office, week by week, in drivers, keys and loyalty.

**Shape:**
- **Weeks one to three, the rise.** Collections, a gate racket taken off the Widow's men, and the office filling. Every job is witnessed, and witnesses talk.
- **Midpoint, week four.** Tom, Ron and Danny take the Widow's tobacco off the quay and break her. Play turns from taking ground to holding it, with everyone owing Tom.
- **Week five.** In week one, Tom chose how hard to lean on the docker Lee Drewery. Lee is now unpicked and hungry, and he sells the next job's timing. DS Ellis's raid takes Ron, who will not name Tom. Danny tipped Lee towards Ellis.
- **Week six, the loss.** Drivers drift to Danny, the office is searched, and Tom is hunted in his own town. A van clips him on the dock road, which hurts him but does not kill him.

**Last act:** Danny holds the office. Tom wins men back door by door, by what the record says of him. The first gun of the game comes out on the ferry landing at night, and the meeting is face to face. The ending choice has been taught by smaller ones throughout. Tom can give Ellis both Danny and himself to free Ron, or he can keep the firm and leave Ron inside. Either way, Ron's last word is spoken at the prison gate or on the office step.

## Recognition

I recognise none of the three as a published game or a project I know of. They read as sibling drafts of one studio's unreleased project, sharing its canon (Tom, Ron, Sheila, the Widow, Darren).
