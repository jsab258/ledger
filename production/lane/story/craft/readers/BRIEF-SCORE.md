# Brief for the scoring readers (fixed before any reader ran, 23 Nov 2026)

You are a senior story editor who has shipped open-world crime and role-playing games. Below are fifteen principles of an exceptional game story, each with a test, and then three competing story plans (bibles) for the same game, labelled Plan A, Plan B and Plan C. They come from one studio; judge them only on the page. Each plan is a design on paper, not a shipped game: judge what the play it describes would make a player feel, not the intentions it states.

The game, in short: a third-person open-town crime game set in a British port town in autumn 1990. The lead, a stranger, inherits his dead uncle's minicab office, a half-dead criminal outfit and a book of debts. About 15 to 20 hours over seven game weeks; the player mostly drives, collects, does jobs, breaks in and fights; talk is typed by the player and answered in voice. The owner's bar: a story players would rank with Red Dead Redemption 2's. The owner's own words about the game: "an exciting, immersive, novel game"; "what the player feels is tension, risk and a rise to power, and every job is tense because the town is watching."

Do this:

1. For each plan and each principle P1 to P15: a mark of 0 (fail), 1 (half) or 2 (pass) by the principle's own test, a quote of at most 20 words from the plan that shows it, and one sentence on what would raise it. Use a table per plan: principle | mark | quote | to raise it.
2. Each plan's total out of 30.
3. For each plan, its three largest lacks against the principles, in plain words.
4. For each plan: could it reach the owner's bar by revision (keeping its premise, its central relationship and its structure), or does the story need a fundamental rethink? Answer "revision" or "rethink", how sure you are (per cent), and why in three sentences.
5. If you answered rethink for any plan: in at most 300 words, sketch what a story for this same game (same setting, same minicab office, same port town, crime) should be instead: the lead's want and need, the central relationship, the opponent, the shape across seven weeks, the midpoint, the loss and the last act. Keep within these rules: no alcohol and no gambling shown or spoken of; no children anywhere; no sexual content; tobacco is fine; the lead cannot die; a car hurts and never kills; guns rare and late.
6. Last: do you recognise any of these plans as a published game or a project you know of? Name it or say none.

Plain words, British spelling. Mark every principle for every plan; do not skip.
