# Reading of Plans A, B and C

All three plans share one voice, one research file and one instinct: each turns the crime game inside out. In A the lead is driven. In B the lead is collected from. In C the lead gives the prize away. The writing is often very fine. But the owner asked for "tension, risk and a rise to power", and none of the three is built on that. Each is an elegy with crime in the background. Keep that in mind behind the marks.

**Total: 20 / 30**

**Three largest lacks**
1. Tom is a passenger in his own story. His want is to avoid something, and the worst thing is done to Ron while Tom watches. The climax is a driving test he sits as a candidate.
2. There is no rise to power and little danger. What is at stake is dignity, a licence and a head count. The town watches Tom with pity, not menace.
3. The last act is the quietest stretch: 65 minutes, "Choices. None that is new," and an opponent who cannot be fought.

**Verdict: rethink, 70% sure.** The premise, a lost man who will not ask the way and a docker learning to drive, is tender, but it produces quiet and not tension. To bring in risk and a rise to power you would have to change Tom's want, the climax and the opponent's threat, which leaves the bond as the only thing standing. Revision can raise the score, but it cannot reach the owner's bar without losing what the plan is.

The premise is a funeral game, so a weekly ceremony with no bodily danger is built into it. It is a beautiful, sad story, but it is not the game the owner described.

Note on P15: the forced brass run is exactly what makes P7 work. The two principles pull against each other here, and I marked each by its own test.

**Three largest lacks**
1. The main loss falls on a light, optional character. Juris has nowhere near a third of the hours behind him.
2. Tom's own body is rarely at risk, and the arc gives power away. The owner asked for a rise.
3. There are loops and spread: a nightly fare, nine crew, and side stories with no stated tie to the question.

**Verdict: revision, 60% sure.** Its structure already does what RDR2 does: an early ordinary deed that comes back, an opponent who wants the same thing and wins in view, and a last act that pays off what was planted. The fixes fit inside the premise. Put Juris in the cab from week one, make weeks one to four a real climb (the ship as the base of the outfit's night trade), and put Tom's own liberty on the line in weeks five and six. The doubt is whether a story about letting go can satisfy an owner who asked for a rise to power. RDR2 is a fall too, so it can, but only if the climb before it is felt.

## A story for this game instead (for A and B)

**Want:** to sit in Mickey's chair for real, and make the office the firm that runs the Hook's nights by Christmas. **Need:** one person who stays because he chooses to, not because he is owed or afraid.

**Central relationship:** Ron Kirby, Mickey's doorman. He rides every job and teaches Tom who to squeeze and who to spare. He keeps Mickey's two rules: no widows, no old dockers.

**Opponent:** Danny Cammack, Mickey's old driver, back in week one as Tom's best earner. He wants the same chair. Each week the player sees one gain of his: a round, a driver, a debtor.

**Shape:**
- **Week 1:** Inheritance, collections, a carry past a constable. To cover Danny, Tom writes one false line in the legal fare book.
- **Week 2:** Take back Mickey's rounds; the first break-in.
- **Week 3:** Seize the cold-store night trade from the Widow, who holds the dock gate. First strain: Ron refuses to squeeze an old docker, and Danny does it.
- **Week 4 (midpoint):** The bonded-warehouse job against the Widow comes off. The office now holds ground, and play turns from collecting to holding. Every rank, round and gate is watched and contested.
- **Week 5:** Second strain. Danny's men put an old docker in the infirmary in Tom's name, and Tom covers for him.
- **Week 6 (loss):** DS Ellis reads the fare book, and the week-one line ties the office to the warehouse. Danny gives Tom up. Ron takes the charge in Tom's place and goes down. Tom loses the office, the drivers and his standing.
- **Week 7 (last act):** Tom wins men back one door at a time, by what he did to each of them. He faces Danny on the quay, where Danny holds the only gun in the game. The ending choice is taught by every squeeze-or-spare choice before it: keep the chair and leave Ron inside, or give Ellis the true book and his own liberty to get Ron out. Ron's last scene, at the cell door or on the quay, is the bond's last word.

## Recognition

None. I know no published game or outside project matching any of the three. Plan A's title, *The Knowledge*, matches the studio's own v3 page named in the principles. The dock history (the Dock Labour Scheme's end, managers driving cars off a Russian ship at Hull) is real, but no game I know is built on it.
