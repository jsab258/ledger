**Overall: 9.** This is the most fully built story of the four. Nearly every plant has a payoff: the sticking seat, the coat with no sleeves, the eleven miles, the debt book. The central question, who drove that night, turns the earlier scenes inside out and is fair. The lead's flaw is something the player does with his hands (he pays unless the player chooses to take), and it pays off at the climax, when only the people he let give him something will stand for him. All three endings come from what the player wrote and allowed. It loses a point because the big emotional scenes belong to the doorman and the bookkeeper more than to the lead, and because several key beats are fixed.

**What holds it back:**
1. The lead is mostly a witness to the doorman and bookkeeper's secret. His own change is only seen in how others treat him.
2. Some choices barely matter: "licensed or refused, play goes on", "nothing keeps him off the gate", and the turn is "fixed for every player".
3. Outside threat and menace are thin. The rival and landlord are small forces next to the quiet mystery.
