**Held back by:**

**Overall: 8.** V is the most tightly built page of the four. Small objects (a painted bollard, a stuck seat, a mileage line, a handbrake) are planted in play and come back as the truth. The lead's flaw is played with the hands. The doorman bond is the warmest relationship on any of these pages, and the endings answer the lead's want with a single image each. My reservations are real, though. The truth arrives as a told paragraph of backstory. The climax is a ledger, a signature and a Customs officer reading aloud, which risks feeling like paperwork. The lead is deliberately blank, and the most dramatic acts belong to the doorman and the bookkeeper.

**Held back by:**
1. The turn's truth is delivered as backstory exposition ("What was true:...") rather than played.
2. The climax is set in records and signatures (the inspector's reading, the fare book), so the register risks anticlimax.
3. The lead is a cipher, and the cast is crowded. The others carry the drama, and the cut-out notebook pages are never paid off.

---

**Ranking:** V > D > H > Q.

V edges D because everything on its page interlocks. Its plants pay off, its choices come back, and its turn recasts what the reader has already seen. D's collapse and traitor can be read from the first lines. D has the stronger single emotional arc, the dying man saving his friend, but V earns its ending through the player's own hands and choices, where D's arrives on rails the page has already announced.
