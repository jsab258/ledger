**Judgement.** If built competently inside its frame, would this story stand at or above the best shipped story here? **NO. Confidence about 65%.** It is better made on paper than most shipped bibles, and it would likely sit with the second and third stories here. It falls short of the best for four reasons:
- Its lead is a witness at its turn and at its gravest scenes.
- Its one secret is guessable from the first act, by design.
- Tom's own reckoning is a staged copy of her night, steered by a fixed ambush.
- Its climax is signing a form whose cost to Tom is an off-screen court date at Easter.

**Faults that can be mended on paper:**
1. **The no-night hole.** A player who declines the lock-up job "goes on colder", yet the endings, Sunday's line and Reese's reading of "Saturday" all assume Tom drove a night. Nothing covers that road.
2. **A two-suspect mystery.** Nobody else was in the office, so once Ron is cleared at dawn the night reveal is confirmation, not surprise. The seat clue and "I don't drive" are also overt.
3. **The notebook writes the player's conclusions for him.** "I still had the wrong man" is false for the player who suspected Sheila.
4. **The 24th ignores the plan.** Three ways in on the board, the same ambush every time: fiction on rails.
5. **The lead is off-centre at the turn.** He watches the corridor, and the Bishop's death is off-screen.
6. **Signing has no felt price.** The right answer is obvious, its cost is abstract, and "Burn Both" is a dead end rather than a temptation.
7. **Act III is overloaded with procedure and runs three drives in one week.** VAT officer, Investigation Division, Ellis, Crown Court and bail; then the 24th, Reese's miles and the hill race. Why a VAT officer can demand a signed admission is legally thin.
8. **Few crime verbs.** For a hands-first crime game, Tom commits almost no crime with his own hands before hour 13.
9. **Convenience.** Danny is always waiting. Shiner is always on hand.

**Things that can only be judged once built:**
- Whether minicab fares and coin counting hold for 7.5 hours.
- Whether an unvoiced lead with no past registers in two-handers, with a notebook as his inner voice.
- Whether the comic set pieces land, which needs timing and regional performance.
- Whether the hold-to-take press reads as character or as friction.
- Whether players notice the owed-and-owing economy.
- Whether the mileometer and the bollard read as revelation.
- Whether the gap on the 24th produces a real choice under pressure or players game it.
- Whether a cast of fourteen in dialect stays clear to players outside Britain.
- Whether players accept stakes this small (£4 a week, a fire with nothing taken) as a crime story.

**My NO rests on the first list.** That makes it a mendable NO. The built-only risks would cut both ways.

# C. Changes, most important first (each a change of shape, inside the frame)

1. **Give every road its own night, and let the plan matter.** The 24th must come to every player. If he declines the job, Danny comes to him: the lads take the tin, or take Ron at the rank. Let the plan on the board change who is caught and where the gap opens. The choice to leave or go back must not depend on a fixed ambush. *Answers: E1, the no-night hole, the rails risk (X4 in spirit).*

2. **Make the mystery a three-way problem, and make the turn the "why".** Give a third credible suspect a real sheet gap that night, for example Kevin or Terry, so clearing Ron at dawn still leaves a field. Move "I don't drive" to after the Bishop's tally is found. Let Ron's motive (learning to drive so it would never be her again) be found by Tom's hands in a scene, not said once in the corridor. *Answers: T1, S5 risk.*

3. **Put Tom inside the loss.** The Bishop should die with Tom in the room, or ask for Tom and give his account to Tom, not off-screen at ten past four in the morning. In the corridor, give Tom the one act that brings Ron and Sheila face to face. *Answers: H1 on the lead, C1.*

4. **Give signing a price played with the hands.** Charged, Tom loses his licence until Easter. In the "both names" ending Ron, test passed, drives and Tom rides in the passenger seat: the seat inverted. Not signing should be the comfortable road: he keeps the wheel and the firm, and loses the people. *Answers: E1, C1, the obvious-choice risk.*

5. **Cut Act III to one procedure and one climax drive.** Merge Reese's reading and Ellis's charges into one authority. Choose either the miles for Reese or the race up the hill as the final drive, not both after the 24th. End on hands, not paperwork. *Answers: X5 and pacing risk, the told-in-talk risk (S4) in the last act.*

6. **Put crime into Tom's hands by week two.** Write each week's dusk job as a full scene with Ron where Tom breaks a law with his own hands. Getting Mickey's number back at the terminal becomes a real break-in, and so on. Then Saturday at the wheel is a man going against a habit he has learned, and the middle is not an asserted loop. *Answers: X3/S3 risk, the gap between the frame and the plot, the asserted middle.*

Separately, stop the notebook writing conclusions. Let it record evidence and let the player hold the belief. This is a small fix that does a lot of work.
