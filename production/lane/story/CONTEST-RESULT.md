## The winner

C, The Passenger, on the best total of ranks (4, against 5 and 9). Two of three readers put it first; the third put it second. No reader would single any of the three out yet. The critic and the player both said C came nearest, and for the same reason: it is the only one where the story is a person. The editor said B came nearest, for its turn.

What they praised in C: Ron and the lessons, and the line that turns them over ("I can drive, boss. Near enough"); the Fifth (the foreman's coat, the effigy, "Car one. You on your own?", Ada's "Tom"); the Widow as a lawful opponent who prices, keeps every deal and wins by asking what Tom never asks; the handbrake as the last input; the name box and the man with no past.

## What C must mend (named by two or more readers, or a breach of the frame)

1. The gate hut book. All three: a careful woman does not write her hold over a man into a ledger for the player to find by torch. The turn must be found another way.
2. The Widow telephoning Customs breaks her own rule (she keeps every deal) and throws her hold away. The cause of the inspection must be someone else's, or nobody's.
3. "You came to this town in October, Mr Nowak" is somebody asking about Tom's past. The frame forbids it. The third name must go in the box without anyone asking.
4. Why a VAT officer wants a driver's name for a journey nobody paid for. It needs a real reason in his remit, and the form with one box needs to be a real form.
5. The fare-book line does not match the night: Ron drove a burned man somewhere, and the line says only "landing to warehouse row and return".
6. The midpoint is announced when Ron tells Tom of the offer; and four and a half hours without Ron risks a slow, joyless middle. The empty seat must ache, not drag: it needs its own comedy and an opponent who comes at him in more than one way.
7. "Ask each person what they want" must not become a round of boxes to tick.
8. The handbrake must mean something the player understands before he touches it.
9. The friend who seemed to sell out but was protecting the firm is a known shape (the editor). It has to be earned by how it is found and what it costs, and Ron must not be merely noble.
10. Danny and Agar are thin.
11. Two widows called Jensen (a fault in all three treatments): Ada is Walter Jensen's widow and Maureen is Jensen, the Widow. Say what they are to each other, or change a name.

## Ideas from the losers that C can take without losing its shape

From B, No Charge:
- The staged answer: Tapper and Shiner in borrowed car coats off the last ferry, a threat made of theatre, planned on the office board. It is the comedy the empty-seat stretch lacks, and it gives the player a job to do against Danny with Ron's own gang while Ron is gone. (Critic.)
- The count: the fare book and Tom's own Ledger side by side, a find made with the hands. It can replace the gate-hut book as the way the player learns what the Widow bought. (Critic.)
- Two widows in the back of the cab, both looking at the back of Tom's head. (Player.)
- Ada's "A club is a thing you're in. A charity is a thing done to you", which gives the whole street Tom's flaw. (Critic.)
- Sheila's line entering Tom on the day he came, "15.10. Ferry landing. One. No charge.", shown in her kitchen on the Thursday; it sets up the Suitcase's last line. (Critic, player.)
- The Friday errands as doors knocked on, a short round driven backwards, instead of telephone calls alone. (Player.)

From A, Twice:
- Sheila calling him nothing at all after the Fifth, until he reads the line himself; and Sheila in the customer's chair on the wrong side of her own counter. (Critic.)
- The full public bench at the hearing, a gift nobody let him refuse. (Critic.)
- Agar's "Your uncle's friends light them for nothing, and I buy what is left", to give Agar a motive in one speech. (Critic, player.)
- Danny's "something of theirs that got used", to give Danny one real scene. (Player.)
- The night's fare coming over the radio in Pauline's ordinary voice. (Player.)
- Ron's "That's all I'll say in a car with a radio in it." (Critic.)

Not taken: B's second reveal (nobody burned the warehouse) and its whip-round ending, which two readers each called deflating and familiar; A's open club with no mystery, which all three marked down.
