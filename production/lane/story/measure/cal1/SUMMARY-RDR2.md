## What it is about

In 1899, in a fictional American West that is being closed in by railways, telegraph wires, banks and federal agents, a gang of outlaws is breaking apart. Its leader still talks of one last score that will let everyone retire and live free, and every job that follows is called the last one. The story is told through the gang's most trusted enforcer, a man in his thirties raised in the gang since boyhood. Over the long story he is asked to keep faith with a leader whose ideals are going bad, and he learns he is dying of tuberculosis. The question the story puts to him is what he does with the time he has left, and for whom. After his death the story passes to a younger man of the gang, who tries to live straight with a wife and son and is hunted for it.

## The lead

The lead is a hard, practical man who collects debts, robs trains, hunts for the camp's food and fights when told to. He keeps a journal of sketches and notes, which the player can read. He loves the gang's leader like a father and has never seriously questioned him. The leader's older partner has been the only one who could argue with him, and the lead listens to that partner too. The lead wants to stay loyal, to keep the others alive, and to be somebody better than the debt collector he has been. Early on he does not believe the life can end; later he does not believe it can be mended, only that some people in it can still be got out.

## The people who matter

The gang's leader is a charismatic talker who preaches freedom and loyalty and plans a new life in the islands of the South Seas. His older partner is the gang's planner and conscience, a patient man who still believes in the leader. The lead's closest rival in the leader's favour is a newer recruit, a loud, violent man who arrives mid-story and speaks of strength and of the old days. The lead's younger fellow, a gunman who ran away and came back, has a former lover and a young son and a deep distrust of the lead. A widow the gang pulls out of trouble in the snow becomes a hard, capable member and the lead's friend. A quiet, principled gunman with a code of his own becomes the lead's steadiest companion. There is the gang's matriarch, an older woman who runs the camp's morale, and a cook, a preacher, an idle old uncle and others, each with a few scenes of their own in camp. Against them stand an oil baron, whose train the gang robs, and a private detective agency he hires, whose lead agent becomes the gang's hunter. A federal investigator appears later and ties the story to what follows.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The story opens in a blizzard in the mountains of the north in 1899. A ferry robbery in a western town has gone wrong, money has been lost, men are dead or taken, and the gang has fled on foot and horseback into deep snow. The player rides with the lead and the leader's partner, learns to walk in drifts and to follow tracks, and finds a ruined mining camp for shelter. The first tasks are to find the younger gunman, who was left behind with a fever in a cabin, and to bring him back through the snow. The lead and others raid a rival gang's hideout in the hills, rescue the widow, and find news of a train that carries money. The gang robs the train, and with this the story's first lasting cost arrives: the train belongs to the oil baron, who answers by sending the detective agency after them. This chapter teaches the controls: riding, hunting, shooting from cover, slowing time to mark targets, and talking, and it is short. The player has no choice about what happens in it. Missions are linear and the player follows marked routes.

## The middle

The long middle is about half the story and is built from four stretches, each fixed to a new camp. The camp is the home of the story: a place with tents, a fire, a ledger of money, and people who talk when the player walks up. Here the player may donate money, do chores, help a friend, listen to songs and stories, sleep and eat, and keep the lead's horse and weapons. The camp changes with each chapter as the money and the mood change.

The first stretch, in the foothills near a small cattle town, is made of short, varied missions. The lead collects debts for a loan the gang makes to townsfolk, rescues two men from a hanging, rides out on hunting and fishing trips with the gang's cook and others, robs a second train with his younger fellow, and takes part in a failed raid on a bank. The detective agency's local agent tries to turn the lead against the gang, and he refuses. The raid ends in a shooting that kills many lawmen in that town, and the gang flees south. The player cannot avoid it.

The second stretch, in plantation country to the south, is about a feud between two old landowning families, one tied to the land and the other to the new money. Their quarrel offers a chance at the gold rumoured to be hidden between them. The leader and his partner plan to set them against each other. The lead helps both sides, is made a deputy by both, and escalates a fight that the player must walk into with a weapon in hand. Both families double-cross the gang. One ambushes it in a town and kills a gang member. The other kidnaps the younger gunman's young son and passes him to a crime boss in a large southern port city. The gang destroys both families in a battle at a mansion and moves nearer the city to find the boy.

The third stretch is the port city itself, with trams, theatres and slums. The gang finds and rescues the boy and then turns on the crime boss, who had betrayed it. The leader takes his revenge on him in a way the lead and his younger fellow find shocking, and from here the lead begins to doubt. The gang plans a bank robbery, which will pay for a passage to a free island. The robbery goes wrong. The detective agency's men kill the leader's partner and take the younger gunman. The lead, the leader, the new recruit and two others board a ship, and a storm wrecks it.

The fourth stretch is a Caribbean island, which has no collectibles or side tasks. The survivors wash ashore on a sugar island ruled by a colonel and worked by its native people. The lead and his companions are made to fight alongside the rebels, and the missions are linear: a ride to a camp, a night raid, a sustained defence. The player has little choice, and the island ends with the colonel dead and the survivors on a boat home. The leader's confidence is gone when they return. The rest of the gang is waiting, and the detective agency attacks its camp, which they fight off.

Across the middle, the player may choose between helping strangers or robbing them, between fighting or talking, between honour and the lack of it. These choices move the honour level, a running measure that changes how people speak to the lead and what he wears and gets paid. A few missions offer a decision with a small effect on how a scene ends. Most do not, and the main events are fixed.

## The turn

After the island, the leader grows suspicious. He believes the agency has an informer among his people and is driven by one last great robbery. He lifts the new recruit to the place of the old partner, who is now dead. The lead goes behind his back to free his younger fellow from a prison, and this costs him the leader's trust. The leader, now cold and secretive, begins to call the lead disloyal.

Then the lead is diagnosed. He has been coughing for most of the story, and after a fight with a debtor he is told by a doctor that he has tuberculosis and will not live long. He has no cure to look for. What the diagnosis changes is what he does. He stops treating the gang as a thing to keep alive and starts treating its people as lives to get out. He tells his younger fellow to take the woman and the boy and run. He throws himself into helping a native people called to the edge of war with the army, which the leader opposes. Where the player's honour has been high, he takes the news calmly and sets about doing good. Where it has been low, he takes it badly but reaches the same decision. In both cases the lead from this point wants less for himself. The player's hands do the same work as before, but the aim of it has changed, and the camp thins as some people leave.

## The last act

The leader and his new lieutenant plan a last great robbery of an army payroll train and persuade a young native warrior to attack a fort as a diversion. The warrior dies in the attack. The oil baron is killed, the leader leaves the lead behind to be caught by the army, and the lead escapes with the help of the principled gunman, who stays with the native people.

The payroll robbery is made and the money is hidden, but the detective agency raids the camp. The younger gunman is thought dead, his former lover is taken, and the leader and his people abandon her. The lead and the widow ride to the agency's base in a town and fight through it, the player shooting down many agents, some from a clock tower, to reach her. Before dying, the agency's agent tells the lead that the new recruit has been its informer since the gang came back from the island.

The lead brings this to the leader at the camp. The recruit denies it, and the leader sides with him against the lead. The younger gunman returns alive and tells the leader that he has been abandoned. As they are about to shoot one another, a force of agents and soldiers attacks and everyone scatters. The player fights through a cliff and a town, with no choice.

## The endings

The last choice is a prompt as the lead is cornered on a mountain: help the younger gunman escape to his family, or go back for the stolen money, which is hidden in a cave nearby. In the sources there is disagreement about whether honour or only this choice decides what follows. All agree that the choice sets the main shape of the ending, and that the honour level the player has built up colours the final scenes.

If the lead helps the younger gunman, he holds off the pursuing agents on the cliffs while his friend escapes with his family. Then he fights the recruit with his fists. The leader stops the recruit from killing him but will not help. The lead, wounded and with a failing chest, tells his former companions that he tried to change, and the leader leaves him. He dies alone on the mountain at sunrise, with a deer in the last frame of his visions.

If the lead goes for the money, he returns to the camp and the cave, where the recruit overpowers him in a fight. The leader again stops the killing, and the lead's pleas fail. The recruit then shoots the lead dead while he is down. The last image is of a wolf in the visions that have followed the lead from the time of the diagnosis.

Either way, the story then turns to the younger gunman, and an epilogue in 1907. He works on a ranch under an assumed name and does farm labour, and his past catches up with him when bounty hunters attack. His former lover leaves with their son, doubting he can change. He takes a bank loan, buys land and with the widow, the principled gunman and the old uncle builds a house on a plot of his own, and the player's hands are given over to fencing, chopping wood, fishing and fatherhood. His former lover returns and he proposes to her. He learns the recruit is alive and leads another gang. He and the widow and the principled gunman attack the recruit's mountain camp, and the leader appears and shoots the recruit, then leaves. The younger gunman kills the recruit and takes the old gang's money to pay his debt, and he marries. A final scene shows a federal investigator learning where he lives, which sets up events some years later.
