## What it is about

In 1403 the kingdom of Bohemia is at war with itself. The rightful king is a prisoner, and his half-brother, the usurper, holds the country with foreign troops. A man-at-arms of low birth is sent with his lord and friend to carry a message to a wavering castle lord. The errand fails in the first hours and grows into a bandit war in a country of castles, then a hunt through the silver city of Kuttenberg, then a siege. Beneath it runs the lead's wish to kill the men who murdered his mother and foster father, and the question of what that wish makes of him. The lead rides, fights, talks, sneaks, steals and spares, and the game keeps count. It reads the count back to him in the last scene. The tone swings between crude comedy, mostly from the lead's friend, and killing.

## The lead

The lead is a blacksmith's son from a village the usurper's army burned, killing his mother and foster father. His true father is a lord of the rightful party, whom he serves as a man-at-arms, and he is now also bodyguard and confidant to a young nobleman. The soldier who killed his parents is one of the usurper's officers, and a knight stole the sword the foster father forged for the lord. He wants three things: to carry out his father's business, to win the wavering lord over; his parents' killer; and the sword back.

The player controls him throughout. The player chooses how he fights, whom he trusts, how he gets past guards and clerks, and how much he steals, kills or spares. A dirty lead is turned away at doors, so washing and dress matter. Good choices are often costly.

## The people who matter

The friend is the young nobleman, a hot-headed womaniser who brawls and gets the pair into trouble. The lead is a commoner who does the work. They bicker, but the friendship carries the story.

The lead's father is the lord who sends him out and holds out the sword at the close.

The wavering lord holds a castle and says he has not chosen a side. He has chosen the usurper.

The bandit chief leads the band that ambushes the lead, and is in truth a rebel captain working against the wavering lord and the usurper. He is the lead's captor, then his ally, and at the end offers him a place at his side.

The priest works for the rightful party, and for one stretch the player controls him.

The spy is a young woman working with the bandit chief. She frees the lead from prison and stays with the group.

The sword thief is the knight who serves the wavering lord. The officer is the usurper's man who killed the lead's parents.

The margrave is a powerful noble who keeps an uneasy truce with the usurper and will not always help.

The broker lives in the Jewish quarter of Kuttenberg, protects it, and trades in news. He turns out to be the blacksmith's son by another woman, which makes him the lead's foster brother.

The mercenary captain leads a band hostile to the usurper. A foreign mercenary leader joins the group and later betrays it.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The story opens in the middle of a siege, then goes back some weeks. The lead and his friend, with squires, travel through woodland to the wavering lord's castle to deliver a letter and win the lord from the usurper. Soldiers hunting bandits stop them and the tension passes. At a camp the player learns talk and fighting.

Bandits attack. The squires are killed. The lead is shot with an arrow after shouting to let a party of women flee. An outlaw catches him and they fall from a cliff together. The outlaw dies and breaks the fall, but the lead is badly hurt, and the player is back to the weak, clumsy lead of the first story's start. The friend carries him through a forest. In a fever the lead relives his village burning. A peasant herbalist nurses them, a bandit tracks them down, and the friend kills him but is hurt. The player feeds the lead, gathers herbs, helps brew medicine and washes. The two walk to the castle, where they are turned away: the armour and the letter are gone and they look like beggars. In the village the lead hears that the wavering lord will attend a noble wedding and plans to find work there. The friend starts a tavern brawl and both are put in the stocks. The friend blames the lead and walks away.

The lord does not come to the wedding, and the groom slips away. The friend returns and the two make peace. Another brawl follows, both are jailed, and the friend is sentenced to hang for poaching. The wavering lord arrives in time, frees him and offers to talk if they help end the bandit trouble.

## The middle

The middle is most of the game and falls into two regions of unequal size. The first, rural, holds twelve of the thirty-three main quests. The second, around Kuttenberg, holds twenty, and one quest is counted in both. Around them stand optional stories, seventeen listed for the first region and twenty-two for the second, though the list is incomplete. Most offer a twist, a moral choice or several ways through, and consequences can arrive hours later.

A village reports a pit to hell opened by a mining disaster, with cattle killed, and the lead sides with a young priest who wants reasons or with a foreman who wants old rites. Two fencing guilds feud in Kuttenberg, and the lead helps the winner set up a weekly tournament. An old man sets him mischievous tasks, such as stealing maypoles and driving sheep onto clean laundry. A series about getting into a wedding uninvited runs alongside the main wedding. None of these is stated to change the main story.

In the first region the lead and his friend go to a fortress held by bandits. A captured bandit reveals that the groom is in league with them. The lead can tell the wavering lord or keep quiet. If he tells, the lord goes after the groom, who can be spared or killed. The lord leads an assault on the fortress, the force walks into a trap, most of the soldiers die, and the bandit chief takes the lead and his friend.

The priest finds them and helps the chief put down a rebellion by the sword thief. The prisoners are freed and learn that the chief wages a guerrilla war on the lord, who had used them. The lord, the sword thief and the officer storm the fortress. The lead, the priest and the chief are jailed in the castle and face execution, and the friend is held for ransom. The spy frees them. The lead kills the sword thief, takes back the sword, and learns that the usurper means to gather his army in Kuttenberg. Under torture the prisoners have named the margrave's spy in Kuttenberg, and the group escapes through tunnels.

The second region shifts the hands toward talking, sneaking and disguise. The margrave tells the group to end the banditry because of his truce, and will not pay the friend's ransom. The lead finds the margrave's spy in the Jewish quarter under the broker's protection, warns him, and learns that the friend is held in a castle of the wavering lord. The lead and the bandit chief hire the mercenary captain's band, infiltrate the castle and free the friend and the foreign mercenary.

The lead then takes a long task as a servant. To get into the Kuttenberg council he must win a certificate and the servant's clothes from a clerk, by persuasion, a bribe or theft, then spend about twenty real minutes serving councillors and the usurper. Repeated mistakes get him thrown out. The player also controls the priest at a meeting of loyal lords in Raborsch, winning three nobles with small favours, among them a duel and the swapping of papers in a stable.

## The turn

The turn comes at the council. Serving at the table, the lead hears the usurper and the officer order a massacre of the Jewish people of Kuttenberg. The player cannot stop it in the room. He rides for Raborsch, where the wavering lord's soldiers attack the loyal lords. The attack is beaten off, but several lords are captured.

In Kuttenberg the lead helps the broker lead his people out of the quarter, and the broker is revealed to be his foster brother. The usurper's army stands in the city, the lords are his hostages, and the group has to make its own plan.

## The last act

The group captures the wavering lord, using mercenaries and a stolen bombard to storm his castle. The lord offers an idea. The usurper's silver lies in his residence in Kuttenberg, which also holds the captured lords, and taking it may force him out. Part of the party poses as a papal legate to cause a distraction. The rest go through the mines, take the silver and free the lords. The plan works. The foreign mercenary tries to take the silver and escapes in the struggle.

The group falls back to a fortress at Suchdol, and the usurper's army leaves Bohemia. A militia from Prague, led by the officer, the wavering lord and the foreign mercenary, besieges the fort. The mercenary captain mortally wounds the officer. The siege is the last long stretch, with catapult fire to dodge, ladders to push over, and hunger. During it the lead can find the dying officer and either kill him or leave him to die. The lead and the broker slip out to fetch help from the margrave and two lords. The broker is wounded and taken by the foreign mercenary, and the lead can rescue him or leave him. Either way the lead returns with a relief force and the attackers are driven off.

In the final quest the lead storms the outer bailey, kills the commander at the portcullis and lifts it. The siege ends. A lord speaks, and the lead can sharpen his damaged sword. He can talk with the bandit chief, who offers him a place at his side, and with the wounded spy. The friend talks about his coming wedding; the lead can coax more from him by persuasion, press him by coercion, or drop it. Then the lead has a vision of his parents, and his father wakes him and gives him the sword.

## The endings

The main story has one ending: the relief force breaks the siege, the usurper is forced out, and the lead's father gives him back his sword. Only the last conversation and the epilogue change.

In the vision his mother and foster father speak about all that he has done and been forced to do. The game weighs it. It counts whether he revealed the groom's betrayal, whether he stopped a massacre in the first wedding's village, whether he killed the sword thief in an honourable duel or without a fight, whether he stopped the mercenary captain burning a village, whether he saved the broker, whether he killed the officer cruelly or mercifully, and whether he spared the foreign mercenary after the betrayal. Crimes along the way count too, such as theft, banditry and needless killing. Last, he is asked whether he is sorry, and saying he has no regrets counts against him.

If he has acted honourably and shows remorse, his parents say they are proud and promise to stay with him always. If not, they condemn him as lost to his thirst for revenge and leave him for good. If the broker lived, he thanks the lead, and the lead's father is proud that he saved him.

The lead then talks with his father, who offers the sword. If he takes it, he stays a soldier or an adventurer. If he refuses, he settles as a villager or as a burgher in Kuttenberg, with one of three love interests: a woman who has left town, the spy who stays at Suchdol, or the friend. A hidden variant follows if the lead has pursued no love in the game. He returns home to the woman he left behind in the first story. A later update is reported to add a victory screen in which the usurper returns to Hungary in disgrace.

After the credits the mercenary captain tries to shoot an apple from the bandit chief's head and wounds his eye. A last scene shows the usurper learning of the stolen silver and the margrave's gathering army, wrecking his chamber in anger and ordering the army home to Hungary.

One further ending is reached by accident. If the lead piles up crimes and refuses to pay, the guards arrest him. He is shown in a cell, led through a jeering crowd and hanged, and a message says he has been executed for his crimes. An earlier save restores the game.
