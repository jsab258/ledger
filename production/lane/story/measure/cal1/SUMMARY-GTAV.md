## What it is about

Three criminals in and around a large city on the American west coast and the desert country north of it are bound together by an old betrayal and by a run of robberies. One is a retired bank robber who sold out his crew nine years earlier and has lived since in comfortable, resentful hiding. One is a young repo man from the poor south of the city who wants a way up. One is the retired robber's former partner, a violent, unstable drug maker who believes his friend is dead. The story follows a modern American crime plot in which the three are forced together, pressed by government agents and a billionaire, and drawn towards one last enormous robbery of a gold store. It is about debts, loyalty, family and money. The player moves between all three in a large open world, and a final choice decides who lives.

## The lead

There are three leads, and the player is each of them in turn, never a single one for the whole game. The retired robber is a middle-aged father in a large house above the city. His wife spends his money and betrays him, his daughter chases fame, his son lies on a sofa playing games, and he sees a therapist, to whom he cannot tell the truth. He wants a quiet life and cannot stand one. The young repo man works for a car dealer with his loud friend, repossessing cars from people who cannot pay. He is more careful than his friend. The old partner lives in a trailer in the desert, runs a drug and gun business, and rages at anything that crosses him.

The player controls one at a time and can change to another at nearly any moment when no mission is running. Each man's life goes on while he is not being played. Many missions use two or three of them together, and in those the player can switch mid-action, for example to fly the helicopter as one man and shoot from it as another. Each has a special power the player calls on once a meter is full. The retired robber slows time in a gunfight. The young repo man slows time while driving. The old partner goes into a rage that makes him hit harder and take less harm.

## The people who matter

The retired robber's wife, daughter and son are in nearly every one of his scenes. His wife has an affair with her tennis coach and then leaves. His daughter wants to be on a talent show. His son goes into trouble and is later kidnapped. His therapist listens, and he lies to her.

The young repo man's friend is a loud gang member who keeps pulling him back to the street. The car dealer who employs them is a scammer.

The old partner has a nervous companion in his trailer, a biker gang who are his rivals and then his victims, and the drug lord's wife, whom he kidnaps. The dead crewman from the opening is a name he cannot let go.

Around the three stands an old planner, a bitter, wheelchair-bound gadget and heist expert who works with them from his house and organises every big job. Two government agents press the leads. The first agent, who helped fake the retired robber's death, takes a bribe and then calls in favours. The second is a loud, ambitious officer from a rival bureau who wants the crew's help for his own advancement. A rich businessman who owns a film studio and a fortune uses the crew for car thefts and corporate sabotage, and does not pay. A drug lord with a mansion, whose wife the crew has been entangled with, wants money from the retired robber. A private security army serves the powerful and attacks the leads.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The game opens with a bank robbery in a snowbound town in the far north, nine years before the present. The player is, in turn, the retired robber and the old partner, a crew of three plus a driver who dies at once. The player drives, shoots and holds off police in the snow. The job goes wrong. A sniper hits the retired robber and the third crewman, and the old partner flees into a field while the other two are left behind. The player has no choice in any of it.

A funeral follows. The retired robber, officially dead, watches his own burial from a distance, and the sniper stands among the mourners. The story then jumps to the present, where the retired robber lives in the city under a changed name, with his family, on a deal with the first government agent.

The young repo man is shown repossessing cars, and takes the retired robber's car while the robber is hiding in it. The two fall in together, and the young man helps rescue the robber's son from trouble. The robber then discovers his wife with her tennis coach and, with the young man's help, wrecks the house the coach is hiding in, which turns out to belong to the drug lord's wife. The drug lord demands money for the damage. The retired robber turns to his planner, and the planner organises a jewellery store robbery, which the retired robber and the young man carry out with a hired crew. The old partner sees the robbery on the news, realises his friend is alive, and goes to the city.

## The middle

The middle is most of the game and is built from a repeating cycle. A heist is planned, its set-up missions are played one after another, the heist itself is played, and the money comes in. Between them the game fills the time with work forced on the leads, family scenes, the old partner's desert business and optional activities in the open world.

The heists run in order of size. The jewellery store job comes first. The player picks one of two approaches, quiet with gas grenades or loud and violent, and then a driver, a gunman and a computer expert, each of whom takes a share of the take. A better crew costs more and makes fewer mistakes, and a cheaper one can make the mission harder. The player then plays the set-up, such as gathering gear, and then the robbery, switching among the crew during the escape.

The second is a raid on a ship, taken either from the sea or from a helicopter, to steal a prototype from the private army. It pays nothing, and the old partner makes a decision in it that costs the crew the payment. The third is a bank robbery in a small town in the north of the county, with no choice of approach, in which a cheap gunman may die. The fourth is a break-in at the headquarters of the government bureau, done either by parachuting in from a helicopter or by posing as firefighters. The fifth, the last and by far the largest, is the gold store job, and it is saved for the end.

The two government agents force a good deal of the work. The first agent blackmails the retired robber into jobs for his bureau, and the old partner, who has joined the crew, is dragged in too. The second agent, from the rival bureau, wants a raid and a theft, and the bureau has no money, so the crew must rob a bank to pay for it. The billionaire pays nothing. The young repo man is sent to assassinate businessmen so that the billionaire can profit on the stock market, and the crew steals cars for him. The retired robber also works at the billionaire's film studio and later saves its film. The drug lord has the retired robber help remove a cousin who would testify against him.

The family scenes run through all of it. The retired robber's wife leaves him, the family splits, and he sits in therapy. In time he reunites them, rescues his daughter from a stalker and his son from kidnappers, and attends the premiere of the film. The young repo man gets a house and is pulled back into his friend's affairs, including rescuing him from a gang. The old partner fights bikers and rivals in the desert, expands his business, and quarrels with the retired robber. Between missions the player golfs, plays tennis, races, hunts, dives and trades shares.

## The turn

The old partner opens the retired robber's grave in the northern town and finds the body of the crewman he believed to be in prison. The heist was not an accident. The retired robber had made a deal with the first agent to be shot, to vanish with a new name and to pay the agent a bribe, and it cost the crewman his life.

The reveal comes out between the two men in an argument that the retired robber cannot answer. The old partner storms off. Gangsters seize the retired robber, and the young repo man tracks him down and frees him. The player controls the rescue as the young repo man, killing the gangsters in a warehouse. The two older men are later attacked together by both bureaus and the private army, and in that fight they make an uneasy peace.

## The last act

After the break-in at the government bureau and the saving of the film, the three are no longer fighting each other. The billionaire cancels the film for the insurance, plans to turn the studio into flats, and sends the private army after the retired robber's family. The player fights through the retired robber's own house to free his family. The second agent tells the young repo man to kill the old partner, who is a liability. The crew then plans the big job.

The final heist is the gold store. The player picks a quiet approach, with an electromagnetic pulse and trucks through a tunnel, or a loud one, with a train and an attack helicopter, and picks the crew accordingly. The take is close to two hundred million dollars. The player plays each stage, driving, shooting and switching between the three, and the job succeeds.

The three leads are rich and, for a short time, free. The billionaire and the second agent then each go to the young repo man, who is in a position to do as they wish. The billionaire wants the retired robber dead, because the robber is the cause of his losses. The agent wants the old partner dead. The player is the young repo man at the decision.

## The endings

There are three endings, and the player picks one through the young repo man. This is the only large choice that changes the story, and it is permanent.

In the first, the young repo man chooses to kill the old partner, on the billionaire's request. He lures the partner to a meeting and draws a gun, and the partner flees in a truck. The retired robber, who is in on it, drives it into a petrol tanker. The old partner calls them traitors and tells the young man to take the shot, and the young man or the retired robber shoots the fuel and the old partner burns to death. The last image is the two surviving men parting, the retired robber needing time away, and the player then controls the two and shares out the dead man's cut. Both the billionaire and the agent live.

In the second, the young repo man chooses to kill the retired robber, on the agent's request. The old partner refuses to help him. The young man meets the retired robber, who takes a call from his wife about their daughter starting college, and the young man chases him by car and on foot to a power station. After a fight he pushes the robber off a tower, then grabs him, and the player is asked to pull him up or let go. The robber dies either way. The young man then phones his friend to make amends. The last scenes show the old partner disowning him, the dead man's wife cursing him, and the son ringing both men, and the player then controls the young man and the old partner, with the dead man's cut going to his family.

In the third, the young repo man will do neither. He phones the planner and warns the other two, and the three trap the government agents and the private army at a foundry. They then split up to hunt their enemies. The old partner kills the second agent, the retired robber kills the gang member who betrayed the young man's friend, and the young man kills a crime boss who had threatened them. The old partner knocks out the billionaire at his mansion and puts him in a car boot, and the three push the car off a mountain into the sea, where it explodes. All three live and keep their shares. The last scene is the three retired from crime, still friends, awkwardly. The planner sends each of them his untouched cut.
