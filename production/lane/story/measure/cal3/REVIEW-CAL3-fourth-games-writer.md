**Answer: NO.** Confidence about 70%. Built competently, V would be a fine, unusual chamber story, with better clue craft than any of the three shipped stories. It would not stand at or above D as a story players live through.

**Faults of the story itself** (fixable on paper):
1. **The turn lands on the wrong relationship.** The reveal is about Sheila, whom the design deliberately keeps at arm's length, so the player has few scenes of their own with her. The hit lands on a stranger rather than on the bond the player built.
2. **The theme and the drama work against each other.** Asking is the moral answer, and asking Ron before the Fifth removes the midpoint loss. The best-played path gets the weakest story and the least testing.
3. **The red herring breaks itself.** The lessons prove Ron cannot drive. Elimination then points at Sheila before lesson three, and the turn hangs on one pronoun.
4. **Low stakes and a passive climax.** The jeopardy is a roughly £4,000 assessment and a bail date. Nobody is in physical danger after Julie's arm. The last input is to do nothing with the handbrake: elegant on paper, but players will press it, and many will feel the game ended on a VAT inspection.
5. **Act III is a run of errands.** Signatures, a phone box, a gate pick, a padlock. Each is fine; together they are a checklist with no clock tied to a person.
6. **Too many branches for 16.5 hours.** Six endings, four signature options, Ron back or not, the asked-first midpoint, the wind-down road, three hearing outcomes, Kevin three ways. Production will cut, and the cuts will fall on the paths the theme cares most about. Parts of Act I are also referenced ("as approved", "as written") but not in this file.

**Can only be judged built:**
- An unvoiced lead carrying a friendship. The frame helps here (a man who never asks is quiet anyway), but it rests entirely on Ron's performance.
- Typed "what do you want" questions inside scripted scenes: tone and recognising what the player meant.
- Whether a weekly loop of fares, dusk, count and Friday holds nine and a half hours. The dusk jobs are one table line each, so this is asserted, not written.
- Whether three or more 11 mph drives play as tension or as dead time.
- Whether the comedy exists at all.

# C. Changes of shape, most important first

1. **Make Sheila the second bond.** Give Tom and Sheila three hands-scenes before the turn: she rides as navigator on night counts, she teaches him the book line by line, and she refuses car two on a night the firm is short. Then the reveal costs a relationship the player has. Answers H1, T1's payoff, risk 1.
2. **Let the turn be found by hands, not a pronoun.** For example, Sheila must move car two in an emergency, and the player sees her drive it without touching the seat. Ron's "she" becomes the confirmation, not the whole turn. Repair the red herring so a hands-scene gives the player real reason to suspect Ron: in lesson two he is suspiciously good, then lesson three explains why. Answers T1, S5, risk 3.
3. **Asking Ron changes what the Fifth means, not whether it happens.** Shiner's brag happens on every path, so Ron goes to the gate anyway. The asked player knows he was not bought, and still loses the seat. This keeps the hard-hitting moment for everyone and removes a whole branch. Answers H1, risk 2, risk 6.
4. **Put a person on the clock in Act III.** From the Monday, Ron intends to give Ellis a false confession on Friday. The week becomes Tom racing to make the truth readable before Ron condemns himself, and the eleven-mile drive becomes the thing that saves him. Keep the do-nothing handbrake, but put an active, risky beat before it. Answers low stakes, Act III errands, the slow climax, X3 risk.
5. **Cut to three endings, keyed to Ron's seat** (beside him, at the gate, gone), and reduce the four signatures to two choices: her own name, or a lie for her. Answers E1 focus and the branch count.
6. **Make Tom's change a deed, and tighten the middle.** One Act III task cannot be done alone (the gate, the car-two fetch), and the player must ask for hands, so the arc is shown rather than counted. Cut Act II from six weeks to four, and make the loop's shape change visibly after the Fifth. Answers C1 being only asserted, S3, and the weekly-cycle risk. Write the comic set pieces as scenes, not labels (U1).
