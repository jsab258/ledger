## What it is about

In 1899, in a fictional American West that is being closed in by railways, telegraph wires, banks and federal agents, a gang of outlaws is breaking apart. Its leader still talks of one last score that will let everyone retire and live free, and every job that follows is called the last one. The story is told through the gang's most trusted enforcer, a man in his thirties raised in the gang since boyhood. He loves the leader like a father and has done the gang's hard work for years: collecting debts, robbing trains, hunting for the camp's food. As money runs short and the law closes in, he is asked to keep faith with a leader whose promises are wearing thin. The question the story puts to him is what the gang's loyalty is worth, and what he owes the people in it.

## The lead

The lead is a hard, practical man. His jobs are to collect debts, rob trains and coaches, hunt, fish and fight when the leader says so. He keeps a journal of sketches and notes. He has been raised by the leader and by the leader's older partner, and he trusts both. He wants to stay loyal, to keep the others alive, and to be something better than the debt collector he has been. Whether he is honourable or not is partly the player's to decide, mission by mission, and the story follows him either way.

## The people who matter

The gang's leader is a talker who preaches freedom and loyalty and plans a new life far from the law. His older partner is the gang's planner and the one man who can argue with him. The lead's rival in the leader's favour is a newer recruit, loud and violent, who arrives partway through and speaks of strength. The lead's younger fellow is a gunman who ran away and came back, and has a former lover and a young son. A widow whom the gang pulls out of trouble in the snow becomes a capable member and the lead's friend. A quiet, principled gunman with a code of his own becomes his steadiest companion. There is also the gang's matriarch, a cook, a preacher, an idle old uncle and others, each with a few scenes in camp. Against them stand an oil baron, whose train the gang robs, and a private detective agency he hires, whose lead agent becomes the gang's hunter.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The story opens in a blizzard in the mountains in 1899. A ferry robbery in a western town has gone wrong, money has been lost, men are dead or taken, and the gang has fled into deep snow. The player rides with the lead and the leader's partner, wades through drifts, and finds a ruined mining camp for shelter. The first tasks are to find the younger gunman, who was left behind in the storm, and to bring him back. The lead and others raid a rival gang's hideout in the hills, rescue the widow, and find plans for a train that carries money. The gang robs the train, and with this the first lasting cost arrives: the train belongs to the oil baron, who answers by hiring the detective agency to hunt them. The chapter also teaches the controls: riding, hunting, shooting from cover and slowing time to mark targets.

## The middle

The middle fills most of the story and is made of four stretches, each tied to a new camp. The camp is a place of tents, a fire, a ledger of money and people who talk when the player walks up, where the player may donate money and do chores for the gang. The main missions change with each place.

The first stretch, near a small cattle town, is made of short, varied jobs. The lead collects debts for the gang's loans, rescues two men from a hanging, rides out hunting and fishing with the cook, robs a second train with his younger fellow, and joins a bank raid that ends in a shooting killing many lawmen. The gang flees south. In the second stretch, in plantation country, two old landowning families are feuding and rumour puts gold between them. The lead helps both, is made a deputy by both, and pushes the fighting on. Both families betray the gang. One ambushes it in a town and kills a gang member. The other kidnaps the younger gunman's son and sells him to a crime boss in a port city. The gang destroys both families in a battle at a mansion.

The third stretch is the port city, where the gang rescues the boy and then turns on the crime boss. The leader takes his revenge in a way that disturbs the lead and his younger fellow: he feeds the man to an alligator. In one mission the gang needs someone to retrieve a boat from the alligator. The leader says the lead will go, the lead answers "I will?", and the player can climb back into the boat or take the dive. The gang plans a bank robbery to pay for a passage to a free island. It goes wrong. The detective agency's men kill the leader's partner and take the younger gunman. The lead, the leader, the recruit and two others board a ship, and a storm wrecks it.

The fourth stretch is a Caribbean island. The survivors wash ashore on a sugar island ruled by a colonel and worked by its native people, and the lead fights beside the rebels. The island has no collectibles or side missions. On their return the gang reunites, and when armed men close in the leader, worn out, says that next they are going to be shot. Then the agency attacks the camp, and the gang fights it off.

Optional story runs beside all this in about thirty short strands, each a stranger with a problem, of which the game counts ten as a full set. A widow sits at her husband's grave north of a mining town. The lead teaches her to hunt, tracking a rabbit with her and killing wolves on the way home. Days later he shows her how to shoot at bottles. She feeds him, he falls ill and collapses, and he wakes in a bed with money and a letter of thanks. A third visit lets him take what he likes from her house. An inventor in the port city is about to pitch a device to an investor. The lead steers a model submarine through a pond of mines, sinking toy battleships, and then at night places three metal rods on hills in a storm and pulls levers in the inventor's tower. The power wakes a robot that moves briefly and shuts down. Later the lead finds the inventor dead and the robot gone, and finds it on a cliff crying for its maker.

Two scenes are meant to be funny. In a stranger strand, after a circus caravan crashes, the lead recaptures its animals. The zebra is a striped mule, the tiger a painted cougar, the lion a dead dog with a fake mane, and the real lion is found in a barn. And when the gang's last train robbery goes wrong because the train will not slow down, the lead, running beside it, asks whether he should sneak on now.

The gravest scenes in this stretch are two. In the port city the bank robbery goes wrong and the leader's old partner is killed, and without him the leader begins to come apart. In another strand a one-legged veteran, helped by the lead to recover his artificial leg and to hunt a wolf pack, is gored by a boar and gives the lead his horse with his dying words.

## The turn

After the island the leader believes the agency has an informer among his people and grows fixed on one last great robbery. He puts the recruit in the old partner's place. The lead goes behind his back to free the younger fellow from prison, and this costs him the leader's trust. The leader becomes cold and secretive, and the camp thins as people leave.

Then the lead is diagnosed with tuberculosis and told he will not live long. There is no cure to find, so what the diagnosis changes is what he does. He tells his younger fellow to take the woman and the boy and run. He helps a native people on the edge of war with the army, which the leader opposes. In the sources the news is taken calmly if the player's honour is high and badly if it is low, and either way he decides to help others get out. The work is the same, hunting, riding, shooting, but its aim has changed.

## The last act

The leader and the recruit plan a last robbery of an army payroll train and persuade a young native warrior to attack a fort as a diversion. The warrior dies in the attack. The oil baron is killed, the leader leaves the lead behind to be caught by the army, and the principled gunman helps the lead escape, then stays with the native people.

The robbery is made and the money hidden, but the agency raids the camp. The younger gunman is thought dead, his former lover is taken, and the leader and his people abandon her. The lead and the widow ride to the agency's base in a town and shoot their way through it, picking off agents from a clock tower, to reach her. Before dying, the agency's agent tells the lead that the recruit has been its informer since the island.

The lead brings this to the leader at the camp. The recruit denies it, and the leader sides with him. The younger gunman returns alive and says he was abandoned. As they are about to shoot each other, agents and soldiers attack and everyone scatters.

## The endings

The last choice comes as the lead is cornered on a mountain. He can help the younger gunman escape to his family, or go back for the hidden money. The sources differ on what decides the result. One says honour decides it, with a high level giving the lead a quiet death and a low level giving the recruit's bullet. Another says only the choice decides it: helping the younger fellow gives the good ending and going for the money the bad, while honour changes the visions and the lead's reaction to his illness. Both agree that helping the younger fellow is the better ending.

If he helps him, the lead holds off the agents on the cliffs while his friend escapes. He fights the recruit with his fists. The leader stops the recruit from finishing him and does not help. The lead, dying, tells his old companions he tried to change, and the leader leaves him. He dies alone on the mountain watching the sunrise, with a deer in his last visions.

If he goes for the money, he is beaten by the recruit at the cave. The leader stops the killing, and the lead's pleas to him fail. The recruit laughs and shoots him dead. The last image is a wolf.

Either way the story turns to the younger gunman in 1907. He works on a ranch under another name, bounty hunters attack, and his former lover leaves with their son, doubting he can change. He takes a bank loan, buys land, and builds a house with the widow, the principled gunman and the old uncle, working at fencing, chopping and fishing. His former lover returns and he proposes. He learns the recruit is alive and leads a new gang, and he and the widow and the principled gunman attack the camp. The leader appears and shoots the recruit, and leaves. The younger gunman kills the recruit and takes the old gang's money to pay his debt. He marries. In the epilogue one optional strand is a writer with a block who is helped to free a deer from a trap, then stops eating, is found dead, and has his cabin burned by the player. The last scene shows a federal investigator learning where the younger gunman lives.
