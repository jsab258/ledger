## What it is about

Three criminals share a large city on the American west coast and the desert country north of it. One is a retired bank robber living under a false name in a big house with a family that no longer respects him. One is a young repo man from the poor south of the city who wants out of street crime. One is a violent drug maker in a desert trailer who has not forgotten the robber's old crew. A planner who works from his house, two government agents and a billionaire push the three together and into a run of robberies that builds towards one enormous job on a store of gold. The story is a satire of American money, fame and law enforcement, and puts one question: can three men with a buried past and no reason to trust each other pull off the last score and walk away.

## The lead

There are three leads, and the player is each of them in turn. The retired robber is a middle-aged father. His wife has an affair, his daughter chases fame, his son lies on a sofa playing games, and he sees a therapist he cannot be honest with. The sources give his want as boredom with retirement: he craves the thrill of crime, wants to be a major player again, and wants his family to respect him, though his behaviour works against that. The young repo man works with his loud friend for a crooked car dealer. He wants to leave the gang life he sees as destructive and to be a better kind of criminal, with wealth and status from organised crime in place of street work. The old partner runs a drug and gun business from the desert. The sources give his want as chaos and destruction for their own sake, with money a bonus, an ambition to expand his business, and a deep grudge against the retired robber.

The player controls one at a time and can change to another at nearly any moment when no mission is running. Many missions use two or three of them together, and in those the player can switch mid-action, for example to fly the helicopter as one man and shoot from it as another. Each has a power that works once a meter is full. The retired robber slows time in a gunfight, the young repo man slows time while driving, and the old partner goes into a rage that makes him hit harder and take less harm.

## The people who matter

The retired robber's wife leaves him after he attacks her yoga instructor, and she returns later. His daughter wants to be on a television talent show, and his son gets into trouble and is later kidnapped.

The young repo man's friend is a loud gang member who keeps pulling him back to the street. The car dealer who employs them is a scammer.

The old partner has a nervous companion in his trailer and a biker gang who are first his rivals and then his victims.

The planner is a bitter, wheelchair-using gadget and heist expert who organises every big job. The first government agent helped fake the retired robber's death, takes a bribe and then calls in favours. The second is a loud, ambitious officer who forces jobs on the leads to damage a rival agency. A billionaire who co-owns a private security army and has a film studio uses the crew for car thefts and does not pay. The drug lord demands money from the retired robber.

## The opening and the scene players retell

The game opens with a bank robbery in a snowbound town in the far north, nine years before the present. The player is, in turn, the retired robber and the old partner, a crew of three plus a driver who dies at once. The player drives, shoots and holds off police in the snow. The job goes wrong. A sniper hits the retired robber and the third crewman, and the old partner flees into a field while the other two are left behind.

A funeral follows. The retired robber, officially dead, watches his own burial from a distance, and the sniper stands among the mourners. The story then jumps to the present, where the retired robber lives in the city under a changed name on a deal with the first agent.

The young repo man is shown repossessing cars, and takes the retired robber's car while the robber is hiding in it. The retired robber has him crash a car into the dealership and beats the dealer, and the jobless young man asks to be taught. They fall in together after rescuing the robber's son from boat thieves. The robber then finds his wife with her tennis coach and, with the young man, wrecks the house the coach is hiding in, which belongs to the drug lord's mistress. The drug lord demands two and a half million dollars. The planner organises a jewellery store robbery to pay it. The old partner sees the robbery on the news, realises his friend is alive, and wipes out a biker gang and a family of drug rivals before going to the city.

## The middle

The middle is most of the game and is built from a repeating cycle. A heist is planned, its set-up missions are played one after another, the heist itself is played, and the money comes in. Between them the game fills the time with work forced on the leads, family scenes, the old partner's desert business and optional missions. The main list runs to about seventy entries. The optional "strangers and freaks" missions run to nearly sixty more for the three leads, in chains of one to eight.

The heists run in order of size. The jewellery store job comes first. The player picks one of two approaches, quiet with gas grenades or loud and violent, and a driver, a gunman and a computer expert, each taking a share. A better crew costs more and makes fewer mistakes. One source says a cheap crew member who survives rises in skill and is better by the last heist, while the cheapest gunman here cannot be used again. The second is a raid on a ship by sea or helicopter, to steal a secret device from the private army. It pays nothing, and the leads return the device. The third is a small-town bank robbery with no choice of approach, where a cheap gunman means a companion dies though the player still pays him. The fourth is a break-in at the headquarters of the government bureau, by parachute or disguised as firefighters. The fifth, saved for the end, is the gold store job. In these sources the choices change the money and the difficulty of the job, and nothing in the story afterwards.

The agents force a good deal of the work. One mission puts the old partner in a warehouse with a prisoner the agents believe is a spy. The player picks the methods in any order, from pliers to waterboarding to jump leads on a car battery, and revives the man with an adrenaline shot if his heart stops. Each session gives a clue to a man the retired robber then finds at a beach party and kills. The agent orders the prisoner killed. The old partner lets him go, tells him to leave the country for good, and says that torture does not work. The young repo man is sent to assassinate businessmen so the billionaire can profit on the stock market. In a street deal the old partner finds the "cocaine" is drywall, just before the gang attacks. In the yoga scene the retired robber, arguing with his wife at home, charges the instructor and ends in the pool, and she announces the family is leaving.

Two optional stories show the rest. In one, the retired robber answers a cult's online form, then pays larger and larger donations, delivers cars, drives around with a tracker collecting trinkets, buys robes, sleeps in them and runs across the desert in them. At the end the leader asks for a final large donation, and the player can hand it over and get a broken tractor, or keep about two million dollars and receive angry calls. In the other, the old partner takes jobs from an odd pair who steal celebrity souvenirs, which leads to a kidnapped actor. In the last mission he drives the man in the boot to a railway line, and the player decides whether to let him out for a little cash or park on the tracks and let a train hit the car. A third chain has the young repo man ride a photographer's bike beside a limousine and film a celebrity couple at a hotel pool, until the couple crash chasing them.

The family scenes run through all of it. In time the retired robber reunites the family, rescues his daughter from a stalker and his son from kidnappers. The old partner kidnaps the drug lord's wife and takes her into the desert, where the two form a bond.

## The turn

The old partner goes to the far north and learns that the grave of the retired robber holds the body of the crewman he believed to be in prison. The heist was not an accident. The retired robber had made a deal with the first agent to be shot, to vanish with a new name and pay the agent a bribe, and it cost the crewman his life.

The two men draw guns on each other. Gangsters who mistake the retired robber for the old partner's lover attack, and the old partner sabotages his friend's car and later refuses to help. The robber is taken, and the young repo man frees him in a warehouse. The two older men are later attacked together by both bureaus and the private army, and in that fight they make an uneasy peace.

## The last act

After the break-in at the government bureau, the billionaire cancels the film for the insurance, plans to turn the studio into flats, and sends the private army after the retired robber's family. The player fights through the retired robber's house to free them. A lawyer holds the only copy of the film, and the retired robber chases her to the airport, where she is sucked into a jet turbine. The film is saved, and the premiere follows. The second agent tells the young repo man to kill the old partner, who is a liability. The crew then plans the big job.

The final heist is the gold store. The player picks a quiet approach, with an electromagnetic pulse and trucks through a tunnel, or a loud one, with a train and an attack helicopter, and picks the crew accordingly. The take is close to two hundred million dollars. The player plays each stage, switching between the three, and the job succeeds.

The three are rich and, for a short time, free. The billionaire and the second agent each go to the young repo man. The billionaire wants the retired robber dead, because the robber is the cause of his losses, and the agent wants the old partner dead. The player is the young repo man at the decision.

## The endings

There are three endings, picked through the young repo man. What decides each is his choice, and it is permanent. For the third he must call the planner before the mission marker appears.

In the first, he kills the old partner, on the billionaire's request. He lures him to a meeting and draws a gun, and the partner flees in a truck. The retired robber rams it into a petrol tanker. The old partner calls them traitors and tells the young man to take the shot, and the young man or the retired robber shoots the fuel and the old partner burns to death. The last image is the two surviving men parting, the retired robber needing time away, and the player then controls the two and shares out the dead man's cut. Both the billionaire and the agent live.

In the second, he kills the retired robber, on the agent's request. The old partner refuses to help him. The young man meets the retired robber, who takes a call from his wife about their daughter starting college, and chases him to a power station. After a fight he pushes the robber off a tower, then grabs him, and the player is asked to pull him up or let go. The robber dies either way. The young man then phones his friend to make amends. The old partner disowns him, the dead man's wife curses him, the son rings both men, and the player then controls the young man and the old partner, with the dead man's cut going to his family.

In the third, he refuses both and calls the planner, who lures the agents and the private army into an ambush at a foundry with a rumour about the gold. The three survive and split up to hunt their enemies. The old partner kills the second agent, the retired robber kills the gang member who betrayed the young man's friend, and the young man kills a crime boss who threatened them. The old partner knocks the billionaire out at his mansion and puts him in a car boot, and the three push the car off a mountain into the sea, where it explodes. All three live and keep their shares. The last scene is the three retired from crime, still friends, awkwardly. The planner sends each of them his untouched cut.
