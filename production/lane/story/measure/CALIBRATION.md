## What was changed, and why

After round 1 (not counted as the one fix: it was a pilot on three games, run before the eight-game file landed). The ruler put RDR2 well below KCD2, against the players. The causes were in the method: the summaries gave away turns and endings in their first section; only one page told side stories and comic scenes; the ending was tested by how it is computed and not by how it lands; one test ("told, not found") was our own readers' complaint and no pattern in the reviews. The format became version 2 and all four pages were redone. The ruler was rebuilt from the eight-game file as version 2.

After round 2 (the one fix). The ruler now ranked the references as the players do (RDR2 first), passed RDR2 on the moment that hits hard and the ending, passed GTA V on humour and failed KCD2 on rails. It still disagreed in three places, each a test written more strictly than anything the reviews say:
1. "A lead who is somebody" demanded a named flaw. Players praise the leads of all three games; the test failed all three for most readers. The flaw was dropped from the test: a want and a change, or the cost of not changing.
2. "Missions on rails" failed any story with one ending. Players mean missions that fail you for doing it your way. The test was narrowed to that.
3. "A rushed or weak ending" failed any ending picked at the end. No player in the GTA V sample complains of that. The clause was removed.

Round 3 used three new readers. It is the measure.

## Where the ruler agrees with the players, and where not (round 3)

Agrees:
- The order of the three references: RDR2 first (median 66, overall 7, 8, 7), then KCD2 (60; 6, 7, 6), then GTA V (47; 4, 6, 4). The larger study has 47%, 32% and 28% of reviews praising each story.
- RDR2 passes the lead, the bond, the ending that lands, and (two of three readers) the moment that hits hard: what its players praise.
- GTA V passes humour and the bond between its two older leads; its weak marks are the middle as a string of forced jobs and leads who do not change, which is what its unhappy players and three of its eight critics say.
- KCD2 passes the friendship, the people, the comedy and choices that come back; it fails the moment that hits hard (1 of 43 of its reviews praise one) and loses its ending marks for the revenge taken out of the lead's hands.
- No reader's overall mark contradicts his own checklist order, except that the editor ranks RDR2 above KCD2 overall with fewer points, and says why.

Disagrees, and stays so:
- Missions on rails. Players fault it in KCD2 (5 of 43) and RDR2 (3 of 26). After the fix nobody fails it, because a plot summary from public sources does not say where a mission forces the way. The ruler cannot see this fault. It is the main thing LEDGER's pillar (play first) cares about, so it must be judged in the built game, not here.
- GTA V's people. Its players praise the characters more than anything (15 of 18); two of three readers fail its lead test and its "people you could tell apart" test. The page tells the three leads' wants but little of what its players like about them: voice and performance.
- "A turn that surprises" is failed by all three references from every reader. A well-known plot retold in 2,100 words gives its turn away in its cast list.
- KCD2 is praised in general by 30 of 43 of its story reviews and lands at 6 to 7. Its page is the thinnest sourced (one account of the opening, no source for what the hands do in most quests).

## Known limits

1. The Passenger's page is a summary of a plan; the other three are summaries of shipped games made from public plot accounts. A plan states its intentions (choices that come back, a turn planted fairly); a plot account reports only what happened. The format's rule "instances, not claims" narrows this and does not remove it. The Passenger reaching the top of the checklist with every reader in rounds 2 and 3 is partly this: the bible was written, over fifteen rounds, against readers who asked for exactly these things, and the ruler has no item it does not answer on paper.
2. The ruler has a ceiling. It can say that a story has no fault players name; it cannot say how good the story is beyond that. Above about 75 the overall mark carries the judgement.
3. A summary strips performance, music and play. Characters and writing, the two things players praise most, live largely there. The writing is carried at half weight for that reason.
4. The readers are one model family with three lenses, not players. Nine readers across three rounds agreed on the order of the four stories in rounds 2 and 3 (every reader: The Passenger, RDR2, KCD2, GTA V); they could share a blind spot.
5. The eight-game study's samples are small (18 to 43 reviews about the story per game); its shares are directions. Side stories, choices, a sagging middle, too much talk and "seen coming" are named in fewer than five of the eight games and carry no points; they are marked as watch items.
6. The reference pages hold errors of detail (the sources disagree on how RDR2's ending is decided; KCD2's opening rests on one account; GTA V's main encyclopaedia article could not be read). Names of places were replaced, but a reader may still recognise a story.
