# The summary format (one format for all four stories), version 2

Written 20 November 2026 for the story measure. Every story that is put on the ruler is first written down in this one format, at about the same length, so that a reader compares page with page and not a document with a reputation.

## Rules

- About 2,100 words in all (1,900 to 2,300). Plain prose, present tense, British spelling. No bullet lists, no tables, no bold, no italics, no em-dashes.
- Exactly these eight headings, in this order, each as a level-two heading:
  1. What it is about
  2. The lead
  3. The people who matter
  4. The opening and the scene players retell
  5. The middle
  6. The turn
  7. The last act
  8. The endings
- Summarise faithfully. Do not improve the story and do not weaken it. Do not judge it: no praise, no blame, no words such as "famous", "acclaimed", "moving", "weak", "iconic". Say what happens, who wants what, what it costs, and in what order the player meets it.
- Say what the player does as well as what happens, wherever the source says: what the hands are doing in the stretch (riding, driving, robbing, fighting, talking, working), where the player chooses, and what the choice changes. Say plainly where the player has no choice.
- Under "The middle", describe how the long middle stretch is filled: what repeats, what changes, how long it runs relative to the whole.
- Under "The endings", give every ending, what decides which one the player gets, and the last scene or image of each.
- Replace proper names with roles wherever the sense survives it: "the lead", "his father", "the young lord", "the gang's leader", "the bookkeeper", "the doorman". Keep one role for one person throughout. Do not give the story's title, the game's title, the studio, the series, the year of release or the platform. Real place names and dates of the setting may stay (a country, a year), since the setting is part of the story.
- Version 2 additions, made after the first calibration showed the pages were not alike in three ways:
  - "What it is about" says the situation, the lead's position and the question the story puts. It must not give away the turn, who dies, or how it ends; those belong under their own headings.
  - Under "The middle", tell two optional side stories in two or three sentences each (who, what the player does, its turn or choice), if the story has any; and say how much optional story there is beside them.
  - Somewhere under the headings, describe concretely the one or two scenes that are meant to be funny, if the story has any, and the one or two gravest.
  - Give instances, never claims. Do not repeat a source's own claims about the story (that choices matter, that no stretch repeats, that people react); give the instance the source gives, or leave it out. Likewise say that the player has no choice only where the source says so of a named stretch.
- Do not mention performances, actors, music, graphics, sales, awards or reviews.

## Its known limit

A summary strips performance, music and play. The readers judge all four stories from the page alone, alike. A story whose strength lives in an actor's voice or in the feel of the controls loses that here; so does every other.
