**Would it stand at or above the best of the three shipped stories as a story players live through? NO. Confidence about 65%.**

On paper it is more carefully built than any of the shipped plot accounts. It is honest about what is fixed, and its two friendships are written for the hands. It falls short of the best shipped story on three counts:
- The central secret, its turn and its gravest scenes belong to two people beside the lead. Tom's own arc ends at the wall, and that walk decides only the yard, which the bible itself calls the "weather" of the ending.
- The present-day jeopardy for a crime game is a VAT inspection, a lease, a licence and one car into a wall.
- The decisive Friday rests on two Thursday errands plus an optional question button. The question that unlocks Sheila's "to write my own name" is something "the game never tells him when" to ask.

The best shipped story puts the blow and the choice on its lead's own body and bond. This one puts them on the people he drives.

**Faults that can be mended on paper:**
1. The lead watches the central drama. His deed (the wall) decides a side outcome, not the main one.
2. The ending's rule contradicts itself (door or weather), and the burn path rests on a thread that no longer exists.
3. The red herring collapses in scene 12, before the turn.
4. Present-day menace is thin. Danny half-comic, the Widow never threatening and Agar apologising leave no opponent with a face who frightens anyone.
5. Key pay-offs are gated on optional inputs (the "Asked" page, the initials, unpacking the suitcase).
6. Act III crams roughly sixteen beats into 3.5 hours, including a death, a funeral, a car theft, a crash, a break-in, an inspector's week, a kitchen confession, two errands, the yard gate, the drive and the race. The bible's own "5 in the first week; 9 beats in the second" understates it.

**Things that can only be judged once built:**
1. Whether players notice the small clues (the struck-off repair, the sevens like ones, the bollard's paint) in a 15-hour open world.
2. Whether the dialect lines land as written, given they are only "samples of the voice"; scripted delivery of the few load-bearing lines is essential.
3. Whether counting coin, writing fare lines and slow lessons hold up as play over eight hours, or sag.
4. Whether an unvoiced lead with a notebook carries the corridor and the steps.
5. Whether the four comic set pieces work with real timing.
6. Whether the hold-to-take button reads as character or as a chore.

**My answer rests on the first list.** Even assuming a perfect build, faults 1, 4 and 5 put it below the best shipped story. The second list decides whether it reaches even second place.

# C. Changes of shape, most important first

1. **Make the lead's deed decide the main outcome, not the yard.** Merge the walk along the wall with the "truth made whole" condition: the men who stand up for him are also the witnesses (Shiner, the landing men, the tallyman) whose word lets Sheila sign. Agar's padlock becomes the obstacle on the same walk, not a separate door. Then Tom's one change (he asks) is what puts her name on the paper. *Answers C1, H1, and the risk that the lead only watches.*
2. **Fix the ending's door, and make it readable.** State one rule. For example: Sheila signs if and only if Tom brings her the statements and asks her. Spell out the burn input as an act in her kitchen. Delete the dead "washed" path. *Answers E1 and the contradiction between sections 8 and 9.*
3. **Keep the false suspect alive until the morning of the 18th.** In scene 12 the Bishop should say only "he came back for me", with no word of being carried on Ron's back before the car returned. Move the eleven-mile dial reading to the evening of the 17th, so "sure and wrong" holds until Ron's words at the mouth of the row. *Answers T1, S5, and the risk that the turn is guessed early.*
4. **Give the present a threat with a face and hands.** Let Danny's campaign escalate across the four weeks through his drivers, in scenes Tom must meet physically: blocked ranks, a fight at the office door, the lock-up. Or let the Widow's men stand across his road more than once. Make Ellis an active hunter who closes in on car two before the inspector does. All of this fits inside the frame (fights, break-ins, no guns). *Answers the reason to care (X1) and the risk of a crime story that is low on stakes.**
5. **Make the load-bearing "Asked" moments certain.** Offer the question at fixed beats to Sheila in her kitchen and to Ron on the quay wall (the hold-to-take press stays optional elsewhere). Script those few lines instead of leaving them as "samples". *Answers H1, E1, and the risk that optional inputs gate the main pay-offs.*
6. **Give Act III room or cut it.** Either remove the Agar fare plus the Kevin crash, or remove the car-two theft and lock-up, folding the surviving pieces into the merged walk from change 1. Or lengthen Act III to about five hours by trimming the first half of Act II. *Answers X5, X2, and the risk that the last act feels rushed.*
