# Driving study, round 4: what a clumsy newcomer finds

19 Nov 2026, corrected the same day after a fresh review of the tester (GATE-REVIEW-2026-11-19.md).

## What was run

An automatic tester (tools/lane/driving-gate) played a saved copy of the round 3 page twice, keyboard first, then a pretend controller. It crashed at about 10 to 40 mph into lines aimed at lamp posts, parked cars and walls (12 crashes on keys, 4 on the controller), reversed out, got out and in, wedged the car at full lock, pressed random keys, paused, resumed, reset and drove at a person. It ran the game's own physics, not real time on a real PC.

What changed in the tester after the review: steering is now measured on the page's own frame loop and judged on the press, the release and the switch from one side to the other; after a crash the tester no longer mends or moves the car before trying Backspace; a message counts as "damage told" only if it appears after the hit and speaks of damage; a person hit and the whole-run stranded count can now fail. Traffic moves differently each run, so some lines hit a passing car instead of their target.

## The seven checks

1. Never stranded: FAIL. Steps: drive along a clear line into a wall or post at full throttle, let go, then hold S 2.5 s and W 2 s. After a 35 mph hit on posts and chain, a 45 mph hit on a traffic car and four smaller hits (15, 12, 10, 20 mph; 9 damage points) the car moved under 0.1 m on reverse and throttle. Getting out and walking always worked (2.45 m in 1.5 s). Backspace, pressed on the car exactly as the crash left it, put it back on the road with the message "Back on the road", but the car stayed dead: power 0, 0.02 to 0.11 m under throttle in 2 s. Only R mended it (6.2 to 6.6 m in 2 s after R). The whole-run count fails too: 4 of 45 stops on keys and 2 of 13 on the controller left the player pressing and not moving. One of those (in "get out and back in") is the tester's own stop with handbrake and S held together, not a game fault. The earlier "undamaged car stuck after random keys" was the tester's mistake: the person was half out of the car; with them back in, the car drove off.
2. Damage told: FAIL. Steps: any crash above 5 mph closing. Dents and crumples (6 to 32 mph, into a lamp post, parked cars, walls, traffic, posts and chain) never show words about damage within 3 s; only the body shows it. "The car will not drive on. R repairs it." shows once, at the moment of a hit of about 35 mph or more, then nothing. A car killed by smaller hits gets no message at all. After Backspace the only words are "Back on the road", which is wrong for a dead car. The controller has no repair button, and the message names R.
3. Smooth on keys: FAIL. Steps: hold about 30 mph on the long road, then press A, let go, press D, let go, press A, switch to D, switch back to A, let go. Pressing is smooth: the key input takes 0.133 s from 10% to 90% (the page ramps it at 6 per second), the car's steering 0.27 s, the yaw 0.2 s. Letting go is not: the input drops at 12 per second (0.067 s, 0.2 of full lock a frame), and the car's steering falls from about a third of lock to straight in 0.013 to 0.033 s (up to 0.23 of full lock in one frame). Switching A to D or D to A moves the car's steering in 0.065 to 0.069 s (up to 0.22 a frame). The limit is 0.1 s and 0.15 a frame. Separately, the yaw rate jumped 0.85 and 1.37 rad/s in one frame in the middle of holds, 0.3 to 0.65 s after any key change; cause unconfirmed, possibly a kerb.
4. Smooth on pad: FAIL. Steps: hold 30 mph, push the stick over 80 ms, let it spring back, push the other way. The car's steering follows the stick within a frame either way: at stick 0.6 it reaches its move in 0.017 to 0.022 s, and on letting go it drops 0.42 to 0.51 of full lock in one frame. Yaw jumped 1.78 rad/s in one frame 0.45 s into a hold, cause unconfirmed. The tester's pad runs at stick 0.25 and 1.0 were cut short by a collision on the test road, so only stick 0.6 was judged in full.
5. Camera clear: PASS, with a limit. Never inside a wall in any step and no crash picture was one flat colour (largest 19 percent). The check uses the page's own camera collision world, which leaves out lamp posts, parked cars and traffic, so it can only catch walls and buildings and is close to always true by construction.
6. No errors, nothing frozen: PASS on keys, one controller result unconfirmed. No page errors except the blocked fonts. Pause and resume by Esc worked. Pause and resume by Start worked in the first run; in this run the car moved only 0.25 m after resuming, right after the full-stick steering run had hit something and left the car stuck. Re-check next round.
7. Person hurt, never killed: FAIL. Steps: hold one walker still, drive straight at them from 10 m at 12 mph. The car touched the person; they stayed "walking" (pushed aside, then back to their path), were not hurt, and nothing showed on screen within 3 s. The page's notes say a person cannot be hurt, only pushed aside; the rule asks for hurt, never killed, and told.

## The owner's complaints

(a) Stranded by one hit on a pole: CONFIRMED at about 35 mph and over, refuted below. A lamp post at 10 mph (dented) and at 19 mph (crumpled) left the car drivable. Posts and chain at 35 mph killed it until R, as did a lamp post at 37 mph in the first run and a traffic car at 45 mph. The damage stage goes by closing speed (will not drive on from about 35 mph), and 8 damage points from smaller hits kill it too. Cause: damage.js Damage.hit sets power to 0; main.js handle sends Backspace to sim.recover, which moves the car but does not mend it; the only message comes from main.js afterStep, for one big hit only; input.js PAD_EVENTS has no repair.

(b) Keyboard steering jerky: CONFIRMED on letting go and on switching sides, not on pressing. Pressing a key ramps in smoothly. Letting go snaps: the key input falls twice as fast as it rose (input.js keySteerOut 12 against keySteerIn 6), and the driving help then takes the wheels from a third of lock to straight, or across, in one or two frames (vehicle.js handSteer: below 0.02 of input it switches to self-centring). On the stick it is quicker and bigger, because the stick goes straight to the wheels.

## Fixes, most important first

1. Never strand: Backspace also mends, or the wreck limps at walking pace. Gate: 0.5 m of movement within 5 s of every crash, no hidden key, and no stranded stop in the whole run.
2. Repair on the controller, message worded for the device. Gate: a controller wreck recovers with pad buttons only.
3. A lasting line while the car is dead, however it got there, and Backspace not saying "Back on the road" over a dead car. Gate: the line present in every dead row.
4. Damage stage in words after each dent or crumple. Gate: a message with damage words within 1 s of every hit over 5 mph.
5. Smooth the release and the switch on keys: let the key input fall no faster than it rises (keySteerOut at or under 8 per second), and ease the driving help's hand-over to self-centring instead of switching at 0.02 of input. Gate: press, release and A to D each take at least 0.1 s from 10% to 90% in the key input and the car's steering, and no frame changes the car's steering by more than 0.15 of full lock.
6. Ease the stick into the wheels the same way on release. Gate: the same check for the car's steering on the pad.
7. A person hit is hurt, never killed, and the screen says so. Gate: a walker driven at 12 mph ends hurt, not dead, with a message within 3 s.
8. Find the yaw jumps in the middle of holds. Gate: the same check with kerb contact logged.
9. Gate's own: stop without holding S and handbrake together (it counts as a stranded stop and reverses the gearbox); a pad steering road clear of traffic; lines at lamp posts at 30 and 40 mph that traffic cannot cross.
