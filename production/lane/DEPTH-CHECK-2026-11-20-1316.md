# Depth check: is the one-shot's work real? (checkout of 12:46, 20 Nov 2026)

Read only; nothing run, nothing changed. Twelve parts whose "Now:" was "designed" (so all their code is from today), drawn at random per stage (seed 1308) with 1.4.5, 2.5.5 and 2.6.10 forced in: stage A 1.4.5, 2.5.5, 2.6.10, 3.4.2; stage B 2.7.6, 1.3.11, 3.2.7; stage C 1.7.4, 3.1.2, 3.5.5; stage D 3.1.3, 6.2.5. Judged against the checklist line, the addendum, and DESIGN.md where a copy was at hand.

Totals. Behaviour real 5 (3.4.2, 2.7.6, 3.2.7, 3.1.3, 6.2.5); thin 5 (2.5.5, 1.3.11, 1.7.4, 3.1.2, 3.5.5); fake 2 (1.4.5, 2.6.10). Test strong 4 (3.1.2, 3.5.5, 3.1.3, 6.2.5); weak 7 (2.5.5, 2.6.10, 3.4.2, 2.7.6, 1.3.11, 3.2.7, 1.7.4); none 1 (1.4.5). Real behaviour with a strong test: 2 of 12 (3.1.3, 6.2.5). The quick count called 10 of these 12 "done". Of those 10, one is fake (2.6.10), four are thin (2.5.5, 1.3.11, 3.1.2, 3.5.5), and five have only weak tests.

The pattern behind the gap:
- Most parts are written twice: a "rule piece" (a pure function in src/game/pieces/) with rows in test/cases/*.json, and a system file that is meant to use it. The rows all pass, but in several parts the game never calls the function, or ignores what it returns. Those rows prove the rule table, not the game. The 491 rule rows are the main reason "a test touches it" is true almost everywhere.
- The in-game tests (six files, about 430 lines for roughly 170 parts) often set state by hand, call the system's function directly, and check a flag or a record, not what the player meets.
- Several features end in data nobody reads: a list that is filled and only ever pruned, an event that nothing listens for, a string written to the Ledger.

Part by part:

1.4.5 Reaching the witness (A). Behaviour: fake. Test: none. No code anywhere names it. DESIGN 2 "The walk": a witness who means to tell sets off and is seen going, and Tom can reach them first. In the build, statements are still the old nightly batch in police.js (`// run once per night: who went to the police about what they saw`), and the real town's report arrives as an hourly record (`logLine(... + ' went to the police about the damage', 'police')`) with nobody walking anywhere. The one trace of a walk is a multiplier that is worked out and never used: `walkStartsTimes: edge ? T.edgeWalkStartsSoonerTimes : 1` (pieces/law.js). Nothing to intercept, no test.

2.5.5 Finding an unseen deed (A). Behaviour: thin. Test: weak. A38 says whoever's routine next passes the trace finds it (the owner on return, the cleaner at six), and the finder becomes an aftermath witness. The game handles bodies only, and the "routine" is one made-up passer at a fixed hour, wherever the body lies: `EYES.fns.found({ t: b.t, trace: 'a body' }, [{ id: 'the first dock worker', t: (Math.floor(b.t / 1440) + 1) * 1440 + T.bodyFoundAtHour * 60 }])`. The finder is not a person, holds no memory and reports nothing; the result is a Ledger line and `ellisEvent('body')`. Forced doors, glass and missing things are never found by game code. The real town's own `found` list is copied into `x.found` and never read. The rule rows test `found()` properly, but the in-game check (`t.ok(w.found >= 1, 'people whose routines pass it found the damage (A38)')`) follows a deed Ada saw, so it is not an unseen deed at all, and the number comes from the older town code.

2.6.10 Police after a deed (A). Behaviour: fake. Test: weak. A36: an officer goes to the spot and stays, a body brings tape and Ellis next morning, the spot is watched until morning. The rule function returns a fixed description: `{ officer: true, withinGameMinutes: ..., tape: !!deed.body, torchAtNight: true, ellisNextMorning: !!deed.body, ... }`. The game ignores most of it. It only pushes an entry onto a list and writes an internal log line: `x.watched.push({ x: E.x, z: E.z, untilT: ..., tape: r.tape, e: E.id }); logLine('An officer went to the spot: ' + ...)`. Nothing reads `watched` (`watchedNear` is exported and never called, and the list is only ever pruned), no officer is moved, no tape or torch appears, and `G.log` is never shown to the player. The Ellis step fires at once, not next morning, and on a killing it fires twice. The rule rows check the fixed object; there is no in-game check, although crime.mjs lists 2.6.10 in its header.

3.4.2 Wrinkles (A). Behaviour: real. Test: weak. Four of the seven wrinkles change the fare in play: the hurry has a deadline and a tip (`return { paid: f.pay + (made ? T.hurryTipPence : 0) }`), the runner bolts and starts a foot chase (`startChase(P.id, { why: 'runner' })`), the wait keeps the cab at a door or drives off, and the parcel puts an unlawful parcel under the coat. The face (Alison) asks a real question. The tail is lines only: Wayne says "Stay behind it", but there is no tail mechanic, and the fact is learned whatever Tom does (`if (F_.wrinkle === 'tail') return { paid: f.pay };`). The test checks the data, not the play: `['talker', 'parcel', 'tail', 'runner', 'wait', 'hurry', 'face'].every((w) => sp.wr.includes(w))`, plus "no two fares alike" and the `mayStage` rows. No test drives any wrinkle to its ending.

2.7.6 Fencing (B). Behaviour: real. Test: weak. Reachable in play (`"Show Rita what he has to sell"` in her shop when Tom holds goods from a search). It runs a haggle of at most three offers through choices, priced from her purse and how hot the goods are; the money goes into the tin (`earn(r.price, true, ...)`); she refuses her own goods and bars him after. Small gap: "she will remember he walked" is a Ledger line only, with no state kept. The in-game test presses keys and then asserts only `f2.h.length === 1 && f2.h[0].offers <= 3`. The tin and the goods count are collected but never checked, so a sale that paid nothing would pass. The `haggle` rule rows are good, but the row "the-fence-pays-0.4-into-the-tin" tests `sell()`, whose price the game throws away (`r0` is used only for `r0.ok`).

1.3.11 Marks that last (B). Behaviour: thin. Test: weak. ER-MARK-01: a marked Tom is caught more, and marks fade. The fading exists (`marked = () => E.marks(dayNow() - X('foot').beatenDay)`). "Caught more" exists only in the rule function: `caught()` rises to a mark rung when `s.marked` is set, but neither game call to `E.caught` (foot.js, lines 169 and 271) passes `marked`, and real witnesses go through seeing.js, which knows nothing of marks. The only effect in play is one greeting line, and because the line is chosen by `GREET[g][(dayNow() + P.id.length) % 2]`, half the time a bruised Tom hears `'"That coat again."'` instead of `'"Somebody has been at your face."'`. The limp is computed and used nowhere. The rule rows ("marked-he-is-caught-more") pass against a path the game never takes; there is no in-game test.

3.2.7 Calling on people (B). Behaviour: real (small). Test: weak. A hotspot appears only at the friend's place, in her hours, with her there (`if (!P || P.dead || hourNow() < f.fromHour || hourNow() >= f.untilHour) continue;` then `'Sit down with ' + ...`). A second call inside 20 game hours gets "You again"; a visit after a seen crime asks about last night, and a lie ends the friendship. Weak points: the scene is the same two written lines every time, and any visit without a question counts as honest (`honest = true`), so four repeat calls make the friendship "hold" (3.2.5) with no honesty involved. Tests: only the `visit`/`friendship` rule rows, which check a function returning fixed values; nothing in play is tested.

1.7.4 Men at his side (C). Behaviour: thin. Test: weak. "With me" makes the man follow at about 2 m on the street and records a sighting every two game minutes (`act('stood_with', ...)`), and he is moved to Tom if more than 40 m behind. A16's "stands in a fight: he takes the second man, so two against two is a fair fight" is not built: fight.js `allies()` counts only the opponent's lads (`if ((P.lad && Q.lad) || ...)`), and the rule `atSide = (men) => ({ hisSide: 1 + men.length, ... })` is never called by the game. Waiting at doors and riding in the car are not handled (he follows only `t.space === 'street'`). Test: the rule row checks the unused `atSide`; in game, rise.mjs checks only the flag `c.man('ron').with`.

3.1.2 Act II, the Rise and the Squeeze (C). Behaviour: thin. Test: strong. The frame is real: beats fire on states rather than days, the act ends at the sit-down or after 7 weeks, and dead weeks are counted. But eight of the nine beats are a subtitle and a Ledger line with no effect (`tell(b.says, '', 8); write(b.says, ...)`). "The ground is open, and all three come sooner" does not make the rivals come sooner, and "DS Ellis joins the street crimes to the fire: one case" changes nothing. `emit('acts.beat', ...)` has no listener. Only "collision" (a short scene at Ada's) plays out. The test is end to end for what exists: real state (a bought car, a real recruit) fires "fleet" and "firstRecruit", and a sit-down played by key presses opens Act III with the letter. It would catch the frame breaking. It does not cover the checklist's "every beat reachable" (2 of 9 beats in game, 3 in the rows).

3.5.5 Sit-downs (C). Behaviour: thin. Test: strong. One scene with three answers (accept, defy, counter if he knows something). The outcome is a string (`state: 'under ' + s.head` / `'at war with ' + s.head` / `'holds ... off'`), stored in `x.sitDown` and written to the Ledger. Nothing reads it: endings, the dial and rival behaviour are the same whichever answer is given, and all three just end Act II. The test plays the scene by key press and checks `/at war with Mr Agar/` and that Act III opens; the rule rows cover all three answers. Strong for what is built, but what is built is close to a token.

3.1.3 Act III, the Ledger Comes Due (D). Behaviour: real. Test: strong. It matches DESIGN's Act III almost line for line: the courteous letter, one item each morning at 9 (`if (d >= x.insp.startDay && h === 9 ...) inspectionMorning()`), produce it or ask in writing, graded by how far the books are off, Sheila's tip if she trusts him, Ellis's trade, the successor's offer, and the result feeding the ending (`booksHold: a.insp ? a.insp.result === 'the books hold' : ...`). Shortfalls: it is five short scenes, not DESIGN's 2 to 3 hours; "on the last day he can reach only a few people" is missing; and asking in writing can be repeated every morning without limit (`if (choice === 'in writing') return { ..., grade: 'put off a day', ... }` and the day never advances), so a player can stall the inspection, and with it the ending, for ever. The test runs five mornings through the scene, checks the grades and "the books hold", and shows the ending changes when the result is "do not hold"; the rule rows cover fails, questions and the fifth morning.

6.2.5 Tom's reading (D). Behaviour: real (small). Test: strong. Built only from signs shown in play (`sign()` is called by Ellis's stages, the inspector, the friend, the trade and the successor). It shows on the full-stop key as a page, is written into the Ledger at each act's end (`on('act.end', (e) => write('How things stand at the end of Act ' + ...))`), and holds back an ending that would cost him until its sign is shown (`mayLand`). Small: four fixed sentences, and the key is never taught to the player. The test adds an inspector sign and checks the reading turns from quiet; the rule rows check the expected ending for each mix of signs.
