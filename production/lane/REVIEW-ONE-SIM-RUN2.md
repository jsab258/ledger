## Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES

The 12 earlier findings are closed (table below). The front door and the bridge do what the tests say they do, the measurements are honestly made and honestly limited, nothing is published, nothing is claimed as allowed that the allowlist does not carry, and nothing under ledger/ or ue-probe/ was changed. There are no em-dashes.

But the bridge's contract has holes a player would fall through at the swap, and the list of differences misses two of the largest changes for the player: in the real week only the one damage deed ever reaches the police, and a second deed wipes out the first. The three must-fixes are about records and the bridge's own checks, not the build. They must be done before step 4 starts, not before anything else.

## Findings, worst first

1. **must. The differences miss that, in the real week, only the damage deed reaches the police. His violence never does.** TownWeek.h NineReports (lines 121 to 138) reports only the week's one deed topic, as Offence::Damage. A sighting passed through `town.saw` goes into the talk but never into a statement. Measured here through the bridge: Darren sees him hit a man (saw, rung 4, Tuesday 12:30). Over six days nobody goes to the police, nobody comes for him, and DS Ellis comes once, for "talk". In the prototype the same blow brings a statement and five hours in the cells (DIFFERENCES.md line 110, prototype column). DIFFERENCES.md section 5 (lines 106 to 115) lists cell times for a wounding (8) and a robbery (12) as if the bridge could reach them. Only the raw front door can (`sim.policeReport`, `sim.takeIn`, used by T10's held week at checks.mjs line 305). The bridge has no call for them.
   Fix: add a row to section 5 and a line to "The short answer": "Through the bridge only the damage deed is reported. A blow, a weapon or a killing seen by a witness becomes talk and can bring DS Ellis, but never a statement or an arrest." Add it to the decisions at the end: report violence through the bridge (a call over `policeReport` and `takeIn`, held to the rows that exist for them), or accept the gap for the first swap. In BRIDGE.md "Not done yet", name it.

2. **must. One deed a week: a second deed silently wipes out the first one's witnesses.** TownWeek.h Deed (lines 103 to 106) overwrites DeedTopic, DeedDay, DeedAt and Witnesses. Measured here: Rita's window seen by Darren on Tuesday at 12:30 leads to Darren's statement on Wednesday at nine and an arrest on Thursday at ten. Add a second deed that evening (a crate on the quay, nobody seeing it), and Darren never goes to the police and nobody comes for him. A player who learns this can erase a seen deed by doing an unseen one. Neither BRIDGE.md nor DIFFERENCES.md says the real week holds one deed. The prototype holds every act as its own event.
   Fix: in the bridge, refuse a second `deed` in the same week with a plain reason (or keep the first until the Core changes), and test that refusal in T11. In DIFFERENCES.md, add the row "how many deeds the week follows: prototype every one, real rules one". Add a finding for the Core's owner in PROGRESS: Deed replaces the earlier deed's witnesses, and no golden row covers two deeds.

3. **must. Old prototype saves: the recommendation goes against the prototype's own standing rule.** BRIDGE.md line 81 and DIFFERENCES.md line 149 recommend that a save from before the swap "is not offered", because "the prototype's save version must go up". The prototype has kept old saves loading through every change and says so each time: CHANGELOG.md lines 144, 217, 222 and 325, DESIGN_NOTES.md lines 28 and 36, and a test for it (test/oldsave.mjs). The task said old saves must keep loading. Nothing forces the version up. saving.js restore (lines 62 to 70) already fills in keys an older save lacks (`measures`), so a missing `town` key can be handled the same way.
   Fix: change the recommendation to: a save with no `town` opens a fresh town at the save's clock (`openTown({ startMinute: o.t })`), and each first-hand memory about him in the old save's minds is fed in at that minute through `town.saw` (rung from the memory's face score and `who`), with the damage deed through `town.deed`. Keep the save version, and add the case to test/oldsave.mjs in step 4. Say what the player loses. Measured here: a town opened on Wednesday at 14:00 has Monday's ask "undelivered" and the tea never asked, because Ada asks at ten. So a week loaded after a beat has passed skips that beat. If he prefers "not offered", that is a scope choice for his list, with the cost stated, not a default.

4. **should. `deed` accepts witnesses and areas the town does not know, and says nothing.** one-sim-bridge.mjs lines 194 to 197 pass any id through, and `deed` returns nothing. Measured: a deed seen by 'pauline' (one of the prototype's 19 people with no match) in area 'no_such_area' is taken. Nobody holds the story, nobody finds the damage, nobody goes to the police, and no error is raised. `tick(now, area)` takes any area string too. At the swap this is the usual case, not a rare one: 19 of the prototype's 28 people have no id in the cast.
   Fix: in `deed`, check each witness id against `sim.people()` and the area against the cast file's areas. Refuse an unknown area. Return the witnesses taken and the ones dropped, as `saw` already returns true or false. Do the same for `tick`'s area. Add both to T11.

5. **should. Three acts skip the out-of-turn check that BRIDGE.md says every act has.** BRIDGE.md line 61: "The bridge refuses an act out of turn and says why." `teaMinute`, `weekAsk` and `weekAsksNow` (one-sim-bridge.mjs lines 209, 212 and 213) do not go through `before()`. Measured: `teaMinute` three days ahead of the clock and `teaMinute(10)` in the past were both accepted. `weekAsk` six days ahead was accepted and returned true.
   Fix: give the three the same two checks as `before()` (not before the town's clock; the hour already ticked), without its talk call, so that T8 still plays the reference week's order. Run T8 again, and add the three to T11's refusals.

6. **should. A save whose pieces disagree is loaded as if whole.** one-sim-bridge.mjs lines 101 to 122 check the kind, the version, the cast, and that the texts look like JSON objects. They do not check that the pieces fit together. Measured, each accepted without a word: `nextHour` 0 on a save at minute 3,480 (the next tick replays 59 hours over a town that has already lived them); `nextHour` 100 hours ahead (100 hours never run); `memories` empty (every person's memory lost, and a second save is no longer the same as the first); `at` of 10^12 (the town is dead, because every later clock "goes back"); `at` with a fraction. BRIDGE.md line 80 says a save with "a piece missing" is refused. The memories are a piece.
   Fix: require `at` to be a whole number; `nextHour` to be floor(at/60)+1, or at/60 when `at` is on the hour; and a memory text for every person in the cast. Add three of these to T11.

7. **should. The cast mark changes with line endings, so a Windows checkout can refuse every save.** `castMark` (one-sim-bridge.mjs lines 49 to 53) hashes the raw text. Measured: the same cast file with CRLF line endings refuses a save made with LF ("made with another cast file"). The repository has no .gitattributes line for .json, the PC is Windows, and the golden table already had to be stripped of CRs for the same reason (one-sim.sh table_current). Any edit to the cast file, even a space, refuses all saves too. That runs into finding 3.
   Fix: mark the cast after removing a BOM and normalising line endings, or mark the parsed cast's people and places. Say in BRIDGE.md what happens to saves when the cast really changes: refused with a plain message, or loaded with the people still present.

8. **should. The differences miss the prototype's later naming of a coat, a cab or a man.** events.js `link` (lines 48 to 73): a witness who remembers "a coat" or "the cab" turns it into him once they learn whose coat or cab it is, and a witness who saw "a man" does the same once they know him, if they saw his face well enough. The Ledger, the names and the liking all follow. In the real rules a story names him only if its first witness recognised him, or if it was told as known (Gossip.h lines 236 to 241, his A5 ruling of 30 September). A shape, a mark or a face belongs to Suspecting alone, which is not behind the door. Section 4's "Is it him" row covers naming at the moment of seeing, not later.
   Fix: add a row to section 4. For the player: a witness who saw only the cab will not later come to name him, unless Suspecting is put behind the door.

9. **should. The differences do not say what the real town can be told.** The prototype's town knows 52 kinds of act, good and bad, each with how newsworthy it is, how it reads and how grave a crime it is (kinds.js). Its talk picks the story to pass on by heat times newsworthiness, never tells people about acts they took part in or that were done to them, and keeps private kinds quiet (gossip.js lines 23 to 39). Its people bring a story up to his face by heat (gossip.js lines 118 to 151). Through the bridge the real town gets one damage deed and sightings named by a fact string. Nothing says which of the 52 kinds become facts, whether good deeds (a debt forgiven, a good word, rent paid) reach it at all, or how the real rounds choose what to pass on. Section 8 names catching two people talking, but not the remarks made to his face.
   Fix: add a section "What the town is told": a table of the 52 kinds, each marked as a deed, a sighting by fact name, or the prototype's own. Say what the real rounds pass first, read in TownRounds.h and Gossip.h. Say that remarks to his face rest on the prototype's heat, which has no counterpart. Then add a decision if any kind has no home.

10. **minor. PROGRESS's five-line summary says more than the evidence shows.** PROGRESS.md line 11: "Driven by a clock in odd steps it gives the C# Core's rows at 225 of 225 hours". That holds only for talk 'by the hour', which is not the bridge's default. The default, 'by the minute', the order the game plays, matches 129 of 225 hours (BRIDGE.md line 126). BRIDGE.md says this plainly. The summary does not.
    Fix: "Played the reference week's way it gives the C# Core's rows at 225 of 225 hours; played the game's way (the default) 129 of 225, the rest differing only in the count of stories and at one hour in who found the damage (finding 6 for the Core's owner)."

Two tools not on the allowlist, one of them not handed over, and one claim not backed.** The Python zstandard module (BSD 3-clause, a tool, never shipped) has no allowlist line and no decision record, and the handover in PROGRESS (lines 66 to 72) names only Playwright. 12. **minor. M3 counts out of 28 people, but two of them can never hear the story.** gossip.js line 74 keeps the police (DS Ellis, PC Ibbotson) out of all talk, and measure.mjs line 116 counts holders out of all 28 named people. So "24 or 25 of 28" (DIFFERENCES.md line 88) is really 24 or 25 of the 26 who can hear it. That strengthens the point, but the number is off.
    Fix: give the denominator as 26, with the reason, or count the police in a note of their own.

13. **minor. A batch of hours and a clock skip inside it.** `tick` returns every hour crossed (one-sim-bridge.mjs lines 166 to 173). If the prototype's handler for an arrest hour calls its own `skipTo` to the release time (the obvious way to write step 4), the nested ticks deliver later hours first, and the rest of the outer batch then arrives late and out of order. That matters on a long jump. During sleep the prototype's skip moves in small steps (clock.js line 72), so the risk is small but real.
    Fix: one line in BRIDGE.md "What the prototype calls each tick": handle the whole list first, and start any clock skip after the loop.
