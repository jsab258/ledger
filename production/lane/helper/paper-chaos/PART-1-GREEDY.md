# Part 1: the greedy exploiter (15 schemes)

28 November 2026. Paper chaos test, job 4. Each scheme is played as a greedy player would, looking for money that keeps coming. Every step is traced to the blueprint (ledger-blueprint-2026-11-28).

Step marks: H handled (the part or rule cited answers it; "rule only" means a general rule in BLUEPRINT-12 states the answer but no part gives it working values yet), S seam (two rules that do not connect, or contradict), M missing (nothing designed), X special case (would work only with something made for this one scheme).

Questions: Q1 would anything break; Q2 do the rules answer it end to end with no special case; Q3 is it fair and enjoyable (no lasting easy win, no dead end, nothing pointless). Verdict: pass, or fail on the question named. Fix numbers refer to the register at the end of this file, merged and ranked in the summary.

## G01 The fare grinder
Frank drives fares from dawn to midnight every day, sleeps through the dead hours and saves every pound for cars.
1. Takes fares at the rank and off the radio. H: EC-01 (80p then £1.10 a mile), 3.4.1 fare people, 3.4.3 Pauline and the radio.
2. Waits through dead hours to the next wave. H: 7.11 (wait until each known appointment; a wait stops for what the town has for him).
3. Keeps the takings. H: 2.7.1 (fares enter the book), RT-17 (pocket, tin, book).
4. Buys a third and fourth car on terms. H: EC-03 (£90 down, £22 a week), 2.15.1.
5. Wins the ferry rank and a second rank by presence. H: 2.15.2 (five days in a fortnight).
6. The other firm notices fewer fares. H rule only: G20 and decision 21 (the other firm's owner and drivers as people).
- Q1 no. Q2 yes. Q3 yes: fares are the slowest lever (£55 an hour, G27's run), capped by ranks and accounts.
- Verdict: pass.

## G02 The fare war
Frank tells regulars he will carry them for half the fare, to starve the other firm and take its ranks.
1. Offers a fare at half price in talk. M: the fare is a fixed formula (EC-01); nothing lets Frank set or cut his own fares, though RT-14 reads an offer's amount exactly.
2. Passengers switch to Mickey's. S: G20 (fare demand follows what the town says of Mickey's) and decision 21 (the other firm reacts to being undercut) promise a reaction to undercutting (and decision 21's row points to 2.9.1, the outfit's asks, a wrong pointer), but 2.15.3 moves fares to the other firm only after a hit-and-run and never the other way.
3. The other firm cuts back or its drivers come asking for work. M: 2.15.3 defines the firm only as a sink for fares after a hit-and-run; no state for a firm losing trade.
4. Frank takes its rank. H: 2.15.2 (a rank won by presence or a word).
5. The flows balance at the lower fare. S: EC-12 and 2.7.7 audit flows, but with no price lever the cut has no flow to audit.
- Q1 no. Q2 no: decision 21 promises what no part designs. Q3 unclear: if built as a free win it would end the other firm with no cost.
- Verdict: fail Q2. Fixes: F07.

## G03 Tobacco at the gate, for ever
Frank buys two cases a week off a boat at the dock gate and sells them round the yards, never more, every week of the run and after.
1. Meets the boat and buys a case. S: EC-07 sets the price (£100) but the hands of it (the meet, carrying a case, where it is kept) are in no part (drill gap C1; 1.1.4 carrying covers a body, not goods by weight).
2. Carries and stores the cases at the office. H: 1.6.9 office storage, G13 objects carry owners and facts.
3. Sells to stalls, the caff's back counter and men in the yards at 80p a pack. H: EC-07 (£160 a case), 2.2.1 a sale is an act with its slots.
4. Repeats at two cases a week. H: EC-07 (two cases a week pass unseen).
5. The newsagent loses trade, says so at two losing weeks and shuts in week 7. H: EC-04, economy 5.2.
6. Week 8 onwards: nothing rises against him. S: EC-07's fixed "two cases pass" sets a ceiling with no learning by customs or buyers, against G27 (each resource costs more the more it is used).
- Q1 no. Q2 no (the hands of smuggling are missing). Q3 no: £120 a week for ever with no rising pressure once the newsagent has gone.
- Verdict: fail Q3. Fixes: F02, F12.

## G04 After the ending, the round pays for ever
The greedy player reaches an ending in week 7 with a fair round and two fronts, then keeps playing to farm them.
1. Ends the story. H: 3.1.4, RT2-04.
2. Play continues after the ending. H: RT2-21 (the clock runs on; fronts and rounds keep paying if the business survived).
3. Rivals stay on their rungs and climb no more; Ellis's stage holds. H: RT2-21.
4. Raises the round to £40 a shop now that nothing climbs. S: EC-06 and 5.1 show the greedy round ends richer after the story (week 13: £1,667 against £1,207) because the pressures that punish it are story-bound; RT2-21 freezes rivals and Ellis, so only shopkeepers' tempers remain.
5. Shopkeepers refuse and tell after three squeezed weeks. H: 2.17 fold rule, EC-06.
6. Telling reaches Ellis, whose stage holds. S: RT2-21 holds Ellis's stage, so a told round has nowhere to climb.
- Q1 no. Q2 yes. Q3 no: once the story ends, squeezing dominates, the very case G27 and EC-12 forbid.
- Verdict: fail Q3. Fixes: F01.

## G05 The landlord
Frank buys the empty unit and two flats and lives on the rent (his decision 16, "any building can change hands, rent as weekly takings").
1. Finds who sells a building. M: drill gap B3; decision 16 and G20 state the rule, but no seller, price or go-between (solicitor, agent) is written.
2. Agrees a price. M: no price for a building or a share (FIT-G8 and 2.7.8 list none).
3. Lets the unit and collects weekly rent. H rule only: decision 16.
4. A tenant falls behind. S: 2.8.2's debtor rule (pays from his purse; begs or refuses by regard, nerve and grudge) fits a tenant but is written only for Mickey's book; RT-16 covers only Frank's own rent.
5. Decides which pot the rent goes to. S: 2.7.1 (clean book) against 2.7.2 (night tin); rent from tenants is clean money but no part says so.
6. A squeezed tenant tells the town. H rule only: G04 (break his word at a cost the town hears), G26.
- Q1 no. Q2 no. Q3 unclear: with no price and no tenant pressure, rent would be money that keeps coming.
- Verdict: fail Q2. Fixes: F04, F09.

## G06 A lift a day
Frank lifts one small thing from a different shop every day and fences it.
1. Picks a shop and lifts a radio. H: 1.6.3 (lifts and the hot mark), WI-P05 shoplifting.
2. Leaves unseen. H: 2.2.1 seven slots, 1.4 being seen.
3. Fences it at about a third of retail, less for each piece the same week. H: 2.7.6.
4. The shop finds the loss and adds a lock or a man in the back. H rule only: G27 (owners who lose things unseen add a lock, a dog, a man sleeping in or a new hour).
5. Three unseen lifts on one street become a town question. H rule only: G27; 2.5.4 covers seen deeds only.
6. Shops restock weekly and the cycle repeats. H: 2.7.5.
- Q1 no. Q2 yes (by G27 as a rule). Q3 yes: the lift is the best lever (£339 an hour) but falls with each piece, cools in a day or two and draws arrests at ten a week (economy 5.5).
- Verdict: pass (the town's answer rests on G27 having no working values yet).

## Fix register (this part)
- F01 (law 5, time; G27): after the ending, the town's learning keeps running. Rivals may freeze on their rungs, but each keeps its weekly pressure, shopkeepers re-test the fold rule each fortnight, and a told squeeze can still move Ellis. Mends G04.
- F02 (law 4, exchange; G27): every repeatable income names who learns from it and what they do. Customs learns a pattern at the gate, buyers' prices fall as a market fills, a newsagent tells. No fixed allowance without a learner. Mends G03.
- F03 (law 2, things; G18): insurance as people. A policy is a fact on a business, a claim brings a named loss adjuster with a temper who reads the fire's seat and the owner's purse, and pays, delays or refuses. Mends G07.
- F04 (law 4; G20, decision 16): how any building or share changes hands. A named seller, a price from the rent or the rateable value at 1988 rents, a go-between (a solicitor or an agent) who knows and may talk, and the landlord's written consent for a lease. Mends G05, G07.
- F05 (law 2; G13): every buyer has a written list of what it takes, whole or in parts, and the scrap yard takes a whole car at a parts price and asks no question it would not ask a stranger. Mends G09.
- F06 (law 4; G04, G10): information as goods. A held fact sold to a third party is priced by the buyer's need and greed and becomes a told version with Frank as source. Mends G11.
- F07 (law 4; G20, decision 21): businesses compete on price and trade. A business can change its price; demand moves by the price and by what the town says; a losing firm shows it (fewer cars, a driver looking for work) and reacts by its owner's temper. Mends G02.
- F09 (law 4; 2.8.2): one debtor rule for anyone who owes Frank, tenants included (rent arrears, letters, a bailiff by law dial).
- F12 (values, no new rule): set the hands of a tobacco meet and the takings of the two later accounts.
