# Part 2: the killer who leaves no witnesses (15 schemes)

28 November 2026. Paper chaos test, job 4. Each scheme is played by a player who answers every witness with a killing and tries to leave nobody who can tell. Canon holds throughout: Frank cannot die (7.9), a car hurts and never kills (1.2.5), guns are rare and late and wound but never kill before War (1.3.10), no animal is ever hurt (4.11.1), no children anywhere.

Marks and questions as in Part 1: H handled (rule only = a general rule with no working values), S seam, M missing, X special case; Q1 breaks, Q2 end to end by rules, Q3 fair and enjoyable. Fix numbers continue the register from Part 1.

## K01 The witness on her walk
A woman sees Frank lift a radio. He catches her on the walk to the phone box, knocks her out, drags her to the quay and rolls her in.
1. She sets off to tell and is seen going. H: 2.5.1, 1.4.5 (at least 30 real seconds in his sight).
2. He runs her down. H: 1.5.2 (she runs at 5.2 m/s against his 5.5 m/s).
3. He knocks her down in the street. H: 1.3.7 body rule, 2.2.1 (a new act with seven slots, seen by whoever is about), 2.5.3.
4. Drags her to the quay edge and rolls her in. H: CS-16, 1.6.7 (0.9 m/s, 3 s off the edge).
5. Unconscious and face down she drowns in about two minutes. H: 1.3.5, his decision 6.
6. The body is found or not. S: 1.6.7 still says "one sunk is never found"; 2.5.5 (as fixed by C-354) says a dock body comes up in days to weeks unless weighted. The conflict was settled in 2.5.5 but the old line in 1.6.7 was never struck.
7. The inquiry. H: CS-27, 2.6.10 (tape, Ellis next morning), 2.4.2 (a killing never cools).
- Q1 no (the newer rule wins by C-354). Q2 no: two parts give opposite answers to one question. Q3 yes: the killing is a bigger deed than the lift it hid, which is the right lesson.
- Verdict: fail Q2. Fixes: F13.

## K02 The weighted body
Frank kills a man who saw him at the fish dock, chains him to a yard weight and sinks him at three in the morning, unseen.
1. Kills him unseen. H: 1.3.6, RT2-11, 2.2.3 (nobody in sight or light).
2. Takes a chain and weight from the yard. M: no object list for weights, no act of weighting a body, no time or noise for it.
3. Sinks him off the fish dock. H: CS-16 (3 s off the edge).
4. The body stays down. M: 2.5.5 says weighted bodies stay down "perhaps never", with no value for the perhaps.
5. The missing man becomes the town's question. H: 2.5.5 (his landlady, his mates at the gate, Ellis ask).
6. The inquiry runs without a body. H: CS-27 (missing long enough to be reported; no charge without a witness, evidence or confession). The number of days before a missing man is reported is not set.
7. Frank does it again to the next man who saw something. S: nothing makes the second and third disappearance cost more than the first. G27 raises "three small unseen deeds" to a town question but a missing man is not small, and CS-27 runs each inquiry alone.
- Q1 no. Q2 no (steps 2 and 4). Q3 no: an unseen killing with a weighted body is a clean, repeatable way to delete any person, the lasting easy win G27 forbids.
- Verdict: fail Q2 and Q3. Fixes: F13, F14.

## Fix register (this part)
- F13 (law 2, things; CS-16, 2.5.5): disposing of a body is a set of acts with objects, time, noise and traces, like any other act: weighting (with a named object from a named place), digging (a spade, a time, a sound, turned earth as a trace), sinking and leaving. Each states how often and when the body comes back. Strike the stale "one sunk is never found" in 1.6.7. Mends K01, K02, K09.
- F14 (law 1, knowing; law 3, people; G27): a missing person is a deed of its own that accumulates. His ties are read (who he last spoke to, who he owed, who threatened him), the days before he is reported are set by who misses him, and each further disappearance in the Hook raises the answer a step (CID treats two as linked; three bring the squad, as the long heat does for a constable). Mends K02.
- F15 (law 8, the player acts only through what he can do and say; 1.7.2): every verb Frank can do himself, he can order, through the one send rule, with the man's nerve and temper deciding whether he does it, does less or refuses; an order he refuses is a held fact against Frank. Mends K08.
- F17 (law 8): every soft-edge talk scene states which hand acts it allows (none, or a short fixed list such as strike and grab, played as acts), so a deed there is never typed text. Mends K11.
- F18 (law 3; 2.6.3): a statement is taken wherever the witness is (a bed, a door) by the officer the report brings, on the next morning's round; the station at nine is only the default. Mends K12.
- F19 (law 3; G26): loyalty is a reading, not a flag. "Keeps quiet for" weighs against a tie to the victim; a person who loved the dead man weighs grudge against silence. Mends K07.
- F20 (law 8; G19): one rule for a person in a seat (a cab, a bench, a bed): grab, pull out and strike at short reach, with the seated man's chance to get out first, and a passenger driven off his way asks, then tries the door, by nerve. Mends K06, K11 in part.
