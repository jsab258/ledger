# Part 6: the hoarder and collector (15 schemes)

28 November 2026. Paper chaos test, job 4. Each scheme is played by a player who stores, steals and keeps: goods, cars, guns, keys, papers, money.

Marks and questions as in Part 1. Fix numbers continue the register. The storage spine: two things under the coat and one in the hand (1.6.9); the rest at the office in a locked drawer, the yard shed and the tin; the cab's boot (GV-01); objects carry their owner and their history (G13); an office search finds what is stored (2.6.9).

## H01 The radio hoard
Frank lifts thirty radios over a month, stores them in the yard shed until the talk cools, then sells two a week to Rita at the full rate.
1. Lifts the radios. H: 1.6.3, WI-P05.
2. Carries them back one at a time. H: 1.6.9, 1.1.4.
3. Stores thirty in the shed. M: the shed, the drawer and the boot have no capacity; 1.6.9 limits what he carries, never what he stores.
4. Waits for talk to cool. H: 2.4.2 (a lift a day or two), 2.7.6 (Rita refuses goods still hot in talk).
5. Sells two a week. H: 2.7.6 (less for each further piece the same week), 2.7.5 (only what is in her purse).
6. Owners never forget their goods. H: 1.6.3 (he recognises his own goods in a shop window); Rita holds nothing, so the risk is at the second-hand shop and pawn.
7. Ellis searches the office once, with notice. H: 2.6.9 (what is stored counts for the case).
- Q1 no. Q2 no (step 3). Q3 no until capped: unlimited storage turns slow cooling into a steady income.
- Verdict: fail Q2. Fixes: F36.

## H02 The stash under the boards
Frank hides £400 of night money and a revolver behind a loose board in the empty unit on the parade.
1. Gets into the empty unit. H: 1.6.1, G14.
2. Hides the bag behind a board. M: no act puts a thing somewhere in the world other than the office, the boot or the water; G13 says anyone may find what is left in sight, but a hidden thing has no rule.
3. Somebody finds it. M: who can find a hidden stash, by what (a search, a workman, a new tenant), is not written.
4. A man who saw him go in. H: 2.2.1, 2.5.5.
- Q1 no. Q2 no. Q3 unclear: a safe stash outside the office would beat the office search for ever.
- Verdict: fail Q2. Fixes: F36.

## H03 The painted cars
Frank takes three parked cars, has Shaun respray them and keeps them in the yard as spares.
1. Takes each car by force. H: 1.2.10 (loud; reported).
2. Respray and plates. H: 1.2.9 (earlier sightings no longer name that car).
3. Keeps them in the yard. M: the yard's room for cars is not stated (as H01).
4. The owner sees his car on the rank. S: G13 says owners know their things anywhere; 1.2.9 says paint and plates stop sightings naming the car. Whether the owner knows his own car under new paint is not said.
5. The police trace a car. H: G13 (evidence keeps a chain), RT2-16 (every plate noted).
- Q1 no. Q2 no (step 4). Q3 unclear.
- Verdict: fail Q2. Fixes: F37, F36.

## Fix register (this part)
- F36 (law 2, things): every place that holds things states how much it holds (the drawer, the shed, the yard, the boot, a lock-up), and any object can be put anywhere in the world as an act with slots; in sight it is found by the next routine (2.5.5), hidden it is found only by a search, a dog, a workman's or new tenant's routine, or a person who saw it put there. Mends H01, H02, H03, H07.
- F37 (law 2; G13): each kind of object states what change stops whom knowing it. Paint and plates beat witnesses' marks; the owner's close look (the dent, the seat cover, the engraving) beats paint; melting, breaking up or scrapping beats everyone. Mends H03.
- F07 is widened: a business Frank holds can sell goods at a price it sets, and demand follows the price and the town's talk. Mends H07.
