# Depth research: DOCKS (smuggling, cargo, the port's powers)

28 November 2026. Job 3, area 2 of 5. All sources read on 28 Nov 2026 unless marked. Alcohol, drugs on screen and gambling are left out of anything the game would use; tobacco is allowed. GTA VI: gameplay only.

## The plain top

What to copy:
- GTA VI: goods go in bags; a man carries two, the second filling a hand so he cannot fight properly. Bags can be put down, passed on, or kept in a car boot or garage. Each buyer pays his own price per kind of goods; arrest probably loses the bag. These are the hands LEDGER lacks.
- GTA Online special cargo: crates bought on a run, stored (16, 42 or 111), sold on a run; destroyed crates are lost; the price per crate rises with the sale's size.
- Red Dead Redemption 2: shops refuse stolen goods; fences take certain kinds; gold goes to one fence, stolen coaches to one barn.
- Assassin's Creed IV: board a ship to take all its cargo; sink it and only half floats. After a boarding, choose between repair, lowering your fame, or adding the ship to your fleet.

What players hate (only 2 critic pieces on this topic were reached):
- The same boarding a hundred times (Game Informer on Assassin's Creed IV).
- Long drives and "more busywork" as profits grow (GamesRadar on GTA Online).

The 1990 facts:
- The Dock Labour Scheme ended on 3 July 1989. Within three weeks 2,288 registered dockers, a quarter, were redundant or under notice. That was 69 at Liverpool (5%), 242 at Hull (36%), 442 at Grimsby and Immingham (60%), and 37 at Tees and Hartlepool (7%).
- Scheme ports had 9,280 dockers in May 1989, against 25,770 in 1979. The strike ended in early August 1989. Employers said fewer than half of 9,221 strikers were still out by then.
- Customs officers may board any ship within port limits, stay aboard and search any part of it. A master who refuses a stationed officer room or a safe gangway was fined £50.
- Customs front-line staff came to about 3,630 staff years in 1990-91.
- Bonded warehouses were approved by Customs on conditions. Breaking a condition cost £100.
- The Mersey Docks and Harbour Company had its own police, 102 officers in 1989. Tees and Hartlepool had 21 special constables.
- In the old checking system (a 1970 example), tally clerks listed each load, gate police checked the list, the receiver checked the seal, and shortages were charged to the carrier.
- Cigarettes: the most popular packet of 20 cost about 180p in 1990, of which 131p was tax. In Belgium the same category cost about 110p. The 1990 Budget added 10p a packet from 23 March. A traveller from the EC could bring 200 cigarettes until 1992.

The design questions are numbered D1 to D10 in part C. They cover:
- What smuggling is with the hands.
- What a cargo is, and who notices a loss.
- Customs as people with a want and a fear.
- The Harbour Board and the dockers as powers.

## C. The drill's gaps as design questions

From the implication drill, area C1, and EC-07 and RT-03 in the blueprint.

D1. The hands of smuggling (gap B: the meet, carrying a case, where it is kept, who buys). Should a case of cigarettes be a carried thing that fills a hand, like GTA VI's second bag, so Frank cannot fight or run freely with it? Where may it be kept: the office, the car boot, a lock-up? Is each place safe only until a search?

D2. Who buys. RDR2 has fences that take only certain goods, and GTA VI shows each buyer's prices. Should yard buyers of cigarettes be named people with a limit each week, and does the newsagent who loses trade (EC-04) know who sold them?

D3. A cargo with an owner (gap B: no cargo with an owner, a manifest and a loss someone notices). Should every load on the dock road carry an owner, a tally list, a seal and an insurer? Does a loss become known when the receiver finds a broken seal or a short count (the 1970 practice)?

D4. Who learns of a loss, and when. Is it the carrier (charged for shortages), the insurer, or the port police at the gate? How many game days pass before each knows, and what does each do?

D5. Customs as people (gap B: Customs "has no officers"; "an officer who never reports, by trade", RT-03). Should Customs have named officers with a routine, and a rummage crew that boards ships? The law lets them board and search any part of a ship in port. What does a Customs officer want (seizures, a clean record) and fear (a complaint, being bought)?

D6. Customs as a charging body (story needs S19). Customs prosecuted its own cases in 1990. Should a Customs case follow a different path from a police case in JUSTICE?

D7. The bonded store. The law makes a bonded warehouse an approved "place of security" with conditions, and the keeper is fined for a breach. Does LEDGER's bonded store (4.2.3) have a keeper who fears losing his approval, and is he a lever?

D8. The Harbour Board (gap B: no powers table for non-rival factions). Mersey had 102 port police and Tees 21 specials. Should the Harbour Board be a power with its own police, a want (traffic, rent) and a fear (losing trade to roll-on ports)? Is the clerk who sells shipment times (4.2.3) its weak point?

D9. The dockers after 1989 (gap C: dock workers noticing). A quarter were laid off within three weeks, and the rest had lost the scheme's protection. Should laid-off dockers be the pool Frank recruits from (CREW need road)? Do working dockers notice and talk, and to whom?

D10. Price gap and risk. A packet cost about 180p in Britain and about 110p in Belgium. The legal allowance was 200 cigarettes until 1992. Should EC-07's £100 a case and £160 sold be checked against that gap? Should a load past the gate draw Customs within a set time (EC-07 says three weeks), or after named signs?
