# Blind review of LEDGER's eight design drafts

28 November 2026. Helper job 7, part 1 of 3. An independent check by a session that saw none of the drafts being made. Binding order used: canon.md, then his rulings (RULINGS.md and the rulings of 25 to 27 November quoted in the job brief), then the blueprint of 28 November. The hero is Frank.

How it was checked: every draft read in full; every blueprint part a draft leans on, or should have leaned on, read at its source (chapter and part id given); every number a draft calls sourced opened in the research file it cites. Before calling anything missing I read the general rule above it (BLUEPRINT-12) and searched the blueprint by meaning as well as by word. Finding ids (E-1, CR-1 and so on) are used again in parts 2 and 3.

## Seams between drafts (and with blueprint parts they do not mention)

- X-1 Two tins. The tin of 2.7.2 is at the office. The things draft adds "the flat's floorboard tin" (T1, from 4.3.2's catalogue line) with a different size and a different finder (a warrant only), and the property draft puts "the tin under the floorboards" in the flat (P8.2). Nobody says which tin night money lives in. If it can be the flat's, the office search of 2.6.9 never reaches it. Fix (law 2): one tin, at the office; the flat may hold a stash under T2's rules, found as stashes are.
- X-2 What a front washes. Three figures: the economy design's £60 a week a front (2.7.3, conflict C-332, "the design wins"); the property draft's believable takings, launderette £180 and caff £300 a week (P5.3), which economy E6.2 adds to the cap; EC-04's real takings, launderette £420 and caff £750 a week. Fix (law 4): one figure, set in EC-04's shop data, quoted by both drafts.
- X-3 Money after the ending has nothing it can reach. EC-12's run puts the richest style at about £1,200 at week 13. Property prices the empty unit at about £10,400 and a flat at about £8,320 (P2, rent times eight); economy prices the other firm at "its usual week times 20" (E9.1), about £5,200 on takings or about £760 on its owner's profit (the unit is not defined). Fix (law 4): price every late buy against EC-12's curve and name the unit.
- X-4 Customs. Docks D6 gives Customs charges no court scene. The story frame (3.1.3, "Story (24 Nov)") has Ron's Customs charge before the magistrates every eight days, once in the last week (also C-335). The VAT inspector of Act III is Customs and Excise too (3.1.3), so E13's "senior officer" and D5's "senior officer at the Custom House" should be the same person or plainly two.
- X-5 The boot. 1.6.9 is ruled ("the cab's boot also holds the envelope, a spare coat or a box"). Docks D1.2 and things T1 make it four cases. Either may be right, but it changes a ruled part and goes to him.
- X-6 Places that do not exist. Decision 19: no new places; finds move to places that exist. The courting outings use "the pictures" (the Tivoli, off the map by G21) and "the park" (on no map); things T5 uses "the boatyard" (on no map; the ship's chandler on the quay road exists, 4.3.3); courting C8 buys a scarf "at the draper's" (no draper on Quay Street, 4.3.3 and RULINGS 3 Oct).
- X-7 Pad players and lies. Ruling 4: pad players get every power typists have. Letters (C7.1 "built from suggested lines or typed"), selling a fact (E5), planting a story (L4) and false rumours (G01) all let a typist make up a claim about a named person; no draft says how a pad player builds one (whom about, what deed, true or false). L5.2 only offers claims Frank holds in ink. Fix (law 8): a claim builder on the pad, one person and one deed from the Ledger's people and the deed list, marked true or invented.
- X-8 Traced notes. E1.3: a robber "can be found and the money traced while it is still on him". T8: notes "are not traced in the Hook". Pick one; T8's is simpler.
- X-9 Insurer timing. T3.2: a townsperson's car comes back through "his insurer (property draft P6) ... two to six weeks". P6.3: the adjuster pays in four weeks or delays eight. Use P6.
- X-10 No new town-wide events (his ruling). Justice J9.3 sends a county squad after three disappearances; the squad of LH-02 changes the whole town's nights (night crowd two thirds, fares down a third, rounds paid late). That is a new trigger for a town-wide event. Fix (law 3): disappearances raise Ellis's stage and bring CID visits to the dead men's ties, never the squad.
- X-11 Notes on objects. The Ledger is a book Frank carries (L1, L6), but it alone escapes T7 (books are objects), J2 (evidence is kept at arrest) and E1.3 (a robber takes what is in the pocket). See L-2.

## 1. Economy (DESIGN-DRAFT-ECONOMY-2026-11-28.md)

(a) Canon, rulings, content
- E-1 E2.3 and E2.4 model a switching line per fare (5% to 15% by greed) and a fare war. Canon OPEN 3: cab fares "few and chosen ... no taxi-sim parts"; canon: "NOT a taxi simulator". A per-passenger price memory is taxi-sim depth. Keep his ruling 3 (his board, his price) at business level only: a price gap moves the other firm's week and the drivers' takings by one rule.
- E-2 E9.1 buys out the other firm. 2.15.3 is ruled: one other firm, "a business, never a fourth rival", and its ruled test moves fares to it after a hit-and-run. A bought-out firm breaks that test until E2.5's newcomer arrives four weeks later. Say so, or keep it unbuyable.
- E-3 RT2-18 (unchanged by any draft) revokes the licence for good on a second stop while suspended, "so the business does not survive". His ruling: the firm is never lost whole. The economy draft lists the firm's floor (a) but leaves this standing. Fix (law 6): a revoked licence is a fortnight longer and a fine, never for good.

(b) Special cases
- E-4 E8.2 adds a closure after six squeezed weeks, for the time after the ending only. EC-04 (four losing weeks shut a shop) and 2.17 (after three squeezed weeks the owner refuses and tells) already answer a squeezed shop at any time. A rule for one period is a special case: let EC-04 and 2.17 simply keep running after the ending.

(c) Contradictions
- E-5 E6.2 and the data table give the wash cap as £10 plus 80% of all fares. The blueprint replaced that (2.7.3, conflict C-332): 40% of drivers' fares, 80% of Frank's own, plus £60 a week a front. RT-01 still quotes the old cap too; fix both. See X-2.
- E-6 E9.2: "One cash gate in the story only (Ellis's £800)". The story frame has a second: Ron goes inside "unless Frank pays to stop it" (BLUEPRINT.md, the story chapter). Either the claim or the frame changes.
- E-7 E1.3 rules cash at arrest; RT-17 still says "a constable's search ... keeps what it finds", cash included, for a street search with no arrest. Extend E1.3's rule to every search.
- E-8 E9.1 against X-3: the after-ending buys are out of reach.
- E-9 E13: the second inspector comes "a week later". Act III is one week of five mornings (3.1.3), and the books open on its last day. A week's delay puts the remaining mornings after the ending. Fix (law 7): the next officer takes the next morning; the items already graded stand.

(d) Sources
- E-10 E2.2 credits the 60p plus 80p a mile tariff to "Jafar's ruling 3". It is his ruling of 24 November (advisor 372, C-393, 3.4.1). Figure fine, citation wrong.
- E-11 E6.2's "1990s Customs practice" for the wash cap comes from a question in the economy research (question 12), not a source row.
- E-12 E12.2's £50 cheque guarantee: the research says £50 from 1977 and that banks could set £100 or £250 from 1989. True for some cards only.
- E-13 E1.3 cites PACE s.19 for keeping cash at arrest. The research's s.19 is seizure by an officer on premises; property taken from a detained man falls under sections the justice research lists as not read (ss. 21, 54). Mark it own knowledge until read.

(e) Depth that does not earn its place
- E-14 Cut the per-fare switching lines (E2.3) and the "known loss" machinery (E2.4) to one business rule (E-1).
- E-15 E5's information price (need times greed, £10 to £60) has no rule for who wants which fact; without one it is a menu of buyers. Keep it as talk: any person who holds a want the fact answers may pay, by E3's haggle.

(f) Build cost
- E-16 E2.3 needs each fare person to hold every firm's price; few fares, much state. Cut with E-14.

(g) Gaps
- E-17 A safe (E1.4) can be bought nowhere: no shop sells one (2.7.8, 4.3.3).
- E-18 H07's street selling "without a front is a talk act to passers-by whom E2.3 governs": E2.3 is about fares. Selling goods to passers-by has no rule.

Rating: adopt with fixes. Fixes: E-1, E-2, E-3, E-4, E-5, E-6, E-7, E-9, E-14, E-17, E-18; numbers by X-2 and X-3.

## 2. Crew and rivals (DESIGN-DRAFT-CREW-AND-RIVALS-2026-11-28.md)

(a) Canon, rulings, content
- CR-1 R8.1 lists Father Walsh as a settler of disputes between criminal firms, at a price that doubles. Canon's content rule: religion "present as part of life, never mocked, never a mechanic". A priest as a priced service in crime is a mechanic. Keep him as a man who may speak for peace in talk, unpriced; the Fixer and an old dock man remain settlers.

(b) Special cases
- None found. R10 (order any verb) and R16 (talk with several) are genuinely general.

(c) Contradictions
- CR-2 R11's grave-ask rule: "greed times the price offered, plus his grudge, plus loyalty, reaches his nerve times the weight times 2". Three faults. Units: greed is a dial from 0 to 1 and price is pounds, so any price over £2 clears every threshold. Direction: 2.14 defines nerve as "how hard to frighten"; here a hard man needs more to do violence, the reverse of R4, where a high nerve resists breaking. Law is missing: a man of law 0.9 takes a killing for enough money. Fix (law 3): price in weeks of his own money, as 2.5.2's bribe rule already does; nerve on the doing side; law weighed against.
- CR-3 R9 ("no set-piece defence battles") against the story's need S12 and conflict C-337: the player is called to one authored crisis per post. The draft's question 3 offers option B but does not say the story already asks for it.
- CR-4 R6.2: an unpaid man leaves at three weeks; RT2-06 and EC-02: drivers and Pauline leave at two. Two thresholds for one deed (not paying a man). Use two for all, or say why crew differ.
- CR-5 R12 calls tie strength "grudge". Grudge is one of the five temper dials (2.14: how long they hold a wrong). Rename it tie.

(d) Sources
- CR-6 The "1995 split" vacuum case is marked unverified in the crew research (the Guardian piece was not reached). Two of three outcomes are sourced; the third is a design choice.

(e) Depth
- CR-7 R6.3's three pay kinds (wage, by the job, junior partner) each make a weekly obligation and a Ledger line. Two do the work: a night cut per job (2.7.2 already) and a weekly retainer for a posted man (EC economy 5.8). Drop the junior partner until a scheme needs it.

(f) Build cost
- CR-8 R16 puts several live speakers in one talk. Each extra speaker adds a model call against the 2 s first-word bar (RULINGS 7 to 28 Oct; 2.14.1 measured two serial calls at about 2.8 s). Budget it: one live reply per turn, the others' cut-ins from written lines.
- CR-9 R10 asks every crewman to carry out every verb Frank has, including planting, digging and arson. G10 already promises it, so it is owed anyway, but it is the costliest line in the draft.

(g) Gaps
- CR-10 R3 and R15 need each rival man's regard for his head. 2.14 lists it open ("rival men's regard for their head (a tie, no data)"); C-342 still stands.
- CR-11 R15.2: "two weeks without contact lower his loyalty". Contact is no act anywhere: a meeting, a note, a phone box? WI-P06's paid informer, who tells one true thing a week, has the same hole. Name the act.

Rating: adopt with fixes. Fixes: CR-1, CR-2, CR-3, CR-4, CR-5, CR-7, CR-8, CR-10, CR-11.

## 3. Docks (DESIGN-DRAFT-DOCKS-2026-11-28.md)

(b) Special cases

(c) Contradictions

(e) Depth

The draft's own question 2 recommends small theft (a box, a few cases), under which the insurer and receiver steps never fire. Cut D4 to: every load has an owner and a count; the tally clerk finds a loss at his next count and tells.

(f) Build cost

(g) Gaps

## 8. The Ledger (DESIGN-DRAFT-LEDGER-2026-11-28.md)

(a) Canon, rulings, content
- None found.

(b) Special cases
- L-1 L7 grounds a claim on whether Frank's own book holds a line for it. That is fair, but it was built for one scheme (P13). It must cover false claims about secrets by the same test, or a made-up secret that happens to fit does better than a true one Frank cannot source.
- L-2 The Ledger is a book in his pocket that no rule touches. T7 makes every book an object that can be burnt or taken; WI-P03 keeps evidence at an arrest, and a book listing his deeds is evidence; E1.3's robber takes from the pocket. The draft says a constable "hands it back" (WI-P03 says evidence is kept). Either the book is diegetic and obeys the object rules (Frank keeps it in shorthand a reader cannot use as proof, and a loss costs him the book's lines until he rewrites the open matters), or it is not a real object.

(c) Contradictions
- L-3 L5.3 stops the clock while the book is open. RT-18 (decided): "Opening the Ledger ... stops Frank but not the town"; a pausing Ledger is an assist, off by default.
