# Depth research: JUSTICE (police bail, seized goods, the magistrates' court, wounds)

28 November 2026. Job 3, area 5 of 5 (the smaller one). All sources read on 28 Nov 2026 unless marked. Law below is as it stood around 1990, read from legislation.gov.uk point-in-time or as-enacted versions. I am not a lawyer; this is research for a game, not legal advice.

## The plain top

The 1990 facts:
- Police bail in 1990 meant a duty to come back to a station, or appear at a magistrates' court, at a time and place the custody officer set; he could later write to say no need (PACE s47).
- The police could not attach conditions in 1990. The Bail Act 1976 allowed conditions "but only by a court" (s3(6)). They could ask for a surety. Police were first given power to set conditions by the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 (s27), and power to arrest for failing to answer police bail by s29 of that Act.
- Failing to surrender without reasonable cause was an offence; the bailed person had to prove the cause; up to 3 months and a level 5 fine, or 12 months if sent up (Bail Act s6).
- Seized goods could be kept "so long as is necessary": as evidence, for examination, or to find the lawful owner if they were believed stolen. Goods were not to be kept for evidence if a photograph would do. Things taken because they could hurt someone or help an escape had to be returned once the person left custody or got bail (PACE s22).
- An owner or a police officer could ask a magistrates' court to order goods delivered to the person who appeared to be the owner. The true owner then had six months to sue whoever held them. Unclaimed goods could be sold after a year (Police (Property) Act 1897).
- Magistrates' courts took 64 days on average in 1990 from first listing to the end of an indictable case. That was 127 days from the offence. 22% of cases ended at the first appearance, and the average case had 2.5 adjournments of 26 days each.
- Wounds: no rule made doctors report knife wounds in 1990. The GMC first asked doctors to report knife and blade wounds from 12 October 2009, and before that only gunshot wounds carried that duty. In 1990 a doctor was bound by confidentiality, with a public-interest exception (not confirmed in a 1990 text).

What to copy from games (brief):
- Red Dead Redemption 2: a bounty is a debt paid at any post office; unpaid, it brings bounty hunters.
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II: a small theft brings a fine and days in the stocks. Running makes the fine grow (200 to over 2,000 groschen in one writer's case), townspeople remember, and repeat crimes bring a lasting brand.
- GTA VI: arrest or death takes everything in the pocket (job 1).

The design questions are numbered J1 to J6 in part C. They ask:
- What date and duty does police bail carry, and with no conditions?
- Which rule wins on cash taken at arrest?
- How do seized goods go back, and does the owner learn who had them?
- Does a lesser charge reach the court?
- Who knows about a wound?

## C. The drill's gaps as design questions

From the implication drill, area A2 and B4.

J1. Police bail (gap B: "police bail has no date, no conditions and no return"). In 1990 police bail had a date and a place and no conditions. Should Frank's police bail carry a written date to answer at the station, with failing to answer an offence of its own? Should the "conditions" a player might expect be left out, because the police could not set them until 1995? Could the station write to say "no need to attend"?

J2. Cash at arrest (gap B: RT-17 keeps the cash found against WI-P03 handing money back on release). PACE s22 lets police keep what may be evidence or may be stolen, and return the rest. Should money in his pocket go back on release unless the officer believes it is the proceeds of a named crime? If so, does that belief need a sign the player can read?

J3. Seized goods and their owner (gap B: no return rule; does the owner learn Frank had them?). Under the 1897 Act an owner applies to the magistrates. Should a seized stolen radio go back to its owner through a court order, so the owner learns where it was found? Is the six-month window a hook for a later visit?

J4. Lesser charges (an edge by design: "lesser charges never reach a court", 2.6.9). Real cases took about nine weeks from first listing, with two or three adjournments. Should a lesser charge stay off-stage by design, or appear as dated letters and one morning at the court that costs Frank a working day?

J5. Wounds (gap B: wound reporting by treaters is a rule only). In 1990 no rule made a doctor report a stab wound, though gunshot wounds were later treated differently. Should a hospital doctor be a person who may tell, weighing his own temper, rather than an automatic report? Should a back-street patch-up be the safe but costly choice?

J6. What the town remembers. KCD2 shows townspeople remembering a crime and a fine that grows if you run. Should answering bail on time be a fact the town and the police both note, so running costs standing as well as law?
