# Design draft: the Ledger

28 November 2026. Job 6, area 8 of 8. A first full draft for the Ledger pass: the book Frank carries. Binding order: canon.md, Jafar's rulings, the blueprint. Sources: LEDGER-INTERFACE-RESEARCH-2026-11-28.md (job 5), the paper chaos test (job 4), the seven drafts before this one (areas 1 to 7) and the blueprint. Kept diegetic throughout: the Ledger is a pocket book in Frank's hand, holding only what he could know.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint has (chapter 6):
- People: what Frank knows of each person and what he believes they know of him, with how sure in words (said to his face is sure, saw them looking is "thinks so", two heads together is "guesses"); any person found in three inputs (6.2.1).
- Day book: events in order, about 22 words a line, nothing erased, a wrong belief struck through with the day and the reason, every entry traced to something Frank saw, heard or was told (6.2.2).
- Open: jobs, debts, promises and appointments, each with a person and a landmark, and what Frank carries (6.2.3).
- Money in words (6.2.4); Frank's reading at Full stop and each act's end, built only from the signs he was given (6.2.5).
- The office board as the place of cars and drivers (1.7.3); letters and papers opened as a page and kept (6.10); hooks as Ledger People lines Frank is sure of, a "guesses" line used is a bluff (RT-07).

What the research and the chaos test found missing:
- Holdings, crew terms, arrangements, a front's week and planted stories have no page (research questions 1 to 6).
- Taps from play to any answer, how long lists stay readable over seven weeks, whether reading stops the clock, and what the world holds instead (research questions 7 to 10; Pentiment's lost leads, Yakuza 0's hidden holdings, Shadow of War's unsorted army).
- A claim is weighed by fit, never by how Frank could know it, so a second-run player's perfect bluffs bite (F40; chaos P13 failed Q1).

## (b) The rules

### L1. The book is Frank's, and holds only what he could know (law 1, knowing; law 8)
1. Every line is in Frank's words, traced to something he saw, heard, was told or did (6.2.2). The book never states the true state of the world, only what he holds and when he last saw it.
2. Certainty is shown without numbers, by hand:
   - ink, plain: what he saw or was told to his face ("Rita paid me £40, Tuesday");
   - ink, with "thinks so": what he read from a look or a manner;
   - pencil, with "they say": what reached him by talk or guess.
   A pencilled line inked over when it is confirmed keeps both; a wrong line is struck through, left legible, with the day and the reason (as a constable's pocket book is kept, research).
3. Lines go stale honestly: where the world shows a thing, the book says when he last looked ("Tin: about forty when I counted, Tuesday"; "Shed: twelve radios, Sunday").
4. Handwriting by default, with a plain-print switch for legibility at the size AX-01 sets (question 3).

### L2. Six fixed pages that grow by lines, not screens (law 1)

| Page | What it holds | Sorted by |
|---|---|---|
| Open (the first page) | every matter with a date: debts due Friday, appointments, a bail date, a court date, an unanswered letter, a front's matter this week, an arrangement falling due, her next outing | date, soonest first; carried forward each morning |
| People | one entry per person: what Frank knows, what he thinks they know of him; for his men, their terms and loyalty in his words (crew draft R12); for her, what she said and did (courting draft) | tabs by surname, and a tab for his men |
| Holdings | by place: what is in the drawer, shed, boot, tins and each stash, and who else knows (things draft T1, T2); each building, lease, share and front with its papers and its week (property draft P5); each car with its driver and terms | by place, then by kind |
| Arrangements | one line per agreement: with whom, its terms, the next date it falls due, and what breaks it (crew draft R1; a bought officer; a round; the gate officer, docks draft D5) | next due date |
| Day book | a plain diary, about 22 words a line (6.2.2), with a margin mark on any line tied to an open matter | date tabs, one per day |
| Money in words | as 6.2.4: pocket, tin, what is owed and owing by Friday | none needed |

Frank's reading (6.2.5) is written at the back, at Full stop and each act's end.

### L3. A front's week and the round (law 4)
1. Each front has one line a week on its Holdings entry, as Sheila says it aloud at the count ("Caff: £30, Tuesday short"). The last four weeks are kept in full; older weeks fold into one line a month ("June: about £120, two short weeks"). Balance figures.
2. A matter at a front (property draft P5.4) goes on Open until dealt with or let go.

### L4. Planted stories and what came back (law 1, law 7)
1. A story Frank tells on purpose is a Day book line with whom he told and when, and a pencil line on each hearer's People entry.
2. When a version reaches him again, through anyone, it is written beside the first in that teller's words, with the day ("Came back from Rita, Thursday: Cammack's man is a grass"). Frank compares the two himself, as Obra Dinn's pencil and print let the player do (research). The book never says where the story went between.

### L5. Taps from play to any answer (law 8)
1. One input opens the book at Open; one turns to a page by its tab; one finds a line by tab or date. No answer is more than three inputs from play (6.2.1's standard, made general).
2. In a talk scene, lines Frank holds in ink appear among the suggested lines when they bear on the talk (a hook, a debt, a promise), so pad players reach every claim that typists can make (ruling 4).
3. Opening the book is an act: Frank stops and takes it out. It cannot be opened while running, fighting, driving or carrying two-hand things. While it is open the clock stops, as in talk (question 1).
4. No search box: 1990 had none. Tabs, the margin mark and Open's dates do the finding (research: Outer Wilds' asterisk, Pentiment's lost leads).

### L6. What lives in the world instead (law 2)
1. Where the world shows a fact at a glance in its place, the book names the place and when he last looked, and no more:
   - the office board for cars and drivers (1.7.3);
   - Sheila saying the week aloud at the count;
   - the tin's weight and the notes counted in the hand;
   - letters on the mat and in the drawer (courting draft C7: a letter is an object, and the book only notes where it is kept);
   - the radio, the paper on the caff counter, the agent's board, the council's demand.
2. Papers stay objects (things draft T7): the book holds Frank's note of a paper ("Bail sheet: answer Tuesday 14th, in my coat"), never the paper itself. Lose the coat and he still has the note, not the sheet.

### L7. A claim is weighed by how Frank could know it (law 1; fix F40)
1. G03 weighs a claim by its fit with what the hearer holds. It now also weighs how the speaker could know it.
2. A secret is a fact the hearer believes few people hold. Said by Frank with no source the hearer can see (Frank holds no line for it from a teller, a paper or his own eyes in this game), it is met with "Who told you that?": alarm and grudge rise, the hearer does not yield, and he tells others of the strange thing Frank knew.
3. Said with a source he can see (Mickey's real book, Darren's word, a letter Frank read), it works as a hook (RT-07).
4. The book is how the game knows what Frank could know: a claim with an ink line is grounded; a claim without one is a bluff, however true. This is the same for typed lines and suggested lines.

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- In the world: Frank takes a small black book from his inside pocket and opens it; the pages are lined, the hand his, the pencil lines lighter. He reads standing still in a doorway or at the office desk.
- In the book: Open first, every page one tab away, any line one tab or date further.
- In talk: what he holds in ink becomes a line he can say.
- The book never shows a bar, a percentage, a map marker or a star; the town and the world carry the rest (L6).

## (e) Edges

- Writing his own notes: no free text in 1990's book; the Day book is written by what Frank does, and the player's own marks are the margin mark and inking over a pencil line. Balance choice, open to playtest.
- Losing the book: it is in his pocket and goes where he goes; a constable's search lists it as property and hands it back (WI-P03); Ellis may read a page if she holds him, a new fact for her (law 1).
- A second-run player who knows every secret: L7; his knowledge is a danger, not a key.
- Reading in a chase: L5.3; he must stop, and stopping is seen.

## (f) Build stage

- Stage 1: Open, People, Money in words, the Day book (most already specified in chapter 6), L5 taps, L7.
- Stage 2: Holdings and Arrangements, L3 a front's week, L1 pencil and ink.

- Can wait for playtesting: weeks kept in full; whether the player may add marks; the pause rule.
