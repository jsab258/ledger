# Design draft: crew and rivals

28 November 2026. Job 6, area 2 of 8. A first full draft for the crew and rivals pass. Binding order: canon.md, Jafar's rulings, the blueprint. Sources: DEPTH-RESEARCH-CREW-2026-11-28.md (job 3), the implication drill (job 2, gap areas B and C), the paper chaos test (job 4) and the blueprint.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint has:
- Orders in the field (1.7.1), eight send verbs through named men (1.7.2), men at his side (1.7.4), striking his own (WI-P01).
- Recruiting by need or by hook (2.11.1); crew who break after a sign (2.11.2, with the pressure figure "not yet set"); turning, paying and playing off rivals' men (WI-P06).
- Three rivals on the same ladder of noticed, visit, pressure, threat, sit-down (2.10.1); rival eyes (2.10.2); pressures and three answers (2.10.3); the Fixer and his one truce (2.10.4); what lifts a rival (RT2-07); bases and posts (RT2-08, RT2-09); fallbacks when a principal dies (RT2-05, WI-P02); sit-downs (3.5.5); the dial (RT-12).
- General rules: ties as data, so a truce can be brokered and a leak set two gangs on each other (G08); rivals and crew use Frank's verbs (G10); one ledger of obligations (G04); readings per person (G26).

What the research, the drill and the chaos test found missing:
- No state for common cause with a rival, nobody who notices it, nothing that shows standing arrangements (drill gap B; research C1 to C3).
- The vacuum when a head falls is fixed to the lieutenant; taking a beaten firm's men in is undesigned (research C4, C5).
- Nerve pressure has no figure; "unpaid" under the half split is undefined; recruits' families notice nothing (research C6 to C8).
- Repeated defence of holdings is the commonest crew complaint in the critics read (3 of 5 pieces); killing the boss outright beats management in 2 (research, praise and chores table).
- Third-party settlement exists only as Garbutt's single truce (research C12).
- Chaos failures: ordering a killing (K08, F15), loyalty as a flag (K07, F19), who answers a rumour by force (S03, F22), go-between menace (S08, F24), grave asks to strangers (T08, F25), plants (P03, F38). Two people rules with no other home are drafted here because crew play leans on them: talk with several (T09, F26) and when a person tells what he holds (T15, F28).

## (b) The rules

### R1. Arrangements and common cause (law 3, people; law 1, knowing)
1. An arrangement is a G04 obligation between two parties (Frank and a rival, or two rivals) with spoken terms: what each does, what each leaves alone, for how long. It is made at a sit-down (3.5.5), through a settler (R8) or by a deal in talk.
2. States, each lifted by an event, never by points:
   - none;
   - arrangement: terms agreed (say, his drivers untaxed at the gate for a fortnight);
   - common cause: the two act together at least once against a third (a joint answer to a pressure, men side by side at a post);
   - broken: one side breaks a term, and everyone who hears holds it as a broken word (G04).
3. Who notices: anyone who sees the two sides' men together or doing a joint act catches it as a deed (2.2.1); the third rival learns it through his own eyes (2.10.2) and reads common cause against him as a deed on his ladder (RT2-07, G08).
4. How it shows: in the world (the other side's men not stopped at a post, two crews at one table in the caff, the gate tax not asked); in talk (men say so); in the Ledger as one line with its terms and its date due (area 8).

### R2. When a head falls (law 3, law 7)
1. RT2-05 stands: the lieutenant takes the ladder and counts as having seen the deed. What he does next follows his temper, one of three:
   - accept: a new balance; he offers terms within a week (as Manchester accepted a new balance in 1991, research);
   - avenge: if he holds a name for the deed, his ladder climbs two rungs against that person at once (as Glasgow in 1991, research);
   - split: men of low loyalty to him leave; each goes by R3.
2. A firm that loses its head and its lieutenant, or its base and a sit-down, is beaten: its ladder falls to "noticed" under the role's generic holder (WI-P02), and it must climb again. Canon keeps three rivals (2.15.3): the firm is weakened, never removed.
3. A beaten firm's posts and rackets are unheld for two weeks; the first side to put a man on one holds it (RT2-09). Balance figure.

### R3. Taking men in (law 3)
1. A beaten firm's men decide one at a time: stay with the new head, go to another rival, go to Frank if asked and his reading with them allows it (WI-P06 conditions: a lost sit-down or a lost head now counts), or leave the Hook.
2. A taken man keeps his grudge and one hook on his old head; his ties (R5) come with him.

### R4. Nerve pressure (law 3)
1. Each man holds pressure from named events, each with a size (research C6):

| Event | Pressure | Falls by |
|---|---|---|
| A fight lost in sight of others | 0.10 | 0.05 a quiet week |
| Arrested or questioned | 0.15 | 0.05 a quiet week |
| A mate hurt or taken in | 0.10 | 0.05 a quiet week |
| A threat to his family (talk only, adults only; decision 5) | 0.20 | when the threat is answered |
| A week unpaid (R6) | 0.10 | when paid |
| A grass punished in the town (research: an arson a week after two women went to the police) | 0.15 | 0.05 a quiet week |

2. Pressure above his nerve: a sign comes first (R7), then he asks to step back (a talk scene); pressure above nerve plus 0.3: he walks, or goes to the police or a rival by his law, loyalty and grudge. Frank's word (regard), pay and rest lower it. Balance figures.

### R5. A recruit's family and workplace (law 3)
1. Every recruit has ties (G08): kin and workmates, all adults. They notice new money, late hours and marks, and act by temper: a wife asks Frank to leave him be, a brother asks for work, a workmate at the gate talks.
2. Family is a pressure source (R4), a witness pool and a lever for others (a rival may hire a man's brother; Empire of Sin's model, research).

### R6. Unpaid under the split (law 4)
1. Drivers keep half their takings and the firm pays fuel and cover (2.7.1). Unpaid means either the firm missing fuel or cover money, or a man's agreed night cut not paid from the tin. Both are debts Frank owes (G04).
2. One week: the man says so. Two weeks: his loyalty falls a band and he may skim (G10). Three weeks: he leaves or sells what he knows. Pauline and drivers already leave after two unpaid weeks (RT2-06); that stands.
3. Men may be on different terms: a wage, by the job, or as a junior partner with a share (research: all three side by side). The term is agreed in talk and is a Ledger line.

### R7. Signs before betrayal (law 1, law 3)
1. A man who has turned (paid by a rival or the police, R14 or WI-P06) shows signs a watcher can catch: new money (a coat, a car part), gaps in his routine, questions beyond his job, being seen with the other side.
2. Ron and trusted men pass one sign at a time to Frank in words. A break never comes without at least one sign that could have been seen (2.11.2).

### R8. Settlement through a third party (law 3, law 4)
1. Any two parties in dispute may ask a settler both respect: the Fixer (2.10.4), Father Walsh, an old man of the docks. Settlement takes place in a café, an office or the back of a cab (content rule).
2. The settler names terms (a payment, a post given back, a man stood down). Each side weighs them by its reading of the other and of the settler. Agreed terms are G04 obligations both hold; breaking them costs regard with the settler and with everyone who heard.
3. A settler settles the same pair again at a higher price each time (double) and refuses the third time. Garbutt's two-week truce stays as his separate product, once a game. Balance figures.

### R9. Holdings defend themselves through their men (law 3, law 5)
1. No set-piece defence battles. A holding (a post, a front, a round) is held by the man on it. A rival's go at it comes only from his ladder's pressure (2.10.3), at most once per holding per two weeks, and is resolved by the send rule (1.7.2) on the next night: the defender's nerve and numbers against the attacker's, the watchers, the light.
2. Frank learns the next morning, or before if a tip reaches him in time, and may choose to be there; then it is an ordinary fight.
3. A lost holding is lost until retaken; the firm is never lost whole (ruling 7).

### R10. Anything Frank can do, he can order (law 8; fix F15)
1. Every verb Frank can do himself he can order through a named man, by the send rule: the man's nerve and temper decide whether he does it, does less or refuses.
2. A refused order is a held fact the man keeps (a hold on Frank, LH-15).

### R11. Every ask carries its weight (law 4; fix F25)
1. An ask's weight is the deed table's seriousness (2.5.2: damage 0.3, assault 0.5, wounding or robbery 0.7, a killing 1.0). A man does it if greed times the price offered, plus his grudge against the target, plus loyalty (crew only), reaches his nerve times the weight times 2. Balance figure.
2. A stranger offered a grave act usually refuses; a refusal from a man of law 0.5 or more makes him likely to tell (2.5.2).

### R12. Loyalty is a reading, not a flag (law 3; fix F19)
1. "Keeps quiet for" (2.14) is weighed against tie to the victim. When Frank harms one of a person's own, that person compares grudge (tie strength to the victim) with loyalty to Frank; grudge higher, he tells, to the police by his law dial or to a rival.

### R13. Who answers a told fact with force (law 3; fix F22; decisions 4 and 37)
1. Only people with a fight role (rivals' men, crews) or a grudge tie to the act (kin of the wronged, the mate harmed) may answer a believed rumour with force, by temper and the version's strength. Decision 4: shown only if Frank is there.
2. The ordinary town answers only together and never by force: avoiding, refusing, shutting early (G27).

### R14. A go-between carries his backer's weight (law 3; fix F24)
1. An ask through a go-between uses the menace and regard of whoever the hearer believes is behind it, times the strength of that belief (0 to 1), plus the go-between's own.
2. Deeds done in Frank's name grow each hearer's reading of Frank (G26).

### R15. Plants (law 3; fix F38)
1. Frank may place a man in a rival's crew. The rival hires by its own recruiting rule (need, a word from someone it trusts).
2. Each week the plant weighs loyalty to Frank against regard earned in his new crew; two weeks without contact lower his loyalty to Frank a band. He is found out through signs (R7) like anyone else.

### R16. Talk with several (law 1, law 8; fix F26)
1. Talk with more than one person is one talk: everyone present holds what was said; the one addressed answers; others cut in if their mouth is 0.6 or more or the matter touches them. Each decides by his own temper. Suggested lines can address all or one (ruling 4).

### R17. When a person tells what he holds (law 1; fix F28)
1. Asked for a fact he holds, a person tells if his mouth plus his regard for the asker, minus what telling costs him, minus his tie to the fact's subject, reaches 0.5. The Core decides it in both brain modes (2.14.1), so the choice of mode changes words, never what is told. Balance figure.

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- He talks: asks a man to join, agrees terms, orders a send by two inputs (1.7.2), calls a settler from a phone box.
- He sees the crew in the world: on the office board (1.7.3), at posts, at his side; a man's mood in his body and words (5.2.6); signs of turning as things he notices.
- He hears arrangements in talk and sees them in the town (an untaxed gate, two crews at one table).
- The Ledger keeps: per man, his terms, where he was put, what Frank knows of him, signs noticed; per rival, the rung as Frank guesses it in words, any arrangement and its date due (area 8). No numbers on screen (OUT-06).

## (e) Edges

- Hiring every man in the Hook: men of high loyalty to their heads refuse; the ones taken bring ties and grudges; more men, more who know (G27, G32).
- Killing every rival head: each death is a killing that never cools (2.4.2), the firm is beaten but returns under its generic holder (R2.2), and the town's fear hardens (G27).
- Ordering an atrocity: content rule first (no children, no sexual content); otherwise R11 makes most men refuse, and the refusal is held against Frank.
- A rival asked to ally against canon's frame: arrangements cannot remove a rival or merge two (2.15.3).
