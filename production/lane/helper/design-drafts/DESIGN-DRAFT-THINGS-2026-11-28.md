# Design draft: things

28 November 2026. Job 6, area 7 of 8. A first full draft for the things pass: how much places hold, putting things anywhere, damage to the top step, public mending, bodies, the water's edge, books, and what change stops whom knowing a thing. Binding order: canon.md, Jafar's rulings, the blueprint. Sources: the paper chaos test (job 4) and its fix register, the implication drill (job 2), the docks, property and justice drafts (areas 3, 4 and 6) and the blueprint. Content rule: a car hurts and never kills; violence only as canon allows.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint has:
- Carrying: two things under the coat and one in the hand or a holdall; the rest lives in the office's locked drawer, the yard shed and the tin; a search finds what is stored; the boot holds the envelope, a spare coat or a box (1.6.9).
- Objects carry owners and facts; evidence keeps a chain (G13). Damage changes what an object does until mended (G19). A wrecked cab is off the road a day; Shaun's paint and new plates stop earlier sightings naming a car (1.2.9). The firm's floor: car two never lost more than a day (RT2-06).
- Bodies: dragged, carried, put in a boot, rolled into the dock; a car pushed off the quay with a body is "a big loud deed" (CS-16). A sunk body comes up in days to weeks unless weighted, "and then perhaps never"; a buried one is found by search, dog or smell (2.5.5).
- The quay edge stops Frank and cars; things thrown in are gone and whoever saw keeps it (4.2.4).
- The inspector grades five items from the fare book (RT-01).

What the chaos test found missing (fix register, job 4):
- No capacity for any place, and no act puts a thing anywhere but the office, the boot or the water (F36; H01, H02, H03, H07).
- No rule for what change stops an owner knowing his thing (F37; H03).
- No top step of damage, nobody who mends public things, and the tin protected by a special line (F31; R02, R03, R04, R11).
- Two water-edge rules that disagree (F30; R01).
- The fare book is a record but not an object (F32; R05).
- Body disposal has no acts for weighting or digging, and a stale "one sunk is never found" in 1.6.7 (F13; K01, K02, K09).
- No answer for a person whose way Frank blocks (F29; R10).

## (b) The rules

### T1. Every place states how much it holds (law 2, things; fix F36)
1. Bulk follows the docks draft (D1): pocket, coat, one hand, two hands. A car takes a place of its own.
2. Each place that holds things has a size, as data:

| Place | Holds | Who reaches it by routine |
|---|---|---|
| Frank on foot | two coat things and one hand thing (1.6.9) | a constable's search |
| A cab's boot | four two-hand things, or eight one-hand | a traffic stop that searches; a mechanic |
| The office's locked drawer | six coat things | Sheila (key), an office search |
| The yard shed | twelve two-hand things, or twenty-four one-hand | the drivers, Shaun, an office search |
| The yard | the firm's cars and two more | anyone passing the yard gate |
| The tin | money and two coat things | Sheila, Frank |
| The flat's floorboard tin | money and four coat things | a warrant search (justice draft) |
| A lock-up | twenty two-hand things, or one car | its owner, the landlord |
| A room in an empty unit | as a lock-up | the agent showing it, a new tenant |

3. A place that is full takes nothing more. Balance figures.

### T2. Anything can be put anywhere, and someone may find it (law 2, law 1; fix F36)
1. Putting a thing down, in or behind something is an act with the seven slots (2.2.1), taking time by bulk: a pocket thing 2 s, a case 5 s, hidden behind a board or under a floor 20 s. Balance figures.
2. A thing in sight is found by whoever's routine next passes (2.5.5). The finder holds it as a found thing: he hands it in, keeps it or tells, by temper.
3. A hidden thing is found only by: a search of that spot (police, a rival's men, a burglar), a dog (food, blood, a body), a workman's or a new occupier's routine (a refit, a repair, the first week after premises change hands, property draft P2), or a person who saw it put there.
4. Whoever finds a stash holds it (G13), and it carries any owner it had. Frank's stash in the empty unit is safe only until the unit is shown, let or repaired, or until the man who saw him go in tells.

### T3. Damage has a top step: destroyed (law 2; fix F31)
1. Every object climbs one ladder: whole, marked (a dent, a scratch), damaged (works badly), broken (does not work until mended), destroyed (gone; replaced, never mended).
2. Each kind states who replaces it, at what cost and how long, by its owner's purse:

| Thing | Destroyed by | Replaced by, cost, time |
|---|---|---|
| A firm car other than car two | fire, the dock, the scrap yard | the lot, on terms (EC-03), a week |
| Car two | as above | the firm's floor (RT2-06): a second-hand car on terms within a day |
| A townsperson's car | as above | his insurer (property draft P6) or his purse, two to six weeks |
| A shop window | a brick | the glazier, a day; the owner pays |
| The tin | thrown, burnt, given away | a new tin; what was in it is gone |
| A book or paper | burnt, soaked, taken | rule T7 |

3. The tin is an object like any other: it can be thrown in the dock, burnt or given away, and the night money in it is gone with it (2.7.7 counts it as a flow out). 2.7.2's line that the tin is lost only by theft or search is read as "lost by any act that takes it from Frank".

### T4. Public things are mended on a delay (law 2, law 3; fix F31)
1. Every public thing has an owner who mends it: street lamps, railings and benches the council's men; phone kiosks British Telecom (BT owned them in 1990; own knowledge); a post box the Royal Mail.
2. The first breakage is mended in three game days for a lamp, five for a kiosk. A second within a month is mended the next day, and the beat constable walks that stretch more often at night (G27). A third brings a stronger fitting, and a line in the paper if Alison hears of it (2.4.5). Balance figures.
3. A dark stretch is noticed by the constable on his round and by those who live there; they talk. Breaking a lamp or a kiosk is criminal damage, a deed seen or found (2.5.5).

### T5. Body disposal is a set of acts (law 2; fix F13)
1. Each act needs named things from named places, takes time and makes noise:

| Act | Needs | Time and noise | Comes back |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rolling into the dock | the quay edge (CS-16) | 3 s, a splash heard at 15 m | up in three to fourteen days (2.5.5) |
| Weighting with rope | rope and a weight (the fish dock's yard weights, a boatyard anchor) | a minute | up in two to four weeks, when the rope gives |
| Weighting with chain | chain and a weight (the boatyard) | a minute, a clank heard at 10 m | stays down until the Harbour Board's dredger works that berth (each berth every eight weeks, a timetable) or a diver goes down for another reason |
| Digging a grave | a spade (the yard shed, an allotment hut) | 40 game minutes, digging heard at 20 m at night | turned earth shows two weeks; an allotment holder or his dog finds it within days |
| Leaving in a lock-up or a shed | a key | a minute | smell after four days brings the neighbour or the owner |

2. Nothing comes back by a chance roll (2.5.5): each return is a routine or a named event. Balance figures.
3. Carrying or dumping a body is still read as a killing seen, at the grave band (CS-16). A missing man accumulates (justice draft, J9).
4. 1.6.7's stale "one sunk is never found" is struck; 2.5.5 and this table stand.

### T6. One water-edge rule (law 2; fix F30)
1. Frank never goes into the water by his own step and no car leaves the quay anywhere: bollards and the edge stop it and a car backs out (4.2.4). CS-16's line about a car pushed off the quay is struck (question 1).
2. Things can be thrown in; what goes in is gone, and whoever saw it keeps it (4.2.4). A body can be rolled in (T5).
3. A man put in climbs out at the nearest ladder within 60 real seconds; an unconscious man face down drowns in about two minutes (4.2.4, his decision 6).

### T7. Books are objects and records at once (law 2, law 7; fix F32)
1. Every book or paper (the fare book, the debt book, Mickey's real book, Pauline's dispatch log, the drivers' sheets, the bank book, the bonded store's count, the Board's account) is an object in a place with an owner, and a record of facts. Reading it teaches those facts (law 1); destroying it removes them from that place only, never from memories or copies.
2. The fare book can be burnt, soaked or taken. What survives: Sheila's memory, the drivers' sheets in the drawer, Pauline's log, the bank's paying-in slips.
3. Sheila can rebuild the last four weeks from her memory and the sheets, at a week of evenings, if she is still loyal; she writes it honestly, so it holds or is thin by what really happened (question 3).
4. Act III grades from what exists (RT-01). A missing book not rebuilt is itself an item that falls: the inspector's first question is where it is.

### T8. What change stops whom knowing a thing (law 1, law 2; fix F37)

| Kind | Change | Beats | Does not beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| A car | respray and new plates (1.2.9) | witnesses who knew it by colour, sign or plate | its owner looking closely within 2 m or from inside (the dent, the seat cover) |
| A car | scrapping or breaking for parts (E4.2) | everyone | the yard man, who keeps the plate |
| A radio or a watch | scratching off the serial number | the police trace | the owner's own scratch or mark at face distance (1.6.3) |
| Jewellery | melting (a man who does it) | everyone | the man who melted it |
| A coat | washing (blood) or changing it | a witness who knew only the coat | anyone who knows his face |
| A weapon | the dock | everyone | whoever saw it go in |
| Notes | spending | everyone | nobody; notes are not traced in the Hook |

### T9. A way blocked (law 3, people; fix F29)
1. Anyone whose way is blocked by Frank asks him to move, asks again more firmly, and then, by temper, pushes past (a shove, an act with slots that Frank may answer), waits up to 30 s, or goes round, uses another door or comes back later. He remembers it as rudeness (a step of regard).
2. Sheila asks twice, then says she will do it when he has finished playing silly beggars, and comes back in an hour. A count she cannot reach that day is a missed count and reads as put off (RT2-06).
3. Nobody stands frozen and nobody passes through anybody (8.10).

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- In the world: he puts a bag behind a board, a case in the boot, a radio in the shed; a full shed has no room ("Nothing more's going in there"); he sees a lamp out and, days later, the council van; he sees the spade missing from the shed.
- In the Ledger (area 8): holdings by place, in Frank's words: what is in the drawer, the shed, the boot, the floorboard tin and each stash he made, and who else knows of each. Books he holds and books he has destroyed are listed by name.

## (e) Edges

- Hiding things all over town: each stash is a fact for anyone who saw and a find for the next refit; nothing is safe for ever.
- Burning the inspector's books in Act III: T7.4; the missing book falls.
- Throwing every weapon in the dock: allowed; whoever saw keeps it; the gun seller's stock is small (RT2-14).
- Pushing a car to the quay edge: the bollards stop it in character; Frank cannot lift a car.
- Breaking every lamp every night: T4 mends faster and the constable walks there more; the town talks.

## (h) Questions only Jafar can decide

1. Which water-edge rule stands?
   - A. No car leaves the quay anywhere; CS-16's line is struck (recommended: 4.2.4 is already tested on every quay edge, and a car never kills by canon).
   - B. One slipway where a car can roll in, with divers and a trace.
2. How long does a body weighted with chain stay down?
   - A. Until the Harbour Board's dredger works that berth, every eight weeks (recommended: a routine, never a chance roll).
   - B. For ever.
   - C. Two to four weeks, like rope.
3. If the fare book is destroyed, may Sheila rebuild it?
   - A. Yes, honestly, in a week of evenings, if loyal (recommended: a person answers the loss, and an honest rebuild cannot hide what really happened).
   - B. No; the book is gone and that item falls.
   - C. The fare book cannot be destroyed.

Decided here without asking: capacities and times are balance figures matched to 1.6.9 and the docks draft; public owners follow who owned them in 1990; the tin follows the general object rule rather than its own line; the blocked way follows 8.10's standard that nobody freezes.
