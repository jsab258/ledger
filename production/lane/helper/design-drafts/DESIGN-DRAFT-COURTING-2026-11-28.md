# Design draft: courting

28 November 2026. Job 6, area 5 of 8. A first full draft for the courting pass. Binding order: canon.md, Jafar's rulings, the blueprint. Sources: DEPTH-RESEARCH-COURTING-2026-11-28.md (job 3), the implication drill (job 2, area B6), the paper chaos test (job 4) and the blueprint. Content rule throughout: affection only, nothing sexual, no marriage, no drink or betting in any place or line, no children.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint has:
- G06 and 3.2.8: courting is a "can-you" under the people rules, women only, each by her own dials (unattached, temper, regard, what she knows of him); nothing explicit; lightly scripted; no marriage, so it goes as far as walking out. She may stay loyal, help trap a rival, be frightened and leave; a rival's former partner may want revenge and be drawn to Frank. Ellis can be courted while she stays the bent detective (decision 31); Danby stays a friend (decision 9).
- Calling on people: at her hours, under two real minutes, no score; a second call inside the set gap draws a cooler door (3.2.7).
- The friendship that holds at the close: at least three calls, no cooler door at the last, no killing known as fact (3.2.5, RT2-04).
- Alison Sedman writes the local paper's local page, and it prints only what she knows and chose to write (2.4.5). Letters reach Frank on the mat (2.4.5, G21). Off-limits typed lines are answered in character (RT-15).

What the research, the drill and the chaos test found missing:
- The steps of courting: "no states, steps or thresholds are written" (drill B6, research K1).
- What draws her, time as the price, places liked and tired of, being seen together, how it shows without a bar, letters, presents, what ends it, and what sets the rival's former partner moving (research K2 to K10).
- The friendship state counts calls and not what she holds of him, so three hollow calls of lies buy the best ending (F39; chaos P05 failed Q2 and Q3).
- Frank cannot write a letter at all (F23; chaos S07 failed Q2). Drafted here as C7 because letters belong to courting first; it covers any letter Frank writes.

## (b) The rules

### C1. The courting ladder: named states lifted by events (law 3, people; law 7)
Courting has five states and two side states. Each is a fact she and Frank hold, never a number. Each step is lifted by named events, and she may always refuse.

| State | What it means | Lifted to the next by |
|---|---|---|
| Known | she knows his name and face | a call in which he asks her to go somewhere and she says yes (C2 decides) |
| Keeping company | she will meet him at an arranged time and place | two outings that went well (C4) and being seen on them by two people who know either of them (C6) |
| Seen together | the street says they are courting | he asks her to walk out with him and she says yes (C2), after a third outing that went well |
| Walking out | an understanding: in the street's eyes she is his girl and he her man | the top of the ladder; there is no marriage |
| Cooling (side) | she is hurt or doubtful: she takes calls but refuses outings | an amends she accepts (G07) returns her to the state she cooled from |
| Ended (side) | she has ended it | C9 |

1. A woman who is not unattached cannot be courted: asking her is a remark that she, and her man if he hears of it, take by temper.
2. Every talk act on the ladder is a suggested line on a pad as well as a typed one (ruling 4): "Would you come to the pictures on Saturday?", "Will you walk out with me?", "I'm sorry about Thursday."
3. Typed lines past the content rule are answered in her own words as RT-15 says, and remembered.

### C2. What draws her: traits seen in deeds, never coins (law 3, law 1)
1. Each woman who can be courted has a short written list of what draws her (three things) and what she cannot abide (two things), as data. Alison's draws: a man who keeps his word, honesty about the fire and the warehouse row, kindness to people with less (the drivers, Ada). She cannot abide being lied to, or being shown off.
2. A draw counts only as a fact she holds about him (law 1): something she saw, was told by him and later found true, or heard through the town's talk (2.4.1). She says yes to a step when she holds at least one fact for each of two draws, holds none of the things she cannot abide, and her regard is 0.5 or more (G07). Balance figures.
3. The same deed reads differently to different women: one admires a man who stands up to a bully, another fears him. The list decides, not the player's intent.
4. Nothing is bought: no present, favour or number of calls lifts a step by itself (research: the "kindness coins" warning).

### C3. Time is the price (law 5, time)
1. She has a routine: Alison's press days twice a week, her mother's Sunday, her evenings. Calls and outings happen only inside it (3.2.7).
2. An outing takes an evening, two to three game hours, in which Frank is not at the office, the rank or the gate. Whatever happens there in that time happens without him.
3. An appointment missed without word is remembered: it cools her by one state at once unless a reason reaches her before the hour (a note by hand, a message through Pauline). A reason that later proves a lie is a lie found out (C9).
4. Calling again inside the set gap draws the cooler door (3.2.7); calling at an hour outside her routine finds her out, or annoyed.

### C4. Outings and places liked and tired of (law 2, law 3)
1. An outing is an arranged visit to a place: the pictures, the caff, a walk along the front, the park, a drive out to the coast in a car, the chapel hall's evening. No place where drink or betting is the point.
2. An outing goes well when he is on time, the place is one she likes and is not tired of, nothing frightens or shames her there, and the talk includes at least one true thing (a confidence, C5). It goes badly if two of these fail. Balance figures.
3. Each woman likes some places and dislikes others (data). Each outing to the same place moves her liking down one step: liked, then fine, then tired of; a place tired of is refused ("Not the pictures again"). A place rests back up one step for each two weeks unvisited (research: GTA IV's memory of places). Balance figures.
4. Being late by more than a quarter of an hour, or turning up after a fight with the marks of it, counts against the outing.

### C5. Confidences, and what a friendship holds (law 7, story reads the world; fix F39)
1. A confidence is a talk act: Frank tells her something true about himself that costs him something to say (his past, his worry about the firm, a deed short of a killing). It is a suggested line in every call.
2. She holds each confidence as a fact from him. If she later learns it was true from elsewhere, her trust holds firmer; if she learns it was false, it is a lie found out (C9).
3. A friendship, or a courtship, that holds at the close needs all of: at least three calls with no cooler door at the last (the floor, as RT2-04), at least one confidence she holds and has not found false, no lie she holds as a lie and has not forgiven, and no killing by Frank known as fact (question 1).
4. Lies never found out cost nothing in themselves, but a friendship built only of them holds no confidence, so at the close she is a woman who liked a man she never knew, and the close reads it so. This applies to Danby and any friend by the same rule.

### C6. Being seen together (law 1, knowing)
1. An outing in public is an act with watchers by their routines (2.2.1). Anyone who knows either of them and sees them remembers it and tells it by the ordinary rules (2.4.1).
2. Once two such watchers have told it, the courtship is a street fact: her family hears, the cab office hears, a rival hears.
3. Each who hears acts by place and temper: her mother approves or disapproves by what she knows of Frank and says so to her daughter (a pressure C2 reads); a rival may use her as a lever (a word to frighten her, a question to draw facts from her), which is a deed against her and reaches Frank as talk; Ellis notes who Frank cares for.
4. Being seen on an outing with another woman is told the same way and reaches her.

### C7. Letters and notes (law 8, the player acts through what he can do and say; fix F23)
1. Frank can write a note or a letter at the office desk or the flat table: talk on paper, built from suggested lines or typed. It is his claim (G01).
2. He signs it or not. Unsigned, the reader holds it as a told version from an unknown source; signed, as from Frank. His hand is a mark: a reader who has seen his writing knows it at face strength, and a compared sample proves it.
3. Sent by post it arrives the next morning first class (22p) or in two days second class (17p); delivered by hand, the carrier is seen at the door like any act. Prices from research.
4. A letter is an object with an owner and a place: kept in a drawer, a coat, a tin; found and read by anyone who searches or snoops (her mother, a searching officer, a burglar). What it says becomes a fact for whoever reads it.
5. Courting letters: she writes when they are walking out and a day passes without a call, or when away. A letter of hers left unanswered for a week cools her by one state. Balance figure.

### C8. Presents that are not coins (law 2, law 3)
1. A present is a real thing bought at a real shop at its price (economy draft) or made, with an owner and, if stolen, a hot mark (E4).
2. She judges it by fit: does it answer something she said (a scarf after she mentioned the cold on the front; a book by a writer she named) or something on her list? A present that fits makes the next outing go well or helps an amends; one that does not is thanked and set aside.
3. Too much too soon frightens or offends: before walking out, a present over £5 is refused or returned (balance figure). More than one present a fortnight is pressing and cools her.
4. A present can be seen worn or kept, and talked of. A stolen present she learns of is a lie found out and a deed she knows.

### C9. What ends it (law 3, law 6, recovery)
1. Any of these ends it, in person or by letter:
   - a lie found out about something that matters to her, unless he owned it before she heard it from elsewhere (owning it in time cools her instead);
   - learning of a killing by Frank as fact (3.2.5);
   - neglect: no call for three weeks while walking out cools her, a fourth ends it;
   - being seen courting another woman;
   - fear: a threat made to her because of him, or violence she sees that frightens her by temper; a frightened woman may leave town (3.2.8), a soft-edge departure;
   - her family's word, if her temper bends to it.
2. Ended is a fact she keeps (2.3.1). She can be courted again once, from Known, after an amends she accepts and two weeks; a second ending is final. Balance figures.
3. Ending kindly: if Frank ends it himself, honestly and in person, she keeps her regard and the friendship may still hold.

### C10. A rival's former partner (law 3)
1. A woman who was a rival head's or lieutenant's partner, or left him, has her dials and her own memory of that man.
2. What sets her moving:
   - She learns that Frank hurt, jailed or broke her man and holds a grudge of 0.5 or more: she wants revenge, through people and talk only. She tells Ellis or the rival's successor what she knows of Frank, or arranges a meeting where men wait.
   - Her own regard for her man was low and she learns Frank broke him: her regard for Frank rises a step, and she may be courted by C1. She knows her man's business and is a source.
   - Ellis presses her: she is a witness by the ordinary rules (area 6).
3. Her revenge is never by her own hand and never sexual; it is information and a setup.

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- In the world: he calls at her hours; asks with a line; meets her at the stop; sits beside her at the pictures; walks the front; writes at the desk and posts at the box on the corner; buys a scarf at the draper's.
- He sees it in her: how she greets him by state (his name, his first name, a smile across the street), whether she saves him a seat or waits at the stop, a letter on the mat, a present worn, a cooler door, a returned present, her mother's look. Others tell him: "Your Alison was asking after you."
- In the Ledger (area 8), in Frank's hand: what she said and did, the next arrangement ("Pictures, Saturday 7"), her letters kept, what he told her and what he holds back, and a line naming where they stand in words the street would use ("walking out since the Tuesday"). No bar, no heart, no number.

## (e) Edges

- Courting several women at once: allowed by the rules, and each sees or hears of the others through ordinary talk (C6.4), which ends it with any who learns.
- Asking a married woman, or one walking out with another man: C1.1; her man may hear.
- Pushing for more than walking out (marriage, anything physical): answered in her own words as RT-15 and the content rule say; nothing exists past walking out.
- Courting Ellis: by the same rules (decision 31); what she knows of his deeds is far more than most, and her law dial weighs it.
- Courting to use her (to get at the paper, or at a rival's facts): allowed; if she learns it, it is a lie found out.

## (f) Build stage
