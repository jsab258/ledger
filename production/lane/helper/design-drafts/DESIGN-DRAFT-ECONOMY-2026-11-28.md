# Design draft: the economy

28 November 2026. Job 6, area 1 of 8. A first full draft for next week's economy pass to review and adopt or change. Binding order: canon.md, then Jafar's rulings (including those of 25 to 27 November quoted in the job brief), then the blueprint of 28 November. The hero is Frank. Sources: ECONOMY-RESEARCH-2026-11-28.md (job 1), PRICE-BOOK-1990-2026-11-28.md (job 5), PAPER-CHAOS-TEST-2026-11-28.md and its part files (job 4), the blueprint chapters and economy files.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint already has:
- Three money places: the pocket, the tin (night money that cannot be explained) and the book (clean money) (RT-17, 2.7.1, 2.7.2). The tin is lost only to a break-in or the office search (2.7.2).
- The wash: at Sheila's daily count the book hides £10 plus 80% of fares really driven; over that she warns, thinks less of him, and each week over is recorded for Act III (2.7.3; graded in RT-01).
- Purses and tills: everybody pays only from what they have; shops restock weekly and remember (2.7.5). Prices move only on a board, for a cause (EC-05).
- Fencing at about a third of retail, less per piece in a week, buyers that differ, a 7-day buy-back at plus a quarter (2.7.6, 1.6.3, EC-10).
- Loans, blackmail, protection, Ellis's price, the money curve (EC-06 to EC-12); the firm's floor (RT2-06: office, radio and car two never lost); missed rent (RT-16).
- General rules: one ledger of favours and debts (G04), the firm and every business by its week and people (G20), the town adapts (G27), every run accounted for (G31).

What the research and the chaos test found missing:
- Cash as a physical thing outside the tin: what a beating, a mugging or an arrest takes from the pocket; a safe place other than the tin (economy research Part 3, questions 1 to 4).
- Frank cannot set his own fares; decision 21's fare war has no design (chaos G02, fix F07; Jafar's ruling 3 now says every business sets its own prices).
- Repeated incomes with no learner: tobacco within the gate pays for ever (G03, F02).
- After the ending nothing pushes back; the greedy round dominates (G04, P14, F01).
- Buyers' lists do not say what they take whole or in parts (G09, F05); information has no price (G11, F06); one debtor rule for everyone who owes Frank (F09).
- Money that stops mattering is the commonest complaint in the critics read (7 of 19 pieces, economy research Part 1).
- Act III's inspector has no fallback (R06, F33); the books as objects (R05, F32) are drafted in area 7.

## (b) The rules

Each rule sits under one of the eight laws and applies to every case of its kind.

### E1. Cash is a thing (law 2, things)
1. Money exists only as notes and coins in a place: a pocket, the tin, a safe, a till, a purse, a stash (area 7, rule T2) or a bank book. Nothing holds money abstractly.
2. A pocket holds any amount, but notes past a bulk line show: up to £300 in notes is unseen; above it the coat bulges and the bulge is a mark (G11) a watcher can catch and a searcher always finds. Balance figure.
3. What misfortune takes:
   - beaten and robbed (4.3.8, "sometimes robbed"): the robber takes from the pocket by his greed, from half to all; a man Frank knows by name takes less if he fears Frank. What he takes is a held fact; he can be found and the money traced while it is still on him.
   - mugged without a fight: the same, by the mugger's nerve and greed.
   - arrested: the custody sergeant lists all money (WI-P03). Notes up to what the fare book explains for a week (the last week's washed takings plus £20) are handed back on release. Any excess the police reasonably believe came from crime (PACE 1984 s.19, economy research) is kept as evidence until his case ends: returned when no charge stands at the close, kept if the charge is read against him. The amount kept is told to him at the desk.
4. A safe is a lock (G14) with a key or combination held by a named person. It is found by an office search only if the searcher learns it is there (a told fact, a seen key) or by a search of that room.
5. A bank or building society book holds clean money only. Paying in more than the book explains is an act the clerk remembers; the bank's records are what an inspector or a court reads.

### E2. Every business sets its prices on its own board (law 4, exchange; Jafar's ruling 3)
1. Each business has a board: its prices in chalk or print, set by its owner. The owner sets them from costs and temper; EC-05's squeeze still pushes them up.
2. Frank's firm starts on Jafar's tariff: about 60p to start, then 80p a mile, a quarter more at night (22:00 to 06:00) and on Sundays. Frank changes it at the office chalkboard (1.7.3): one act, said aloud by Pauline on the radio and by the drivers to their fares. A change takes effect next morning.
3. A buyer reacts by means, temper and options. A fare knows the prices of the firms he has used or been told of. He switches when the difference he knows of is more than his switching line: 15% for a man of low greed, 5% for a man of high greed, never if he is loyal to a driver or tied to a firm. Balance figures.
4. A business that loses trade notices it as its own week (G20): two weeks below its usual takings is a known loss. Its owner then reacts by temper: cuts his own prices, sends a man to ask why, or talks to the town. The other cab firm (2.15.3, decision 21) is such a business: it now has a usual week and reacts to fares lost for any cause, not only after a hit-and-run.
5. A trade left with no seller of its kind in the Hook draws a newcomer: four game weeks after the last seller stops, a named outsider takes the empty premises or starts from a front room (ruling 3). Balance figure.
6. Selling below cost is allowed; the book shows the loss, and drivers on the half split refuse to work a rank where their half no longer pays the week (rule E11).

### E3. Haggling (law 4)
1. Three offers at most (1.6.3, 2.7.5). The answer comes from the buyer's purse, greed, how hot the goods are and his reading of Frank (G26).
2. Standing moves willingness, not a hidden discount: a seller who reads Frank as trusted opens nearer his last price; one who reads him as feared gives way sooner and holds a grudge (G26, G07). Walking out ends the haggle; coming back the same day starts from the seller's first price.

### E4. Stolen goods and their buyers (law 2, law 4; fix F05)
1. Every stolen thing carries its owner (G13) and a hot mark that cools as talk of the deed cools (2.4.2) and with distance; an owner knows his thing anywhere (1.6.3).
2. Every buyer has a written list: what he takes, whole or in parts, his rate as a share of retail, his purse for the week, and what he refuses. The scrap yard takes a whole car and pays a parts price (a fifth of the car's value, balance figure), breaks it within two days and asks no question it would not ask a stranger; the plate is a fact the yard man keeps.
3. Others sell stolen goods too: shopkeepers are sometimes offered goods by other thieves (economy research question 19), so Rita and the pawn have rivals and a hot radio may already be on a shelf.

### E5. Information is goods (law 4; fix F06)
1. A fact Frank holds can be sold to anyone who wants it: a rival (G10), the Fixer (2.10.4), a detective in trade for a charge (2.6.9). The price is the buyer's need times his greed, from £10 to £60. Balance figures.
2. Sold, it becomes a told version with Frank as source (G01). The subject may learn who sold it by gossip.

### E6. The pots and the wash (law 4, law 1)
1. The pots stay as RT-17 and 2.7.1 to 2.7.3.
2. The wash cap is what an officer watching a night's takings would believe against fuel bought and bookings logged (economy research question 12; 1990s Customs practice): £10 plus 80% of fares really driven, plus a front's believable takings (area 4).
3. Heat slows it: while Ellis is at "investigating" or later, or a Customs letter is out, Sheila washes at three quarters of the cap and says why. Balance figure.

### E7. Every repeat income has a learner (law 4, law 5; fix F02; G27)
1. Every source of money Frank uses more than once names who learns from it, what signal he reads and what he does next. The rule applies to every repeatable source; the table gives the first set.

| Source | Learner | Signal | Step |
|---|---|---|---|

2. A source with no learner may not be built. Balance figures throughout.

### E8. After the ending the town keeps learning (law 5; fix F01)
1. Rivals stay on their rungs and climb no more and Ellis's stage holds (RT2-21), but each rival keeps its weekly pressure, every learner in E7 keeps learning, and told squeezes still reach the station as talk.
2. A business the round has squeezed for six weeks after the ending closes or moves out; its premises come up for let (area 4), so a round shrinks unless it is fair. Balance figure.
3. The newcomer rule (E2.5) runs after the ending as before.

### E9. What money buys, start to finish (law 4)
1. Money always has a use, through people and places rather than numbers (GD-07, canon):
   - Act I: rent, the debts, coal and kindnesses (G07).
   - Act II: cars on terms, a driver's half, men's cuts, a front's share, Ellis's price, the solicitor.
   - Act III: bail, the brief, repairs, keeping men loyal through the inspection.
   - After the ending: buying the building or the parade's empty unit (area 4), buying out the other firm (price: its usual week times 20, balance figure), a second car for the ranks, standing in the street (paying for a widow's flowers, a street collection, G08), and answering a newcomer.
2. One cash gate in the story only (Ellis's £800, EC-11), as the research advises, so money never becomes a wall.

### E10. One debtor rule (law 4; fix F09)
1. Anyone who owes Frank (a borrower, a tenant, a shop on his tick, a man he paid off) owes by the same rule (2.8.2): pays from his purse, begs or refuses by regard, nerve and grudge.
2. Frank's answers: wait, front up (2.8.2), write a letter (area 5, rule C7), or, for a written debt, the county court through the solicitor (a soft-edge scene, a fee, a date three weeks on). Balance figures.

### E11. Upkeep only where it makes a choice (law 4)
1. Weekly costs are only those a choice turns on: rent (RT-16), terms on cars (EC-03), the drivers' half, men's cuts, a bought officer's money, the solicitor's retainer. Each can be skipped at a stated price said by a person.
2. Hospital costs days, never money (ruling 6): no hospital bill anywhere.

### E12. The town's own money (law 4, law 3)
1. Wages arrive on Friday: purses are fullest on Friday evening and thinnest on Wednesday, which moves fares, takings, muggers' choices and debtors' answers.
2. Shops keep tick books for regulars (G04); cheques with a £50 guarantee card are taken for larger sums (economy research, 1990 facts).

### E13. The inspector and the inspection have fallbacks (law 7; fix F33)
1. The VAT inspection is a role, not a man: if the inspector is hurt, gone or dead, the office sends its senior officer with a new letter a week later; a second loss sends an officer from the regional office. The books are graded as before (RT-01).

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- He carries money in his coat; a bulge shows in a shop window's reflection and in how people look. He hides more in the tin, a safe or a stash (area 7).
- He reads prices on boards in the world: a café's menu card, the chip shop's board, his own chalkboard. He changes his tariff by chalking it.
- He hears trade in words: Sheila at the count ("Thin week, love. The other firm's doing the hospital run for 50p"), drivers grumbling, a fare saying the other firm is cheaper.
- He haggles in talk (three offers; suggested lines on the pad, ruling 4).
- The Ledger's day book records what he paid and took in his hand; the holdings page (area 8) says what is where in words: "Tin: about £200. Under the boards at the unit: the revolver and £150."

## (e) Edges

- A tariff of nothing: drivers refuse to drive at a loss and Pauline says so; the office still answers the phone.
- Buying a whole shop's stock: the owner sells what is on the shelf, restocks next week, and remembers.
- Carrying hundreds in the pocket: allowed, seen and taken.
- Trying to buy the bank or a public building: the bank is not for sale (canon, WI-P12); the town hall is off the map (G21).
- Trying to wash more than the book will stand: Sheila refuses past a quarter over and says why; the record of weeks over stands (2.7.3).

## (h) Questions only Jafar can decide

1. What does a beating take from Frank's pocket?
   - A. The robber takes half to all by his greed, pocket only (recommended: a real loss with a way to win it back, as in Yakuza 0, and never the tin).
   - B. Always everything in the pocket.
   - C. Never money; only marks and talk.
2. Can the police keep cash at an arrest?
   - A. Yes, cash above what the fare book explains, held until his case ends (recommended: 1990 law, PACE s.19, and a reason to wash).
   - B. No, all cash returned on release, as WI-P03 says today.
   - C. All cash kept until the case ends.
3. After the ending, may the other cab firm be bought out?
   - A. Yes, at its usual week times 20, through its owner's temper (recommended: a lasting use for money that is about people and place).
   - B. No, it stays the other firm for ever.
   - C. Yes, but only before the ending.

Decided here without asking, and why: the bulk line instead of a pocket cap (keeps cash physical without a number on screen, canon); a learner for every repeat income (G27 already decided the principle); no hospital bills (ruling 6); the newcomer's timing as a balance figure (ruling 3 says "in time").
