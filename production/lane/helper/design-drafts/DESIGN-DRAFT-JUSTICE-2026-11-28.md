# Design draft: justice

28 November 2026. Job 6, area 6 of 8. A first full draft for the justice pass. Binding order: canon.md, Jafar's rulings, the blueprint. Sources: DEPTH-RESEARCH-JUSTICE-2026-11-28.md (job 3), the implication drill (job 2, areas A2 and B4), the paper chaos test (job 4), the economy and docks drafts (areas 1 and 3) and the blueprint. The law below is as it stood around 1990; this is design for a game, not legal advice.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint has:
- Arrest needs a signed statement from someone who knew it was him, or an officer who saw it; a statement is made at the station at nine the morning after the call (2.6.3). A signed statement stays on file and can be taken back (WI-P07).
- Custody as hours; property booked at arrest, paper money handed back on release, the envelope held a week if it looks like evidence (2.6.4, WI-P03).
- An arrest ends in a caution, or a charge and bail; a charge joins Ellis's case and is read at the ending; no court scene and no prison for a lesser charge; a killing charge alone has the magistrates' scenes and remand (2.6.9, LH-07).
- Ellis's four stages (2.6.5); everyone who knows is leaned on (LH-04); the inquiry into a death (CS-27); 999 from a box brings a constable to the spot (RT2-19); the hospital as a soft-edge scene where violence is a deed seen by many (WI-P11); typed text that claims an act is talk, never an act (RT-15).

What the research, the drill and the chaos test found missing:
- Police bail has no date and no return (drill B4; research J1).
- Cash at arrest: RT-17 keeps it, WI-P03 hands it back (research J2; settled in the economy draft, E1.3, and restated here).
- Seized goods have no return rule, and the owner never learns where they were (research J3).
- A statement only at the station, so a witness in hospital is a delayed witness (F18; chaos K12).
- A tip is not weighed and a locked place has no warrant rule (F21; chaos S06).
- Deeds done by talk (deception, posing as police) are not in the deed table (F27; chaos T06).
- Soft-edge scenes do not say which hand acts they allow (F17; chaos K11). No rule for a person in a seat (F20; chaos K06).
- Who may report a wound (research J5). A missing person does not accumulate (F14; chaos K02, with area 7).

## (b) The rules

### J1. Police bail: a date, a place, no conditions (law 3, law 5)
1. Bail after arrest is a duty to come back: to the station on a written date while inquiries go on (two to four game weeks, balance figure), or to the magistrates' court on a charge (PACE 1984 s.47, research).
2. The police set no conditions: in 1990 only a court could (Bail Act 1976 s.3(6), research). Frank may go where he likes and see whom he likes; anything he does to a witness meanwhile is a new deed (WI-P07, LH-04).
3. The station may write to say he need not come ("no further action"); the letter lands on the mat.
4. Failing to answer without good cause is an offence of its own (Bail Act s.6): a warrant is issued, the constable calls at the office the next morning, the charge joins the case, and the street hears that Frank has run. Answering on time is noted by the station and lowers no one's opinion.
5. Court dates on a charge fall about nine weeks on (64 days from first listing in 1990, research), so most fall after the books open and are read at the close, as 2.6.9 already does. A date that falls inside the story takes a morning off screen and ends in a fine or a discharge read as one line; never prison for a lesser charge.

### J2. What is taken at arrest and what comes back (law 2, things)
1. Cash follows the economy draft (E1.3): notes and coins are listed; on release they come back, except an amount the custody sergeant believes is the proceeds of a named crime, read as more than last week's washed takings plus £20 (PACE s.19 and s.22, research). Kept cash comes back when the case ends without a charge on it.
2. Lawful things not needed as evidence come back on release; a photograph will do for many (PACE s.22). A weapon is kept.
3. Stolen goods are kept until the owner is traced. The owner gets them back through the police or a magistrates' order (Police (Property) Act 1897, research), and in getting them he learns where they were found ("in a drawer at Mickey's"). That is a fact he holds, read by his temper (law 1).
4. A Customs seizure is Customs' own road (docks draft, D6).

### J3. A statement is taken where the witness is (law 3; fix F18)
1. The default stays: the station at nine the morning after the call (2.6.3).
2. A witness who cannot come (in hospital, laid up at home, too frightened to leave the house) is visited by the officer the report brings, on his next morning round, and signs at the bed or the door.
3. A witness in hospital signs once she can talk: the next day for a serious hurt, at once for a light one (1.3.11 sets the days in hospital, not the days before she can talk). Balance figures.
4. Hurting a witness to stop her is a deed of its own and adds to the case (WI-P07); it delays her statement by a day at most.

### J4. Tips and warrants (law 1, knowing; fix F21)
1. A tip (a 999 call from a box, a word to the beat constable, a letter, C7 in the courting draft) is a claim weighed by G03 against what the station holds.
2. Fit decides the answer:

| Fit | Answer |
|---|---|
| None: names no one the station knows of, or a deed it has no sign of | noted; Ellis hears of it at her next look at the book |
| Some: names a person or place the station already wonders about | the beat constable calls or asks round the next day |
| Strong: fits a reported deed and names a locked place | a magistrate's warrant the next day (PACE s.8) and a search that day |
| A deed in progress at a spot | a constable to the spot that night (RT2-19) |

3. A tip that proves false lowers the next tip from the same box or voice one step; two false tips from one box and the station stops sending anyone on its word. Balance figures.
4. The man searched is told only "information received". He asks who; his suspicion falls on whoever his facts point to (law 1), which may be Frank or someone else.

### J5. Deeds done by talk (law 3, people; fix F27)
1. G19's deed table gains three rows:

| Deed | 1990 offence | Seriousness | Street reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Getting money or goods by a lie | obtaining property by deception (Theft Act 1968 s.15) | as theft of the same value | "a con man" |
| Posing as a police officer, in talk or in uniform | impersonating a constable (Police Act 1964 s.52) | a lesser charge; the station takes it as against itself, so Ellis's interest rises one stage | "pretending to be the law" |
| A false promise for money | obtaining by deception | as theft of the sum | "took his money and never did it" |

2. The victim holds the deed once he learns the truth (G03: a lie found out). He learns it when the truth meets him: the real police call, the promised thing never comes, a neighbour says. He reports it by temper and law dial.
3. Offence names are from own knowledge and are to be checked against the 1990 texts before the build.

### J6. Hand acts at the soft edge (law 8; fix F17)
1. Every soft-edge talk scene states the hand acts it allows. Those acts are played as acts (a key or a pad button, as on the street), never typed. Typed text that claims an act stays talk (RT-15).

| Scene | Hand acts allowed |
|---|---|
| A hospital visit (WI-P11) | strike, grab, leave |
| A caff table, a front room, a call on a friend | strike, grab, leave |
| The Custom House interview (docks D6), the station interview (2.6.11) | none; leave only when let go |
| The solicitor, the council counter, the magistrates (LH-07) | leave |
| A courting outing (courting draft, C4) | leave, and strike if someone else starts it |

2. A deed done in a scene is seen by everyone the scene holds, at face distance.

### J7. A person in a seat (law 8; fix F20)
1. One rule for anyone sitting (a cab seat, a bench, a bed, a chair): Frank can grab, pull out and strike at short reach. The seated person has a chance to get up or out first, by nerve and how near the door is; a person in a bed has none.
2. A passenger driven off his way first asks, then protests, then tries the door when the car stops or slows under about 10 mph, by nerve. At speed he stays in and remembers, and shouts to the first person he sees when he can. Balance figures.
3. Pulling a passenger out of a cab is a deed by G19 like pulling a driver out (1.2.10).

### J8. Wounds and who may report them (law 1, law 3)
1. In 1990 no rule made a doctor report a stab wound or a beating (research: the GMC asked for knife wounds to be reported only from 2009). The casualty doctor is a person who weighs it.
2. He tells the police when the patient tells him how it happened and names a person, when the police are already asking about that night, or when his own law dial is high and the wound is grave. Ambulance crew and nurses are witnesses of what they saw and heard. Balance rule.
3. Gunshot wounds are always reported to the police (research says only that they were treated differently later; a design choice, and guns are rare and late by canon).
4. Hospital costs days, never money (ruling 6). A back-street patch-up (a named person who once nursed) costs a favour or a few pounds and the same days, and no doctor writes anything; she knows, and can tell.

### J9. A missing person accumulates (law 1, law 3; fix F14)
1. A person who vanishes is missed by those whose routines cross his: a landlady or family after a day or two, workmates after three days, a man alone after a week. The first to miss him reports him. Balance figures.
2. The police read his ties: who last spoke to him, whom he owed, who had threatened him, where he was last seen. Those people are visited (LH-04).
3. Each further disappearance in the Hook raises the answer: two make CID treat them as linked and Ellis's interest rises one stage; three bring a squad from the county, as the long heat does for a constable. Balance figures.
4. Whether and when a body comes back is area 7 (body disposal acts).

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- In the world: the custody sergeant's counter with his property on it; a bail sheet in his pocket with the date; a letter on the mat ("no further action", a warrant, a court date); the constable at the office door; the doctor in casualty; the landlady asking after her lodger.
- In talk: suggested lines for every act above (ruling 4), including "I'm from CID" and "Information received, was it?".
- In the Ledger (area 8): open matters as papers, each with its date: bail sheets, charge sheets, property receipts, a court date, a warrant he has heard of. What Frank knows of each witness's state ("Mrs Coverdale signed at the hospital on Wednesday").

## (e) Edges

- Hiding in the flat on the bail date: J1.4; the constable calls next morning, and the street hears.
- Tipping off the police about everyone, every night: J4.3 wears the box out; the station also notes the voice.
- Posing as police repeatedly: each victim who learns raises Ellis one stage once; the tunic is a mark (G11).
- Putting every witness in hospital: each is a deed and a day's delay at most (J3); Q3 holds.
- Killing in a hospital ward: J6 allows strike; the ward is full of witnesses; the police are called at once (WI-P11).

## (h) Questions only Jafar can decide

1. Should a doctor report a stab wound or a beating?
   - A. Only by his own weighing, as J8 (recommended: true to 1990 and makes the doctor a person).
   - B. Always, as a later age would.
   - C. Never.
2. How heavy is posing as a police officer?
   - A. A lesser charge that raises Ellis's interest one stage (recommended: it works once and then the law takes it personally).
   - B. A serious charge, read with the killings.
   - C. A lesser charge only.
3. Should repeated disappearances bring a squad from the county?
   - A. Yes: two linked, three bring a squad (recommended: the lasting easy win G27 forbids is closed by people, not a cap).
   - B. Two linked only; no squad.
   - C. Each inquiry runs alone.

Decided here without asking: bail dates and no conditions follow the 1990 law; court dates about nine weeks on follow the 1990 figures, which keeps 2.6.9's reading at the close true; cash at arrest follows the economy draft; seized goods follow PACE s.22 and the 1897 Act.
