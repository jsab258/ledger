# The Ledger interface: research (job 5, part 1)

28 November 2026. For the design passes that start next week. Every page below was read in full on 28 November 2026 unless marked otherwise; nothing rests on search snippets. Counts are counts within the sources read here (one to four per game: 24 sources on games and 8 on period objects), not a survey: read them as "this is what the reviews I read say", never as a share of all players.

## The short answer

The games that handle knowledge best make the record itself the play: Obra Dinn's logbook, Outer Wilds' web of clues, Her Story's search box. Their critics praise the book and almost never the menus. The games that handle holdings worst bolt a spreadsheet onto an action game: Shadow of War's army screens have no sort or search, and Yakuza 0's business screens mix five kinds of rating, do not mark what you own on the map, and sit five clicks and a walk from play. Big open-world games (RDR2, Witcher 3, KCD2) win praise for the written content of their books (Arthur's sketches, codex entries) and lose it for the menus around them (clunky, cluttered, tiny text). The newest, GTA VI, puts its phone in the hero's hand without pausing the game, and its review still notes submenus. Pentiment, the closest in spirit to LEDGER (a small town, people who remember, time that runs out), is praised for its journal of people and criticised for notes sorted so that the player loses track of open leads after time jumps.

For LEDGER the pattern suggests: a book whose pages read like Frank's own hand, a few fixed pages that grow by entries rather than by screens, and the world carrying the rest. The questions in section C are for the design pass to settle; nothing here decides.

## Method

Questions written first (NOTES.md): per game, (1) what it shows of what the player knows, owns, owes and has set in motion; (2) how many steps from play to an answer; (3) how a long list is handled after many hours; (4) what is praised; (5) what is hated, with how many sources say so. Sources were reviews, developer posts, guides and wikis, read from the outside; no videos or game files. Step counts are from the source where it gives them, otherwise marked "inferred" from the source's description.

### Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018)

What it shows. Arthur's journal records each mission from his point of view, adds sketches of animals, herbs and places and a diary entry at each chapter's start; a full journal "can clear 500 pages" ([TheGamer, Stacey Henley, 6 April 2021](https://www.thegamer.com/red-dead-redemption-2-journal/)). The camp's upgrades are bought in a physical ledger beside the tithing box: put money in the box, press one button at the book, choose from eight categories such as provisions, ammunition and lodgings ([PowerPyx guide](https://powerpyx.com/red-dead-redemption-2-how-to-upgrade-camp)). The store catalogue has a "fully written, lengthy catalog entry" for every weapon and outfit ([Kotaku, Kirk Hamilton, 25 October 2018](https://kotaku.com/red-dead-redemption-2-the-kotaku-review-1829984369)).

Steps. The ledger is reached by walking to it in camp and pressing one button (PowerPyx). Steps to the journal were not found in the sources read.

Long lists. No sort, filter or search was found in the sources read.

Praise. Henley calls the journal her favourite thing in the game because "it's the smallest moments that really mean something", and notes it is "impossible to fail" (TheGamer). Kotaku praises the catalogue writing.

Complaints. Kotaku says an "unclear user interface" keeps players "at arm's length", that progression is "often confusingly laid out and poorly explained", and that Rockstar hides numbers "at almost every opportunity", which Hamilton counts partly as a strength. Looting runs "an involved animation that takes several seconds". Henley adds that a first 90-hour run can be played "virtually journal free": the best-written record is skippable.

Rests on: 3 sources (one review, one feature, one guide).

### Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2 (2025)

What it shows. A journal of quests, a codex of history and people, and a map. Choosing a quest step in the journal shows its place on the map; one button pins it with a red flag ([GamingBolt, Matthew Carmosino, 5 February 2025](https://gamingbolt.com/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-guide-13-tips-and-tricks-you-need-to-know)).

Steps. Open the journal tab, select the step, the map shows it: about three (GamingBolt; count inferred).

Praise. "Reams of codex entries giving in depth background information" ([TheSixthAxis, Gareth Chadwick, 3 February 2025](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2025/02/03/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-review/)).

Complaints. The same review: the look is a "skeumorphic medieval style", but "the maps, inventory, quest log screens slide a lot of details and important information off the side of the screens"; its list of faults reads "Menu UI is cluttered and awkward".

Rests on: 2 sources.

### Disco Elysium (2019)

What it shows. The thought cabinet holds ideas Harry has picked up; equipping one costs a slot and in-game time before it becomes part of him. RPGamer calls it "a representation of the player character's brain space" and says "it's best to think of [it] as the game's perk system" ([RPGamer review](https://rpgamer.com/review/disco-elysium-review/)).

Complaints. The console accessibility review asks "What the hell is a thought cabinet?", says the game introduces uncommon terms without explaining them, and that the reviewer "internalized three thoughts and nothing happened"; text options are tiny and the text slider's three sizes are all too small ([Can I Play That?, Coty Craven, 31 March 2021](https://caniplaythat.com/2021/03/31/disco-elysium-the-final-cut-can-i-play-that-accessibility-review/)).

Note for LEDGER. A record of beliefs rather than facts is novel; the complaint is about naming and legibility, not the idea.

Rests on: 2 sources.

### Outer Wilds (2019)

What it shows. The ship log keeps everything learned across time loops, in a map mode and a "rumour" mode where entries are linked by arrows. The developers added this detective board because "through playtests we've been having some issues conveying" that the game is a mystery; it "maps your discoveries by how they point to each other" and grows "into an intricate web of photos and clues" ([Mobius Digital blog, Alex Beachum, 19 January 2018](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/news/archives/01-2018)).

Long lists. The web is the sort: entries cluster by place and connect by cause. An orange asterisk marks an entry with information still to find ([Edge review, issue of 20 June 2019, score 9](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20190620/282278141843961)).

Praise. Edge: knowledge "stored in your ship's log as an everexpanding web of rumours" is what opens the game, not resources or upgrades, and following its vague hints usually finds "something significant".

Complaints. None about the log in Edge; the developers' own note shows the first version was not understood.

Rests on: 2 sources.

### Return of the Obra Dinn (2018)

What it shows. A logbook with "a full crew and passenger manifest, annotated deck maps", a glossary and a sketch of everyone aboard; the player pencils in names and fates, and once three are right "the game will let you know by properly typesetting your penciled notes" ([GameSpot review](https://gamespot.com/reviews/return-of-the-obra-dinn-review-the-good-ship/1900-6417017/)).

Praise. GameSpot calls the book interface "a masterpiece of connected design".

Complaints. None in the source read.

Note for LEDGER. Pencil turning to print is a way to show certainty without a number.

Rests on: 1 source.

### Her Story (2015)

What it shows. A 1990s police computer: type a word, see clips in which it is spoken. Results are capped at five a search, biased to the earliest clips; the player can tag clips with his own words and queue them ([Giant Bomb review](https://giantbomb.com/reviews/her-story-review/1900-705/)).

Complaints. "There's no easy way to simply watch every video in order, even after you've collected all 200+", and the last hour felt like typing random words to finish (Giant Bomb).

Note for LEDGER. A period device can be the search; the limit is part of the fiction.

Rests on: 1 source.

### Hitman (World of Assassination, 2016 to 2021)

What it shows. Mission stories (guided leads that appear when the player overhears something) and a separate intel tab.

Praise and complaints. "By default, Hitman 2 does a lot of hand holding"; following a mission story "strips away the puzzle element in favour of basic execution"; with prompts off the information is still "logged in a separate intel tab", and the reviewer prefers hints disabled ([PC Gamer, Phil Savage, 8 November 2018, score 84](https://pcgamer.com/hitman-2-review)).

Note for LEDGER. A lead the player wrote down himself (or overheard) feels like his; one the game walks him through does not.

Rests on: 1 source.

### Middle-earth: Shadow of War (2017)

What it shows. An army of named orc captains with traits, placed in garrisons and fortresses, managed in menus.

Complaints. "The menu systems for your Orcs and weapons is the part that feels most overburdened. It's grating that there's no way to sort or search through your own army", and "There's no overview screen that lists out what effects you currently have active" ([GameSpot review](https://www.Gamespot.Com/reviews/middle-earth-shadow-of-war-review/1900-6416783/)). "Too many menus, too much clutter on the screen, too many half-baked features" ([PC Gamer, Andy Kelly, 5 October 2017, score 73](https://pcgamer.com/middle-earth-shadow-of-war-review)).

Note for LEDGER. This is the crew page's warning: a list of named men grows, and without a way to find "who is where" or "who can drive" it becomes work.

Rests on: 2 sources, both complaining.

### Yakuza 0 (2015, West 2017)

What it shows. Kiryu's real-estate business: areas, shops bought, staff assigned, ratings.

Complaints (one detailed UX analysis listing nine problems; [Maria Meireles, SuperJump, 8 October 2020](https://medium.com/super-jump/a-ux-analysis-of-real-estate-royale-yakuza-0s-game-within-a-game-876b57ad1640)):
- the agency menu uses five rating formats: arrows, letters D to S, numbers, stars and symbols;
- owned stores are not marked on the area map, so the player cycles through store cards to find what he owns;
- staff influence is five unlabelled numbers, and each employee's three ratings are only visible after clicking in;
- checking business information from the world map "takes five clicks and a movement"; some players used internet guides to find their own properties; the author's redesign brings it to two clicks;
- the business office shares a map colour with story places.

Note for LEDGER. A front's week and the property list are exactly where this goes wrong: several scales, nothing marked on the map, answers several screens deep.

Rests on: 1 source.

### GTA VI (2026; gameplay only, no story)

What it shows. The phone is pulled out by the character, D-pad up; the game keeps running. Apps cover banking, contacts, ride hailing, a car scanner (locks, alarm, tracker, value), vehicle delivery, sleep and tiredness, messages and photos; no app tracks jobs, which come by calls ([BeatCop guide, 19 November 2026](https://beatcopgame.com/how-to-use-the-phone-in-gta-6/)).

Steps. About three presses to open any app; four or five to call a contact (same guide, counted from its instructions).

Complaints. The review notes controls "hard to keep straight", with "multiple actions mapped to one button or a submenu" ([Kotaku, Zack Zwiezen, 18 November 2026](https://kotaku.com/grand-theft-auto-6-the-kotaku-review-2000757842)).

Note for LEDGER. A held object that does not pause the world makes looking a risk, which suits a man reading his book in a cold office.

Rests on: 2 sources.

### Sleeping Dogs (2012)

What it shows. Wei Shen's "all-powerful magic police phone" taps street cameras; separate Face, Cop and Triad ratings drive skill trees ([PC Gamer, Tom Senior, 6 September 2012, score 74](https://www.pcgamer.com/sleeping-dogs-review/)).

Note for LEDGER. Three visible standing meters are what LEDGER has ruled out (no numbers on screen); the lesson is the opposite one.

Rests on: 1 source.

### The Witcher 3 (2015; remaster 2026)

What it shows. A journal with quests in tabs by kind (main, secondary, contracts, treasure hunts) and a bestiary of monster weaknesses, filled by fights and books ([GamePressure guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/thewitcher3/categories-of-quests/zc7858); [GameRevolution review, score 7](https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=67931)).

Complaints. "The quest tracker regularly fails to show progression", "it can be difficult to toggle between different layers of the user interface", "the font on the menus is far too small" (GameRevolution). The interface "feels like a first draft"; the menu system is "clunky and tedious" ([Game Rant, 30 May 2015](https://gamerant.com/witcher-3-wild-hunt-reviews/)).

Eleven years on, the 2026 remaster gives incomplete questlines their own map marker and adds a "Bard's Ballad" mode "designed to reduce cognitive load" ([GamesRadar, Anthony McGlynn, 28 September 2026](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/the-witcher/the-witcher-3-remaster-overhauls-skill-trees-adds-new-quest-markers-brings-a-mode-to-reduce-cognitive-load-and-more-cd-projekt-red-reveals-in-patch-notes/)).

Rests on: 4 sources (2 reviews, 1 guide, 1 news piece); both reviews complain.

### Pentiment (2022)

What it shows. Andreas's journal keeps "a list of all the characters Andreas meets" with notes on appearance and background, and it "won't provide concrete answers" ([Game Rant, Evan Regan, 22 November 2022](https://gamerant.com/pentiment-most-common-mistakes/)). Quests are written in period script.

Complaints. "The chaotic menu navigation"; investigation notes "sorted in a confusing way"; "I lose track of open points to investigate", worst after time jumps; the reviewer still calls these small flaws ([Galaxus, Domagoj Belancic, 14 November 2022](https://www.galaxus.ch/en/page/pentiment-review-a-gripping-medieval-thriller-with-unique-graphics-25512)).

Note for LEDGER. The closest model: a town that remembers, time that runs out, a journal of people. Its failure is exactly LEDGER's risk with a seven-week day book: open matters lost among entries.

Rests on: 2 sources.

### What the reviews read agree on

- Written content is praised, frames are blamed: RDR2's journal, KCD2's codex, Obra Dinn's book are praised; the menus around them are where complaints land (RDR2, KCD2, Witcher 3, Shadow of War, Yakuza 0, Pentiment: 6 of the 13 games, in 8 of the 24 game sources).
- Lists of things the player owns or commands, with no sort, search or map marking, draw the sharpest complaints (Shadow of War, Yakuza 0: 3 of 3 sources).
- Records that are the play (Obra Dinn, Outer Wilds, Her Story) draw praise for the record itself; the only complaint in the 4 sources is Her Story's lack of a way to watch clips in order.
- Tiny text is named for Witcher 3 and Disco Elysium (2 games, 2 sources).

## C. Questions for the design pass

These are questions, not decisions.

1. Holdings. Should what Frank owns (cars, a front's share, the tin, stored goods) be one page of short lines in his hand ("Car 3, Terry's, terms £22 Fri"), or should most of it live in the world (cars on the yard, goods in the shed, the tin's weight) with the book only listing what he cannot see? Yakuza 0's complaint is about owned things not marked where they are.
2. Crew. Shadow of War's complaint is no sort and no "who is where". The office board (1.7.3) already places cars and drivers. Is the board the crew page, with the Ledger only holding each man's terms and what Frank knows of him?
3. Arrangements. A truce, a bought constable, a protection round are agreements with terms (G04). Should each be one written line with its terms and the next date it is due, like a diary entry carried forward, and nothing else?
4. A front's week. Should a front show as Sheila saying its takings aloud at the count, and the Ledger as one line per week ("Caff: £30, Tues short"), rather than any table? How many weeks back should the book keep before a summary line?
5. Courtship. Should a courtship have no page at all, only what Frank has written about her on the People page and what the town says, so that the state is never a meter?
6. Planted stories. Should a story Frank planted appear as a line with whom he told and when, and later the version that came back to him, so he can compare (Obra Dinn's pencil and print)?
7. Taps. What is the most steps from play to any answer: one key to open the book, one to turn to a page, one to find a line? Should search exist at all in 1990, or should the day book be findable by date tabs and a margin mark for open matters (Outer Wilds' asterisk)?
8. The seven-week day book. Pentiment's players lost open leads after time jumps. Should open matters always sit on their own page, carried forward each morning, so the day book can stay a plain diary?
9. Pausing. GTA VI does not pause for the phone. Should reading the Ledger stop the clock (as talk does) or let the world go on, so that reading in the street is a risk?
10. What lives in the world: Sheila saying the week aloud, the board on the office wall, the tin's contents counted in the hand, letters on the mat, the radio. Which of these should hold a fact so completely that the Ledger does not repeat it?
11. Certainty. Should Frank's guesses be written differently from what he knows (pencil and ink, or "they say" and "I saw"), so the book shows certainty without a number? Should a wrong entry be struck through and left legible, as a constable's pocket book requires, rather than erased?
12. Legibility. Two of the games read had text too small to read on a television. What is the smallest Ledger handwriting the accessibility rules (AX-01, 24 units) allow, and is there a plain-print option like Pentiment's?
