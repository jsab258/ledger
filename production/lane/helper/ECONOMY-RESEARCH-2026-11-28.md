# LEDGER economy research, 28 November 2026

Job 1 of 2 for the economy pass. Research only: what other games do with money, what Britain around 1990 did with it, and the questions this raises. Read against the blueprint zip of 28 Nov (economy files, study 6, the prior research index, the Money page, rules G04, G05, G13, G20, canon.md, RULINGS.md). Every source was read on 28 Nov 2026 unless marked. UNVERIFIED means one fan page, a page that disagrees with another, or a figure seen but not read in full. Nothing here was measured in play.

Name note: canon.md in the zip calls the hero Tom Nowak; the blueprint chapters call him Frank since run 5 of 24 Nov (changelog line 30 cites "canon and RULINGS of 24 Nov"), but the canon.md and RULINGS.md in this zip do not carry that rename. This file says "the hero".

## The short version

What LEDGER should copy:
- Cash you can lose. GTA VI caps pocket cash at $20,000 and takes it all on arrest or death while the bank is safe; Scarface loses all dirty cash on death or arrest; Yakuza 0's Mr. Shakedown takes all you carry. The pocket is LEDGER's gap.
- Stolen goods with a mark that fades: KCD2's red hand lapses after 1 to 2 game weeks in storage; guards take what you carry, not what you stored.
- Buyers with purses and their own rates: KCD2's fence holds about 200 groschen and restocks; each GTA VI fence pays its own rate by kind of goods, and some refuse.
- Dirty money worthless until converted: GTA VI bank jobs pay marked bills that cannot be banked until fenced.
- A laundering cut that rises with heat and is haggled (Scarface).
- An extortion meter with a breaking point (The Godfather): push past it and the owner pays nothing and fights.
- Standing bends prices and access: RDR2 honour discounts reach 50%; Sleeping Dogs' Face gates the best goods; a KCD2 brand stops every shop trading with you.

What critics hate (19 pieces coded by hand; Steam was unreachable): money that stops mattering (7: Sleeping Dogs 3, KCD2, RDR2, GTA V, Empire of Sin); repetition and chores in earning, collecting and defending (9: Scarface 3, Empire of Sin 3, KCD2, Godfather, Yakuza 0); waiting for money (Empire of Sin); business menus spread over many screens (Yakuza 0).

The 1990 facts that matter:
- Cash pay was fading but common: 78% of manual workers were paid in cash in 1979, about 39% of wage payments in 1986; 27% of adults had no bank account in 1985. No 1990 figure found.
- A guarantee card covered a £50 cheque (since 1977); from 1989 banks could set £100 or £250. Cheques peaked in 1990.
- Pawns are redeemable for six months; small unredeemed ones pass to the broker, larger ones are sold after notice, any surplus to the owner.
- Smuggled cigarettes were a small trade (about 3% early in the decade against 21% by 2000, one unsourced figure); duty-free was 200 cigarettes a journey.
- In 1990s cases VAT officers caught cash businesses by watching a night's cash-up and checking purchases against declared takings.
- Police could seize anything on premises they believed was got by crime; confiscation needed £10,000 of benefit.
- A private hire operator must log every booking before the journey and show the log to a constable or council officer on request.

Design questions, by heading:
- Cash as a thing: does the pocket have a cap, and what does a beating, an arrest or a mugging take?
- Prices from people: does standing move prices, or only who will serve him?
- Stolen goods and fences: does heat fade by time, place or talk?
- The firm's books: the fare book is a record a constable can read; what does that do to the wash?
- Dirty money and laundering: set the owed wash cap as takings a VAT officer would believe against fuel and bookings.
- What money buys over the whole game: what is left to want after the story (F3)?
- The town's own economy: cheques, tick books, Friday wages, shops offered stolen goods by others.
- Upkeep only where it makes a choice: keep MP-03, drop costs that only make you wait.
- Managing money without a spreadsheet: one place, owned things on the paper map.

## Part 3: design questions for the economy pass

Each question names what raises it. None is decided here.

Cash as a thing
1. Does the hero's pocket have a cap, with money over it pushed to the tin? (GTA VI's $20,000.)
2. What does each misfortune take from the pocket: a beating (1.3.9's small bill), an arrest (PACE s19 lets police take money they believe came from crime), a mugging? Scarface and GTA VI take all; RDR2 a share; Yakuza 0 all, with a way to win it back.
3. Is there a safe place besides the tin, and what trail does it leave? A building society book, a bank account (the bank is the town's one CCTV), a stash in the yard. A deposit the inspection can read is a choice: safety against evidence.
4. Can the hero rob other people's boxes the way KCD2 is said to let him rob a merchant's chest (UNVERIFIED)? The shops' margins (prices file s5) then sit somewhere physical.

Prices from people
5. Does the town's reading of the hero move prices (RDR2 up to 50%), or only who will serve him (KCD2's brand, Sleeping Dogs' Face)? Canon's "no price tables" suggests the second: refusals and favours in words.
6. Keep the three-offer haggle (2.7.5) rather than KCD2's meter? KCD2 shows walking out costing reputation works.

Stolen goods and fences
7. Does an item's heat fade by time (KCD2 1 to 2 weeks), by distance (selling out of the Hook), or only by talk cooling (2.4.2)?
8. Give each buyer a rate in words by kind of goods and a list of refusals (GTA VI)? 2.7.6 already has buyers differ; this makes it a choice of where to go.
9. Owners know their things anywhere (G13): does a pawn ticket give the owner and the police a trail? 1990 pawns needed a receipt and a six-month hold.

The firm's books
10. The fare book is a legal booking record a constable may read (1976 Act s56). Should Ellis be able to ask for it, and does a padded book then become evidence, not only an Act III grade?
11. Drivers' past: in 1991 police were said to withhold convictions from councils. A driver with a record could be on the books unseen: a hook, or a risk.

Dirty money and laundering
