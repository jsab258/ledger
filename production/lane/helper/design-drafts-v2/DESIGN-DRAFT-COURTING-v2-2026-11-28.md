# Design draft: courting (version 2)

28 November 2026. Helper job 8, area 7 of 8. Version 2 of DESIGN-DRAFT-COURTING-2026-11-28.md, revised from the blind check of 28 November. Same structure and numbering as version 1. Binding order: canon.md, Jafar's rulings, the blueprint. Every finding was checked at its source; the change table at the end says what was accepted or rejected. Content rule throughout: affection only, nothing sexual, no marriage, no drink or betting anywhere, no children.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint has:
- G06 and 3.2.8: courting is a "can-you" under the people rules, women only, each by her own dials; nothing explicit; lightly scripted; no marriage, so it goes as far as walking out. She may stay loyal, help trap a rival, be frightened and leave; a rival's former partner may want revenge. Ellis can be courted while she stays the bent detective (decision 31); Danby stays a friend (decision 9).
- Calling on people: at her hours, under two real minutes, no score; a second call inside the set gap draws a cooler door (3.2.7).
- The friendship that holds at the close (3.2.5, ruled; RT2-04): at least three calls, no cooler door at the last, no killing known as fact; 3.2.5 calls it a friendship "built from short calls and the honesty in them".
- Places that exist (decision 19; G21 keeps the Tivoli and the like off the map): the caff, the tea room, the quay and the basin with its fishing spots, the chapel hall's Tuesday evening of tea and talk (4.10), the allotments and pigeon loft on Chapel Lane.
- Letters reach Frank on the mat (2.4.5, G21).

What was missing or wrong (research, chaos test, blind check):
- Steps of courting, what draws her, being seen together, letters, what ends it, the former partner.
- Outings to places off the map (C-2, X-6); counters the player must manage (C-5); an outing's real minutes with nothing to do (C-6); drink "not the point" rather than absent (C-1); a draper who does not exist; a special limit on striking (J-2).

## (b) The rules

### C1. The courting ladder: named states lifted by events (law 3, people; law 7)
Courting has four states and two side states. Each is a fact she and Frank hold, never a number. Each step is lifted by named events, and she may always refuse.

| State | What it means | Lifted to the next by |
|---|---|---|
| Known | she knows his name and face | a call in which he asks her to go somewhere and she says yes (C2) |
| Keeping company | she will meet him at an arranged time and place | two outings that went well (C4) and being seen on them by two people who know either of them (C6) |
| Seen together | the street says they are courting | he asks her to walk out with him and she says yes (C2), after a third outing that went well |
| Walking out | an understanding: in the street's eyes she is his girl and he her man | the top; there is no marriage |
| Cooling (side) | she is hurt or doubtful: she takes calls but refuses outings | an amends she accepts (G07) returns her to the state she cooled from |
| Ended (side) | she has ended it | C9 |

1. A woman who is not unattached cannot be courted: asking her is a remark that she, and her man if he hears of it, take by temper.
2. Every talk act on the ladder is a suggested line on a pad as well as a typed one (ruling 4).
3. Typed lines past the content rule are answered in her own words as RT-15 says, and remembered.
4. Who can be courted: any unattached woman among the regulars whose data gives her lists (C2), by G06's one rule; Alison and Ellis are written first.

### C2. What draws her: traits seen in deeds, never coins (law 3, law 1)
1. Each woman who can be courted has a short written list of what draws her (three things) and what she cannot abide (two things), as data. Alison's draws: a man who keeps his word, honesty about the fire and the warehouse row, kindness to people with less. She cannot abide being lied to, or being shown off.
2. A draw counts only as a fact she holds about him (law 1). She says yes to a step when she holds at least one fact for each of two draws, holds none of the things she cannot abide, and her regard is 0.5 or more (G07). Balance figures.
3. The same deed reads differently to different women; the list decides, not the player's intent.
4. Nothing is bought: no present, favour or number of calls lifts a step by itself (research: the "kindness coins" warning).

### C3. Time is the price (law 5, time)
1. She has a routine: Alison's press days twice a week, her mother's Sunday, her evenings. Calls and outings happen only inside it (3.2.7).
2. An outing is played as a short call (3.2.7's two-minute scene) at the place, after which the clock moves on two to three game hours, in which Frank is not at the office, the rank or the gate; whatever happens there happens without him (mends C-6).
3. An appointment missed without word cools her by one state unless a reason reaches her before the hour (a note by hand, a message through Pauline). A reason that later proves a lie is a lie found out (C9).
4. Calling again inside the set gap draws the cooler door (3.2.7).

### C4. Outings (law 2, law 3)
1. An outing is an arranged visit to a place on the map (decision 19): the caff, the tea room, a walk along the quay and the basin, the chapel hall's Tuesday evening, the allotments on a Sunday afternoon. There is no drink or betting anywhere (canon; mends C-1). A request for a place off the map (the pictures, the coast) is met by the woman naming one that exists ("We could walk along the basin instead"), as G21 moves finds (mends C-2, C-7, X-6).
2. An outing goes well when he is on time, nothing frightens or shames her there, and the talk includes at least one true thing (a confidence, C5). It goes badly if two of these fail. Each woman likes some places and dislikes others (data); a disliked place counts as one failure. No place fatigue counter (mends C-5).
3. Being late by more than a quarter of an hour, or turning up after a fight with the marks of it, counts against the outing.
4. Whatever Frank does there is an act like anywhere else (justice J6: strike, grab, leave); she reads it by her temper and list (C9.1). (Mends J-2.)

### C5. Confidences, and what a friendship holds (law 7, story reads the world)
1. A confidence is a talk act: Frank tells her something true about himself that costs him something to say (his past, his worry about the firm, a deed short of a killing). It is a suggested line in every call.
2. She holds each confidence as a fact from him. Learned true from elsewhere, her trust holds firmer; learned false, it is a lie found out (C9).
3. The close reads a friendship or courtship by one rule, for each person RT2-04 names (Alison or Danby; Ron and Sheila too if the story's open choice 4, C-346, adds them). It holds when all of these are true: at least three calls with no cooler door at the last (RT2-04's floor); no killing by Frank known as fact; and, if Jafar agrees, at least one confidence she holds and has not found false, and no lie she holds as a lie and has not forgiven. The last two conditions change ruled 3.2.5 and wait on his word (summary, question 2; C-4); until then the close reads RT2-04 as written.
4. A courtship with Ellis is played by the same ladder (decision 31) and shapes her case and her talk, but she is not among the people RT2-04 reads at the close (mends C-3).
5. Lies never found out cost nothing in themselves; under C5.3's recommended conditions a friendship built only of them holds no confidence and does not hold at the close.

### C6. Being seen together (law 1, knowing)
1. An outing in public is an act with watchers by their routines (2.2.1). Anyone who knows either of them and sees them remembers it and tells it by the ordinary rules (2.4.1).
2. Once two such watchers have told it, the courtship is a street fact: her family hears, the cab office hears, a rival hears.
3. Each who hears acts by place and temper: her mother approves or disapproves and says so to her daughter (a pressure C2 reads); a rival may use her as a lever, which is a deed against her and reaches Frank as talk; Ellis notes who Frank cares for.
4. Being seen on an outing with another woman is told the same way and reaches her.

### C7. Letters and notes (law 8)
1. Frank can write a note or a letter at the office desk or the flat table: talk on paper, built from suggested lines or typed. It is his claim (G01). A pad player builds any claim about a named person and deed with the Ledger's claim builder (Ledger L5.4, X-7).
2. He signs it or not. Unsigned, the reader holds it as a told version from an unknown source; signed, as from Frank. His hand is a mark: a reader who has seen his writing knows it at face strength, and a compared sample proves it.
3. Sent by post it arrives the next morning first class (22p) or in two days second class (17p); delivered by hand, the carrier is seen at the door like any act.
4. A letter is an object with an owner and a place (things T7); found and read by anyone who searches or snoops. What it says becomes a fact for whoever reads it.
5. She writes when they are walking out and a day passes without a call, or when away. No cooling counter for an unanswered letter (mends C-5); her letters are things to answer or not, read by her temper at the next call.

### C8. Presents that are not coins (law 2, law 3)
1. A present is a real thing bought at a shop that exists at its price (the newsagent's, the second-hand shop, the pawn) or made, with an owner and, if stolen, a hot mark (economy E4.1). (No draper exists on Quay Street; mends X-6.)
2. She judges it by fit: does it answer something she said, or something on her list? A present that fits makes the next outing go well or helps an amends; one that does not is thanked and set aside.
3. A present is read through her list like any deed: showering her with them reads as showing off to a woman who cannot abide it. No price line and no per-fortnight limit (mends C-5).
4. A present can be seen worn or kept, and talked of. A stolen present she learns of is a lie found out and a deed she knows.

### C9. What ends it (law 3, law 6, recovery)
1. Any of these ends it, in person or by letter:
   - a lie found out about something that matters to her, unless he owned it before she heard it from elsewhere (owning it in time cools her instead);
   - learning of a killing by Frank as fact (3.2.5);
   - neglect: three weeks without a call while walking out cools her, a fourth ends it;
   - being seen courting another woman;
   - fear: a threat made to her because of him, or violence she sees that frightens her by temper; a frightened woman may leave the Hook for good, to kin out of town, with one letter later (3.2.8, G21);
   - her family's word, if her temper bends to it.
2. Ended is a fact she keeps (2.3.1). She can be courted again once, from Known, after an amends she accepts and two weeks; a second ending is final. Balance figures.
3. Ending kindly: if Frank ends it himself, honestly and in person, she keeps her regard and the friendship may still hold.

### C10. A rival's former partner (law 3)
1. A woman who was a rival head's or lieutenant's partner, or left him, has her dials and her own memory of that man.
2. What sets her moving:
   - she learns that Frank hurt, jailed or broke her man and holds a grudge of 0.5 or more: she wants revenge, through people and talk only (she tells Ellis or the rival's successor what she knows, or arranges a meeting where men wait);
   - her own regard for her man was low and she learns Frank broke him: her regard for Frank rises a step, and she may be courted by C1; she knows her man's business and is a source;
   - Ellis presses her: she is a witness by the ordinary rules.
3. Her revenge is never by her own hand and never sexual; it is information and a setup.

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- In the world: he calls at her hours; asks with a line; meets her at the stop; sits with her at the caff or the chapel hall; walks the basin; writes at the desk and posts at the box on the corner.
- He sees it in her: how she greets him by state, whether she waits at the stop, a letter on the mat, a present worn, a cooler door, her mother's look. Others tell him: "Your Alison was asking after you."
- In the Ledger, in Frank's hand: what she said and did, the next arrangement, her letters kept, what he told her and what he holds back, where they stand in words the street would use. No bar, no heart, no number.

## (e) Edges

- Courting several women at once: allowed, and each hears of the others through ordinary talk (C6.4).
- Asking a married woman: C1.1.
- Pushing for more than walking out: answered in her own words; nothing exists past walking out.
- Courting Ellis: by the same rules (decision 31); her law dial weighs what she knows; not read at the close (C5.4).
- Courting to use her: allowed; if she learns it, a lie found out.
- Asking her to the pictures: she names a place that exists (C4.1).

## (f) Build stage

- Stage 3: Walking out, C9, C10.
