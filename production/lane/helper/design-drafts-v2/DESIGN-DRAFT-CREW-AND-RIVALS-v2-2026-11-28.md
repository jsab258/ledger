## (b) The rules

### R1. Arrangements and common cause (law 3, people; law 1, knowing)
1. An arrangement is a G04 obligation between two parties (Frank and a rival, or two rivals) with spoken terms: what each does, what each leaves alone, for how long. It is made at a sit-down (3.5.5), through a settler (R8) or by a deal in talk. An agreement among three is read as the arrangements between each pair at the table.
2. States, each lifted by an event, never by points: none; arrangement (terms agreed); common cause (the two act together at least once against a third); broken (one side breaks a term, and everyone who hears holds it as a broken word, G04).
3. Who notices: anyone who sees the two sides' men together or doing a joint act catches it as a deed (2.2.1); the third rival learns it through his own eyes (2.10.2) and reads common cause against him as a deed on his ladder (RT2-07, G08).
4. How it shows: in the world (the other side's men not stopped at a post, two crews at one table in the caff); in talk; in the Ledger as one line with its terms and its date due.

### R2. When a head falls (law 3, law 7, law 1)
1. RT2-05 stands for the ladder: the lieutenant takes it at the same rung. What he knows of the deed is what his firm's people hold of it, no more (law 1; mends BK03, BF06): RT2-05's "counts as having seen the deed" is read this way. What he does next follows his temper:
   - accept: a new balance; he offers terms within a week (Manchester 1991, research);
   - avenge: if he holds a name for the deed, true or planted (a cap left behind points by G11), his ladder climbs two rungs against that person at once (Glasgow 1991, research);
   - split: men of low loyalty to him leave; each goes by R3.
2. A firm that loses its head and its lieutenant, or its base and a sit-down, is beaten: its ladder falls to "noticed" under the role's generic holder (WI-P02), and it climbs again. Canon keeps three rivals (2.15.3): weakened, never removed.
3. A beaten firm's posts and rackets are unheld for two weeks; the first side to put a man on one holds it (RT2-09). Balance figure.

### R3. Taking men in (law 3)
1. A beaten firm's men decide one at a time: stay with the new head, go to another rival, go to Frank if asked and his reading with them allows it (WI-P06; a lost sit-down or a lost head now counts), or leave the Hook.
2. A taken man keeps his grudge and one hook on his old head; his ties (R5) come with him.

### R4. Nerve pressure (law 3)
1. Each man holds pressure from named events (research C6):

| Event | Pressure | Falls by |
|---|---|---|
| A fight lost in sight of others | 0.10 | 0.05 a quiet week |
| Arrested or questioned | 0.15 | 0.05 a quiet week |
| A mate hurt or taken in | 0.10 | 0.05 a quiet week |
| A threat to his family (talk only, adults only; decision 5) | 0.20 | when the threat is answered |
| A week unpaid (R6) | 0.10 | when paid |
| A grass punished in the town | 0.15 | 0.05 a quiet week |

2. Pressure above his nerve: a sign comes first (R7), then he asks to step back (a talk scene); pressure above nerve plus 0.3: he walks, or goes to a rival, or to the police by his law (Mickey's people never to the police, R12). Frank's word, pay and rest lower it. Balance figures.

### R5. A recruit's family and workplace (law 3)
1. Every recruit has ties (G08): kin and workmates, all adults. They notice new money, late hours and marks, and act by temper: a wife asks Frank to leave him be, a brother asks for work, a workmate at the gate talks.
2. Family is a pressure source (R4), a witness pool and a lever for others.

### R6. Unpaid under the split (law 4)
1. Drivers keep half their takings and the firm pays fuel and cover (2.7.1). Unpaid means the firm missing fuel or cover money, or a man's agreed pay not paid from the tin. Both are debts Frank owes (G04).
2. One threshold for everyone Frank pays (mends CR-4; RT2-06 and EC-02 already use it): one week unpaid, the man says so and his loyalty falls a band; two weeks, he leaves, and by his grudge may sell what he knows. Paid and asked, he may come back, by his grudge, as Pauline does (RT2-06).
3. Two kinds of pay (mends CR-7): a night cut per job (2.7.2) and a weekly retainer for a man posted (economy design 5.8, £40). Each is agreed in talk and is a Ledger line.

### R7. Signs before betrayal (law 1, law 3)
1. A man who has turned (paid by a rival or the police) shows signs a watcher can catch: new money, gaps in his routine, questions beyond his job, being seen with the other side.
2. Ron and trusted men pass one sign at a time to Frank in words. A break never comes without at least one sign that could have been seen (2.11.2).

### R8. Settlement through a third party (law 3, law 4)
1. Any two parties in dispute may ask a settler both respect: the Fixer (2.10.4) or an old man of the docks. Settlement takes place in a café, an office or the back of a cab (content rule). Father Walsh may speak for peace in talk, unasked for money and unpriced, as any person of regard may; he is no settler (canon: religion never a mechanic; mends CR-1).
2. The settler names terms (a payment, a post given back, a man stood down). Each side weighs them by its reading of the other and of the settler. Agreed terms are G04 obligations; breaking them costs regard with the settler and everyone who heard.
3. A settler settles the same pair again at double the price and refuses the third time. Garbutt's two-week truce stays as his separate product, once a game. Balance figures.

### R9. Holdings defend themselves through their men (law 3, law 5, law 7)
1. No repeated defence chores. A holding (a post, a front, a round) is held by the man on it. A rival's go at it comes only from his ladder's pressure (2.10.3), at most once per holding per two weeks, and is resolved by the send rule (1.7.2) on the next night: the defender's nerve and numbers against the attacker's, the watchers, the light.
2. The story's one authored crisis per post (S12, C-337: the landing by night, the cold store before dawn, the dock gate by day) is a written scene that reads the world (law 7): it fires when the post is held and its rival reaches pressure on it, calls Frank once by a person (the post's man, Pauline's radio); if he does not come, the post's man meets it alone by the send rule (R9.1). It is the only set piece; every other go at a holding follows R9.1. RT2-09's daily stare and three-day count stay as written until the keeper settles the rest of C-337. (Mends CR-3.)
3. Frank learns of a go the next morning, or before if a tip reaches him in time, and may choose to be there; then it is an ordinary fight.
4. A lost holding is lost until retaken; the firm is never lost whole.

### R10. Anything Frank can do, he can order (law 8)
1. Every verb Frank can do himself he can order through a named man, by the send rule: the man's nerve and temper decide whether he does it, does less or refuses. (Costly to build, CR-9, but G10 already promises it.)
2. A refused order is a held fact the man keeps (a hold on Frank, LH-15).

### R11. Every ask carries its weight (law 3; mends CR-2)
1. An ask's weight is the deed table's seriousness (2.5.2: damage 0.3, assault 0.5, wounding or robbery 0.7, a killing 1.0; theft by value, justice J5.3). A man does it if greed + 0.15 x price (weeks of his own money, at most 2) + 0.3 x grudge against the target + 0.3 x loyalty to the asker (crew only) + 0.2 x (nerve - 0.5) reaches 0.55 + 0.4 x law x (0.5 + weight). Price is measured as 2.5.2's bribe rule measures it; nerve is on the doing side (a man hard to frighten is readier to do violence); law weighs against. Balance figures.
2. Worked check: a stranger of law 0.9 offered two weeks' money to kill refuses (0.8 against 1.09); a man of law 0.15 and nerve 0.7 takes it (0.84 against 0.64); a loyal crewman of law 0.4 refuses unpaid and takes it at two weeks' money.
3. A refusal from a man of law 0.5 or more makes him likely to tell (2.5.2).

### R12. Loyalty is a reading, not a flag (law 3)
1. "Keeps quiet for" (2.14) is weighed against tie to the victim (named "tie", not "grudge", which is a temper dial in 2.14; mends CR-5). When Frank harms one of a person's own, that person compares his tie to the victim with his loyalty to Frank; tie higher, he acts against Frank: to a rival, to the street, or to the police by his law.
2. Mickey's people (Ron, Sheila, Pauline, Kevin, Terry, the outfit's man: 2.5.2) never go to the police about one of their own (his ruling of 1 Oct). Tie higher, they act privately: leave, refuse him, confront him, or tell a rival. (Mends CR-12, K07.)

### R13. Who answers a told fact with force (law 3)
1. Only people with a fight role (rivals' men, criminal crews) or a grudge tie to the act (kin of the wronged, the mate harmed) may answer a believed rumour with force, by temper and the version's strength. Decision 4: shown only if Frank is there. (Dockers' work gangs are not criminal crews.)
2. The ordinary town answers only together and never by force: avoiding, refusing, shutting early (G27).

### R14. A go-between carries his backer's weight (law 3)
1. An ask through a go-between uses the menace and regard of whoever the hearer believes is behind it, times the strength of that belief (0 to 1), plus the go-between's own.
2. Deeds done in Frank's name grow each hearer's reading of Frank (G26).

### R15. Plants and informers (law 3, law 8)
1. Frank may place a man in a rival's crew. The rival hires by its own recruiting rule (need, a word from someone it trusts).
2. Contact is an act (mends CR-11, BF10): a meeting at a set place and hour, a note left at a set spot, or a call to a phone box at a set time, each an act with its slots (2.2.1) that a watcher can catch. Each week the plant weighs loyalty to Frank against regard earned in his new crew; a week with no contact lowers his loyalty to Frank a band and is a week with no report. The same contact rule serves a paid informer (WI-P06).
3. He is found out through signs (R7) like anyone else; a rival who finds him may turn him and feed Frank lies (G10).

### R16. Talk with several (law 1, law 8)
1. Talk with more than one person is one talk: everyone present holds what was said; the one addressed answers; others cut in if their mouth is 0.6 or more or the matter touches them. Each decides by his own temper. Suggested lines can address all or one (ruling 4).
2. One live reply per turn; the others' cut-ins are written lines chosen by the Core, so the 2 s first-word bar holds (mends CR-8).

### R17. When a person tells what he holds (law 1)
1. Asked for a fact he holds, a person tells if his mouth plus his regard for the asker, minus what telling costs him, minus his tie to the fact's subject, reaches 0.5. The Core decides it in both brain modes (2.14.1). Balance figure.

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- He talks: asks a man to join, agrees terms, orders a send by two inputs (1.7.2), calls a settler from a phone box, sets a contact time with a plant.
- He sees the crew in the world: on the office board, at posts, at his side; a man's mood in his body and words; signs of turning as things he notices.
- The Ledger keeps per man his terms, where he was put, signs noticed, contact times; per rival the rung as Frank guesses it in words and any arrangement. No numbers on screen (OUT-06).

## (e) Edges

- Hiring every man in the Hook: men of high loyalty to their heads refuse; those taken bring ties and grudges; more men, more who know (G27).
- Killing every rival head: each death never cools; the firm is beaten but returns under its generic holder (R2.2).
- Ordering an atrocity: content rule first; otherwise R11 makes most men refuse, and the refusal is held against Frank.
- A rival asked to merge with another: arrangements cannot remove or merge rivals (2.15.3).
