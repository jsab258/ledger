28 November 2026. Helper job 8, area 1 of 8. Version 2 of DESIGN-DRAFT-ECONOMY-2026-11-28.md, revised from the blind check (BLIND-REVIEW, BLIND-CHAOS and BLIND-REPLAY of 28 November). Same structure and numbering as version 1. Binding order: canon.md, then Jafar's rulings (including those of 25 to 27 November), then the blueprint of 28 November. The hero is Frank. Every finding was checked at its source before it was acted on; the change table at the end says what was accepted, what was rejected and why.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint already has:
- Three money places: the pocket, the tin (night money at the office that cannot be explained) and the book (clean money) (RT-17, 2.7.1, 2.7.2). There is one tin and it is at the office (2.7.2); the things draft (T1) and the property draft (P8.2) say the same (X-1).
- The wash (2.7.3 with conflict C-332, where the economy design wins): see E6.2 for the one figure.
- Purses and tills (2.7.5); shops have a week, and four losing weeks shut a shop, said at two (EC-04); prices move only on a board, for a cause (EC-05).
- Fencing at about a third of retail, buyers that differ, a 7-day buy-back at plus a quarter (2.7.6, 1.6.3, EC-10).
- Loans, blackmail, protection, Ellis's price, the money curve (EC-06 to EC-12); the firm's floor (RT2-06); missed rent (RT-16).
- General rules: one ledger of favours and debts (G04), every business by its week and people (G20), the town adapts (G27), every run accounted for (G31).

What was missing, from the research, the chaos test and the blind check:
- Cash as a physical thing outside the tin (economy research Part 3, questions 1 to 4).
- Frank setting his own fares (Jafar's ruling 3), at business level only, since canon wants no taxi-sim parts (canon, OPEN 3).
- A ceiling on demand: nothing stopped a price ring (BLIND-CHAOS BG05).
- Repeated incomes with no learner (G03), and learners a rota could reset (BP08).
- After the ending nothing pushed back (G04, P14).
- Selling goods in the street (H07).
- Act III's inspector had no fallback inside a one-week act (R06), and an item asked for in writing had no grade (BR09).

## (b) The rules

Each rule sits under one of the eight laws and applies to every case of its kind.

### E1. Cash is a thing (law 2, things)
1. Money exists only as notes and coins in a place: a pocket, the tin at the office, a till, a purse, a stash (things T2) or a bank book. Nothing holds money abstractly. Notes carry no mark that traces them in the Hook (things T8, X-8).
2. A pocket holds any amount, but notes past a bulk line show: up to £300 in notes is unseen; above it the coat bulges and the bulge is a mark (G11) a watcher can catch and a searcher always finds. Balance figure.
3. What misfortune takes:
   - Beaten and robbed, or mugged without a fight: the robber takes from the pocket by his greed and nerve, from half to all; a man who knows Frank by name takes less if he fears him. The robber is then a person holding the money in his own pocket until he spends it; finding him is finding a person (G01, G13), and what he still holds can be taken back. Never the tin.
   - Any police search, with or without an arrest (one rule; mends RT-17's street search, E-7): notes up to what the fare book explains for a week (the last week's washed takings plus £20) stay with Frank or are handed back on release. The excess is held against a receipt, told to him at the desk or on the pavement, and becomes a lead in Ellis's file (2.6.9). It is returned when no charge stands at the close, or earlier if Frank shows where it came from; it is kept if a charge is read against him. Basis: PACE 1984 s.19 lets an officer seize what he believes came from crime (economy research); how property taken from a detained man was kept in 1990 (ss. 21 and 54) is own knowledge, not read.
4. A safe exists only where the map data places one (G14), with a key or combination held by a named person. No shop in the Hook sells one (2.7.8, 4.3.3), so Frank keeps money in the tin, his pocket or a stash. (E-17.)
5. A bank or building society book holds clean money only. Paying in more than the book explains is an act the clerk remembers; the bank's records are what an inspector or a court reads.

### E2. Every business sets its prices on its own board (law 4, exchange; Jafar's ruling 3)
1. Each business has a board: its prices in chalk or print, set by its owner from costs and temper; EC-05's squeeze still pushes them up.
2. Frank's firm starts on Jafar's tariff of 24 November (advisor 372, conflict C-393, part 3.4.1): about 60p to start, then 80p a mile, a quarter more at night (22:00 to 06:00) and on Sundays. Frank changes it at the office chalkboard (1.7.3): one act, said aloud by Pauline on the radio and by the drivers. A change takes effect next morning.
3. Demand moves at business level, by one rule (law 4; mends E-1, E-14, E-16, E-21, BF03). A business's week (EC-04 for a shop, EC-02 for a car) is set against two things the town knows: the cheapest seller of the same thing, and the buyer's other way (for a cab: the bus at 45p, walking, a friend's lift; for goods: the shop that sells them, or going without).
   - For each tenth its board stands above the cheapest known seller of the same thing, it loses a tenth of its trade to him; for each tenth below, it takes a tenth of his, up to all of it.
   - For each quarter its board stands above its own starting price (for the firm, Jafar's tariff), a fifth of its trade goes to the other way and to no rival, whatever the rivals charge. A dearer board can never win back trade from the other way.
   - Fares stay few and chosen (3.4.1: people on routines, with no price in their choice); the rule changes how many come, never how a single fare thinks. No fare holds a price memory.
   Balance figures. Worked check: a ring at double the tariff loses four fifths of its trade to the bus and walking and takes in less than before, so it is no lasting win; a Friday tariff a quarter higher takes in about the same.
4. An owner reacts to his own week by EC-04's existing line: at two losing weeks he says so, then reacts by temper (cuts his own price, sends a man to ask why, or talks to the town). The other cab firm (2.15.3, decision 21) is such a business: it has a usual week and reacts to trade lost for any cause. No other "known loss" machinery.
5. An opening draws a newcomer (law 3, law 4): a trade left with no seller in the Hook, or premises standing empty to let, draws a named outsider within four game weeks (two weeks for premises let at or under their usual rent), who takes the premises or starts from a front room. This is where tenants come from (property P7.4, P-11) and who answers a dead trade (Jafar's ruling 3). Balance figures.
6. Selling below cost is allowed; the book shows the loss, and drivers on the half split refuse to work a rank where their half no longer pays the week (EC-02, E11).

### E3. Haggling (law 4)
1. Three offers at most (1.6.3, 2.7.5). The answer comes from the buyer's purse, greed, how hot the goods are and his reading of Frank (G26).
2. Standing moves willingness, not a hidden discount: a seller who reads Frank as trusted opens nearer his last price; one who reads him as feared gives way sooner and holds a grudge (G26, G07). Walking out ends the haggle; coming back the same day starts from the seller's first price.

### E4. Stolen goods and their buyers (law 2, law 4)
1. Every stolen thing carries its owner (G13) and a hot mark that cools as talk of the deed cools (2.4.2) and with distance; an owner knows his thing anywhere (1.6.3).
2. Every buyer has a written list: what he takes, whole or in parts, his rate as a share of retail, his purse for the week, and what he refuses. The scrap yard takes a whole car at a fifth of its value (balance figure), breaks it within two days and asks no question it would not ask a stranger; the plate is a fact the yard man keeps.
3. Others sell stolen goods too (economy research question 19), so Rita and the pawn have rivals and a hot radio may already be on a shelf.
4. Selling in the street (mends E-18). A man selling goods from a box or a car boot sells to a named passer-by, who weighs the price against his purse and his other way (E2.3: the shop's price, or going without), holds who sold him what (G01), and knows a hot thing if its owner told the street. The stock carries its owner and any hot mark (E4.1). The shop whose trade he takes counts it as a cause on its week (EC-04) and says so. A constable who sees a man selling from a boot may stop and ask (2.6.7); what follows is the search rule (E1.3, things T1). No licence system is modelled.

### E5. Information is goods (law 4)
1. A fact Frank holds can be sold to anyone who holds a want it answers: a rival (G10), the Fixer (2.10.4), a detective in trade for a charge (2.6.9). The price is set in talk by E3's haggle, from the buyer's purse and how much the fact matters to him; no price table (mends E-15).
2. Sold, it becomes a told version with Frank as source (G01). The subject may learn who sold it by gossip.
3. A made-up fact is sold the same way. Pad players build it with the Ledger's claim builder (Ledger L5.4, X-7): one person and one deed, marked true or invented.

### E6. The pots and the wash (law 4, law 1)
1. The pots stay as RT-17 and 2.7.1 to 2.7.3. One tin, at the office (X-1).
2. One wash figure, quoted the same way here, in the property draft (P5.3) and by RT-01 (mends E-5, X-2): at each count the book can hide £10 a day, plus 40% of the drivers' fares and 80% of Frank's own fares since the last count, plus £60 a week for each front Frank holds a share in (2.7.3 with C-332; the £60 is the figure the economy run used, NUMBERS-RUN M4). The £60 lives in EC-04's shop data as each shop's wash column, the same for every shop, so there is one source. A front's real takings (EC-04: launderette £420, caff £750) are its own business and not a wash figure. RT-01's "£10 plus 80% of real fares" is replaced by this limit (for the keeper).
3. Heat slows it: while Ellis is at "investigating" or later, or a Customs letter is out, Sheila washes at three quarters of the limit and says why. While the licence is suspended only the £10 a day (2.7.3). Balance figure.
4. Over the limit, Sheila warns first (2.7.3), then answers by her temper and regard for Frank (2.14): she may put it through, thinking less of him, or refuse that count and say why. No fixed refusal line. Each week over is recorded for Act III; three counts asked to be put off running and she leaves (RT2-06). (Mends E-19, BF14.)

### E7. Every repeat income has a learner (law 4, law 5; G27)
1. Every source of money Frank uses more than once names who learns from it, what signal he reads and what he does next.
2. Learners remember the firm, not the man carrying (mends BP08, D-3, BF22). What a learner has learned falls by half for each four weeks without the signal, and never to nothing. The same rule stands in the docks draft (D3.3, D5.3).

3. A source with no learner may not be built. Balance figures throughout.

### E8. The town keeps learning (law 5)
1. After the ending rivals stay on their rungs and climb no more and Ellis's stage holds (RT2-21), but each rival keeps its weekly pressure, every learner in E7 keeps learning, and told squeezes still reach the station as talk.
2. A shop squeezed past what it can bear (EC-05) for six weeks in all closes or moves out, at any time in the game, before or after the ending; its premises come up to let (E2.5, property P7.4). This sits beside EC-04 (four losing weeks) and 2.17 (an owner with grudge refuses and tells at three) and catches the owner without grudge who keeps paying. Kept because EC-04 alone leaves the greedy round richer after the story (economy run F4: £1,667 against £1,207 at week 13; BLIND-REPLAY G04). Balance figure.
3. The newcomer rule (E2.5) runs at all times.

### E9. What money buys, start to finish (law 4)
1. Money always has a use, through people and places rather than numbers (GD-07, canon):
   - Act I: rent, the debts, coal and kindnesses (G07).
   - Act II: cars on terms, a driver's half, men's cuts, a front's share or a lease (property P2), Ellis's price, the solicitor.
   - Act III: bail, the brief, repairs, keeping men loyal through the inspection.
   - After the ending: a share in any business, including the other cab firm (property P2's share row; the other firm keeps running under its owner, so its ruled place in 2.15.3 is kept), more leases, more cars, standing in the street (a widow's flowers, a street collection, G08), and answering a newcomer. No whole business and no freehold is bought outright: at 1990 prices none is within the money curve (EC-12: the richest style holds about £1,200 at week 13), so the property draft has no purchase row (P2, X-3). (Mends E-2, E-8, X-3, BF01, BP04.)
2. A share is priced on the business's usual week before any cause Frank made (EC-04's usual week, fixed before the cause), so a price war or a squeeze never discounts it; an owner under pressure sells only through 2.17's fold rule, which makes the sale a squeeze the town hears of (BF01).
3. Cash gates in the story are few and each has a way round (fail forward): Ellis's £800 (EC-11); paying to stop Ron going inside (the story frame, BLUEPRINT.md); and the £200 a rival head asks during a squad's visit (LH-15). (Mends E-6.)

### E10. One debtor rule (law 4)
1. Anyone who owes Frank (a borrower, a tenant, a shop on his tick, a man he paid off) owes by the same rule (2.8.2): pays from his purse, begs or refuses by regard, nerve and grudge.
2. Frank's answers: wait, front up (2.8.2), write a letter (courting C7), or, for a written debt, the county court through the solicitor (a soft-edge scene, a fee, a date three weeks on). Balance figures.

### E11. Upkeep only where it makes a choice (law 4)
1. Weekly costs are only those a choice turns on: rent (RT-16), terms on cars (EC-03), the drivers' half, men's cuts, a bought officer's money, the solicitor's retainer, a lease's rent (property P2). Each can be skipped at a stated price said by a person.
2. Hospital costs days, never money (ruling 6): no hospital bill anywhere.

### E12. The town's own money (law 4, law 3)
1. Wages arrive on Friday: purses are fullest on Friday evening and thinnest on Wednesday, which moves takings, muggers' choices and debtors' answers.
2. Shops keep tick books for regulars (G04); cheques with a guarantee card are taken for larger sums (£50 on most cards; from 1989 some banks set £100 or £250: economy research).

### E13. The inspection has fallbacks and every item a grade (law 7)
1. The VAT inspection is a role, not a man, two deep (BF04): if the inspector is hurt, gone or dead, the senior officer of Customs and Excise at the Custom House takes the next morning with a new letter; a second loss sends an officer from the regional office the morning after. This senior officer is the same person as the docks draft's senior officer at the Custom House (docks D5, X-4). Items already graded stand; the remaining mornings run on; the books open on the last day as RT-01 says. (Mends E-9.)
2. An item Frank asks for in writing (3.1.3) is graded the next morning from what exists, beside that morning's item; asking again for the same item reads it as thin; asking never stops the clock or the books opening on the last day (mends BR09, E-20, BF18).

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- He carries money in his coat; a bulge shows in a shop window's reflection and in how people look. He keeps more in the tin at the office or a stash (things T2).
- He reads prices on boards in the world and changes his tariff by chalking it.
- He hears trade in words: Sheila at the count ("Thin week, love. Half the hospital lot are taking the bus now"), drivers grumbling, Pauline.
- He haggles in talk (three offers; suggested lines on the pad, ruling 4); a made-up claim is built with the Ledger's claim builder.
- The Ledger's Money and Holdings pages (Ledger L2) say in words what is where.

## (e) Edges

- A tariff of nothing: drivers refuse to drive at a loss and Pauline says so; the office still answers the phone.
- A tariff at double, alone or agreed with the other firm: the town takes the bus or walks (E2.3); the drivers grumble about empty nights.
- Buying a whole shop's stock: the owner sells what is on the shelf, restocks next week, and remembers.
- Carrying hundreds in the pocket: allowed, seen and taken.
- Trying to buy the bank, a public building, a shop or a building outright: the owner says it is not for sale to him (canon, WI-P12; property P1.3); a share or a lease is offered instead where the owner would.
- Washing more than the book will stand: Sheila warns, then answers by temper (E6.4).
