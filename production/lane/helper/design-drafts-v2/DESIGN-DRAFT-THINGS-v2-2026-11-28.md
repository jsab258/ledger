# Design draft: things (version 2)

28 November 2026. Helper job 8, area 2 of 8. Version 2 of DESIGN-DRAFT-THINGS-2026-11-28.md, revised from the blind check of 28 November. Same structure and numbering as version 1. Binding order: canon.md, Jafar's rulings, the blueprint. Every finding was checked at its source; the change table at the end says what was accepted or rejected. Content rule: a car hurts and never kills; violence only as canon allows.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint has:
- Carrying: two things under the coat and one in the hand or a holdall; the rest at the office in a locked drawer, the yard shed and the tin; a search finds what is carried or stored; "the cab's boot also holds the envelope, a spare coat or a box" (1.6.9, ruled).
- Objects carry owners and facts; evidence keeps a chain (G13). Doors, locks and hiding places are one system in the map data (G14). Damage changes what an object does until mended (G19). The firm's floor: car two never lost more than a day (RT2-06). Fire leaves a seat read by the brigade, insurers and police (G18).
- Bodies: dragged, carried, put in a boot, rolled into the dock (CS-16). A sunk body comes up in days to weeks; a buried or hidden one is found by search, dog or smell (2.5.5).
- The quay edge stops Frank and cars; things thrown in are gone and whoever saw keeps it (4.2.4).

What was missing (paper chaos fix register; blind check):
- Capacity of places and an act for putting a thing down or hiding it (F36), resting on G14's listed hiding places rather than every surface (T-4, T-8, T-9).
- What change stops an owner knowing his thing (F37).
- A top step of damage for objects and for buildings (F31, BR02).
- One water-edge rule (F30).
- Books as objects, the Ledger included (F32, T-10, BR06).
- Body disposal as acts, with things from places that exist (X-6) and returns inside the story (T-6), and which ground can be dug (BK10).
- A blocked way (F29).

## (b) The rules

### T1. Every place states how much it holds (law 2, things)
1. Bulk follows the docks draft (D1.1): pocket, coat, one hand, two hands. A car takes a place of its own.
2. Each place that holds things has a size, as data:

| Place | Holds | Who reaches it by routine |
|---|---|---|
| Frank on foot | two coat things and one hand thing (1.6.9) | any police search (economy E1.3) |
| A cab's boot | the envelope, a spare coat or a box (1.6.9, ruled; a box is one two-hand thing, such as a case) | a traffic stop that searches; a mechanic |
| The office's locked drawer | six coat things | Sheila (key), an office search |
| The yard shed | twelve two-hand things, or twenty-four one-hand | the drivers, Shaun, an office search |
| The yard | the firm's cars and two more | anyone passing the yard gate |
| The tin, at the office | money and two coat things | Sheila, Frank, a break-in, the office search (2.7.2) |
| A lock-up | twenty two-hand things, or one car | its owner, the landlord |
| A room in an empty unit | as a lock-up | the agent showing it, a new tenant |
| Each hiding place in the map data (T2) | its size class | its finders, listed with it |

3. There is one tin and it is at the office (2.7.2). The flat above holds no second tin; it has hiding places like any premises (T2), found as stashes are (X-1; economy E6.1, property P8.2 say the same).
4. A place that is full takes nothing more. Balance figures.
5. The boot is held at 1.6.9's ruled size. Version 1 and the docks draft wanted four cases; that changes a ruled part and is a question for Jafar (summary, question 1).

### T2. Putting things down and hiding them (law 2, law 1)
1. Putting a thing down in sight is possible anywhere: it is an act with the seven slots (2.2.1), 2 s for a pocket thing, 5 s for a two-hand thing, and is found by whoever's routine next passes (2.5.5). The finder holds it as a found thing: he hands it in, keeps it or tells, by temper.
2. Hiding a thing uses a hiding place listed in the map data (G14): behind a loose board, under floorboards, in a cistern, in a coal hole, in a hedge bottom. Each listed place has a size class (coat thing, two-hand thing, body) and its finders by routine (the occupier, a workman at a refit, a new occupier's first week, a dog, a search of that room). Hiding takes 20 s for a coat or two-hand thing and a minute for a body. Balance figures. (Mends T-4, T-8, T-9: the stash rule sits on G14's list, not on every surface.)
3. A hidden thing is found only by: a search of that spot (police, a rival's men, a burglar), a dog (food, blood, a body), its listed finders' routines, or a person who saw it put there.
4. Whoever finds a stash holds it (G13), and it carries any owner it had. A stash in premises Frank leases is safe only until they are shown, let or repaired, or until the man who saw him go in tells.

### T3. Damage has a top step: destroyed (law 2)
1. Every object climbs one ladder: whole, marked, damaged, broken, destroyed (gone; replaced, never mended).
2. Each kind states who replaces it, at what cost and how long, by its owner's purse:

| Thing | Destroyed by | Replaced by, cost, time |
|---|---|---|
| A firm car other than car two | fire, the dock, the scrap yard | the lot, on terms (EC-03), a week; the firm's cover pays later by P6 |
| Car two | as above | the firm's floor (RT2-06): a second-hand car on terms within a day |
| A townsperson's car | as above | his insurer by property P6.3 (paid in four weeks, or delayed to eight, or refused) or his purse |
| A shop window | a brick | the glazier, a day; the owner pays |
| A building (a shop, a flat, a shed) | fire (G18), a lorry | boarded the next day by its owner; rebuilt by the owner on his insurer's money (P6) four game weeks after the insurer pays; with no cover or a refused claim it stays boarded until a newcomer takes it (economy E2.5). The street kit shows a fixed set of states: burnt, boarded, scaffolded, rebuilt (4.2.2's burnt warehouse shows a burnt state can be built) |
| The tin | thrown, burnt, given away | a new tin; what was in it is gone |
| A book or paper, the Ledger included | burnt, soaked, taken, lost | T7 |

3. The tin is an object like any other: thrown in the dock, burnt or given away, the night money in it is gone (2.7.7 counts it as a flow out). 2.7.2's line that the tin is lost only by theft or search is read as "lost by any act that takes it from Frank".
4. Balance figures: the rebuild time.

### T4. Public things are mended on a delay (law 2, law 3)
1. Every public thing has an owner who mends it: street lamps, railings and benches the council's men; phone kiosks British Telecom (own knowledge for 1990); a post box the Royal Mail.
2. The first breakage is mended in three game days for a lamp, five for a kiosk. A second within a month is mended the next day, and the beat constable walks that stretch more often at night (G27). A third brings a stronger fitting, and a line in the paper if Alison hears of it (2.4.5). Balance figures.
3. A dark stretch is noticed by the constable and those who live there; they talk. Breaking a lamp or a kiosk is criminal damage, a deed seen or found (2.5.5).

### T5. Body disposal is a set of acts (law 2)
1. Each act needs named things from places that exist (decision 19), takes time and makes noise:

| Act | Needs | Time and noise | Comes back |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rolling into the dock | the quay edge (CS-16) | 3 s, a splash heard at 15 m | up in three to fourteen days (2.5.5) |
| Weighting with rope | rope and a weight (the chandler's store on the quay road sells rope; the fish dock's yard weights) | a minute | up in two to four weeks, when the rope gives |
| Weighting with chain | chain (the chandler's store) and a weight | a minute, a clank heard at 10 m | stays down until police divers search that berth (T5.2) |
| Digging a grave | a spade (the yard shed, an allotment hut) and diggable ground | 40 game minutes, digging heard at 20 m at night | turned earth shows two weeks; the allotment holder or his dog finds it within days |
| Hiding a body indoors or in a lock-up | a body-size hiding place (T2.2) or a key | a minute | smell after four days to anyone whose routine passes the wall or door (2.5.5) |

2. A weighted body is found when police divers search the berth where a missing-man inquiry's facts point (justice J9.2: last seen, the last fare, the cab on the quay road, who threatened him); a body in that berth is found the day they search it (CS-16 and LH-02 already have divers). No dredger timetable (mends T-3, T-6, BF23).
3. Diggable ground is marked in the map data: earth only (the allotments on Chapel Lane and any other earth the map marks). Floors, yards laid in stone and quays cannot be dug; a body put under floorboards is a body hidden indoors (row five). (Mends BK10, BF09.)
4. The chandler who sold chain or rope remembers the buyer and when (G13); a buyer at night or a stranger is remembered better.
5. Nothing comes back by a chance roll (2.5.5): each return is a routine, a smell or a search. Carrying or dumping a body is still read as a killing seen, at the grave band (CS-16). A missing man is missed (justice J9).
6. 1.6.7's stale "one sunk is never found" is struck; 2.5.5 and this table stand.

### T6. One water-edge rule (law 2)
1. Frank never goes into the water by his own step and no car leaves the quay anywhere: bollards and the edge stop it and a car backs out (4.2.4). CS-16's line about a car pushed off the quay is struck.
2. Things can be thrown in; what goes in is gone, and whoever saw it keeps it (4.2.4). A body can be rolled in (T5).
3. A man put in climbs out at the nearest ladder within 60 real seconds; an unconscious man face down drowns in about two minutes (4.2.4, his decision 6).

### T7. Books are objects and records at once (law 2, law 1, law 7)
1. Every book or paper (the fare book, the debt book, Mickey's real book, Pauline's dispatch log, the drivers' sheets, the bank book, the bonded store's count and its receipt and dispatch papers, the Board's account, the Ledger) is an object in a place with an owner, and a record of facts. Reading it teaches those facts (law 1); destroying it removes them from that place only, never from memories or copies.
2. Every check reads a record against the things it records and against other records, never against itself: a stock check counts the stock against receipts and dispatch papers; a book of takings is read against fuel bought and bookings logged. A false entry is found when they disagree, and whoever wrote it becomes a knower who can be asked (LH-04). (Mends BS09, BF12; docks D7.3 uses it.)
3. The fare book can be burnt, soaked or taken. What survives: Sheila's memory, the drivers' sheets in the drawer, Pauline's log, the bank's paying-in slips.
4. Sheila, if still loyal, rebuilds the last four weeks in a week of evenings from her memory and the sheets, as the book stood, wash included. The inspector grades rebuilt weeks one step harsher for being written after the event (holds reads thin, thin reads falls). A missing book not rebuilt is itself an item that falls: the inspector's first question is where it is. (Mends T-7, R05.)
5. The Ledger is an object under this rule (mends T-10, L-2, X-11, BR06, BF17). It can be lost, taken, thrown or burnt. Frank starts a new book at once, holding the Open page and the People entries he can recall (dated matters, people he knows by name); the old Day book and the pencil lines are gone. Whoever finds the old book reads what Frank's shorthand lets a stranger read (Ledger L1.4): names, places and dates, not deeds. The police list it with his property at an arrest and hand it back, as they would a diary that proves nothing on its own (WI-P03 keeps evidence; this is not). Ledger L6.3 says the same.

### T8. What change stops whom knowing a thing (law 1, law 2)

| Kind | Change | Beats | Does not beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| A car | respray and new plates (1.2.9) | witnesses who knew it by colour, sign or plate | its owner looking closely within 2 m or from inside |
| A car | scrapping or breaking for parts (economy E4.2) | everyone | the yard man, who keeps the plate |
| A radio or a watch | scratching off the serial number | the police trace | the owner's own mark at face distance (1.6.3) |
| Jewellery | melting (a man who does it) | everyone | the man who melted it |
| A coat | washing (blood) or changing it | a witness who knew only the coat | anyone who knows his face |
| A weapon | the dock | everyone | whoever saw it go in |
| Notes | spending | everyone | nobody: notes are not traced in the Hook (economy E1.1, X-8) |

### T9. A way blocked (law 3, people)
1. Anyone whose way is blocked by Frank asks him to move, asks again more firmly, and then, by temper, pushes past (a shove, an act with slots that Frank may answer), waits up to 30 s, or goes round, uses another door or comes back later. He remembers it as rudeness (a step of regard).
2. Sheila asks twice, then says she will do it when he has finished playing silly beggars, and comes back in an hour. A count she cannot reach that day is a missed count and reads as put off (RT2-06).
3. Nobody stands frozen and nobody passes through anybody (8.10).

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- In the world: he puts a bag behind a loose board he has seen, a case in the boot, a radio in the shed; a full shed has no room ("Nothing more's going in there"); he sees a lamp out and, days later, the council van; boarded windows on a burnt shop, then scaffolding.
- In the Ledger (area 8): holdings by place, in Frank's words, and who else knows of each.

## (e) Edges

- Hiding things all over town: each stash is a fact for anyone who saw and a find for the listed finders; nothing is safe for ever.
- Hiding a thing where the map lists no hiding place: it is put down in sight (T2.1) and found by the next routine.
- Burning the inspector's books in Act III: T7.4.
- Throwing the Ledger in the dock: T7.5; a new book with what he can recall.
- Burning the parade: boarded the next day, rebuilt on the insurers' money in time; the town talks (G27).
- Digging a grave in the office floor: it cannot be dug; the body is hidden under the boards and smells in four days.
- Pushing a car to the quay edge: the bollards stop it; Frank cannot lift a car.
