# Design draft: the Ledger (version 2)

28 November 2026. Helper job 8, area 8 of 8. Version 2 of DESIGN-DRAFT-LEDGER-2026-11-28.md, revised from the blind check of 28 November. Same structure and numbering as version 1. Binding order: canon.md, Jafar's rulings, the blueprint. Every finding was checked at its source; the change table at the end says what was accepted or rejected. Kept diegetic throughout: the Ledger is a pocket book in Frank's hand, holding only what he could know, and it is an object like any other book.

## (a) What exists and what is missing

What the blueprint has (chapter 6 and elsewhere):
- People, Day book, Open, Money in words, Frank's reading (6.2.1 to 6.2.5); the office board (1.7.3); letters and papers kept (6.10); hooks as People lines Frank is sure of, a "guesses" line used is a bluff "that works only if believed" (RT-07).
- RT-18 (decided): the clock stops in talk and the pause menu only; opening the Ledger stops Frank but not the town; an assist "the Ledger pauses the town" is offered, off by default.
- Books are objects and records (things T7); evidence is kept at an arrest (WI-P03).

What was missing or wrong (research, chaos test, blind check):
- Holdings, arrangements, a front's week and planted stories had no page.
- Version 1 paused the clock against RT-18 (L-3); the book was exempt from every rule other books obey (L-2, X-11, BR06); a claim about a secret with no source could never work, even as a lucky bluff, against RT-07 (L-1, L-4, BT10); a pad player could not make up a claim (X-7, BT01).

## (b) The rules

### L1. The book is Frank's, and holds only what he could know (law 1, knowing; law 8)
1. Every line is in Frank's words, traced to something he saw, heard, was told or did (6.2.2). The book never states the true state of the world, only what he holds and when he last saw it.
2. Certainty is shown without numbers, by hand: ink, plain (seen or told to his face); ink with "thinks so" (read from a look or a manner); pencil with "they say" (talk or guess). A pencilled line inked over when confirmed keeps both; a wrong line is struck through, left legible, with the day and the reason (as a constable's pocket book is kept, research).
3. Lines go stale honestly: where the world shows a thing, the book says when he last looked ("Tin: about forty when I counted, Tuesday").
4. Frank writes in his own shorthand, as a working man's pocket book is kept: a stranger who reads it can make out names, places and dates (who he knows, his appointments), but not deeds. That is what a finder learns (L6.3, things T7.5).
5. Handwriting by default, with a plain-print switch in settings for legibility at the size AX-01 sets.

### L2. Six fixed pages that grow by lines, not screens (law 1)

| Page | What it holds | Sorted by |
|---|---|---|
| Open (the first page) | every matter with a date: debts due Friday, appointments, a bail or court date, a contact time with a plant, a front's matter this week, an arrangement falling due, her next outing | date, soonest first; carried forward each morning |
| People | one entry per person: what Frank knows, what he thinks they know of him; for his men, their terms in his words (crew R6, R12); for her, what she said and did (courting) | tabs by surname, and a tab for his men |
| Holdings | by place: what is in the drawer, the shed, the boot, the tin at the office and each stash, and who else knows (things T1, T2); each lease, share and front with its papers and its week (property P2, P5); each car with its driver and terms | by place, then by kind |
| Arrangements | one line per agreement: with whom, its terms, the next date it falls due, and what breaks it (crew R1; a bought officer; a round; the gate officer, docks D5) | next due date |
| Day book | a plain diary, about 22 words a line (6.2.2), with a margin mark on any line tied to an open matter | date tabs, one per day |
| Money in words | as 6.2.4: pocket, tin, what is owed and owing by Friday | none needed |

Frank's reading (6.2.5) is written at the back, at Full stop and each act's end.

### L3. A front's week and the round (law 4)
1. Each front has one line a week on its Holdings entry, as Sheila says it at the count. The last four weeks are kept in full; older weeks fold into one line a month. Balance figures.
2. The firm's one front matter a week (property P5.4) goes on Open until dealt with or let go.

### L4. Planted stories and what came back (law 1, law 7)
1. A story Frank tells on purpose is a Day book line with whom he told and when, and a pencil line on each hearer's People entry.
2. When a version reaches him again, it is written beside the first in that teller's words, with the day. Frank compares the two himself (research: Obra Dinn's pencil and print). The book never says where the story went between.

### L5. Taps from play to any answer, and the claim builder (law 8)
1. One input opens the book at Open; one turns to a page by its tab; one finds a line by tab or date. No answer is more than three inputs from play (6.2.1's standard, made general).
2. In a talk scene, lines Frank holds in ink or pencil appear among the suggested lines when they bear on the talk (a hook, a debt, a promise).
3. Opening the book is an act: Frank stops and takes it out. It cannot be opened while running, fighting, driving or carrying a two-hand thing. The town goes on while he reads (RT-18, decided), so reading in the street is seen and is a risk; the assist "the Ledger pauses the town" is offered in settings, off by default (mends L-3).
4. The claim builder (mends X-7, L-6, BT01, BF13): wherever a typist could make a claim about a person (talk, a letter, a tip, the sale of a fact), the pad offers one person (from the People page or anyone present) and one deed (from the deed list: the deed table's rows and kinds of fact such as "grasses", "skims", "owes", "was at", "set the fire"), marked true or invented. "True" is offered only for what Frank holds in ink or pencil; anything can be marked invented. Typed claims are read into the same shape, so typists and pad players make the same claims (ruling 4). The hearer weighs it by G03 and L7.
5. No search box: 1990 had none. Tabs, the margin mark and Open's dates do the finding.

### L6. What lives in the world instead (law 2)
1. Where the world shows a fact at a glance in its place, the book names the place and when he last looked, and no more: the office board, Sheila's count, the tin in the hand, letters on the mat and in the drawer (courting C7), the radio, the paper, the agent's board, the council's demand.
2. Papers stay objects (things T7): the book holds Frank's note of a paper ("Bail sheet: answer Tuesday 14th, in my coat"), never the paper itself.
3. The book is an object too (mends L-2, X-11, T-10, BR06, BF17; things T7.5 says the same). It can be lost, taken, thrown or burnt. Frank starts a new book at once, holding the Open page and the People entries he can recall (dated matters, people he knows by name); the old Day book and the pencil lines are gone. Whoever finds or takes the old one reads what L1.4 allows. A robber who takes the coat takes the book with it (economy E1.3). At an arrest the police list it with his property and hand it back on release, as a diary that proves nothing by itself; Ellis, holding him, may read a page and learn the names and dates in it (law 1).

### L7. A claim about a secret is weighed by fit and by how the speaker could know it (law 1)
One rule for every claim about a secret, true or false alike (mends L-1, L-4, BT10, BF15):
1. A secret is a fact the hearer believes few people hold. The hearer weighs the claim by fit with what he holds (G03) and by how the speaker could know it.
2. With a source the hearer can see (Mickey's real book, Darren's word, a letter Frank read, his own eyes), a true claim works as a hook (RT-07).
3. With no source in sight, the hearer's nerve decides: a hearer of high nerve asks "Who told you that?", his alarm and grudge rise, he does not yield, and he tells others of the strange thing Frank knew; a hearer of low nerve may still yield, alarmed, and holds a grudge. This is RT-07's "a bluff works only if believed", made exact, and it is the same whether the claim is true or invented.
4. The book is how the game knows what Frank could know: a claim with an ink or pencil line has a source Frank can name; a claim without one is a bluff, however true. Typed and built claims are weighed alike.

## (d) The player's hands and eyes

- In the world: Frank takes a small black book from his inside pocket and opens it; the pages are lined, the hand his, the pencil lines lighter. He reads standing in a doorway or at the office desk, and the street goes on around him.
- In the book: Open first, every page one tab away, any line one tab or date further.
- In talk: what he holds becomes a line he can say; the claim builder makes any other.
- The book never shows a bar, a percentage, a map marker or a star.

## (e) Edges

- Writing his own notes: the Day book is written by what Frank does; the player's own marks are the margin mark and inking over a pencil line. Open to playtest.
- Throwing the book in the dock: L6.3; a new book with what he can recall, the old one gone.
- A rival's men take it in a robbery: they learn names, places and dates (L1.4).
- A second-run player who knows every secret: L7; a hard man asks who told him, a soft one may yield and remember.
- Reading in a chase: L5.3; he must stop, and the town does not.

## (f) Build stage

- Stage 1: Open, People, Money in words, the Day book, L5 taps and the claim builder, L7.
