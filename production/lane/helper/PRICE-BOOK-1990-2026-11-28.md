# A 1990 price book for LEDGER (job 5, part 2)

28 November 2026. What everyday things cost in Britain in 1990, for the design passes. Every source was read in full on 28 November 2026; nothing here rests on a search snippet. Alcohol and gambling prices met on the way are left out by the content rule.

## The short answer

Official 1990 figures were found for the staples and the state's own charges: a loaf (50p), a pint of milk (31p), 20 cigarettes (161p), four-star petrol (44.87p a litre, about £2.04 a gallon), stamps (20p and 15p, then 22p and 17p from 17 September), the colour TV licence (£71), council rent (£23.36 a week in England), private rent (£40 a week in England), unemployment benefit (£37.35 a week for a single person from April) and manual men's pay outside factories (£227.10 a week). A Humber docker's pay was found only for 1987, under the old Dock Labour Scheme (£296.12 a week in Hull).

The café, the chip shop, the barber, the cinema, the launderette, clothes, a used car, a minicab's fare and a shop assistant's or labourer's wage were not found in any 1990 primary source that could be read as text; they are listed at the end with what was found nearest. The 1990 Retail Prices Index average-price tables and the New Earnings Survey occupation tables that would settle most of them are in print and in the ONS archive but not online as text.

## How to read the status column

- Official: a government figure (Hansard written answer, London Gazette), one source.
- Official, two agree: two government figures that agree.
- Derived: arithmetic on an official figure; the working is shown.
- Unverified: one secondary source (a history site or a museum page), or a figure from another year; not checked against a second.
- Inferred: no figure found; a judgement with its reasoning.
