## 2. The form

Reconstructed from what the blueprint asks of a key scene, since the lane's form could not be read:
- RT-02: who, where, what is at stake, what the player can choose, what comes back; at least one choice and one return; no system the game lacks.
- SE1 (part 1): ready when (states), the chair that carries it, its sign, its three routes, fallback 1, fallback 2, what it leaves if it lapses.
- 3.6.6 and C-349: Frank's lines fully written in key scenes.
- Decision 36: in the five heaviest scenes (the quay, the night between, the yard, the Landing, the pen), Frank's lines in two or three variants by the town's reading of him (G26: feared, trusted, quiet, cruel, generous). Written here as [feared], [trusted], [quiet]; cruel uses feared's lines with harder words, generous uses trusted's.
- MEM-08: the speaker names a deed of Frank's they hold, where there is one. Shown as {deed held}: the line slot filled from that speaker's memory.
- 3.5.5 and 3.6.5: only a plain yes is taken; what cannot be undone asks twice; Leave is always there.
- 7.1 and 3.2.7: any watched stretch 60 s at most and skippable; a talk scene under 2 real minutes.
- Names: {name} is what the speaker calls him by the ladder (the new owner, Nowak, Frank, Frankie; Sheila withholds his name until she trusts him).
- Mickey is described only in his two approved lines (3.1.6), which are not in the zip. Lines here that touch Mickey state facts the blueprint already holds (his arrangement, his two books, his runs, that he once got a second car), never his character; any a reviewer reads as description is swapped for an approved line.
- Content rule strict: no drink anywhere (tea, cigarettes, a flask of tea), no gambling, no children, nothing sexual, drugs never on screen, no slurs.

Script: NAME: line. Frank's decision lines are numbered; the first decision line shown is never the recommended one.

## 3. The scenes

### 01 The first morning: the half-written line (week 1) [lane's, header only]
- Ready when: Frank's first morning in the office; the book's chair present.
- Chair: the book (Sheila; then Pauline; then the hired clerk).
- Variants: Sheila gone before the morning (killed on the first night): Pauline hands him the fare book and asks him to finish Mickey's line "since it's your name on the door now"; F1 offered the same.
- Fallback 1: the book's next occupant. Fallback 2: the line sits unfinished in Mickey's hand; A1 then reads the blank.
- Leaves: whether the line is finished, in whose name; this is what Ellis reads in week 5 (scene 14).

### 02 Night one: Ron's ask, the claim, the landing (week 1)
- Ready when: the first night, after dark; the bond's chair alive; the old firm's routine runs (2.9.1: asks on nights 1, 3, 5, 7).
- Where: Mickey's office, then the ferry landing after ten.
- At stake: the outfit's claim on Mickey's; Frank's first word on what he means to be; his £20 cut (2.9.1 economy line).
- Sign before: Ron in a dark coat at the counter at nine, an envelope on the counter, saying nothing until Pauline has gone for her tea.

RON: Mickey did a run on these nights. Envelope down to the landing after ten. A man says "Mickey's?" and you say yes.
FRANK: And if I say no?
RON: Then I take it back and Sheila writes it down. Nobody shoots anybody. Not for one envelope.
FRANK: What's in it?
RON: Not my business. Wasn't Mickey's either, he used to say.

Frank's choice:
1. "Leave it on the counter. I'm not running Mickey's errands." (the ask unanswered; written in the Ledger)
2. "Give it here. Show me the way." (the landing)
3. "You take it, Ron. I'll be in the car." (the ghost's route: Ron carries; the landing men hold that Frank came but stood off)

If 2 or 3, on the walk down Quay Street:

FRANK: By Christmas, every night fare on the Hook comes through this office. Every door, every quay. When people say Mickey's, they'll mean me.
RON: (a while) Mickey said something like that. In 1971.
FRANK: What happened?
RON: He got a second car.

At the landing (scene runs under 60 s watched; then play):

MAN AT THE LANDING: Mickey's?
FRANK: Mickey's.

Variants:
- Ron dead or held before night one: the outfit's man comes to the office door himself (RT2-05) with the envelope and the same three lines; the claim is said to whoever walks with Frank, or to nobody, and is then a line in the Ledger only.
- Frank refuses (1): Ron leaves; next morning Sheila says, without looking up: "Ron left your envelope. He said you were tired." The landing men hold it.
- A constable stops Frank on the way (the beat at that hour, 2.6.1): the envelope is kept if searched (2.9.1); the scene ends in the ask failed as a state the outfit hears.
- Routes: ghost (3, or carry it unseen by the yards' lane); talker (2, and talk to the landing man); schemer (2, and watch the boat to learn the run; a fact for week 4).
- Returns: the tin up £20 next morning; the landing men's regard; Ron hears the claim and repeats it once to Sheila, so the claim is a held fact of Frank's (MEM-08 material for the whole game).
- Fallback 1: the outfit's man at the door. Fallback 2: the ask unanswered. Leaves: the ask's state (2.9.1).

### 03 The funeral tea: three heads under one roof (week 1)
- Ready when: Mickey's funeral (proposed calendar fact, SE2), mid-week; the tea in the office's front room afterwards.
- Chairs: each rival head; the family (June); the parish (Father Walsh).
- At stake: first looks. Each head notices Frank (RT2-07: noticed on the first thing its people see of him); what Frank says here is the first thing they hold.
- Sign before: Sheila puts out cups and a second urn the morning of the funeral and says three of the people coming "will look at you like a shop window".

Tea, sandwiches, cigarettes. June stands at the back and does not take a cup.

AGAR: (offering his hand) Geoffrey Agar. I did Mickey's lease, years ago. You'll want someone to read your papers now. The council can be particular about a licence.
FRANK: I'll read them myself, Mr Agar. Slowly.
AGAR: Of course. Slowly is how most people read a summons.

THE WIDOW: Maureen Jensen. Your uncle and me had an understanding about the dock gate. It was a good understanding. It kept people's cars on four wheels.
FRANK: I'll need to hear what I'm understanding before I understand it.
THE WIDOW: Ron'll tell you. Ron knows everything and says nothing. Mickey's best buy.

DANNY: (by the window, jacket too good for a funeral) Danny. I drove for Mickey. Car two. (holds up a key on a ring, puts it away) Kept a spare. Sentimental.
FRANK: That's my car.
DANNY: It's the firm's car. Firms change hands. Ask anyone.

Frank's choice, with Danny (the only one who can be pushed here):
1. "Give me the key, Danny." (Danny laughs and leaves; a held fact: Frank asked and was refused, in front of the room)
2. "Keep it. Something to remember him by." (Danny reads him as soft; or, at the trusted reading of the room, as generous)
3. Say nothing; let Ron see Danny out. (quiet)

Variants:
- A head dead before the tea (killed on night one or two): his lieutenant comes in his place and says less (RT2-05).
- June present: she speaks once, only to Frank, on the step: "He left you the office. He left me a letter with the priest. We'll see which is worth more." (opens the family thread). June gone: Father Walsh says it instead, of the letter he holds.
- Frank absent from the tea: the heads come anyway and hold that he did not show his face at his uncle's tea, which every tea-going regular holds as a deed (2.4.1).
- Routes: ghost (stay in the back office and listen through the door: Frank learns one thing each head says to Sheila); talker (work the room); schemer (tell each head something different and see which comes back).
- Returns: each head noticed (rung one) on what he holds; Danny's key is a standing fact in the world (scene 22 reads it).
- Fallback 1: lieutenants come. Fallback 2: no head comes; each notices Frank by his eyes instead (2.10.2). Leaves: each head's first reading of him.

### 04 Alison's fare to the warehouse row (week 1)
- Ready when: a fare booked in Alison's name (3.2.5: her thread starts with her fare to the warehouse row, asking about the fire), on a day Frank drives car two; the press chair alive.
- At stake: the friend thread; the fire's street version (a street fact) against the truth (never the street's; ruling 30 Sep).
- Sign before: Pauline over the radio: "Fare for the warehouse row. Lady says she wants the owner. Not a driver, the owner."

Alison in the back seat, a notebook on her knee she does not open.

ALISON: You're the nephew.
FRANK: I'm the driver, today.
ALISON: Pull in by the third one. The black one. (looks) Your uncle's cars were on this row the night it went up. Two of them. One at eleven, one at one.
FRANK: You've read the book?
ALISON: I've read the drivers. Drivers talk. Books don't, unless someone makes them.
FRANK: What do you want from me?
ALISON: Nothing yet. I want to see if you lie as easily as the rest of this street.

Frank's choice:
1. "He didn't tell me anything. I met him twice." (a true confidence: costs him nothing much, but true; she holds it)
2. "Two cars on a busy night. That's a cab firm." (a deflection; not a lie)
3. "Mickey was nowhere near it." (a lie he cannot know is true; she holds it as a claim she can test)

ALISON: (after 1) All right. That's one. (she pays, exactly, and tips him nothing) I'll ring for a car again. Ask for you.

Variants:
- Iveson dead or gone: she says "the man who sold out of it is gone, which is convenient" and the fire thread runs through Ellis (RT2-05).
- Frank does not drive that day: Kevin takes the fare; Alison asks Kevin for the owner and leaves a message with Pauline ("tell him the black one on the row"); the thread opens a day later as a call.
- Alison gone (killed, moved): the friend thread opens through Danby instead, as a call at his door (3.2.7), the paper's side passes to a Pilot staff reporter, who only writes, and the fire thread runs on through Ellis (WI-P02).
- Routes: ghost (drive, say nothing; she holds his silence, neither truth nor lie); talker (1 or 2); schemer (3, then feed her a version of the night that points at a rival; a planted claim, G01).
- Returns: the first call counted (3.2.7); a confidence held (C-346); the street version reaches Frank as a fact he can now repeat.
- Fallback 1: Danby's call. Fallback 2: the fare never comes; the thread opens when Frank calls on either at a place their routine allows. Leaves: what she holds of him.

### 05 Sunday: "So which is it going to be?" (week 1)
- Ready when: Sunday of week 1; the book's chair at the office by her routine.
- At stake: what the firm is; the street learns the answer (3.1.1).
- Sign before: Saturday night Sheila locks the inner office in front of him and says, "Tomorrow you tell me."

Sheila at the counter with the fare book closed. Pauline's radio off for once.

SHEILA: I told Mickey I'd look at whoever came. I've looked. {deed held}. So which is it going to be?
(the slot: the worst or loudest deed of the week she holds; for example "You fronted Darren twenty-two pounds he'll never pay" or "You put a man on the pavement on Tuesday")

Frank's choice:
1. "Wind it down. Cabs, fares, nothing else."
2. "Take it over. All of it."
3. "I'll tell you when I know."

SHEILA: (after 1) Then Mickey's arrangement ends tonight, and the landing will know by Tuesday. You'll have freedom and no friends.
SHEILA: (after 2) Then you're what he was. God help you. (she unlocks nothing yet; the real book waits on her trust, 3.1.6)
SHEILA: (after 3) Fine. I'll watch what you do. That's what I'd have done anyway.

Variants:

- Routes: ghost (3); talker (1 or 2); schemer (say 1 and keep running the landing: the street hears "wind it down" and then sees the asks taken; a lie held by Sheila).
- Returns: the street's version within a game day; the arrangement kept or ended (2.9.2); rung 2 by a state (RT-04).

FRANK: That's theft, Danny.
DANNY: It's a test drive. Listen. The old man's gone and the Hook's up for grabs. The Widow has the gate, Agar has the council, and you have a radio. I have lads. Lads want work. You give my lads the night fares off the ferry and I keep the dock road quiet for you.

### 11 Bonfire Night at the Landing (week 4) [heavy]

FRANK [trusted]: I've got you. Ron, the car. We're going to the infirmary, now.
FRANK [feared]: Nobody touches him. You (to the nearest man of either side): you saw who did that. You'll say so.
FRANK [quiet]: (kneels, says nothing to the men, only to the old man) Hold that. Press. I'll ring from the box.
