# Depth research: COURTING

28 November 2026. Job 3, area 4 of 5. All sources read on 28 Nov 2026 unless marked. Content rule applied throughout: a date and affection only, nothing sexual; no alcohol or gambling (pubs, drink and betting left out of anything the game would use); no children. Where a reference game has sexual, drinking or betting content, only its non-sexual, non-drinking structure is reported.

## The plain top

What to copy:
- GTA IV: a hidden "like" figure rises after good dates and falls when you neglect her. A date works when your clothes, your car and the kind of outing suit her. If the figure falls below about 60% she ends it. Each repeat visit to the same place lowers her opinion of it until she refuses to go there. She takes calls only at certain hours. Nothing is shown as a bar, only in what she says and does.
- Persona 5: time is the price. A confidant is free only on certain days and at certain times. Conversation choices add to a hidden value, and some steps close for good if missed by a date.
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II: romance lives inside quests and choices, not a meter. A wrong act or a missed window rules it out, and one romance can rule out another.
- San Andreas: each partner likes certain places and pace and dislikes others. A date fails if you are late, lost, or treat her badly.

What players hate or mock (critics and design talks read: 6):
- Romance as a vending machine: put in gifts or kindness, get affection out (a GDC 2022 talk and a Game Developer essay, 2).
- Dates that go nowhere: partners vanish after a date or two (GamesBeat on Sleeping Dogs, 1).
- Dates out of step with the rest of the game, though they humanise the hero (Escapist on Sleeping Dogs, 1, mixed).
- Romance that turns women into prizes to be won (Punished Backlog on KCD2, 1).

The 1990 facts:
- Manual workers met partners in public places more than office workers did, and the gap widened over the century. In the 2000-01 national survey (median meeting year 1991), 51% of manual couples met in public settings against 32% of non-manual couples.
- The north had the most public and the fewest "select" meetings (work, study, clubs).
- In a 1989 Gallup survey, about 17% of couples met at dances and 17% at work (unverified: from a misaligned table).
- Among first marriages of the 1980s, 28% met through family, friends or neighbours, 19% at work and 8% at school or college. Those marrying in the 1990s: 35%, 23% and 8%.
- A first-class stamp cost 22p and a second-class 17p by November 1990.
- Nothing was found on what a first date cost, the phone box charge, or what neighbours said about a couple seen together.

The design questions are numbered K1 to K10 in part C. They ask:
- What steps make up courting?
- Who sees a couple, and what spreads?
- How is a courtship's state shown without a bar?
- What makes a present a courting present?
- What sets off the rival's former partner?

## C. The drill's gaps as design questions

From the implication drill, area B6. The blueprint already decides there is no score by design (3.2.8, re-check) and that memory is permanent (2.3.1).

K1. The steps of courting (gap B: "no states, steps or thresholds are written"). Should courting be a short ladder of named states, like the rival ladder (noticed, a walk, seen together, walking out, an understanding), each lifted by events rather than points? KCD2 ties steps to quests; Persona ties them to time and closes some for good. Which fits LEDGER's week?

K2. What moves it on. Clough's talk warns against "kindness coins". Should Alison be drawn to a mix of Frank's traits as she sees them (steady, honest in the calls, how he treats the cabmen), so the player becomes a kind of man rather than paying in favours?

K3. Time as the price. Persona and GTA IV make the partner free only at her hours. Should Alison have a routine (her shifts, her mother's day), so courting costs Frank evenings he could spend on the firm?

K4. Places she likes and tires of. GTA IV lowers a partner's opinion of a place with each repeat. Should the pictures, the café, the front and the seaside each be liked or tired of, remembered, and talked about? (Content rule: no pubs or dances where drink is the point.)

K5. Being seen together (gap B, same as A1). In the north, couples met in public, and public is where people watch. Who sees Frank and Alison, and what spreads: to her family, to the cab office, to a rival who wants a lever?

K6. Showing where it stands without a bar (gap C). GTA IV hides its figure but shows it in calls and refusals. Should a courtship show only through her words, her letters and her small acts (she saves him a seat, she stops ringing), with the Ledger's People page holding only what she has said?

K7. Letters. A stamp cost 17p or 22p. Should letters be a courting channel, kept as objects, readable later by someone else (her mother, a searching officer)?

K8. A present (gaps C: present as an object, present seen, present not tied to courting, feedback in words only). Should a present be a real object with an owner and a shop price, which others can see her wear or keep? Does it count only if it fits her tastes, as in San Andreas and Stardew? How do we stop it becoming a coin?

K9. Ending it. GTA IV ends a romance when the like falls below a line; San Andreas when a partner catches another; KCD2 when a choice rules it out. Which acts end Alison's courtship: a lie found out, a killing learned as fact (3.2.5 already), neglect, being seen with another woman?

K10. The rival's former partner who wants revenge (gap C: no trigger). What sets her moving: seeing Frank courting, learning what he did to her man, or being approached by the police? Is her revenge information told to someone rather than violence?
