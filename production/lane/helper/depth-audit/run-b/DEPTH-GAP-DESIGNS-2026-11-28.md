## D1 The district as a key (GB3; the data under every law)

The gap: the Hook is "one map file in metres from the ferry landing" (BLUEPRINT.md); counts that should be data are written as fixed numbers in parts: three rivals (RT2-08), three posts (RT2-09), three phone boxes (RT2-19), one patrol car (2.6.7), the beat constable's round (FIT-G5), thirty regulars (3.2.3), rung 4 "fronts and rounds across the Hook" (2.12.2). More districts are promised after release (BLUEPRINT-10) and the foundation is "built for the whole game" (9.14). Fixed later, every one of these is a rewrite of a rule; fixed now, each is a column.

The rule. Every place, door, person (by home), post, phone box, police beat, ground patch (D2), business and rival carries a district id. The Hook is district 1; the six named districts (Copper Row, the Exchange, the Parade, Fairview, Ironside, Gullwing; BLUEPRINT-10) exist now as data with the state "soft edge", reached as time passed (4.1.4) and by letters and addresses (G21). A rule that counts things counts them in the data of the district it runs in, never a number in its own text: "the posts of this district", "the rivals whose home is this district". People are the town's, not a district's: memory (2.3.1) and ties (2.4.1) cross districts, and talk crosses only where two people's routines bring them together, as it does now.

What stays the story's: the rung table (2.12.2) is the story's and reads the starting district; holdings in another district, after release, add to his name and greetings (2.12.4) and to that district's ground (D2), never a fifth rung. Each district's police is a division with its own beat and car; DS Ellis's CID covers the town (CS-27 already says "the local CID").

Data. A district table (id, name, state walked or soft edge, map file, landmarks for the paper map); a district id column on places, doors, posts, boxes, beats, patches, businesses, rivals and people's homes; the save stores ids, never positions alone. A walked district has its own map file in metres from its own origin; KCD2 does the same with two regions and two maps (GamesRadar, 4 Feb 2025).

The player's hands and what he sees. Nothing new at release. The paper map (6.3) becomes one sheet per district, the Hook's first; the others are drawn later, each marking only places he has been or been told of, as now. A soft-edge district appears to him only as landmarks in fares and letters.

Numbers. None to source: every count moves from text to data, with today's values (3 rivals, 3 posts, 3 boxes, 1 car) as the Hook's rows.

Edges. A deed in a soft-edge district is told, never walked (G21); a chase still ends at the edge (4.1.4). After an update makes a district walked, its people keep what they held as soft-edge people (fares, letters); nothing resets.

Stage. Stage 1, the foundation's data. It costs nothing in play and spares every later rule a rewrite.

Combination test. Frank drives a fare with a box in the boot to Copper Row at release (time passed), saves on the way back, and loads that save after the update that makes Copper Row walked. Expected: the fare person, the box (G13 owner and place by district id) and Frank's position load; the box is where the rules put it; no rule's text changed for the new district. A failure here names the rule that still holds a Hook-only number.

## D2 Ground is the doors that pay (GB2; law 3 people, law 1 knowing, law 4 exchange)

The gap: "a sighting on that rival's ground" lifts a rival (RT2-07), the dock gate is "the Widow's ground" (EC-07), the landing men "hand him their end of the Hook" (2.9.2), and taking premises "on a rival's ground" is a sighting (property v2 P2), but nothing says which streets are whose or how that changes. Only the three posts are held as places (RT2-09).

What the references and 1990 say. GTA San Andreas paints turf on the map and wins it by waves of killing (StrategyWiki); Yakuza 0 makes control a share of properties bought in an area (TheGamer, 2022). Both are numbers on a screen, which canon forbids. The British firm of the period held its "manor" by extorting the local businesses, legal and illegal, and pushed against the firms of the next manor (Hobbs, 2002/03). That gives the rule: ground is not a colour, it is who the doors pay.

The rule. The Hook is cut into patches in the map data: a stretch of street with its doors (to be set in map.json; about eight to twelve on today's map, such as the parade front, Quay Street east and west, the fish dock, the dock road and gate, the landing, the cold-store road, the north road lock-ups, the yards' lane, Chapel Lane). Each paying door (a shop, a stall, a front; places.json) pays at most one firm, by 2.17's fold rule and EC-06's protection. A patch is a firm's when that firm collects from more than half its paying doors, or, for a patch with no paying doors (the quay, the landing), when its man holds the post there (RT2-09). Contested: two firms each collect from a door there, or both stand on its post. Open: none. The state is read from the world each day; it is never stored as a score.

What follows, all from rules that exist:
- RT2-07's "sighting on that rival's ground" means a sighting in a patch he holds or contests.
- Asking a door that pays a rival is a deed against that rival (as a hit on his round, RT2-08), because the door tells its collector by the ordinary rules (2.4.1; law 1); a door asked by two firms tells each about the other, which is how wars between manors start.
- A door changes hands only by its owner's choice under the fold rule; a patch changes hands when its doors or its post do. A beaten firm's patches go open for two weeks (crew v2 R2.3, unchanged).
- The landing men's "end of the Hook" (2.9.2) is a list of patches and doors in the data, handed over when the arrangement is kept.
- Rung 4's "fronts and rounds across the Hook" reads as: at least one paying door in more than half the Hook's patches (judgement, to be run), so the rung has a test the town can see.

Data. Patch id on every place and door (with D1's district id); a "pays" fact on each door (firm, day, man); per firm, its collectors' days (timetables.json). No new system: the fold rule, protection, posts and the rival ladders already hold everything else.

The player's hands and what he sees. He learns ground as a newcomer would: a shopkeeper asked "who do you pay?" answers by what she holds and her nerve ("Fridays, a lad of the Widow's"; or nothing, frightened); he sees collectors on their day; men stand where their firm holds. The Ledger's Holdings page lists, by patch, the doors that pay him; People notes what he knows a door pays. Frank's reading at each act's end says it in words: "The fish dock is the Widow's. The parade is ours. The yards are anybody's." No map colour, no marker.

Numbers. Patches and doors per patch: from the map data (to be set). "More than half" and rung 4's reading: judgement, to be run in the economy run with rivals collecting (the run today has no rival collections).

Edges. Public bodies (the chapel, the police station, the Harbour Board's office) hold no ground and pay no one. A door that pays nobody because nobody asked is open, not his. Typed claims ("this street is mine now") are talk (RT-15): ground follows the doors, so the world answers with what the doors do.

Stage. Act II from week 2, when rivals start visiting on sightings (RT2-07; 3.1.2); the patch ids are stage 1 data, the rule stage 2.

Combination test. Frank pays the Widow's weekly protection himself on behalf of the fishmonger, to win the fishmonger's regard and then turn him. Expected: the fishmonger's door still pays the Widow (the payment is hers, by her man); his regard for Frank rises (G07) and his reading may add "generous" (G26); next week the Widow's man hears from the fishmonger that Frank paid (law 1) and the Widow reads it as a move on her door: a sighting on her ground (RT2-07). When Frank then asks the fishmonger to pay him instead, the fold rule decides with the regard he bought. No special case; the generous route is slower than menace and leaves fewer witnesses.

## D3 Company kept (GB1; law 1 knowing)

The gap: who was seen with whom is a fact only for a courting outing (courting v2 C6) and a rival's man (WI-P06; crew v2 R7.1). The companion is already a slot of every act (GV-02 sheds it by "walk apart from Ron"), but outside those two rules nobody holds "Frank was with DS Ellis at the caff", and nobody passes it on.

What the references say. Dwarf Fortress keeps, for each figure, its relationship with everyone it has met: the number of meetings, the time of the last, and reputation (Adams, 2017). LEDGER needs the other side of that: what a third person makes of two people seen together.

The rule. When a person who can name both (the recognition rung of 2.2.2, which needs knowing the person, not only seeing a face) sees two people together (walking side by side, talking within 2 m, at one table, in one car, at one door), she holds a company fact: who, where, when, how. It is a held fact like any sighting (2.3.1, permanent) and is passed by 2.4.1 like any telling. Each viewer keeps, per pair, a count and the last time; three sightings inside two game weeks make the stronger fact "they're thick". What a hearer makes of it is her own reading by G03 and G26, never a rule of its own: a man of Frank's crew who holds Frank "seen with the detective, twice" may read him as talking to the law; a rival who holds his lieutenant "thick with Frank" asks him about it (crew v2 R7). Courting v2 C6 and WI-P06 become cases of this rule and keep their wording.

Data. One more fact kind in the memory record (pair, place, hour, how), stored only by viewers who know both by face, which keeps the state small; a count and last time per viewer and pair.

The player's hands and what he sees. He manages company the 1990 way: meet indoors, in the back of the cab, at a quiet hour, walk apart (GV-02). He sees it in posture (2.4.3) and in what comes back (2.4.4). The People page adds one line per person to Frank's guess: "seen with: Ron (often), DS Ellis (once, the caff, Tuesday)", in words, as GV-02's guesses already are.

Numbers. 2 m and "three in two weeks": judgement, to be run with the bots (G29), checking that a crew-style player is not read as an informer by accident in more than one run in twenty (judgement).

Edges. Strangers and unnamed extras hold nothing (2.2.2); a pair in a crowd of more than about eight is "together" only when talking (judgement). Inside a car at night the viewer holds the plate and "two men" unless she knows both faces in light (2.2.3). Company with a dead or missing man is still held (J9's inquiry reads it).

Stage. From the first week (Frank seen with Ron and with the landing men); stage 1 adds the fact kind, stage 2 the passing values.

Combination test. Frank stages being seen talking with the Widow's lieutenant at the caff, to make Agar think the two firms are allied. Expected: Agar's eyes among the regulars (2.10.2) hold the company fact and tell Agar; Agar weighs it (G03): one sighting is "talks to", not common cause, so his ladder does not move (crew v2 R1.3 needs a joint act); the Widow's own people also hold it and she asks her lieutenant, whose answer is his temper's. To make Agar believe, Frank needs the pair seen three times or a joint act, which costs him the lieutenant's real time. No special case.

## D4 Confidences and questions (GB5; law 1 knowing)

The gap: a request to keep something quiet has no term in 2.4.1's passing rule (searched: none); and his own questions are held by nobody, so asking the grocer about the break-in never reaches the burglar's ear (the earlier drill's gap 14, still open).

What the references say. Crusader Kings III makes a held secret leverage: a hook from a favour or a secret, the strongest from blackmail (Hafer, PC Gamer, 2020); LEDGER has that already (G05, RT-07). Dwarf Fortress spreads rumours only from witnesses and never false ones (Adams, 2017); LEDGER allows lies (G01). Neither holds a request to keep quiet or a question as a fact. Crime fiction does, constantly ("someone's been asking about you").

The rule, part one: asked to keep it. When anyone asks a person to keep a fact from others (or from one named person), the holder marks that fact "kept for" the asker. In 2.4.1's passing test the kept fact carries a cost: it passes only if confidence × tie × (0.5 + 0.9 × mouth) − keep reaches 0.2, with keep = 0.3 + 0.5 × the holder's regard for the asker; to the named person it passes only if mouth is 0.8 or more or the holder bears the asker a grudge (2.14). A broken confidence is itself a fact (who told whom) and travels back by the ordinary rules; when the asker learns it, regard moves (G07) and his reading of the breaker may drop "trusted". Asking also tells: the holder now holds "he wants this kept", so a greedy holder knows it has a price (G04; economy v2 E5, information is goods).

Part two: questions are held. A question that names a person, place or deed leaves the hearer holding "Frank was asking about X" at face strength, Frank as source (G01). It passes by 2.4.1 like any telling, so it reaches X when the hearer and X share a tie. The asked-about person reads it by temper: he may hide, move a thing, come to ask Frank why, or tell his own firm.

Data. Two fact kinds in the memory record (kept-for with asker and the named one; asked-about with asker and subject); keep as a term in the passing test.

The player's hands and what he sees. Typed ("keep this between us", "don't tell Ron") or a suggested line; on the pad, the claim builder (Ledger v2 L5.4) gains "keep this from [person]". People shows what he asked each person to keep ("asked to keep: the tin, from Ron"), as his own act. He learns a confidence was broken when it comes back to him (2.4.4), never by a sign.

Numbers. keep = 0.3 + 0.5 × regard and the 0.8 mouth line: judgement, to be run in the talk and gossip pass with the existing 2.4.1 values (0.8 a hop, 0.2 floor).

Edges. Asking a stranger buys little (regard near zero, keep 0.3). Asking an officer to keep something from the station is answered in character and does not hold: station talk (G16) is a tie of the strongest kind. Asking a dead man's widow to keep something about the death is a deed she holds.

Stage. Week one: Sheila, the tin and Ron (2.7.2; 3.1.1). The fact kinds are stage 1 data; the values come with stage 2's talk.

Combination test. Frank tells Darren (mouth 0.9) a false "secret" about Agar's man and asks him to keep it quiet, so it spreads faster. Expected: asking marks it as worth something; Darren's mouth beats the keep cost with any tie he meets; the story spreads as a told version with Frank as source (G01) and "he said keep it quiet" attached; if found false it costs Frank regard with each who learns (G01) and, with Darren, the knowledge that he was used. The trick works, and the town's rules price it. No special case.

## D5 The written floor and the act menu (GB6; law 8, and the ruling that past the model the world answers in character)

The gap: when live talk is absent (offline at the AI notice, 3.6.7), slow, or spent (8.6.1: "a spending stop and the written lines carry on"), nothing says which written lines answer Frank in the beats, key scenes, sit-downs, Act III's mornings and the last day's calls. Frank is written in the key scenes (C-349) and the street's unprompted lines are written (3.6.3), but suggested lines are "written ahead for hellos, goodbyes and decisions, the small model's otherwise" (3.6.6), so offline even a pad player loses most of his lines, against his ruling that pad players get every power typists have. Whether the small model runs on the player's machine is not said; this design assumes it may not.

The rule. Every act Frank can make in talk is one of RT-14's ten: question, agree, refuse, offer, threat, promise, claim, insult, small talk, leave. The written floor gives each of the ten a written form built from the Ledger's nouns: the claim builder (Ledger v2 L5.4) already builds a claim from a person and a deed; the same builder makes a question ("ask about [person / place / deed]"), an offer ("[amount] for [thing]"), a threat, a promise and a keep (D4). Offline or spent, the talk screen shows the act menu in place of the typing box; the line Frank says is the written template filled in. Every speaker's answer in a beat, key scene, sit-down, morning, call and job offer is chosen from written lines by state (what he holds, his reading of Frank, the act), as the key scenes' reading variants already are (decision 36). Live talk, when there, adds colour and range on top; it never carries a state change the floor cannot.

Data. A line table per scene and speaker, keyed by act and state; the act templates; the floor's count to be made by the story lane against its inventory (about 5,300 lines not yet written today, BLUEPRINT.md "What still blocks stage 1"; the floor adds lines for other speakers' answers by act in the heavy scenes).

The player's hands and what he sees. Online, nothing changes. Offline or spent, the same small sign as today (3.6.1) and the act menu; on a pad, nothing new to learn, since the pad already works from suggested lines.

Numbers. Lines per scene: to be counted by the story lane. The bar: a bot plays the whole story offline through the act menu and reaches every ending its style allows (G29), with no beat waiting on a model.

Edges. A typed line the floor cannot read offline never reaches anyone (the typing box is not shown offline). A player who reaches the spending stop mid-scene finishes the scene on the floor's lines from the next turn. Nothing is lost: states are the model's only output that matters (the Core decides, BLUEPRINT.md "the moat").

Stage. Stage 1: the first hour's talk must run on the floor, since offline is offered before the first talk (3.6.7).

Combination test. A player picks offline at the AI notice, plays to the sit-down at Grace's and bluffs about a secret he cannot source. Expected: the act menu offers "claim: [person] did [deed]"; the speaker's answer comes from the written table by Ledger v2 L7 (fit, and how Frank could know); a low-nerve hearer may yield; the sit-down's outcome is the same state it would be online. No model, no special case.

## D6 Taught, practised, known (GB4; law 3 people, law 1 knowing, law 8; his ruling of 23 Nov)

The gap: "the taught skills and the quarter cap are owed a design" (7.7; C-263). CS-24 names only Ron's fighting lessons and the boxing club's; "streets and people come to be known by practice" has no rule.

What the references say. KCD2 teaches a technique through a person and a test: Tomcat shows the master strike only after Henry wins a duel, drills it from three sides, then unannounced, and the lesson ends with three clean strikes; other lessons cost money (Gamer Guides, 2025, updated 2026). GTA V grows skills by use in small steps and never lowers them (GTA Wiki, 2026). LEDGER takes KCD2's lesson in the world and GTA V's quiet growth, and shows neither as a number.

The rule. A taught skill is a named technique that makes one existing verb better in one way, never a new verb (every verb is open from the start: 1.6.15, his rulings on "can you"). A person can teach it if his own sheet holds it (G09). He teaches when he agrees by any route: trust (his reading holds "trusted", G26), money or a favour (G04, by greed), or fear (his reading holds "feared"; he teaches, and remembers why). The lesson is a short scene in the world at a place and hour his routine allows: he shows it once, Frank does it three times against him (the KCD2 form), and it is Frank's for good, kept if lost (CS-24). Practice: each use of a short list of verbs moves its one parameter a small step toward the cap, quietly: driving (recovery on the wet), fists (wind and the counter window, CS-24), lifting from a pocket (the press time, 1.6.13), tailing (the distance kept, 1.1.6, 1.2.6). The cap holds everything: at most a quarter better (his ruling). Knowledge by practice: a street Frank has walked or driven three times is known; a known street lets Frank's own landmark line (6.9) give the quicker way in a chase or a fare, and the paper map marks it (6.3).

Data. A skills table: skill, verb, parameter, cap 1.25, the teachers (person ids from the cast data), the practice step. A known flag per street.

The player's hands and what he sees. He asks in talk ("teach me that"); the lesson is played with the hands. He knows he has a skill the 1990 way: the teacher says so ("you've got it now"); the Day book holds "Ron showed me the choke in the yard"; the body shows it (a cleaner counter, the car holding the line); Frank's reading at each act's end may say "I can hold a car on the wet now".
