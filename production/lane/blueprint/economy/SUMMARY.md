# The town's economy, 1990: summary (24 Nov 2026)

Decided: a Hook cab fare is 80p plus £1.10 a mile, about £3 a trip, so a week of fares pays the £18 rent. Drivers keep half their takings; more cars come on terms (£90 down, £22 a week). Shops raise prices only when squeezed and shut only for a reason the town can name. Protection is £5 to £15 a shop. Smuggled tobacco makes £60 a case, two cases a week, four once Tom holds the dock gate. Loans are a pound in five a week. Blackmail spends a hook once. DS Ellis costs about £800, then £100 a week.

Rests on: 1990 prices and wages from Parliament's written answers, read in full (cigarettes £1.61, petrol £1.86 a gallon, a manual worker £227 a week, a constable £190 to £318); unsourced figures are marked as judgement.

The run: four play styles over 13 game weeks, 300 runs each. Nobody is rich by week 3 or broke for good, no shop shuts without cause, no crime pays twice the middle one, and greed leaves Tom poorer at the story's end.

Open: three owner choices (design, section 9); a 48-minute day makes the story longer than 20 hours.
