# The long heat: killing a constable, and what follows any killing charge

Proposed parts for the blueprint, 24 Nov 2026 (what-if sweep, his pick B at 22:47). Written in the blueprint's own form (How it works, Standard, Test, Source, Status) for the keeper to fold into 2.6 and 2.16, with one change to 2.5.5 (a missing constable's sunk body is found, LH-01). Every part here is decided by this session within canon and his rulings and recorded in SUMMARY.md's decisions list; he overturns any he disagrees with. The two places that touch violence, scope or an ending are questions for him (Q-H1 and Q-H2 in SUMMARY.md); the parts below carry the recommended answer as a working value until he picks.

His ruling of 22:47, 24 Nov (relayed by the advisor), which this file designs: if nobody saw, an outside murder squad takes over the Hook for about a game week (men stopped in the street, door to door, a reward posted); everyone who knows anything is leaned on and each person's temper decides; his bought constables drop him; the rival heads gain a hold on him. If the squad gets enough (a signed statement, the weapon, his coat matched to a description), he is charged and held on remand, played as visiting-room talks with his people while the case is built; witnesses holding or taking back what they said decide collapse or trial. His two additions: no branch may be a dead end and even the worst branch leaves a way back; bribery is possible, rare, costly and risky (canon: police corruptible as individuals, never as a thesis).

Where it sits in the game's seven weeks: story beats fire on states, not dates (BLUEPRINT-07), so the long heat delays no beat it does not touch; remand days are mostly skipped hours (a visit scene a day), so the squad's week plus eight remand days cost about 6 to 8 real hours of play, not 15 game days of it; the trial and appeal weeks are skipped to their scenes in the same way. If the game's last week (the books' day) falls inside the long heat, the ending is read from where it stands: charged and unresolved reads as charged, never as convicted.

Clock used throughout: 48 real minutes a game day (his ruling of 24 Nov), so a game hour is 2 real minutes and the squad's week is about 5.6 real hours of play, less the skipped empty hours.

Names minted here as placeholders within canon (rule: names the plan needs are minted within canon, placeholders meanwhile): Detective Chief Superintendent Malcolm Aske (head of the squad, from county headquarters), Detective Inspector Pam Hebden (his deputy, who takes Ellis's case if Ellis dies), Detective Constable Roy Stannard (the squad's exhibits officer). The keeper may rename them; none is a real person.

## LH-01 Which road a constable's killing takes

- How it works: A constable dies by Frank's hand by any route the fight rules allow (the finishing choice with a weapon, the rare fatal fall, a shot at War). What follows depends on who knows it was him, by the ordinary perception rules (2.2, RT2-03), never by the old rule that every struck officer knows Frank (see LH-12). Known road: someone who knows Frank (recognition rung) saw it, or another officer saw it: the manhunt at once (its sign, his description said aloud, at least one game hour before any arrest), arrest, charge, and the remand road (LH-07); the squad still comes for its week and builds the case while he is held. Unknown road: nobody saw, or only people who do not know him (a shape, a coat, a voice, a face they cannot name): the squad's week (LH-02 to LH-06). A constable is never simply missing: each carries a personal radio and is called up when he misses his point, so a hidden body brings a search of the Hook that night, and a body sunk in the Hook's water is found by the underwater search unit within two game days (the one exception to "a sunk body is never found", which still holds for everyone else, because a missing officer is searched for and a missing docker is not).
- Standard: The road depends only on who perceived what (FF-05: police know only what a person perceived or a search found); a hidden or sunk constable is found within two game days; the manhunt's sign shows at least one game hour before an arrest (ER-POL-05). Test: B: seeded constable killings: seen by a knower, seen by a stranger, unseen in the open, unseen and sunk; the road taken, the find time and the sign timing logged against this rule.
- Source: his ruling of 22:47, 24 Nov; FF-05; RT2-03; 2.5.5. Status: proposed.

## LH-02 The squad arrives

- How it works: At 08:00 on the morning after the body is found, the squad takes over: Detective Chief Superintendent Aske and Detective Inspector Hebden from county headquarters, with a large inquiry behind them (in 1990 a police killing drew dozens of officers); the game shows it as six detective pairs and uniformed aid on the Hook's streets, standing for the whole. Signs, all in the world and never on a map: a police caravan on the quay road by the landing as the incident room (its door open by day, a queue of residents giving accounts), two unmarked cars and a van, the spot taped for two game days with flowers left at the tape, the beat walked in pairs for the whole week (the street-on-edge second constable is always on), a poster on every board by the second morning. Ellis is not in charge: the squad sits above her and she keeps her own stages for everything else. The town goes quiet for the week: fewer people out after dark (night crowd counts at about two thirds), night fares down by a third, shops paying rounds late, and the rivals stop their own night work at the landing (the squad is bad for every firm, which is why they resent Frank if they think it was him: LH-05). The squad stays seven game days, or until it charges someone; a second killing during the week (LH-04) keeps it seven more.
- Standard: The squad's presence shows in at least four world signs within the first game hour of its arrival; nothing of it on any map or meter; the town's night crowd, fares and rounds fall while it stays and recover over two game weeks after it leaves. Test: B: arrival scripted from a seeded body; signs counted by the tester bot; crowd, fare and round figures logged daily against a no-killing control run.
- Source: his ruling of 22:47; HOLMES (above) for the incident room's thoroughness; the caravan as a mobile incident room is a period practice to be confirmed against a dated 1988 to 1992 photograph (unverified). Status: proposed; the two-thirds crowd and one-third fares are working values to measure in the loops.

## LH-03 Door to door, men stopped, the reward

- How it works: Door to door: on days 1 and 2 a detective and a constable knock at every door on the street of the deed and the two streets that meet it; each resident gives an account drawn from what they truly hold (their memory slots of the night, or nothing) and their temper (2.14). Men stopped: from the first evening, any man out in the Hook after 20:00 who passes a squad pair is stopped and asked his name, address and where he was that night; his coat is noted. Frank is stopped at least once in the first two days, always as a scene: he gives his account in talk, and what he says is written down and later checked against what the knowers say and the fare book (a broken account is a point: LH-05). An alibi is a person who will say he was with them (Ron, Sheila, a fare) and holds only while that person does: Mickey's people never inform on him (ruling of 1 Oct), but whether they lie for him follows their temper and what they actually saw, as for anyone else. His account is kept as a few structured claims (where he was, when, with whom), classified from the talk by the model and checked by the Core against the knowers' slots and the fare book; the talk model never judges whether it holds. The reward: on day 2, posters and the paper's front page offer five thousand pounds for information (a working value, to check against period cases of police killings), with an anonymous number; the reward adds to every knower's pull to talk in proportion to their greed (LH-04). The return: on day 7 at the hour of the killing, the squad stops every car and walker at the spot for an hour, and anyone there that night who passes is asked again.
- Standard: Every account a resident gives matches their stored memory (the claim check's rule: a character says only what the simulation knows); Frank is stopped at least once in days 1 and 2; an alibi stands exactly as long as its giver holds. Test: B: door-to-door accounts compared slot by slot with memory stores for the three streets; Frank's stop logged; an alibi giver's regard lowered mid-week and the alibi's fall logged.
- Source: his ruling of 22:47 (men stopped, door to door, a reward); Crimestoppers 1988 (above); ruling of 25 Sep (a character says only what the simulation knows). The return a week later is common later practice and is to be confirmed for 1990 (unverified). Status: proposed; five thousand pounds is a working value.

## LH-04 Everyone who knows anything is leaned on

- How it works: The knowers are everyone holding anything of the deed or of Frank around it: witnesses of the deed (any slot: a sound, a shape, the coat, the car, a face), anyone who saw him within the game hour before or after with a slot (blood, the coat, the weapon, the car near the spot), anyone gossip reached naming him (they hold hearsay), and his helpers after: the retired doctor who stitched him, the launderette that washed the coat, whoever sold or took the weapon, any crewman with him or who moved the body, a bought officer, a bought Ellis. Each day the squad questions up to four knowers, most-holding first (more slots first, then nearest the spot), and comes back to any who held, every third day. Each visit is a scene in the town Frank can see (detectives at a door, a woman taken to the caravan) and the Ledger notes it as he sees it. Each visit ends one of three ways, decided by the Core, never by the talk model: holds (says nothing useful), gives a lead (says what she holds, unsigned), or signs (a signed statement of what she holds). The pull to talk is her readiness to talk, plus the reward times her greed, plus a little each visit, minus her loyalty to Frank, minus her fear of him if he threatened her (unless her nerve is high, when a threat pushes her to sign instead), minus a little if he paid her (she remembers being paid). Mickey's people never sign about one of their own, and Rita, Wayne, Hal, the scrap dealer, the customs officer and the outfit's man never go to the police because of their trade (2.5.2); a crewman holds while his nerve holds (2.11.2). Frank's answers are the ordinary verbs, now under the squad's eyes: reach a knower first and talk her round, pay, frighten (himself or a sent man) or help; keep his crew steady; give an alibi; lie low. Killing a knower is a second killing: found, it keeps the squad seven days more; it makes Frank the squad's man at once only if her lead or statement had already named him or a slot ties him to her death (FF-05); and every knower who suspects him leans toward signing for the rest of the case (fear of him now pushes them to the police, not away). Two leads pointing at Frank make him the squad's man: a second stop, his office watched from a parked car, his men stopped too.
- Standard: The squad's visit order follows only what each knower holds; each visit's result follows only the knower's temper, the reward and Frank's earlier acts toward her, and the same save gives the same results; Mickey's people and the trade people never sign; a second killing in the week extends the squad and turns the knowers' fear. Test: B: 50 seeded squad weeks with knowers of known tempers; visit order and results against the formula; replays from one save identical; the Mickey's-people and trade rows never signing.
- Source: his ruling of 22:47 (everyone who knows anything leaned on; each person's temper decides); 2.14; 2.5.2; ruling of 1 Oct; MEM-09 (nothing clears a memory). Status: proposed. Working values for the pull (tune rows to add): sign at 0.70 or more, lead at 0.40 or more; reward weight 0.20 times greed; each visit plus 0.10; paid minus 0.30; threatened minus 0.30, or plus 0.30 when nerve is 0.60 or more; all to be measured in the loops.

## LH-05 The bought men, Ellis and the rivals in the squad's week

- How it works: His bought constables drop him: from the squad's arrival until two game weeks after it leaves, Garton and DC Wardell take no money and do no favours; a first offer is refused and kept quiet, a second offer in the week is reported (a lead). Each is also a knower of a different thing (that Frank pays police): if the squad leans on the station (it does once Frank is the squad's man), a bought man's nerve decides whether he owns up to his own payments and names Frank as payer (a lead, and he is lost for good). A bought Ellis keeps taking her hundred a week; once in the week she tells Frank which knower the squad sees tomorrow; she holds, because she has more to lose, unless Frank misses a payment during the week, when she gives the squad a lead (unsigned, since a statement would hang her too) as well as ending the bribe as 2.6.8 says. The rival heads gain a hold: his ruling gives every head a hold, and the rule keeps it inside 2.10.2 (each rival knows only what its people saw or heard) by counting the squad's cost to their business as something every head sees: from day 3 every head is leaned on by the squad's week, and each head whose people hold anything of the deed, or who hears the talk naming Frank, sends word through his man or Garbutt by day 3 that the squad is costing him; on day 5, unless Frank pays (two hundred pounds) or yields something for two game weeks (a post, a rank, a front's take), that head gives the squad a lead. Paying or yielding stops the lead, but the head keeps a hold on Frank (a hook, RT-07) that he can spend once, at any later sit-down, to win one point without a fight (the reverse hook: LH-15); a head with no slot and no talk naming Frank still sends word on day 3, but his day-5 lead is only that the squad should look at the new man at Mickey's, which counts for nothing unless it leads to a piece.
- Standard: Canon: police corruptible as individuals, never as a thesis: the station as a whole never changes, and each bought officer's choice follows his own nerve. Every rival hold is earned by something his people saw or heard (2.10.2: each rival knows only what its people saw or heard); each hold is spent at most once. Test: S and B: a squad week with both constables bought, Ellis bought and a missed payment, and each rival in turn holding or not holding a slot; offers, leads, holds and spends logged against the rule.
- Source: his ruling of 22:47 (bought constables drop him; rival heads gain a hold); canon (police corruptible as individuals); 2.6.8; his ruling of 24 Nov on Ellis's price; LH-15. Status: proposed; two hundred pounds is a working value from GD-07's price curve.

## LH-06 What is enough to charge him

- How it works: The squad counts its pieces, and charges at 4 or more. A signed statement naming Frank (from someone who knows him, or a witness who picks him out at an identification parade): 3. A signed statement of a lower rung (his shape, coat, car, voice, or seeing him near the spot): 1 each, at most 2 from these. The weapon found and linked to him: 2 (linked by his fingerprints when he was bare-handed, compared once his prints are taken: at any arrest in this case under PACE, or already on the force's file from an earlier arrest in Meridian, which is the only way a print points to him before he is a suspect; or by a signed statement that he had it; or by the seller signing); found and not linked: 0, though it ties the deed to the place. His coat seized and matching a description given: 1; with the dead man's blood on it, unwashed: 2 more (blood was grouped in 1990, and a coin-sized stain could be compared in several days). His account broken (an alibi giver who will not back it, the fare book against him): 1. His own admission, to the squad in interview or to anyone who then signs: 3. A bought man's own corruption, owned up or found, is a separate charge of corruption and adds at most 1 to the murder count. A rival's or bought man's lead turns into a point only when it leads the squad to a piece above. Searches: on day 2 the drains within 100 m of the spot and along the routes from it to Mickey's, the landing and the yards' lane; on day 3 the dock within 50 m of the spot and below the quay edge nearest each of those routes, by divers; Frank's office once he is the squad's man, with a sign the evening before (the parked car watching it); a weapon down a drain or in the water outside those reaches is never found. Arrest on suspicion: once the squad holds 2 or more and Frank is the squad's man, it arrests him for questioning, its sign (a car outside, his name in the caff) at least one game hour ahead; held up to 36 game hours, a tape-recorded interview with a solicitor (Frank answers, lies, or says nothing; silence costs nothing in the count); a parade for any witness who saw his face; released on police bail pending inquiries if the count stays under 4, and the week goes on. An account of his found false (an alibi giver who will not back it, the fare book against a stated claim) is the broken-account point above. The count shows nowhere; Frank reads it from what he sees (who was visited, who avoids his eye, what the search took), and his guess per knower is in words on the Ledger's People page (GV-02).
- Standard: A charge follows only from pieces a person perceived or a search found (FF-05); the count is deterministic; every arrest shows its sign at least one game hour ahead; nothing on screen shows the count. Test: B: seeded squad weeks covering each piece alone and in pairs; the charge fires exactly at 4; prints link only with an earlier arrest and bare hands; drains and dock searched only within their reaches.
- Source: his ruling of 22:47 (a signed statement, the weapon, his coat matched to a description); crime-scene-1990 research (local prints only, no databases, stain size and days); PACE detention limits and Code E (above). Status: proposed; the point values and the 100 m and 50 m search reaches are working values to measure in the loops. Gloves: a pair (two pounds, the outfitter's) worn on a night job leave no prints, but a man in gloves after dark is a mark like a cap and scarf (GV-02: witnesses note it) and the gloves carry blood like the coat; the keeper adds them to 1.6.9 and 1.6.10.

## LH-07 The remand road (every killing charge, not only a constable's)

- How it works: Charged with murder, Frank is held overnight, appears next morning before the magistrates in the Exchange (a soft-edge scene, under 2 real minutes), and is remanded in custody: bail for murder was rare and the game never grants it. He is held at the county remand prison, a soft-edge place never walked. Remand lasts until the committal hearing on the eighth game day after the charge (the period's eight-day remand, with the months a real committal took compressed into it, a game choice recorded as such). Each game day except Sunday he has one social visit, played as a talk scene of a few real minutes with one visitor of his choosing from those who would come (Ron, Sheila, a crewman, Garbutt, Alison or June if friends), and on top of it a legal visit from his solicitor whenever he asks, which is private and always offers one act on the case (challenge an identification, test an alibi, find which witness might take back); the rest of the day is skipped, its news told by the next visitor and kept in the Ledger. Through visitors he runs everything: the firm by word (Sheila keeps the books, Ron the door and the rank, Pauline the board: the firm's floor holds, RT2-06), the eight sending verbs carried out by the man he tells, Ellis's money paid from the tin by Sheila, and the case worked from outside: a witness talked round, paid or frightened by someone he sends, the bribe (LH-08), an alibi shored up. Social visits are supervised: an order to frighten or pay a witness given in one is overheard when the visitor's nerve is below the middle (fixed, never a roll), adding the interference point (LH-09); legal visits are never overheard. The rivals each test one of his posts or fronts in the week, as they would any absent man. The squad keeps building: it still visits knowers and its count can rise. This road replaces "22 hours then charged" for every killing charge and supersedes three sentences in the blueprint: 2.6.4's and 4.3.7's "22 for a killing, then ... charged and remanded" (now the road above), RT2-02's "22 hours and a charge, which removes the best turn of every ending" (the best turn is now removed only by a conviction, and a dismissed or acquitted killing leaves the charges list without it, though the town remembers), and 2.6.9's "there is no court scene and no prison" (true still for every lesser charge; a killing charge alone has the magistrates' scenes and remand). It closes C-043, C-105 and C-142 and gives the story's remand for Ron (C-336) its machinery: the same scenes, from the other side of the table.
- Standard: No branch of the remand road is a dead end (his addition of 22:47): every remand day offers at least one act that can change the count, always through the solicitor's legal visit even when no one else will come; a visit is a talk scene of a few real minutes, the rest skipped; the firm's floor holds while he is held. Test: B: a seeded charge with every knower already signed and every helper hostile; the tester confirms an act that can change the count is offered on each remand day; firm floor checked after eight remand days.
- Source: his ruling of 22:47 (charged and held on remand, played as visiting-room talks with his people while the case is built); Hansard 6 Dec 1989 (a visit every weekday); eight-day remands (above); RT2-06; 2.6.4 and C-043. Status: proposed.

## LH-08 The bribe (rare, costly, risky)

- How it works: Once per case, Frank can buy one officer on the case to lose one thing: the exhibits officer, DC Stannard, to lose an exhibit (the weapon or the coat, fifteen hundred pounds), or the detective who took a statement to lose that statement (two and a half thousand pounds for a statement naming him; a lesser statement eight hundred). It is reached only through a go-between who knows the man (Garbutt from rung 3, or a bought Ellis), cash only, from the tin. The go-between says beforehand whether the man is for sale; an honest man is never approached, so the risk is not hidden chance. On a constable's killing the price is doubled (officers on a colleague's murder are the hardest to buy) and only the exhibits officer can be bought. A lost exhibit's points leave the count at once; a lost statement holds only until the squad's next visit to that witness (the inquiry's index shows the gap and she is simply seen again), so it is worth buying only together with turning her (P07). The risks come later and are each a way the town remembers: sold out (the bought detective is now a knower of the payment, and if any later inquiry leans on him, his nerve decides whether he names Frank, which restores the piece and adds an attempt to pervert the course of justice as a charge); squeezed (once, two game weeks later, he asks for five hundred more; refused, he finds the exhibit again); and the go-between knows (Garbutt holds it as a hook he can sell to a rival once). A statement lost does not erase the witness: she still remembers, still tells her sister, and signs again when seen unless she has been turned.
- Standard: Canon: police corruptible as individuals, never as a thesis: one officer, one thing, once a case; the station and the squad never change as a whole; the price is paid only from cash; the go-between names an honest man before any approach. Test: S: one bribe per case; the second attempt refused; the go-between's warning; squeeze and sell-out each forced in a seeded run and the restored piece logged.
- Source: his addition of 22:47 (bribery possible: a rare, costly, risky bribe of one officer on the case, an exhibit or a statement lost, with its own risk of being sold out or squeezed later); canon; 2.6.8. Status: proposed; the prices are working values, scaled from his Ellis price (about eight hundred pounds) and GD-07.

## LH-09 Collapse or trial: the committal hearing

- How it works: On the eighth remand day the committal is heard at the magistrates' court (soft edge, a scene of a few real minutes). As in 1988 to 1992, Frank's solicitor makes the prosecution's witnesses attend and be cross-examined. The count is taken again: each signed witness who attends and stands by her statement keeps her points; one who takes it back (talked round or paid: "I was mistaken") loses them; one who does not come through fear has her statement read anyway (the 1988 Act's rule on a witness kept away by fear), at half its points rounded down, and the count gains one for the interference; a sighting made in poor conditions (light under 0.5, farther than 15 m, or a glimpse under 3 s) counts 1, not 3, as a naming (the Turnbull caution, unverified above). Physical pieces keep their points unless bought (LH-08). Under 4: the case is dismissed and Frank walks out of the court that afternoon, Ron at the door with car two; the killing stays open forever (it never cools) and any knower who signs later can bring a new charge, but the squad does not come back. 4 or more: he is committed for trial at the Crown Court one game week later, still on remand, the visits going on, every witness reachable again on each of those nights. The trial counts once more by the same rules. What a guilty verdict does is his question (Q-H2 in SUMMARY.md); the working answer, recommended, is that guilty opens one appeal week in which one new piece taken away (a witness who takes back, the bribe if unused, a broken identification) quashes it, and only if nothing changes does the game reach its inside ending, said plainly before the verdict as permanent (D27: reloading is never stopped, the game says what is permanent).
- Standard: Every step after a charge (remand days, committal, trial nights, appeal week) offers a way back that a player can find from what he sees (his addition: even the worst branch leaves a way back); frightening a witness away is always weaker than winning her (read at half, plus one for interference); the count at each hearing follows only the rules above. Test: B: seeded committals where each witness holds, takes back or stays away through fear; outcomes against the rule; a worst-case run (every piece held) reaches the appeal week with at least one act offered.
- Source: his ruling of 22:47 (witnesses holding or taking back what they said decide collapse or trial) and his addition (recoverable); committal research on wip; CJA 1988 s.23; D27. Status: proposed; the guilty branch waits on Q-H2.

## LH-15 A hold on Frank (the reverse hook)

- How it works: A hold is a hook another person keeps on Frank: a fact about him that person holds first-hand or bought, which could hurt him if told to the police or a rival (the squad's lead, a bribe he paid, a lie found out). It shows in the Ledger as Frank's guess, in words. The holder spends it once: at a sit-down to win one point without a fight, or by telling it where it hurts. Frank takes a hold back only by trading it for one he holds on that person (RT-07) or by the holder's fate (gone from the Hook, dead, turned to his crew). A hold never expires and is never wiped (MEM-09).
- Standard: Every hold traces to a fact the holder perceived or bought; each is spent at most once. Test: S: a rival hold earned in the long heat, spent at a sit-down, then gone; a second spend refused.
- Source: RT-07 (the same thing the other way round); LH-05; LH-08. Status: proposed (new part).

## LH-10 After the long heat

- How it works: A constable's killing is remembered forever: flowers at the spot for the week, a small plaque on the wall there later (the keeper may drop it if it cannot be confirmed for 1990), his name in the paper and in talk for good. The beat goes to the second constable from the next night and a new constable, named, takes the beat within two game weeks. For the rest of the game every constable treats Frank's known men as having cause at any challenge. Frank's standing reads two ways: hard men and rivals' men hold him higher ("the one the squad were after"), straight people cooler; it shows in greetings and soft keys (2.12.4), never in a number. If the case collapsed, the street knows that too.
- Standard: The killing never cools in talk and is never wiped (canon: killing is rare, permanent, remembered forever); the beat is never left empty more than one night. Test: S: talk about the killing present at days 7, 30 and 49; the beat walked every night.
