# Fares, terraces, doors, dock scale (research, 24 Nov 2026)

Method: WebSearch, then WebFetch on URLs from those searches. "Read" = fetched today and answered by the fetch tool's summariser (not read raw). "Snippet" = seen only as a search-result title. Two fetches were blocked (Wikipedia Grimsby Docks, Port of Hull: permission unanswered; Yorkshire Post St Andrew's Dock article: client error). Not evidence: those pages.

## 1. Fares

### London, 1990 and 1991 (strong)
- Hansard written answer, 18 May 1990, HC vol 172 c559W, https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1990/may/18/london-taxi-fares. READ today. Avg rise about 9.2% from 8 June 1990. Minimum fare 1.00 pounds, including unchanged 40p hire charge, covering first 1,021.5 yd or 3 min 36 s. Then 20p per 340.5 yd or 1 min 12 s up to 6 miles; beyond that 20p per 227 yd or 48 s.
- London Cab Order 1990, SI 1990/1076, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1990/1076/pdfs/uksi_19901076_en.pdf (made 15 May, in force 8 June 1990). READ today. Confirms 40p hire, 20p per 340.5 yd above 9.67 mph, 20p per 72 s below it, 1.00 pound minimum, round up to 20p.
- Hansard, 23 May 1991, vol 191 c564W, https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1991/may/23/taxi-fares-london. READ today. About 8.9% rise from 8 June 1991: 1.00 minimum incl. 40p hire, covers 940.5 yd; 20p per 313.5 yd.
- Computed (inference from above): 1990 London, 1 mile (1,760 yd): 1,760-1,021.5 = 738.5 yd more, 3 steps of 340.5 yd, so 1.00 + 0.60 = 1.60 pounds before extras and traffic delay. 2 miles about 2.60 (1.00 + 0.20 x 5 steps = 2.00 for ~2,722 yd; 3,520 yd needs ~7.3 steps, so 2.60 to 2.80). Metered London per-mile after the first: roughly 1.04 per mile in 1990 (20p per 340.5 yd = 1.035/mile).

### Outside London (weak: no named-town 1988-92 tariff found)
- Sefton (Merseyside) 1991 hackney rate card, 1 mile = 1.20 pounds (5 and 10 miles also on card, not extracted). https://dmnews.co.uk/?p=4054. READ today; a modern blog showing an "original" leaflet; undated beyond 1991, unverified by any council record. Only northern-ish data point: use with caution.
- Mean fares, England and Wales districts, applicable November 1991 (Table 6), ITS Leeds working paper WP382, https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/2190/1/ITS279_WP382_uploadable.pdf. READ today. 2 miles, 2 people, day: 2.12 to 2.49 pounds depending on licensing regime (all-district mean about 2.3); 4 miles, 2 people, day: 3.69 to 4.22 pounds; 4 miles, 4 people, night: 5.33 to 6.14. Best-supported provincial figure. Derived (inference): about 1.1 per mile marginal after a start of around 0.8-1.0; flag fall not given by source. Provincial fares were therefore similar to London meter rates, a little lower.
- ITS WP296 (https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/2263/1/ITS59_WP296_uploadable.pdf): READ; no fare levels, only that councils set fares in ~90% of districts (citing Toner 1989) and counts of districts raising fares 1988-89.
- Hull, Hartlepool, Middlesbrough council pages (hull.gov.uk, hartlepool.gov.uk, middlesbrough.gov.uk): SNIPPET only, current tariffs, not historic. No 1988-92 tariff for any named northern town found. Grimsby: nothing.
- Short ride under 1 mile (inference): about 1.00 to 1.40 pounds in a provincial town (minimum fare plus a step or two); London 1.00 minimum covers 1,021 yd in 1990, so a 0.6-mile ride is exactly the minimum.
- Gap: Toner (1989, WP273) and Pells (1990, WP290) are cited for fare data but were not reached.

## 2. Terraced houses (WEAK: no sourced dimensions found)
- Wikipedia "Byelaw terraced house" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelaw_terraced_house, READ: front garden 6 ft deep in some terraces; 9-inch brick wall; ginnel after every fourth house per the Act (cites Calow 2007 p.15); earlier terraces 8-12 houses; no frontage, depth, height or door figures.
- 1900s.org.uk house, https://1900s.org.uk/1900s-house.htm, READ: rooms (m): parlour 3.23 x 3.79; front bedroom 4.26 x 3.15; middle bedroom 2.56 x 3.62; scullery 2.61 x 3.41. Not Hull/northern-specific (unspecified; site is a typical estate house). No overall frontage given.
- Leeds thesis (Harrison, https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/32589/20/Harrison_109040101_Thesis.pdf): only first 100k of 717k characters read; bay windows/steps may project max 4 ft; no widths found.
- Hull Hessle Road Conservation Area character appraisal (https://www.hull.gov.uk/downloads/file/4588/hessle-road-character-appraisal-and-management-plan), READ: Hessle Road in the area is about 0.8 km; mostly two- and three-storey buildings; mid/late Victorian terraces commonly three storeys (shop frontages on the main road); a ten-foot back passage exists. No frontage widths. The older Hessle Road appraisal (hull.gov.uk file 3196) READ: no measurements; buildings predominantly three storeys.
- Historic England shopping parades, https://cms.historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/iha-shopping-parades/heag116-shopping-parades-iha, READ: INTER-WAR (1918-39) shops 18-20 ft frontage (about 5.5 m), about 50 ft deep. Victorian/Edwardian parade figures not extracted. Parade sizes vary 5 to 42 units.
- Historic England Domestic 2 (heag102) READ: no dimensions; byelaw housing "cramped sites, deep plans".
- Everything below is INFERENCE from general knowledge, not found in a source this session (do not cite as evidence): northern byelaw terraces typically 12-16 ft (3.7-4.9 m) frontage, bay-fronted better ones 15-18 ft; plot depth about 40-60 ft (12-18 m) with yard 10-15 ft behind the main house plus a back lane about 10 ft (the ten-foot passage above supports the lane width); two storeys about 6.5-7.5 m to eaves, ground floor ceiling about 2.6-3 m; front door about 0.8-0.9 m wide leaf, 2.0-2.1 m high; street width about 36 ft (11 m) was the typical byelaw minimum (memory of the model byelaws; unverified); small shops 4.5-6 m frontage.

## 3. Doors per 100 m (all INFERENCE)
- Terraced street, frontage 4.0-5.0 m: about 20-25 houses per 100 m per side; one door per house. Both sides about 40-50 front doors per 100 m of street length; with 5.5 m frontage (bay-fronted) about 18 per side. Shopped parade at 5-6 m: 16-20 shop doors per side per 100 m (some shops with a separate upstairs door).
- Dock road with warehouses: not sourced. Inference: large frontages of 20-60 m each, so about 2-5 doors/gates per side per 100 m, plus loading hoists.

## 4. Real dock scale (weak)
- Grimsby: No.1 Fish Dock opened 1857, extended to 13 acres from 1866 (Historic England, Grimsby HAZ aerial photograph assessment, https://historicengland.org.uk/research/results/reports/7831/GrimsbyHeritageActionZone_AnAssessmentoftheHistoricEnglandArchiveAerialPhotographs), READ today. 13 acres is about 250 m x 210 m if square; real shape is a long rectangle (inference). New fish market 1996 reclaimed over 2.5 ha of dock.
- Hull St Andrew's Dock: opened 24 Sep 1883, extension 1897, closed to shipping 3 Nov 1975 (Hull History Centre, https://hullhistorycentre.org.uk/research/research-guides/history-hull-docks.aspx, READ); Hull council appraisal says filling began late 1980s (https://www.hull.gov.uk/downloads/file/3203/st-andrews-dock-appraisal, READ) whereas History Centre says filled 1985: sources conflict, so for 1988-92 the dock is already closed and part or all filled in Hull. Albert Dock 17 acres, opened 1869. No St Andrew's acreage or quay lengths found in any reached source. Wikipedia Port of Hull not reached.
- Hessle Road: about 0.8 km conservation-area length (above). Distance Hessle Road to the dock not given.
- Implication: a real fishing dock was around 10-20 acres, a few hundred metres per side, and in 1988-92 Hull's and Grimsby's fleets had largely gone (St Andrew's closed 1975; Grimsby fish dock still working at reduced scale).
