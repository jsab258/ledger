## 3. Street widths and kerbs

Period/legal:
- Model Byelaws (from 1877, under Public Health Act 1875): new carriage-road streets at least 36 ft (11 m) wide; carriageway at least 24 ft (7.3 m); a footway each side not less than one-sixth of street width (6 ft / 1.8 m on a 36 ft street, as calculated by the source); kerbs 3 to 7 in high; short non-carriage streets (<100 ft) 24 ft; back streets 16 ft or 13 ft in later byelaws. Source: Robert Huxford, Designing Buildings Wiki, edited 28 Jul 2021 (modern, secondary; citing Knight's Annotated Model Byelaws) https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/The_history_of_the_dimensions_and_design_of_roads%2C_streets_and_carriageways READ. Primary Model Byelaws text not read.
- Form of Bye-laws (1860s) required carriage streets of 36 ft. Survey of Lincoln (modern) https://sites.google.com/site/thesurveyoflincoln/Projects/cityba/regulation READ.
- Town Improvement Clauses Act 1847: at least 30 ft for carriage roads, 20 ft others (Huxford, as above).
- Earlier local acts: Manchester 1830 min 24 ft; Liverpool 1842 24 ft with 6 ft footpaths (Whiterose thesis 458273 vol1, p. unknown, OCR garbled, first 100k chars only) https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/10303/2/458273_vol1.pdf READ partly.
- Hansard 12 Jan 1976: standard new two-lane road carriageway 7.3 m (24 ft); 5.5 m (18 ft) normal minimum on existing light-traffic roads. https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1976/jan/12/roads-minimum-widths READ.
- Hansard 10 May 1973: Roads in Urban Areas (1966) footway minima for new construction vary 6 to 15 ft. https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written_answers/1973/may/10/pavement-width READ. Local shopping street footway at the low end is inferred, not stated.
- Design Bulletin 32 (1977) and Roads in Urban Areas 1966: actual carriageway/footway tables NOT obtained (Huxford says only that they gave narrower new-town carriageways). A later Torfaen design guide (late 1990s, undated) based on DB32 gives residential footway minimum 1.8 m, industrial 2.0 m; carriageway tables not extracted. https://www.torfaen.gov.uk/en/Related-Documents/Forward-Planning/Supplementary-Planning-Guidance/Residential-and-Industrial-Design-Guide-Part-1.pdf READ.
- Measured modern: TRL 661 / Manual for Streets evidence: residential carriageway widths across 145 sites 4 to 10 m, mean about 6.3 m (SD 1.6) (modern, 2000s, includes pre-war sites; only first 100k chars read). https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a79e60ced915d6b1deb426a/manualforstreetsevidence.pdf
- Dock roads, local shopping parades: NOT FOUND. Inferred: 7.3 m carriageway (the 24 ft standard) with 2 to 3 m footways on a shopping parade; dock roads wider (8 to 10 m) with a narrow or single footway. No source.

## 4. Front-to-front distance

- No direct source found. Inferred: houses on the street line under a 36 ft byelaw street gives about 11 m (36 ft) between facades; with 2 to 4 m front gardens/forecourts, 14 to 18 m; pre-1875 streets narrower (6 to 9 m, 20 to 30 ft is an unverified estimate; Town Improvement Clauses 1847 floor of 20 to 30 ft is the only anchor). Lincoln source: two-storey house needed 15 ft clear space to the next building (a rear/side rule, not fronts). Wikipedia Byelaw terraced house gave no widths (READ).
- Back-to-back and byelaw layouts: Harrison, Industrial Archaeology Review 39(2) 2017 (eprints PDF) had its numbers stripped in the fetch; unusable.
