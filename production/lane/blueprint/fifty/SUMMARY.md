# 50 things in LEDGER: summary (24 Nov 2026)

## For Jafar

- The video list (FIFTY.md): of the 50 details a video would show from the first ten hours, 39 are designed, 8 partly, 3 missing. 33 are LEDGER's own: none of the seven catalogue games has them even in a weaker form.
- The outside list (BLIND-50.md), written by a helper that never saw the blueprint: the blueprint fully backs only 15 of 50. 24 are partly there, 11 missing (2 of those ruled out on purpose: gossip that grows, and a rival cab firm that fights back). That leaves 35 a player would ask about that the design cannot fully answer. 18 of them change how the game is built.
- The ten worst gaps that change how the game is built (close before the one-shot):
  1. Talk you can overhear is not wired to deeds: the bench, the counter and the bus stop cannot yet talk about what Frank did (BLIND 9; catalogue rank 3).
  2. Frank cannot start a story or a rumour himself, so he cannot turn talk on a rival (BLIND 7, 10).
  3. How a deed was done (three blows, or kicking a man on the floor) is not kept with the story, so tellings cannot differ (FIFTY 16).
  4. No pool of returning people: a man Frank humiliated never comes back weeks later (BLIND 48).
  5. An ordinary absence (an ill or frightened-off regular) is not noticed; only a killed or missing man is (FIFTY 12).
  6. Clothes do not change how people treat him: the outfits and prices exist, the rule does not (BLIND 46).
  7. Witnesses cannot get details wrong or borrow each other's (BLIND 5; the prior research recommends it).
  8. The cab trade has no street hail, demand does not follow the firm's name, and drivers are not witnesses who report to Frank (FIFTY 36, BLIND 22, 24).
  9. People can still appear or vanish in plain view (FIFTY 49), and the unnamed crowd do not walk to real places (BLIND 19).
  10. Nothing is remembered when Frank walks off mid-talk, and the "can't be talked into the impossible" guard and replay check are owed (FIFTY 50, 26).
- Distinctive enough yet? At the core, yes: witnesses, memory, the police and the Ledger already give a video 30-odd things no other game does. At the surface, not yet: the talk a player overhears, the way clothes and reputation change people, and the people who come back are the gaps a player meets first.

## What was done

1. Outside first: a fresh Opus 5.5 helper, kept away from the project's files, wrote BLIND-50 from one paragraph about the game.
2. Four Sonnet 5.5 readers listed about 290 candidates from the blueprint on lane-2026-11 (chapters 01 to 11, data/, temper/, ai-brain/, what-if/, reality-check/), each with its parts, status as written and whether a gap is build or content. Their lists are in readers/.
3. The lead picked the 50 a video maker would pick (surprising, showable in a short clip, specific, spread across witnesses, gossip, police, the Ledger, the cab trade, money, fights, light and 1990), put each BLIND-50 item to the blueprint, and judged uniqueness against the details catalogue on wip (CATALOGUE.md and the four NOTES files: GTA VI and his video, GTA V, GTA IV, RDR2, KCD2, Sleeping Dogs, Yakuza).
4. A fresh Opus 5.5 reviewer checked 15 rows drawn at random (10 from BLIND-50, 5 from FIFTY) against the blueprint and the catalogue: agreement 13 of 15 on status and 13 of 15 on uniqueness (REVIEW-15.md). All four disagreements went the reviewer's way after a check of the sources: BLIND 13 is designed (RT2-16 already rules the stop on a description); BLIND 10 is partly, like row 7; FIFTY 6 is "nearest GTA VI" (people with traits) and FIFTY 23 "nearest GTA V" (the phone's time and the bells). Its side notes were also taken: BLIND 5 is an open gap, not a ruling; BLIND 39's music is "named only"; FIFTY 6 reworded; FIFTY 23's wrong data file removed.

## Notes and limits

- "Designed" means exact rules and numbers in the blueprint; many are first values, unmeasured (the reality check's 441 unsourced numbers still stand).
- Uniqueness is against the catalogue's seven games only, as their notes show them; a game's feature missing from the notes is not counted. The catalogue itself is features from compilations, without timings.
- The first ten hours were read as about 12 game days at 48 real minutes a game day; FIFTY 47 (the clocks going back) falls at about hour 11.
- Two things seen in passing, not fixed here: data/police.json and the catalogue still say Tom for Frank; the rival-ladder pace (NUMBERS-RUN) and the rent against week-one fares stay open as before.
- Content rule kept throughout: no drink, no gambling, no children; brands fictional.
