# Proposed general rules (25 Nov 2026)

Each rule answers many player questions at once. Rules are ordered by how many failing questions they answer. Class: build means it changes how the game is built (data structures and systems), so it closes before the one-shot; content means animation, numbers, lines or props for the loops. Animation: none means talk and state only; clips means reuse of the small shared set; one or two bodies means new animation. The question ids are rows in QUESTIONS.csv. Working values are not set here; each rule is a direction for Monday's system pass, to be checked against canon, his rulings and the references there.

Words used: the one-shot is the first full build from the blueprint; the loops are the rounds of content work after it; a slot is one thing a person holds about someone (a face, a coat, a deed); dials are each person's temper settings (nerve, greed, grudge, law); the claim check stops live talk saying what a person does not hold; the soft edge is the ring of places just off the walkable map; the fold rule is how a squeezed or threatened person gives way; a rung is a step on Frank's ladder of standing; can-you is how far a question goes beyond what KCD2, RDR2 and GTA V allow.

## G13. Objects carry owners and facts, and are found

Objects carry their owner and the facts of how they moved: a stolen wallet, a planted parcel, a coat, a letter, a body, a dropped glove. Owners know their things anywhere; anyone may find what is left in sight; evidence keeps a chain the police can follow; lifting and planting small things is one verb with readable limits, if he says yes (decision 12); forgeries come only through a person who remembers making them. Each trace kind has a life that weather and time shorten (rain washes shoe marks, not a glove).

## G01. Frank as a source of talk

Anything Frank says that states a fact about someone becomes a told version in the hearer's memory, with Frank as its source, believed by fit and by his regard, and passed on by the gossip rules (2.4.1) like any telling. Telling someone to pass it on, paying a talker, staging a scene for watchers, or feeding one man a story to see where it comes out all follow from this. His conduct (giving a man up, crossing the road to avoid someone, a good deed) is a deed the street tells too. Found false, a claim lowers his regard with whoever learns it and can become a hook on him.
