# Question writer brief

The game: LEDGER, a third-person crime RPG for PC, set in Britain in 1990 (October to December), in a northern-ish English port town called Meridian; the playable map is one district, the Hook, plus its docks. The lead, Frank Nowak, inherits his late uncle's minicab office (Mickey's) and can build a crime empire: crew, bribes, protection, rival gangs fought or played off, a bent detective. The town sees, remembers and gossips: every townsperson is an AI person with memory, nerve, loyalty and a temper, who talks live (free speech with Frank), tells others what they saw, and can go to the police, who act only on what witnesses saw and said. Guns are rare and late; most violence is fists, coshes, knives, bottles are NOT used (no alcohol anywhere). PEGI 18. No alcohol, pubs or gambling shown or spoken of; tobacco allowed; no children anywhere in the game. Cars: arcade-ish 1990 cars, minicabs, a few chases. The promise: "can you" should reach far wider than Kingdom Come: Deliverance II, Red Dead Redemption 2 and GTA V: if a player thinks of something sensible to try, the world should have an answer.

Your job: write about 50 specific player questions from your lens. Each question is ONE concrete situation, phrased "Can Frank ..." or "What happens if Frank ...", naming who, where, when and how where it matters. Never generic ("is there crafting?", "can I customise my car?"). Good examples of the style:
- Can Frank knock out a constable from behind, take his radio and pocketbook, and listen in on the police?
- Can Frank rob a stranger while wearing a balaclava, and will the police then search the Hook for a man of his description?
- Can Frank set two people up to meet in the cafe so that they gossip about a third?
- Can Frank lock a passenger in the back of the cab and drive him somewhere he did not ask to go?
- Can Frank lie to the police in an interview, and do they check his story against what witnesses said?
- Can Frank overhear two people at a bus stop gossiping about him?
- Does the town remember when Frank does a good deed, like carrying an old man's shopping?

Rules: no alcohol, gambling or children in any question; don't name real brands; plain British English; no em-dashes. Vary situations widely within your lens; avoid the seven examples above and obvious near-copies. Do not research the game (it is unreleased); you may draw on your knowledge of other games and of 1990 Britain.

Output: write the questions to the file path you are given, one per line, each line starting with "- ". No numbering, no headings, nothing else. Then reply with just the count.
