## G25. Three routes through every job and beat, each with its own traces

Every job card and every spine beat names at least three routes: talk or menace, stealth or cunning, force. Each route is a set of the job's step states (GD-03 already lets any route meet a step), and each leaves its own traces: talk leaves people who heard him and what he promised; stealth leaves objects moved, doors forced and glimpses; force leaves bodies, marks and witnesses. The quiet routes are written first where the spine today has only force: the yard in week 6, week 7's one fight, and each rival's go at one of his men in week 5. Where the story needs noise (the gun fired at the Landing on every path) it is someone else's noise and Frank's part is read from what was seen. Steps that need a face (the sit-down, the inspector, Ellis reading the line back, Danny on the quay) say so in the Ledger before the week starts, and keep a quiet way through. Posts and rounds can be held through a go-between, who becomes the man the town knows (decision 33). The spine's crew aims get a solo route (decision 34).

## G26. Reputation is the town's reading of Frank, per person

Each person holds a reading of Frank on five words: feared, trusted, quiet, cruel, generous. It is built only from deeds that person holds (seen or told, by the existing memory and gossip rules) and from what the firm visibly does, never shown as a number. The telling already carries restraint and cruelty (GV-06); the reading gathers it per person. ("Reading" here is the town's view of Frank, not Frank's own summary of the signs in 3.1.5.) The reading decides what each route can do with that person: menace works on those who read him feared; trust opens confidences, credit, witnesses who speak for him and Sheila's book; quiet keeps him unsuspected and makes people confide; cruel makes people lie to please him and avoid him; generous lowers the price for the low-greed and invites asks from the high-greed. A threat never carried out, a lie found out, running from a fight, refusing a fight in front of watchers and silence to a rival's visit are all deeds that move the reading. Readings are per person, so a Frank can be feared on the docks and trusted on his street, and the town can disagree about him. They fade towards neutral with people who see nothing new for two game weeks, never with those who hold a deed first hand; there is no floor and no ceiling, but those he hurt never forget. Givers offer jobs by their reading of him; key scenes keep two or three variants of his lines by reading (decision 36); the story director prefers, among beats already ready, one that tests the reading. This replaces any notion of a skill tree: growth is position (7.7), knowledge of streets and people, taught skills (owed a design, C-263) and the reading.

## G27. The town adapts to how Frank plays; nothing dominates

Each route, resource and weapon costs more the more it is used, because the town learns. Fear hardens: each beating a person hears of makes them avoid Frank and tell him less truth, and those out of his reach rise to meet him. Owners who lose things unseen add a lock, a dog, a man sleeping in or a new hour. Three small unseen deeds on one street become a town question: unease, a constable walking by more, guesses. Many holders of a deed make silencing any one of them worth less. People act together by their ties (a drivers' walk-out, a street lookout, shops shut early), never as vigilantes (decision 37). Bribes make bought men who know; kindness plateaus. Simulated today (economy run, 100 seeds a style): no lever pays more than twice the middle one an hour (lift 339, tobacco 240, landing 200, round 182, fares 55 pounds an hour), squeezing shops at 40 pounds leaves Frank poorer by week 7 (224 against 785), and a named want is still out of reach at week 7 in every style. Routes are not yet in the run: add them and hold no route above twice the middle.

## G28. The whole arc paced and measured per style

The seven weeks are paced as a contract and measured per style, not only for the median player. An opening table for hours two to five gives one new freedom each hour. Each act's stages end in a change on the board at most every four real hours, and a job, its fallout and a new face fit a two-hour evening. No two weeks share their main kind of hands (the frame's weekly table already does this); collect-and-pay errands stay under a fifth of Act II. Each week has its own weather and light on the season's figures and a signature place. The hour budget (7.5) and the tension bar (ER-TENS-01) are checked for each style bot every week, with waiting and watching under 10% for quiet styles. Loading after a break offers Frank's summary of the signs (3.1.5) and the week's aim. A short opening is offered on later new games (decision 35). Target: at least 7 of 8 panel players reach an ending.

## G30. A second run is the same people in a freshly drawn week

People stay themselves on every save: their tempers (temper/tempers.json) and pasts are canon's (a session choice within canon, DECISIONS.md 25 Nov). Each new game draws small variation inside them: who is where at a given hour, which happening scenes, which fare people call, the weather on the season's figures, and which rival notices first. Calendar-fixed days are listed in the design (the display Saturday, Remembrance Sunday, the gate chained on Saturday 1 December, rent each Friday); everything else moves on states. Tests: GD-04's two-bot replay by week four; two week-six runs differ in at least twenty street things; four rival-strategy bots end in Decembers that differ in at least five named things; at least half the named differences of a replay come from the town's memory and talk.

## G31. Every run is accounted for, in the town and the close

What a run cost and gave is readable without a menu and told at the end. Every named person has a last state the close or the after-ending town can show. A story closed by a choice says so through a person when it closes. The Ledger's people page shows each person's state as Frank knows it and his own claims to each. The close (two to four minutes, up to five voices chosen by depth of tie) reads the state, never the route; when few hold his deeds first hand it is told in guesses and rumour (decision 38); a late clean-up is told by people who remember the early rackets; the style is recognised in the town's words, never an award. The walk-out is written as a full close. No checklist of missed content: people may mention what Frank never did.

## G32. Difficulty rises by stakes and circumstance, never by levels

No enemy scales with Frank. The run gets harder by stakes (money, things, ground, a person, his body and liberty, the firm, as the frame climbs) and by circumstance (numbers, places, who watches, the escalation dial, the rivals hardening by what they hold of him). The routes shift with the run: stealth easiest early, when nobody knows him; force hardest early, with no men; talk strongest late, with standing. Working alone means fewer who know and harder fights (simulated: careful one against one wins 73%, two against one 29%, with Ron 73%).
