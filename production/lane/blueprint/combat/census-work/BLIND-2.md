# LEDGER: combat, confrontation and stealth census (blind 2)

Independent list, written from design knowledge only. Each line is a feature the game must have or a question it must rule on.

## Feel and controls

- Y001 Combat entry: does a fight start as a mode with its own controls, or do the walking controls simply become fighting controls with no switch?
- Y002 Without lock-on, how is the target chosen: camera direction, stick direction, nearest threat, or a soft bias that the player can always override?
- Y003 Soft target bias must never swing Frank's punch to a bystander or crew member he was not facing.
- Y004 Directional attacks (KCD2 style zones) or a simpler light, heavy and grab set: rule on the depth of the input scheme.
- Y005 Mouse and keyboard as the first-class input, with full rebinding; a pad fully supported as the second.
- Y006 Input buffering window and cancel rules: which moves can be interrupted by a block or dodge, and how early.
- Y007 Hit-stop and camera shake per impact weight, tuned so a jab and a pipe blow feel distinct.
- Y008 Stamina as the only fight resource: what drains it (swinging, blocking, being hit, sprinting) and what Frank looks like when it is low.
- Y009 With no health bar, how does the player read Frank's state: posture, breathing, blood, limp, vision, sound?
- Y010 With no health bar, how does the player read an enemy's state: stagger, guard dropping, wobble, bleeding, voice?
- Y011 Holding a fight stance in public is itself a signal: townspeople react to raised fists before a punch is thrown.
- Y012 Turn speed and commitment: heavy blows lock facing, so flanking matters.
- Y013 Footwork and spacing: step in, step out, circle, with distance as a real tactic.
- Y014 Clinch and grapple layer: grabbing collars, shoving into walls, holding a man for a crew member.
- Y015 Context button rules so "grab" near a table, a wall or a railing does the obvious thing and nothing surprising.
- Y016 Difficulty is in enemy skill and numbers, never in damage sponges.

## Unarmed

- Y017 Unarmed is the core of the game's combat and must carry it alone for the first many hours.
- Y018 Block, parry (perfect block) and its reward: stagger, opening, or a counter.
- Y019 Dodge or slip: a short head movement and a back-step rather than a roll, to keep 1990 street realism.
- Y020 Guard break: a sustained guard can be broken by heavy blows or a grab.
- Y021 Feints: does the player have them, and do skilled enemies feint him?
- Y022 Headbutts, knees, elbows, kicks to the downed: which are in, and which mark Frank as a thug in the town's eyes?
- Y023 Kicking a man on the ground is a severe act socially and legally; rule on whether the player can do it at all.
- Y024 Knockdown and getting up: how long a downed man stays down, and how vulnerable he is.
- Y025 Knockouts from a clean blow to the jaw versus slow wearing down: both paths must exist.
- Y026 Fighting styles by background: the dockers brawl, the ex-boxer guards and jabs, the soldier grapples; enemies read as people.
- Y027 Frank's own style grows from what he does, not from a menu of moves bought with points.
- Y028 Pulling punches: a way to fight to subdue rather than to injure, with a real difference in outcome.
- Y029 Fair fight versus dirty fight: sand in the eyes, a cheap shot from behind, a kick between the legs, each with gossip consequences.
- Y030 Hand injuries: a man who punches a skull or a wall hurts his hand.

## Weapons and surroundings

- Y031 Melee weapon roster fit for 1990 Britain: pipes, bats, wrenches, bottles are out (no alcohol), so rule which glass and objects replace the classic bottle.
- Y032 Knives are common, serious and lethal; rule on how often they appear and how fast they kill.
- Y033 Every improvised weapon has a weight class driving speed, stamina cost and stagger.
- Y034 Weapons break, bend or slip from the hand; how long does a chair leg last?
- Y035 Disarms: the player can knock a weapon away or take it, and so can enemies.
- Y036 Dropped weapons stay in the world and can be picked up by anyone, including bystanders.
- Y037 Carrying a weapon openly versus hidden: who notices, and does a visible bat change how townspeople talk to Frank?
- Y038 Concealment by clothing: a long coat hides a bar, a T-shirt hides nothing.
- Y039 Environmental takedowns: walls, railings, bonnets, shop counters, shop windows, the quay edge.
- Y040 Throwing a man into the harbour: survivable, comic, lethal or barred? Rule on water.
- Y041 Throwing objects: ashtrays, bricks, tins, a dustbin lid; aim without a reticle?
- Y042 Doors as weapons: slamming a door on someone, holding a door shut.
- Y043 Breakable props: plate glass, crates, phone boxes, fruit stalls; who pays and who remembers?
- Y044 Vehicles as weapons: running a man down with a minicab; rule on it, given the firm's reputation and the police.
- Y045 Dock hazards: crane hooks, forklifts, container stacks, slippery quay stone in rain.
- Y046 Weather and surface: wet cobbles affect footing for everyone.
- Y047 Light as a tool: smashing a street lamp or switching off a club's lights to change a fight.
- Y048 Tobacco props in confrontation: a lit cigarette flicked in a face, a lighter as a threat; how far does it go?
- Y049 Tools of Frank's trade (a cab's tyre iron, a jack handle) as the natural first weapons.

## Guns

- Y050 Guns are rare and late: what story beat or reputation level first puts one in Frank's hands?
- Y051 Period-correct guns for 1990 Britain: sawn-off shotguns, old revolvers, a replica converted pistol; no modern hardware.
- Y052 After the 1988 Firearms Act, owning a gun is a serious crime; rule on what possession alone costs Frank.
- Y053 Ammunition is scarce and has to be bought through criminal contacts.
- Y054 Aiming with no lock-on: free aim, a reticle or none, and whether there is aim assist on a pad.
- Y055 A gunshot changes the whole town: everyone in earshot reacts, the police respond in force, and gossip spreads for days.
- Y056 Showing a gun without firing is the most common use: threats, robberies, stand-offs.
- Y057 Who else carries guns, and how does the game warn the player that a man is armed?
- Y058 Gun malfunction and misfire for old, badly kept weapons.
- Y059 Armed police response (ARVs) as a late, overwhelming answer to repeated gun use.
- Y060 Disposing of a gun after use: rivers, dock water, a fence, a contact who melts it down.
- Y061 Being shot is grave: what happens to Frank, given he cannot die and there are no healing items?
- Y062 Crew members carrying guns: does Frank allow it, and what happens when one fires without orders?

## Threats and talk

- Y063 Intimidation as the first and best tool: most confrontations should end in words, not blows.
- Y064 Live AI talk inside confrontations: the threatened man answers in his own words, in character, under the claim check.
- Y065 What Frank can say is bounded: the player types or speaks freely, but violence threatened in talk must map to a real game state.
- Y066 Threat credibility built from reputation, numbers, a weapon shown, and what the target has heard about Frank.
- Y067 Backing down: both sides can save face, and the game remembers who blinked.
- Y068 Escalation ladder: a look, a word, a shove, a fist, a weapon, a gun; each step visible and each step logged.
- Y069 De-escalation: Frank can calm a fight with words, payment, or an apology, and his crew follow his tone.
- Y070 Bribes mid-confrontation: cash offered to a bouncer, a witness or a constable.
- Y071 Bluffing: claiming a backing he does not have; the target may test it later.
- Y072 Lies in confrontation are remembered and can be checked against gossip.
- Y073 Shakedowns and protection collection as a repeated confrontation loop with shop owners who remember last week.
- Y074 Threatening someone's family when there are no children: rule on what threats to relatives are allowed.
- Y075 Menace without torture: the line between an interrogation by pressure and a torture scene, ruled once and held.
- Y076 Interrogation as conversation under threat, where the captive may lie, stall or tell a half-truth.
- Y077 Tempers: a hot-headed man swings before the talk finishes; a cold one waits; each townsperson's temper governs when talk turns to fists.
- Y078 Insults and provocation: the player can goad a man into throwing the first punch, which matters to witnesses and police.
- Y079 Who threw the first punch is tracked and told in gossip and witness statements.
- Y080 Abuse of the live AI: what happens when the player types something that breaks the period or the content rules?
- Y081 Talk must stay responsive in a confrontation: the Long Pause cannot leave a man frozen while a knife is out.

## Stealth and perception

- Y082 Perception model: sight cones, light, distance, movement speed, clothing, and sound, each one readable by the player.
- Y083 No HUD awareness meters, or a subtle one: rule on how the player knows he has been seen.
- Y084 Recognition versus detection: being seen is not the same as being known; a stranger may see Frank and not know him.
- Y085 Faces and memory: a witness who has met Frank before identifies him instantly; one who has not gives a description.
- Y086 Disguise in 1990 terms: a hat, a different coat, a work uniform, a hi-vis docker's vest; how far does it fool people?
- Y087 Social stealth: blending into a crowd, a queue, a dock shift, like Hitman.
- Y088 Trespass rules: which places are private, and does being caught there start a confrontation, a call to police, or a polite ejection?
- Y089 Sneaking speed and posture: a crouch looks suspicious in daylight on a busy street.
- Y090 Shadows and street lamps as the main cover at night.
- Y091 Noise from surfaces: gravel, broken glass, metal stairs, puddles.
- Y092 Dogs: guard dogs at the docks smell and bark; rule on whether Frank can hurt a dog (recommend not).
- Y093 Search behaviour: what guards do when they lose him, how long they look, and what they tell others afterwards.
- Y094 Suspicion persistence: a guard who was uneasy last night is more alert tonight.
- Y095 Distractions: a thrown coin, a phone ringing in a box, a car alarm, a cab horn.
- Y096 Lockpicking and break-in for a 1990 town: Yale locks, padlocks, crowbars, the noise each makes.
- Y097 Alarms: shop bells, wired alarms to the police station, and how long until a response.
- Y098 CCTV is rare in 1990 Britain but exists in some shops and at the docks; rule on it.
- Y099 The camera must not let the player see what Frank could not, or the game must accept and balance it.
- Y100 Hiding places: alleys, skips, a parked van, a cab's back seat, a crowded launderette.
- Y101 Tailing: following a target on foot or by cab without being noticed, a natural minicab activity.
- Y102 Eavesdropping on live talk as stealth gameplay: overheard words feed gossip and leads.

## Takedowns

- Y103 Non-lethal takedown from behind: a chokehold that takes time and can be resisted.
- Y104 Lethal takedown: is it in at all, and if so, how hard is it to do and live with?
- Y105 A strangled man must not die by accident when the player meant only to knock him out, or the risk must be made plain.
- Y106 Knock-out duration and waking: an unconscious man wakes, remembers, and tells someone.
- Y107 Tying up or gagging: are ropes and tape usable, and does a bound man stay bound?
- Y108 Takedown failure states: the target struggles free, shouts, or bites.
- Y109 Takedowns witnessed by others turn stealth into a fight or a flight instantly.
- Y110 A takedown on a much bigger man should be harder or fail.

## Groups and crew

- Y111 Group fights must avoid the queue of enemies taking turns, yet stay readable for the player.
- Y112 Enemy coordination: surrounding, grabbing Frank's arms for a mate, holding back the frightened ones.
- Y113 Morale: men flee when their leader falls or when a knife comes out; a gang is not a hive.
- Y114 Crew orders without a menu wheel that breaks period feel: a word, a gesture, a held key?
- Y115 Crew obey, hesitate or refuse according to their loyalty, temper and what Frank has asked of them before.
- Y116 Crew can be hurt, arrested or quit, but never die permanently if that would end the story; rule on crew death.
- Y117 Crew act on their own: a loyal man steps in when Frank is outnumbered.
- Y118 A crew member's violence is credited to Frank in gossip, and to the man himself with the police.
- Y119 Friendly hits: Frank can hit his own man by accident and he remembers it.
- Y120 Bystanders joining in: a docker defends his mate, a shopkeeper grabs a broom.
- Y121 Crowds forming to watch a street fight, and their effect on space and witnesses.
- Y122 Rival outfits fight each other without the player present, and the town hears about it.
- Y123 Ambushes on Frank: rivals choose time and place, often at the cab office or on a fare.
- Y124 Bodyguarding and escort jobs: protecting a passenger or a crew member.
- Y125 Hired muscle for a single job versus standing crew.

## Fleeing and chases

- Y126 Running away is always a real option and should often be the right one.
- Y127 Foot chases through alleys, back gardens and dock yards, with vaulting and climbing that are not parkour.
- Y128 Vehicle chases in minicabs and police cars on narrow 1990 streets, at believable speeds.
- Y129 Breaking line of sight versus being recognised: escaping a chase does not wipe the witness's memory.
- Y130 Hiding in a cab, switching cars, changing coat during a chase.
- Y131 Letting an enemy flee: he remembers the mercy, or he returns with friends.
- Y132 Catching a fleeing man: tackle, trip, or blocking with the car.

## Injuries, death and bodies

- Y133 Injury model without health bars: localised wounds (cut brow, broken nose, cracked ribs, sprained hand) that change animation and ability.
- Y134 Injuries heal with time and rest, with visits to a doctor or casualty, never with items.
- Y135 A hospital visit after a fight is itself a social event: questions asked, police informed for stab wounds.
- Y136 Struck-off doctors and back-room patch-ups as the criminal route to healing without questions.
- Y137 Visible injuries on Frank's face change how people talk to him for days.
- Y138 Blood and light gore: how much, where it stays, and how quickly it is cleaned from the street and from clothes.
- Y139 Bloodied clothes as evidence and as a social signal: a shirt must be changed.
- Y140 NPC death: when does it happen, and is it ever accidental from a single punch (the real one-punch death)?
- Y141 Death of a named townsperson changes the town permanently; rule on which characters can die.
- Y142 Story-essential characters: if they cannot die, how is that shown without breaking belief?
- Y143 Bodies persist and are found; who finds them, when, and what follows.
- Y144 Moving and hiding bodies: carrying, dragging, a car boot, the harbour; rule on how far this goes.
- Y145 An unconscious man and a dead one must look different up close to an attentive player.
- Y146 Funerals and grief: a death ripples into relatives, friends and rival vengeance.

## Evidence and police

- Y147 Evidence types for 1990: witnesses, fingerprints, blood, dropped items, tyre marks, the cab's plate; no DNA routine yet.
- Y148 Police are people too: named constables with memory and temper, some bent, some honest.
- Y149 Police response time and strength scaled to crime and place: a beat bobby, a panda car, a van of officers.
- Y150 Arrest: being taken in is a game state, not a fail; a night in the cells, an interview, a solicitor.
- Y151 Police interviews as live-talk scenes where Frank can lie, stay silent or bargain.
- Y152 Charges and court: what follows a serious charge, and how a trial or remand is shown without ending the game.
- Y153 Prison time as a jump in time with consequences in the town, or an episode, rather than a game over.
- Y154 Bribing police: who takes money, who reports it, and the risk of an internal inquiry.
- Y155 Witness intimidation and paying for silence as their own risky confrontations.
- Y156 Grasses: crew or townspeople who inform; discovering and dealing with them without torture.
- Y157 Police raids on Frank's businesses as a consequence of heat.
- Y158 Heat that decays slowly and is local to streets and people, not a global star count.
- Y159 Alibis: crew or a fare log can vouch for Frank; the cab firm's books as both alibi and evidence.

## The town's reaction

- Y160 Gossip carries every fight: who won, who started it, how bad, with distortion as it spreads.
- Y161 Reputation splits into feared, respected, liked and despised, each opening and closing different doors.
- Y162 Shops close their doors, prices change and fares dry up after violence nearby.
- Y163 Victims and their families hold grudges and may seek revenge years later in game time.
- Y164 Townspeople call the police, intervene, film nothing (no phones), or pretend not to see, by character.
- Y165 A fight in a church, a hospital or a café carries heavier social cost than one on the docks at night.
- Y166 The town notices patterns: a man who beats a shopkeeper weekly becomes known for it.

## Losing

- Y167 Losing a fight is not death: Frank wakes up somewhere, robbed, hurt, or dumped, and the world moves on.
- Y168 Losses have story weight: what is taken (money, a car, respect), and who saw.
- Y169 Rivals must not be able to beat Frank into an unwinnable spiral; rule on catch-up and recovery.
- Y170 A beaten Frank can be helped by friends, found by crew or picked up by a passing cab driver of his own firm.
- Y171 Mission failure from a lost fight branches the story instead of restarting it.

## Growth and accessibility

- Y172 Learning by doing: Frank's skill in fists, blades, stealth and talk grows with use, like KCD2.
- Y173 Trainers in town: an old boxer at a gym, a docker who teaches dirty tricks.
- Y174 Physical fitness and age: Frank is not a young man; rule on his starting body and its limits.
- Y175 Accessibility: hold-instead-of-tap options, slower timing windows, clear audio cues for blocks, subtitles for every bark and threat.
- Y176 Colour-blind-safe cues for injury and danger, since there are no bars to read.
- Y177 An option to reduce gore and screen shake without changing outcomes.
- Y178 A practice place (a gym, a yard) to learn controls without social cost.

## Sound, camera and animation

- Y179 Impact sound design by surface and body part, close-miked and ugly, not cinematic whooshes.
- Y180 Crowd and street sound reacting to violence: a scream, a shop door slammed, a silence.
- Y181 Camera in close quarters: alleys, cabs, narrow stairwells, small rooms; the camera must never hide the threat behind a wall.
- Y182 Group fight camera framing that keeps flankers on screen without lock-on.
- Y183 Motion matching or equivalent for fluid movement and reactions at AAA grade.
- Y184 Hit reactions that vary by force and direction, with physical blend for stumbling into objects.
- Y185 Facial animation in fights: pain, fear and anger on MetaHuman faces, which must not break.
- Y186 Two-person sync animations for grabs, holds and takedowns that hold up on uneven ground.

## The odd things a curious player tries

- Y187 Punching a crew member, a fare, a police officer, a priest or the shopkeeper who just served him.
- Y188 Attacking someone mid-conversation, while the live AI is still answering.
- Y189 Starting a fight inside the cab office with his own staff watching.
- Y190 Stacking crates to reach a roof, then jumping off it.
- Y191 Driving a cab off the quay into the harbour.
- Y192 Following a man home after a fight and waiting outside his door.
- Y193 Hitting the same man every day to see if he breaks or fights back.
- Y194 Locking someone in a cupboard, a car boot or a cold store.
- Y195 Picking a fight with the toughest man in town on the first day.
- Y196 Wearing a police uniform taken from an unconscious constable.
- Y197 Trying to buy a gun from the first suspicious stranger he meets.
- Y198 Setting fire to a rival's business; rule on arson.
- Y199 Asking a townsperson in live talk to fight someone for him.
- Y200 Standing still and doing nothing while being attacked.
