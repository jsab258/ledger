# Steam reviews in advance (25 Nov 2026)

The five most-helpful positive and five most-helpful negative reviews LEDGER would likely get at launch if it shipped as the blueprint stands today, written in a player's plain voice. Invented players; every claim in them traced to the blueprint (part numbers in brackets after each, for us, not part of the review). Used to see the game from the outside, never published.

## Recommended

1. 31.4 hrs on record
"Robbed the grocer on day two. Walked in the next morning in the same coat and she just looked at me and wouldn't take my money. Changed coats and went back and she said 'have you been in before?' I sat there for a minute. No game has made me feel watched like this. There is no wanted meter, there's a woman walking to a phone box and you have to decide what to do about her."
(2.2.5, 2.3.2, 2.5.1)

2. 22.0 hrs on record
"The best part is the book. When you do something and nobody sees, Frank writes 'Nobody saw.' Then the cleaner turns up at six and it gets crossed out. I started planning jobs around who walks where at what time. The night copper tries the door every seven minutes. Learn it."
(6.1.3, 2.5.5, 2.6.1)

3. 18.7 hrs on record
"I typed 'here's a tenner, forget it' to a witness and she took it. A week later she reminded me I'd paid her and wanted nothing more to do with me. Tried to sweet-talk her into lying for me, reloaded three times, she would not budge. The AI talk is quick and they only know what they've seen. It's the first time AI characters felt like people instead of a chatbot."
(RT-14, 2.5.2, 3.6.1, 8.13)

4. 40.2 hrs on record
"Small map. Doesn't matter. I know every person on Quay Street, where they live, when the lights go off. A man I put in the dock never turned up again and his landlady kept asking everyone, including me. Felt sick. Then Ellis came round. Proper slow-burn crime story, very British, great 1990 detail (H plates, the clocks going back, sodium lamps)."
(2.16, 2.6.5, 4.7, FIFTY 47, 48)

5. 26.9 hrs on record
"No health bars, no minimap, no stats. You read a fight from how a bloke's standing. You find places from directions like 'past the chapel, second left'. Lost a fight and woke up in a woman's kitchen with a cup of tea and the two blokes who robbed me were named and I found them a week later. KCD2 fans, this is your crime game."
(1.3.7, 6.9, 1.3.9, RT2-13)

## Not recommended

1. 6.2 hrs on record
"It's one street and a dock. You can't carjack, can't swim, can't jump, guns barely exist. I wanted a crime sandbox and got a minicab office simulator where I worry about the rent. Clever ideas, but I ran out of things to do in an evening."
(map 0.25 square km, 1.2.10, canon, BLUEPRINT "Out" list)

2. 12.8 hrs on record
"Everyone remembers what you did, but the people chatting on the street never mention it. Two blokes on a bench talking about the weather while I'd half-killed someone outside the chippy the night before. Breaks it."
(BLIND 9, FIFTY 16: the gap SM-05 closes; written as if it shipped open)

3. 9.5 hrs on record
"The AI voices are fast most of the time and then someone takes five seconds and you get a written line instead. And once you go offline or your allowance runs out it's brush-offs. I paid for the game, why is talk metered?"
(3.6.1, R10, Q14: the allowance per copy; the measured long tail)

4. 15.1 hrs on record
"Followed a random woman from the bus stop to see where she lives and she walked round the corner and disappeared. Named characters have lives, the crowd doesn't. For a game that sells itself on a living town, that's the first thing I tested."
(BLIND 19, FIFTY 49: written as if shipped open)

5. 20.3 hrs on record
"Tried to play it straight as an honest cab firm. You hit a ceiling (rung 2) and the story just sort of stops pushing. Either let me play honest or tell me upfront it's a crime game."
(2.12.2 honest firm stops at rung 2, ruled; the Straight Life ending exists, but no honest story path: the CONTENT gap new in this job)

## What the ten say

- Positives cluster on what is designed and LEDGER's own: memory, the walk to the phone box, the Ledger, talk that holds, 1990 detail.
- Negatives 1 and 3 are accepted costs (scale, out-list; a metered service) or a pitch problem; 2 and 4 are build gaps already found by the fifty job; 5 is a content gap new here. Close those three and the most-helpful negative becomes number 1 (size), which LEDGER can live with.
