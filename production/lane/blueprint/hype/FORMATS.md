# The hype formats, and LEDGER's version of each (25 Nov 2026)

What a YouTube or Instagram creator would film if LEDGER came out with the blueprint as it stands on lane-2026-11 today. Built on fifty/ (the 50 things, the outside AI's 50) and what-if/ (216 player questions); it does not repeat them, it points to them by number (FIFTY n, BLIND n, Q n).

## How this was found, and its limits

- Formats came from written sources only (articles, Reddit and Steam write-ups, press coverage of clips), searched on 25 Nov by a Sonnet helper and this session. Notes and 37 source links: research/formats-research.md. No video was watched or downloaded.
- No written source shows view counts, so the ranking below is judgement (how often the press repeats a format, how many games it fits), not measured. Two expected formats, the "GTA killer" scorecard and the 15-second "POV" reel, were not found in written sources at all; they are kept because the brief expects them and they are cheap to check by searching YouTube and TikTok from the PC.
- Comparisons name GTA (V, and VI only by what Rockstar has shown in public), RDR2 and KCD2. KCD2 facts are from press coverage (Gfinity, PC Gamer, GamesRadar, Vice, OpenCritic), listed in the research notes. LEDGER is judged as designed, not built: nothing below is in a packaged game yet.
- Status of each segment: designed (rules and numbers in the blueprint), partly, missing. BUILD or CONTENT follows his cut-off of 24 Nov (advisor 373, the 23:34 line): BUILD changes how the game is built and must close before the one-shot (to Monday's playthrough and the town-fabric pass); CONTENT goes to the loops.

## The ten formats kept, by estimated reach

1. "This game remembers what you did" (NPCs react and remember): KCD2's biggest clip type.
2. "Insane details" and "things you didn't know you could do".
3. "I tried to break the NPCs" (punch, insult, steal, follow, come back next day).
4. "AI NPCs talk back" and "I tried to break the AI".
5. Police systems compared.
6. "Most realistic RPG ever" first-hour reaction and review.
7. "Is this the GTA killer?" scorecard (unconfirmed in writing; kept).
8. The no-kill run (KCD2's "Medieval Batman": zero kills, 535 knockouts, picked up by PC Gamer).
9. "I followed one NPC for a whole day" (in practice a clip moment: KCD2's people walking out of Kuttenberg at night, written up by Vice as "bug or design?").
10. The 15-second reel and the comment section (see REELS.md and SCEPTICS.md).

Trailer-reaction videos have the highest reach of all, but they react to a trailer; that is PITCH.md's business, not the blueprint's.

## 1. "This game remembers what you did"

The KCD2 template (Gfinity, PC Gamer): commit a crime, think nobody saw, skip time, the consequence lands later (an arrest days on, a widow who knows her late husband's hat on your head), cut to the creator's face.

LEDGER's segments:

Against the three: KCD2 is the template and does the delayed arrest and the recognised stolen item; LEDGER matches both and goes further in three ways a clip can show: the walk to the phone box (FIFTY 3), the belief line on screen saying what Frank now believes, and the plain rule that nobody knows what they did not see (KCD2's coverage includes guards "pinning a bath-house attack on the player with no witness", which players called unfair). RDR2 has bounty, witnesses who ride off to report and the bandana; it forgets faster and never says so to your face. GTA V has almost none of this; GTA VI's public footage shows police noting outfit and vehicle (FIFTY 1). Verdict: LEDGER's strongest format; wins against all three if the build delivers the timing.

The decisive difference from the AI demos people mock: in LEDGER the rules decide every outcome and the language model only words it, so "flatter him to max friendship" does not work. That is the clip.

How to read it: LEDGER is not a GTA killer and should never say so. Scale is about 0.25 square km (the Hook and its docks, 620 by 400 m), which is hundreds of times smaller than GTA's or RDR2's maps (their sizes not checked here). It wins clearly on police and people; it is level on setting and talk; it loses on scale, things to do, and probably story until the key scenes reach their bar. Against KCD2, the fairest comparison, LEDGER matches the core (witnesses, crime noticed and reported, a town that judges you) and goes past it on memory and on knowing only what was seen; KCD2 wins on size, combat depth, writing and finish.

## Where LEDGER wins and loses, overall

- Wins: memory that is permanent and said to your face; nobody knows what they did not see; the walk to the phone box; police by statement, not stars; the belief line; live talk that cannot be gamed; 1990 detail.
- Loses: scale; things to do; spectacle (chases, police, guns); the first minute of chaos that makes GTA clips funny; graphics unproven; story not yet at its bar.
- Level: setting, talk, combat weight.
