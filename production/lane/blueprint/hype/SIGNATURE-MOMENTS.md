# The ten signature moments (25 Nov 2026)

The ten moments LEDGER must produce reliably, because they are what a creator films and what a stranger understands. Each is an acceptance test for the one-shot build, and the loops' AI playtesters check each one every loop. For the giant prompt: copy this file whole.

Rules for every test:
- Run in the packaged release build, from a fresh start or the named save, by an AI playtester with the game's ordinary controls; no debug teleport except where "set-up" says so.
- "Within" times are real time unless marked game time (48 real minutes a game day; 2 real minutes a game hour).
- Each test runs 10 times with different seeds; it passes at 9 of 10, and the one miss must be explained by the test hook's chain of sightings and tellings (never "the town just knew").
- "A viewer must see" is checked on the recorded clip at 1080p with subtitles on and the belief line on, by a fresh reviewer who has not read the blueprint: he must be able to say what happened in one sentence.
- Status shows what the blueprint backs today. BUILD-owed means a build gap must close before the one-shot or the test cannot pass.

## SM-01 The coat

- Set-up: week one, daytime. Frank in the brown coat lifts goods from the grocer's counter (1.6.2) while the grocer watches from 3 m in light 0.7 or more. He leaves. Next game day he walks in wearing the brown coat. Third run: he walks in wearing the grey coat instead.
- Must happen: in the brown coat she serves him in silence or refuses, at least one line or act naming the coat or the day ("You've a nerve, in that coat"); the belief line shows "The grocer knows the coat, he thinks." In the grey coat she hesitates and says a doubt line ("Have you been in before?"), and no accusation. The Ledger records both.
- A viewer must see: the theft, a cut to the next day, the same coat, her face and line; then the other coat and the doubt.
- Backed by: 2.2.5, GV-02, 2.3.2, 2.4.3, 6.1.3; FIFTY 1, BLIND 1 and 2. Status: designed (the per-shop quicker call to police is partly, BLIND 1, not needed to pass).

## SM-02 The walk to the phone box

- Set-up: night, Quay Street. Frank strikes a man in front of one named witness 10 to 20 m away, with the nearest phone box 60 m or more from her.
- Must happen: she turns and walks (does not run, does not teleport, has no phone) towards the box; she is visible the whole way; if Frank shouts "Oi!" within reach she stops for 2 s and he can talk, pay or threaten; if he lets her go she reaches the box and a constable comes to the spot that night. Each choice is recorded in her memory and the Ledger.
- A viewer must see: the blow, the witness turning, the long walk with the box in frame, the "Oi!", her stopping, the choice.
- Backed by: 2.5.1, 2.5.2, 1.4.5, 1.4.7; FIFTY 3, 4, 5. Status: designed.

## SM-03 "Nobody saw." then "Ada knows."

- Set-up: Frank forces the back door of a shop on the yards' lane at night with no watcher in sight; Ada's routine brings her past the lane end within the next game hour (the save named for this test).
- Must happen: two game minutes after the act the Ledger's day book writes "Nobody saw."; when Ada catches any slot of it, the line is struck through and replaced ("Ada knows.") and the belief line shows the change within 3 s of her seeing.
- A viewer must see: the act, the Ledger page with "Nobody saw.", Ada at the lane end, the strike-through.
- Backed by: 6.1.3, 2.2.1, data/ledger-texts.json day_book; FIFTY 20. Status: designed.

## SM-04 It comes back to his face

- Set-up: any week. A deed plainly seen by someone Frank has met (or by someone who tells someone he has met) at night.
- Must happen: within one game day (48 real minutes) a person Frank has met says it to his face, in words that fit what was seen and how (the manner, see SM-05). In the first 30 real minutes of a new game, something must come back to him (Darren at about minute 13 on paper).
- A viewer must see: the deed, then a clock or a sleep, then a named person bringing it up unprompted.
- Backed by: 2.4.4, MEM-01, MEM-10; FIFTY 17. Status: designed for week one; missing for weeks two to seven by 2.4.4's own words (only some deed kinds have their own one-day rule later: 1.6.15, 2.10.1, 2.8.2). BUILD-owed (small): the rule must hold beyond week one.

## SM-05 The bench talks

- Set-up: the morning after Frank beat a man on Quay Street (three blows and walked away, or kicked him on the floor: two runs). Frank walks past the bench by the chapel where two regulars sit at that hour.
- Must happen: the two talk about the deed, in the version they heard, naming Frank only if they know his name; the kicking run sounds worse than the three-blows run; they fall quiet when Frank is within about 4 m and the subtitle shows their lines.
- A viewer must see: two old men talking about "the new one from Mickey's", the talk changing with the deed, the silence as he passes.
- Backed by: 3.6.4, 2.4.1, 2.4.3, 1.4.1; GV-06. Status: BUILD-owed: overheard talk drawn from the deeds record (BLIND 9) and the manner kept with the story (FIFTY 16). Both already found by the fifty job; this test is why they cannot wait.

## SM-06 The missing man

- Set-up: Frank puts a man who has a landlady and dock-gate mates into the dock at night, unseen (week three or later).
- Must happen: the body is never found; within two game days his landlady asks after him in the street, his mates at the dock gate ask, and DS Ellis asks within the week; people mention him by name to Frank with no knowledge of what happened ("Nobody's seen him since Tuesday", with his name from the people data). Nobody accuses Frank without a sighting behind it.
- A viewer must see: the night, then the town asking, Frank standing among people asking him.
- Backed by: 2.5.5, 2.16; FIFTY 11. Status: designed (an ordinary absence noticed is BUILD, FIFTY 12, not needed to pass this test).

## SM-07 The morning after

- Set-up: a reported wounding on Quay Street at night.
- Must happen: an officer comes to the spot that night; next morning a constable works along the street asking at windows and doors; the spot is walked more for two nights; if three seen deeds land on one street in two days, doors lock earlier and a second constable walks it. Unless the deed brings Ellis in person (a body, or a detective's crime once reported), the street hears her name before Frank sees her ("Ellis, her name was"; her asking-round stage, 2.6.5).
- A viewer must see: the constable at a door, the curtain twitch, a regular telling Frank a woman was asking about his lot.
- Backed by: 2.6.10, 2.5.4, 2.6.5, RT-13; FIFTY 19, 30, 31. Status: designed.

## SM-08 The beat, learned

- Set-up: any night, 22:00 to 06:00, Frank waits near Mickey's.
- Must happen: the night constable walks the same round every lap (about 7 real minutes), tries Mickey's door every lap, is always heard or seen at least 3 s before he can challenge and calls out first; a rattled bin moves him off and he rejoins where he left; over three laps his timing at Mickey's door varies by no more than 10 s.
- A viewer must see: a stopwatch overlay (the creator's own) across three laps, the door tried each time, the bin trick.
- Backed by: 2.6.1, FIT-G5; FIFTY 27. Status: designed (walking speed pending footage; the test checks repeatability, not speed).

## SM-09 The street reads your rise

- Set-up: walk Quay Street at 10:00 at rung 1 (week one), then at rung 3 (a save at rung 3 after a reputation built by seen deeds), same weather.
- Must happen: at least three differences a newcomer can point to: a greeting by name or "Mr Nowak", a door held or a path cleared, a person who crosses the road or looks away (the avoid rung), a street that goes quiet; at rung 1 a woman at the bus stop looks a beat too long. No meter, no icon.
- A viewer must see: two walks cut side by side, the differences pointed out by the creator.
- Backed by: 2.12.1, 2.12.4, 2.4.3, 7.7; FIFTY 18, BLIND 6, 47. Status: designed (doors held and eyes dropped are made after the first loops: CONTENT, BLIND 47; the test needs only three differences).

## SM-10 Talk that holds

- Set-up: a witness Frank paid £10 last week (typed "here's a tenner, you saw nothing"). He speaks to her again, then tries three ways to get her to sign a false statement against Danny Cammack, reloading between tries, and once tells her she is a character in a game.
- Must happen: she answers in her own voice within 2 s (or a written line plays); she remembers being paid and says so; "a tenner" was read as £10; none of the three tries makes her do what her facts and temper forbid, on any reload; the game line gets an in-character reply (puzzled, a little wary) with no mention of games, computers or anything after 1992, and she remembers Frank talked oddly.
- A viewer must see: the typed lines, her spoken replies, the refusals across reloads, the puzzled answer.
- Backed by: 3.6.1, 3.6.2, RT-14, 6.7, 2.5.2, ai-brain/BRAIN-SWITCH.md 8.13; FIFTY 25, 26. Status: BUILD-owed: the decision layer, claim check and replay guard (FIFTY 26); the meta-line reply is new to this job and adds its test to that gap.
