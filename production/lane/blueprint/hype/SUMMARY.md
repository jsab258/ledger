# The hype-video test: summary (25 Nov 2026, his pick A of 23:52)

## For Jafar, short

Verdicts:
- Against GTA: not a GTA killer and should never say so; it loses on size, things to do and spectacle, and wins clearly on police and on people who remember.
- Against RDR2: level on setting and talk, behind on size, texture and story; ahead on consequence (memory that is permanent and said to your face).
- Against KCD2: the fairest match; level at the core (witnesses, crime reported, a town that judges you) and ahead on two things clips can show: the walk to the phone box, and nobody knowing what they did not see. KCD2 wins on size, combat depth, writing and finish.

Where LEDGER wins: "this game remembers what you did" videos, police comparisons ("most believable", not "best chases"), talk that cannot be tricked, 1990 detail, a clean screen that makes good short clips. Where it loses: "things to do" videos, "break the NPCs" comedy (no car-jacking, no instant chaos), size, graphics not yet proven, a story not yet at its bar.

Of 59 things a creator would film, the design fully backs 37, partly 13, is missing 6, and leaves out 3 on purpose. Of the 20 best 15-second clips, a stranger follows 11 with just the game's subtitles, 8 with the game's other words on screen (the line at the bottom saying what Frank now believes, or Frank's book), 1 needs the creator's caption.

The ten moments the game must always deliver (written as tests the big build must pass and the AI players check every round):
1. The coat: rob her in the brown coat, she knows the coat next day; a new coat leaves her doubting the face.
2. The walk to the phone box: the witness walks, Frank can shout "Oi!", pay, threaten or let her go.
3. Frank's book writes "Nobody saw.", then crosses it out: "Ada knows."
4. What he did is said to his face within a game day (written for week one only so far).
5. Two old men on a bench talk about what Frank did, worse if he kicked the man on the floor (not yet designed).
6. The missing man: his landlady, his mates and Ellis ask after him.
7. The morning after: a constable knocks at the doors, the street hears Ellis's name.
8. The night constable's round, the same every lap, learnable to the minute.
9. The street shows his rise: three visible differences between his first week and later.
10. Talk that holds: the paid witness remembers, nothing impossible however often you reload, a puzzled answer to "you're in a game" (the check behind it not yet built).
Seven work on paper today; 4, 5 and 10 need pieces built first.

New things that change how the game is built (all small, not found by last night's jobs):
- An owner knows his stolen goods wherever he sees them, on Frank's back too, not only in a shop window.
- A dropped coin is something people see, pocket or hand back, and remember.
- An ordinary person notices being followed for a long time.
- One more test for the talk check already owed: the in-character answer to "you're in a game".
The big pieces these videos lean on were already found last night: street talk about Frank's deeds, the talk check, the town's memory beyond week one, and passers-by who go somewhere and never pop in or out of sight.

Your questions:
- Q1 Pulling a driver out of a stopped car (GTA's biggest clip habit; today only parked, empty cars can be taken): B (recommended) keep it out, it fits a cab firm badly and invites the rampage comparison; A allow it, loud, always reported.
- Q2 A run with no crime already reaches the quiet ending (no business, one friendship) but has no story of its own on the way: B (recommended) leave it for release; A write a short honest-firm story later.
