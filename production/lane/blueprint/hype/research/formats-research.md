# Hype video formats: crime games, living-town RPGs, AI-NPC games (2023 to 2026)

Dated 2026-11-25. Method: written sources only, via standard web search plus fetches of URLs from those results. No video was watched or downloaded. No GTA VI story details recorded. Mafia series not used.

## Read this first: limits of the evidence

- The search tool returns articles, not YouTube/TikTok/Instagram pages. **No result showed a per-video view count.** Every "views" field below is "unknown (not shown in sources)". No number has been invented.
- Reach ranking is therefore a judgement from (a) how often press and forums re-report the format, (b) how many different games it is applied to, (c) platform fit. It is not measured. Treat it as a hypothesis to check on this PC with YouTube/TikTok search sorted by view count.
- Comment-section content below is mostly inferred from press and Reddit/Steam write-ups of the reaction, not from reading comment sections. It is labelled "reported" where a source says it, "likely" where it is inference.
- Format names that could not be confirmed with a named video are marked UNCONFIRMED.

## Formats: confirmed, partly confirmed, rejected

### 1. "NPCs react/remember" clip (KCD2 crime-and-memory moments)  [CONFIRMED, via press reposts of player clips]
- Examples (all as reported by press; channel/view count unknown):
  - Reddit posts by @Jattmogger and others, written up by Gfinity: widow recognises stolen gear on the player, horse theft leads to arrest three days later, guards pin a bath-house attack on the player with no witness.
  - PC Gamer first-person write-up: "branded a criminal and hours later still shunned by society".
- Recurring segments: commit crime; leave (or think you left) no witnesses; time-skip; the consequence lands later (arrest, accusation, someone recognising an item); cut to the player's reaction.
- Comments, reported/likely: "this is what Skyrim should have been"; "Bethesda could never"; "I reloaded my save instantly"; players trading their own worst-consequence stories.
- Used for: KCD2 above all; Cyberpunk and RDR2 as the "less reactive" foil.

### 2. "Insane details / things you didn't know" (KCD2 smell, dirt, beggars, social class)  [CONFIRMED]
- Examples: GamesRadar on a clip of NPCs scavenging dropped money by social class (nobles ignore cheap items, beggars pick them up); PC Gamer and GamesRadar on the body-odour system. Channel and views unknown.
- Segments: drop coins in a crowd; get filthy then talk to a noble; stealth with and without smell; "the dev explains".
- Comments, likely: "nobody asked for this level of detail and I love it"; "Rockstar-level detail but for peasants".
- Used for: RDR2 (long-running "details" series), GTA V, KCD2, Cyberpunk (where it often flips to "broken" details).

### 3. "Most realistic RPG ever / Skyrim killer" reaction and review  [CONFIRMED as a theme; no single video identified]
- Examples: mein-mmo coverage of an MMORPG YouTuber (TheLazyPeon) so impressed by KCD2 he questioned his channel's direction; Recapio digest of a long "brutally good" review by Kelski. Views unknown.
- Segments: first-hour reaction; comparison with Skyrim/Oblivion; realism checklist (sleep, hunger, dirt, combat skill).
- Comments, likely: "I have 300 hours in"; "finally an RPG that respects me"; sceptics: "realism is just chores", "saves are limited".
- Used for: KCD2, Oblivion Remastered, RDR2.

### 4. Police and crime systems compared  [PARTLY CONFIRMED]
- Evidence is mostly GTA V versus Cyberpunk 2077 "NPC, police, physics, details" comparisons, heavily covered by press in the 2020 to 2021 launch period (Gamerant, GamingBible, Gamepressure, EssentiallySports on xQc revisiting such a video). Few 2023 to 2026 examples surfaced; KCD2 is now the new "deep" entry in the same template. Views unknown.
- Segments: wanted level, helicopter, escape, hide in a building, bribe/pay fines, jail/death consequence; side-by-side split screen.
- Comments, reported/likely: "GTA V is ten years old and still beats it"; "Cyberpunk cops spawn behind you"; fans of each game defend theirs.
- Used for: GTA V, RDR2, Cyberpunk, KCD2, Saints Row, Watch Dogs.

### 5. "AI NPCs talk back" demo / "I told the AI NPC to ..."  [CONFIRMED]
- Examples: Inverse "I Told an AI-Powered NPC to Poison All the Other NPCs" (Inworld/NVIDIA ACE); PC Gamer "spoke to an Nvidia NPC about his ramen"; Skyrim Mantella and the 2023 ChatGPT-in-Skyrim-VR mods (PC Gamer, Windows Central, UploadVR, Road to VR). Views unknown.
- Segments: ask the NPC about the world; ask it something absurd; ask it to do a task (follow, fetch); break character; show it remembering earlier chat.
- Comments, reported: see sceptic section. Supportive: "this is the future of RPGs", "Skyrim finally has conversation".
- Used for: Skyrim (Mantella), Fallout 4, Where Winds Meet, NVIDIA ACE titles, inZOI.

### 6. "I tried to break the AI NPC" (jailbreak, gaslight, reward-gaming)  [CONFIRMED]
- Example: All Things How write-up of Where Winds Meet players (Reddit user MisterZan25 told a guard a fictional child had died, and the guard grieved; insults ending chats and starting fights; empty promises raising reputation). Views unknown.
- Segments: tell the NPC it is a game; invent a backstory; flatter it to max friendship; push to uncomfortable topics; "they patched it".
- Comments, reported/likely: "it believes anything"; "the writers did all that work for this?"; "unsettling".
- Used for: AI-NPC games only. The non-AI version ("punch, steal, insult, come back next day") is format 1 and 8.

### 7. "I killed/messed with every NPC" sandbox carnage  [CONFIRMED, older and evergreen]
- Examples: GamingBible on a Cyberpunk player killing NPCs and on Fallout 3 and New Vegas wipe-outs; Oficina da Net on a GTA V kill-everyone experiment; TweakTown on GTA players finding creative NPC kills. Views unknown.
- Segments: kill all; see what the game does when the world empties; gets stuck.
- Comments, likely: "who needed that NPC?"; "game is unbeatable now".
- Used for: GTA V, Cyberpunk, Fallout, Skyrim, RDR2.

### 8. Modder "NPCs with memories / guilt" (RDR2)  [CONFIRMED]
- Example: Blurbs, RDR2 mod giving each killed NPC a memory played back, with Twitch-chat-written lines read by text-to-speech (Kotaku, GamesRadar). Views unknown.
- Segments: kill; memory plays; chat-written absurd lines; creator laughing.
- Comments, reported: pure comedy; original intent was guilt, chat made it silly.
- Used for: RDR2, Skyrim, GTA V mods.

### 9. Pacifist / no-kill challenge run  [CONFIRMED]
- Examples: KCD2 "Medieval Batman" zero kills and 535 knockouts (PC Gamer); another zero-kill player, 472 knockouts and 1,741 blocked strikes (GamesRadar, Gamepressure, ITC). Channel/views unknown; reported by press so it travelled.
- Segments: recap stats, hardest fights without killing, how story gates were handled, fast-forward.
- Comments, likely: "respect"; "how did you do [boss]?"; "now do it at hardest difficulty".
- Used for: KCD2, Dishonored-style games, GTA V (no-wanted-level or no-kill runs, UNCONFIRMED for 2023+).

- Used for: KCD2, RDR2, Skyrim, Cyberpunk (as a debunk).

## KCD2 crime, witness, reputation mechanics as covered

- Witnesses need not see the whole crime; a flight on a stolen horse can be tied to a separate assault. Guards act on NPC information and may arrest days later. Source: Gfinity.
- NPCs recognise stolen gear worn by the player (carpenter's widow case). Source: Gfinity.
- Charges stack into fines far above what the player holds; speech checks can fail; minor acts (no torch at night, loitering after tavern closing) can be fined. Source: PC Gamer.
- Being branded a criminal: a visible debuff that halves reputation gain, makes townspeople shun the player, merchants refuse trade, and warns the next serious crime may mean execution. Source: PC Gamer.
- Smell and dirt: grime gives a smell debuff that alerts nearby NPCs, lowers dialogue and Charisma effects, and counters the "Natural Camouflage" stealth perk. Source: PC Gamer, GamesRadar.
- Social class affects behaviour: nobles ignore cheap dropped items, beggars take them. Source: GamesRadar.
- NPC schedules: independent lives (GDC coverage, Digg); counter-example of a nightly mass exit from Kuttenberg that looks like NPCs with no beds (Vice).
- The "Skyrim trick" clip (dropping items to see NPC reactions): Giga.de, German.

## Sceptic comment types

"GTA killer" / indie crime hype (evidence thin; mostly inferred, flagged as such):
- "Looks good in a trailer, show gameplay" (reported in Unrecord coverage, where "is this real or fake" and doubts were the main thread).
- "Tech demo / vertical slice, will never ship".
- "Fifteen years of Rockstar polish cannot be matched by a small team."
- "It's a clone of X with a graphics filter."
- "Only GTA beats GTA", used to shut the thread down.
- Trailer cynicism: "AI slop" accusations on fan trailers (games.gg).

AI-NPC demos (reported in articles):
- "Robotic voice, stilted delivery, no personality" (Engadget).
- "Canned like a phone tree; can't do basic tasks" (Engadget on the PUBG ally).
- "Just a tech demo / scripted" and "an immersive sim already does this better" (PC Gamer).
- "Looks like ordinary NPC AI with a label" (GamesRadar on inZOI).
- "AI will make the internet more annoying"; fatigue with AI content (GamesRadar).
- "It believes anything; rewards can be gamed; hallucinated backstory; breaks the period" (All Things How).
- "Takes work from writers", environmental and labour objections; "makes me feel uncomfortable" (SI.com, All Things How).

## Notes for the project

- The strongest reach pattern is not the AI claim but the visible consequence: act, wait, be recognised. This matches the Ledger's pillars and is filmable without any AI.
- AI-NPC sceptic comments are the same each time: voice, canned replies, "tech demo". A demo that avoids spoken AI dialogue and shows memory through behaviour sidesteps the main objections.
