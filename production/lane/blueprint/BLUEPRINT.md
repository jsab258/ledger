## The game in one page

Britain, Monday 15 October 1990, a grey afternoon. Frank Nowak, 32, walks off the ferry in Meridian, a fictional British port town, with one suitcase and a letter. His uncle Mickey has died and left him Mickey's, a minicab office on Quay Street in the Hook: two tired cars, a radio, Sheila the bookkeeper who will not use his name, Ron the old docker who minds the door and the rank, a book of debts nobody can collect, and an envelope expected at the ferry landing after ten. Fifteen to twenty hours later the port does not move without him.

The minicab firm is the criminal machine: cover, eyes, alibi and risk. The player mostly acts with his hands (jobs, break-ins, fights, driving, working the street) while a living town watches, remembers and talks; talk is where the consequences surface, never the main thing he does. Frank rises in position (what people call him: the new owner, Nowak, Frank, Frankie; who works for him; what he owns, wears and can enter), never in numbers. Three acts: The Inheritance (a week with nothing and nobody), The Rise and the Squeeze (cars, crew, fronts and rackets while three rivals squeeze him), The Ledger Comes Due (the books are opened and the ending is what the town and the books hold); two or three endings.

The bar: Kingdom Come: Deliverance II for immersion and finish; GTA for why people play for hours; GTA VI measured as state of the art, the higher bar wins. The measure: a panel of eight kinds of player recommending it after a whole evening and a whole week.

## What binds every part (canon and his rulings)

- Third person, PC only on Windows; Britain 1990 (window 1988 to 1992); late analogue: phone boxes, cash, paper; CCTV rare.
- Content rule: no alcohol or gambling, shown or spoken of; tobacco stays; no children anywhere (the school stands closed); violence with blood and light gore, no torture or cruelty as spectacle (PEGI 18 within these limits, his ruling of 24 Nov: blows fully animated, the blow on a downed man and beating a pleading man shown, no cut-away); killing possible, rare, permanent, remembered forever; drugs only off screen; no sexual content; no slurs; every brand fictional.
- A car can hurt a person badly and never kills; only the player's car can strike anyone. Fists knock a man out and never kill alone; a fall after a blow can, rarely. Guns rare, late and after melee, through an escalation dial. Reloading is never stopped. Frank cannot die (his ruling of 12 Oct, archived DECISIONS 13 Oct 02:45, carried by canon OPEN 3) and there is no game over.
- Clock: 48 real minutes a game day, so 2 real minutes a game hour (his ruling of 24 Nov, replacing 60 s a game hour of 21 Nov); Act II about 5 game weeks, about 7 game weeks in all; sleeping and waiting skip empty hours; the clock's real hours measured first. The main story aims at about 20 hours (his ruling of 24 Nov), measured in the first loops; seven weeks of full clock would be about 39 hours.
- Map: the Hook plus its docks (the docks cut first if time runs short; kept by his ruling of 24 Nov, chases 1 to 3 minutes, rich in lanes and yards), one map file in metres from the ferry landing (production/lane/blueprint/data/map.json, with its drawing). Camera at the Between height, 3.1 m back, field of view a setting of 75 to 100, default 86.
- No minimap, waypoints or markers; a paper map (Frank shown on it only at a sign or landmark, his ruling of 24 Nov) and street signs. No stats, levels or skill trees; difficulty only as assists. No jump (vaulting and set climbing places only), crouch in, no swimming, no mounts, no free fast travel (his own driver may skip a trip), no crafting, no multiplayer, no mods at release, English only.
- The moat: every act exposes seven perceivable slots; a five-rung identification ladder, its top rung gated by relationship; permanent per-person memory; gossip through schedule crossings; live talk with memory and local voice; the deterministic Core decides every outcome, language models classify and never adjudicate.

## How to read it

Every part has a short entry: how it works; its standard (a number or a pass-or-fail test) and test; the source of that standard (a reference game's measured figure, a dated real-world figure, his ruling or canon, or DECIDED by the design proof for him to overturn); its status; and its coverage.json row id. Statuses:
- measured: the standard rests on a number production/research/lane/measurements/STATUS.md marks solid: five uncut, dated samples read at a step finer than what they measure (advisor 302; his words of 22 Nov). Only these count as measured for the gate;
- ruled: the standard is his ruling or canon (it binds, but it is not a measurement);
- sourced: the standard rests on a dated real-world figure or a published standard (1990 statistics, accessibility guidelines, law), read from its source, not measured by the five-sample rule;
- decided: a design decision with a pass-or-fail standard, his to overturn;
- pending: defined, a number waits on a named, dated study, or rests on a reference-game lead not yet solid;
- unmeasured: defined, no reference figure behind its number yet;
- out: left out by his ruling or canon (kept so nobody builds it);
- outside: Unreal-only work (looks, engine, performance), judged in Unreal against the Hook sheet and KCD2 frames;
- machinery: how the game is made, not met by the player (group 9; no coverage row).

Sources, newest first; on a clash the newer wins unless canon or a ruling says otherwise: canon and RULINGS (on wip); DEFINITIONS-2026-11-21 (its design pass 4 of 23 Nov newest), GTAVI-MAP, FIT-CHECK, CLOUD-FIGURES, PANEL-BAR and PRIVACY-CHECK of 21 Nov; the description copy of 21 Nov (redteam2/GAME-DESCRIPTION-COPY-2); DESIGN-ADDENDUM of 20 Nov; the description inside ONE-SHOT-PROMPT of 20 Nov; VISION of 19 Nov (approved); DESIGN and BUILD-ORDER of 18 Nov; the parts tree.

## The story chapter

Chosen and on paper (keeper run 5). His pick of 24 Nov, "A on top of C", is in RULINGS on wip: first a living town in which Frank builds a crime empire (crew, bribes, rival gangs fought or played off, ground held, rivals' men recruited), people with memory, gossip, loyalty and nerve; above it a light written spine, two or three endings, beats triggered by the empire's and the town's state, no fixed missions. Canon of 24 Nov sets the frame: Danny Cammack, who drove Mickey's car two and kept the key, is the opponent; the big loss is Ron going inside unless Frank pays to stop it (harder in phase 2); the ending counts Ron and Sheila as friends too; Mickey's envelope holds a copy of the night's fares.
