The whole game as one design. Version 1, 23 November 2026, by the blueprint keeper (claude-opus-5-5); updated the same day by keeper runs 2 and 3 (the solid rule for measured, craft standards, the browser build, the live voices hookup, rulings to revisit; the prior research index, the AAA studies and the preparation files folded part by part). ## The game in one page

Britain, Monday 15 October 1990, a grey afternoon. Tom Nowak, 32, walks off the ferry in Meridian, a fictional British port town, with one suitcase and a letter. His uncle Mickey has died and left him Mickey's, a minicab office on Quay Street in the Hook: two tired cars, a radio, Sheila the bookkeeper who will not use his name, Ron the old docker who minds the door and the rank, a book of debts nobody can collect, and an envelope expected at the ferry landing after ten. Fifteen to twenty hours later the port does not move without him.

The minicab firm is the criminal machine: cover, eyes, alibi and risk. The player mostly acts with his hands (jobs, break-ins, fights, driving, working the street) while a living town watches, remembers and talks; talk is where the consequences surface, never the main thing he does. Tom rises in position (what people call him: the new owner, Nowak, Tom, Tommy; who works for him; what he owns, wears and can enter), never in numbers. Three acts: The Inheritance (a week with nothing and nobody), The Rise and the Squeeze (cars, crew, fronts and rackets while three rivals squeeze him), The Ledger Comes Due (the books are opened and the ending is what the town and the books hold); two or three endings.

The bar: Kingdom Come: Deliverance II for immersion and finish; GTA for why people play for hours; GTA VI measured as state of the art, the higher bar wins. The measure: a panel of eight kinds of player recommending it after a whole evening and a whole week.

## What binds every part (canon and his rulings)

- Third person, PC only on Windows; Britain 1990 (window 1988 to 1992); late analogue: phone boxes, cash, paper; CCTV rare.
- Content rule: no alcohol or gambling, shown or spoken of; tobacco stays; no children anywhere (the school stands closed); violence with blood and light gore, no torture or cruelty as spectacle; killing possible, rare, permanent, remembered forever; drugs only off screen; no sexual content; no slurs; every brand fictional.
- A car can hurt a person badly and never kills; only the player's car can strike anyone. Fists knock a man out and never kill alone; a fall after a blow can, rarely. Guns rare, late and after melee, through an escalation dial. Reloading is never stopped. Tom cannot die (his ruling of 12 Oct, archived DECISIONS 13 Oct 02:45, carried by canon OPEN 3) and there is no game over.
- Clock: 60 real seconds a game hour; Act II about 5 game weeks, about 7 weeks in all (21 Nov). Length 15 to 20 hours with side stories inside.
- Map: the Hook plus its docks (the docks cut first if time runs short). Camera at the Between height, 3.1 m back, field of view a setting of 75 to 100, default 86.
- No minimap, waypoints or markers; a paper map and street signs. No stats, levels or skill trees; difficulty only as assists. No jump, no crafting, no multiplayer, no mods at release, English only.
- The moat: every act exposes seven perceivable slots; a five-rung identification ladder, its top rung gated by relationship; permanent per-person memory; gossip through schedule crossings; live talk with memory and local voice; the deterministic Core decides every outcome, language models classify and never adjudicate.

## How to read it

Every part has a short entry: how it works; its standard (a number or a pass-or-fail test) and test; the source of that standard (a reference game's measured figure, a dated real-world figure, his ruling or canon, or DECIDED by the design proof for him to overturn); its status; and its coverage.json row id. Statuses:
- measured: the standard rests on a number production/research/lane/measurements/STATUS.md marks solid: five uncut, dated samples read at a step finer than what they measure (advisor 302; his words of 22 Nov). Only these count as measured for the gate;
- ruled: the standard is his ruling or canon (it binds, but it is not a measurement);
- sourced: the standard rests on a dated real-world figure or a published standard (1990 statistics, accessibility guidelines, law), read from its source, not measured by the five-sample rule;
- decided: a design decision with a pass-or-fail standard, his to overturn;
- pending: defined, a number waits on a named, dated study, or rests on a reference-game lead not yet solid;
- unmeasured: defined, no reference figure behind its number yet;
- out: left out by his ruling or canon (kept so nobody builds it);
- outside: Unreal-only work (looks, engine, performance), judged in Unreal against the Hook sheet and KCD2 frames;
- machinery: how the game is made, not met by the player (group 9; no coverage row).

Sources, newest first; on a clash the newer wins unless canon or a ruling says otherwise: canon and RULINGS (on wip); DEFINITIONS-2026-11-21 (its design pass 4 of 23 Nov newest), GTAVI-MAP, FIT-CHECK, CLOUD-FIGURES, PANEL-BAR and PRIVACY-CHECK of 21 Nov; the description copy of 21 Nov (redteam2/GAME-DESCRIPTION-COPY-2); DESIGN-ADDENDUM of 20 Nov; the description inside ONE-SHOT-PROMPT of 20 Nov; VISION of 19 Nov (approved); DESIGN and BUILD-ORDER of 18 Nov; the parts tree.

## Rulings to revisit

His rulings are not unquestionable (his words of 22 Nov, as relayed in the order of 23 Nov). R8, R9 and R11 are pressed on rather than argued against: their sources recommend keeping, and they are listed so the choice is his, not silent. Where a finding or a measured number argues against one, it is listed here with its evidence and a recommendation, for him as one multiple-choice question. Until he answers, the ruling stands, and his newer words beat his older ones. None of the evidence below is yet solid by the five-sample rule.

- R1 The map: the Hook plus its docks (his ruling of 28 Oct; canon's docks line of 19 Nov). Evidence: reference car chases ran 2 to 8 minutes (19 chases in GTA VI, GTA V, GTA IV and Sleeping Dogs; leads, uploaders' picks, three stars and more, on far bigger maps); the Hook's three loops (1,528, about 1,000 and about 910 m) hold 1 to 3 minutes before every road has been driven twice (design pass 4). Question: (a) keep the map; chases run 1 to 3 minutes and are made rich by lanes, yards, the switch to foot and the search (recommended: the five-sample chase lengths land this week and are checked against it); (b) add one drivable road past the soft edge for chases only (about 2 to 3 km, nothing to enter); (c) decide after the five-sample lengths.
- R2 The clock: 60 real seconds a game hour, Act II about 5 game weeks (his ruling of 21 Nov, his newest on it; about 7 weeks in all by CLK-06). Evidence: the references' days run 48 to 144 real minutes against LEDGER's 24 (CLOUD-FIGURES C2, published figures; GTA VI unconfirmed); the evening is 6 real minutes against GTA V's about 12; the running time comes to 13 to 20 hours against his 15 to 20 (CLK-06); and the numbers run finds rung 3 out of reach for almost every player in a five-week Act II at the written car prices, the cooling rates (set for seven weeks) able to fall at most twice, and an aggressive player able to end the act in its first week (NUMBERS-RUN M3, R1, R4; assumed amounts; its fixes keep the clock). Question: (a) keep 60 s and seven weeks; fix the shortfalls inside it (three bot playthroughs check the hours, a short median fixed by fewer nights skipped; the design proof adopts the numbers run's ladder fixes) (recommended: his newest ruling, chosen with the references in front of him); (b) 90 s a game hour with a shorter calendar; (c) revisit after the first loops' playthroughs.
- R3 The thinking sound (his ruling of 28 Sep: a short thinking sound with a glance before an answer, cut when the answer starts). Evidence: with Pocket the first sound arrives in about 0.2 s (POCKET-SWITCH, 26 Oct), so the sound is cut mid-word; it has been off in play in the Unreal build since his ruling of 7 Oct (no prepared openings). Question: (a) drop it; the glance alone covers the wait (recommended: his newer words of 7 Oct, and the voice is now fast enough); (b) keep it, played only when no sound has arrived after a set time; (c) keep it as ruled.
- R4 The card: the 6 GB line for the game alone and the day's spare card time of 1.7 ms (his ruling of 24 Oct, "for now"). Evidence both ways: the 9 Nov exit review measured 0.21 ms spare by day and 0.19 at night against 14.0 ms, with walking card memory medians of 6,145 to 6,151 MB, already over the line (phase2-plan, FOR-ORDER-16-NOV, via the prior research index); but those were set and measured with the voice on the card, and the live voice is now Pocket on the processor, freeing 4-BUDGETS' 2.8 GB voice reserve. Question: (a) keep both until the newest packaged build is re-measured with Pocket on the processor, then budget people, cars and lamps from what is spare (recommended: nothing is built in Unreal before the prototype is done); (b) raise the game's card line now by the freed reserve; (c) revisit in phase 2, as the ruling already says for the spare time.
- R5 The empty unit, bay 3 (his ruling of 3 Oct: "empty unit to let (whitewashed)"). Evidence: his words of 21 Nov on the port-town footage name card, cardboard and a fallen blind in an empty bay's window (orders item 38); the one dated 1986 to 1990 text says corrugated iron. Question: (a) whitewashed as ruled; (b) bare glass dressed with card, cardboard and a fallen blind (recommended: his newer words); (c) whitewash with that dressing inside.
- R6 Window surrounds (his ruling of 3 Oct: windows with white frames). Evidence: dated Hull photographs of 1982 to 1986 show painted window and door surrounds; doors already follow them (archived DECISIONS 13 Oct). Question: (a) keep white frames and plain surrounds (recommended: the photographs predate 1988 and come from one city); (b) white frames with some surrounds painted; (c) the photographed colours.
- R7 Colour from photographs (his rulings of 21 Sep and 1 Oct: dated photographs govern and win). Evidence: thin: missions complaints are 7.4% of the studied reviews and the "no direction" share is not separated (aaa-studies MISSIONS-AND-SIDE-QUESTS). Question: (a) keep, with landmarks, people who give the way and the Ledger's Open page (recommended); (b) an optional marker in the settings; (c) markers.
- R9 Growth in position only, no stats (his ruling of 30 Oct). Evidence: thin: KCD2's reviews praise strength that is earned, while calling the late hero too strong (two reviews; aaa-studies MONEY-AND-PROGRESSION). Question: (a) keep the cast; each resident speaks from the shared kinds plus about 5 own lines, arriving as data after the first loops (recommended, INVENTORY); (b) keep the cast with the full 30 own lines each, after release; (c) hold the cast at the 44 written people.
