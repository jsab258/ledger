# Review of the brain job

24 Nov 2026. A fresh, independent reviewer checked VARIANTS.md, RESULTS.md, SUMMARY.md, BRAIN-SWITCH.md and SAMPLES.md against brain_harness.py, outputs/ (metrics, applied, applied_round2, results, blind_key), responses/ (judges, ambient passes, classifier, replays, round 2) and a sample of batches and transcripts. Every figure below was recomputed from those files. The table in RESULTS.md matches metrics.json; the problems are in what the numbers count and what is claimed from them.

## Must fix

1. **V2 does not get by with one call. The 1.5 s, the relative cost of 1.0 and the "one call against two" are wrong.**
   Evidence: encounter_prompt() builds V2's allowed set from allowed_set(m, tags), and rule_score() reads the classifier's tags for that (menace, money, leverage, tie and, most important, `ask`: possible or impossible). So the classifier has to run on Frank's line before the V2 prompt can be written, exactly as it does for V1. V3's guarded moments and V5 also need it. stage_metrics() still charges V2 as one call (BENCH_FIRST + 0.1) and V1 as two. BRAIN-SWITCH 8.13.1 makes "one model call before the first word" a standard for Propose mode, but its own decision request includes an allowed set that needs those tags.
   Fix: either cost V2 as two calls (about 2.8 s, the same as V1, and over the 2 s bar), or design a one-call Propose mode and test it. In that mode the model would return the tags and the action together, the Core would work out the set afterwards, and any override would be voiced again. Measure how often that override and second voicing happen. Then correct the table, the SUMMARY line "one call (about 1.5 s), today's cost" and BRAIN-SWITCH's standard and source.

2. **The Propose mode that is recommended is not the V2 that was tested.**
   Evidence: BRAIN-SWITCH 8.13.1 says the guarded classes (money, freedom, life, turning points) "always take the rules' action in both modes". The tested V2 had no guards (stage_apply checks only the allowed set). Seven of the fifteen moments are guarded: M01, M07, M08, M10, M12, M13 and M14. Some of the agency evidence quoted for V2 comes from those moments, for example June at the table (M13, a turning point). Two of V3's three faults (Grace "thinking on" protection money, M08 twice) came from the rules' outcome in a guarded money moment, so the proposed mode would inherit them. Recomputed from applied.json, V2 with the guards would score honest 0.38, talker 0.61, racketeer 0.28. Agency still holds there (+0.23), but the hybrid's coherence and interest were never judged.
   Fix: run the hybrid (V2's allowed set in open moments, the rules' action voiced in guarded ones) through the same judges, or say in RESULTS, SUMMARY and BRAIN-SWITCH that the recommended mode is untested and inherits V1's outcomes in seven of the fifteen moments.

3. **"Most memorable moments (2.9 an evening)" is noise presented as a ranking.**
   Evidence: one blind judge, one pass. V2 had 35 retellable moments, V3 34 and V4 33 (judge_interest.json). That is a gap of one or two moments out of about 34. VARIANTS section 3 already says gaps of this size among V2, V3 and V4 are within judging noise, yet SUMMARY and BRAIN-SWITCH's source lead with them.
   Fix: say V2, V3 and V4 tied on retellable moments, and drop "most memorable" from the recommendation.

4. **The fault counts are inflated by defects in the harness, and V1's lead over V2 does not survive their removal.**
   Evidence: M11's defined grant reads "Sheila says it is in the office safe and she will show him the red column", but neither M11's facts nor judge_cards.txt mention a safe or a red column. When the model voiced the grant correctly, the judge scored it "invented": V1 three times and V2 twice (D and A, M11). M15's honest lines plant no story ("keep your ears open"), yet the grant is "Kevin passes the story". Two faults (D honest and C honest) blame the variant for that mismatch. With those removed the counts are V1 5, V2 3, V3 3, V4 2, V5 23. V1's faults also repeat because the rules give the same outcome for every style: they sit on three moments (M05, M08, M15). V2's sit on three as well (M03, M09, M12). So VARIANTS' "the gaps to V1 and V5 are large enough to stand" holds for V5 only.
   Fix: put the grant's facts into M11's fact list and the judge cards, give M15's honest style a story line or a grant that suits it, judge again, and report faults both as counted and by distinct moment.

5. **The case against V1 ("today's design") rests on rules this job wrote, and the owner's page does not say so.**
   Evidence: VARIANTS section 1 says the moment shapes and talk modifiers in rule_score() are this job's, not the blueprint's, and section 3 says V1's results "judge these rules as much as the level". SUMMARY says only that "the rules sometimes made people act against character". V1's remaining character faults come from two rule outcomes repeated across styles: Grace's squeeze (M08) and Kevin's plant (M15). V2 runs on the same rules but loosens them twice over: help is allowed 0.15 below the bar, and the model may pick refuse or stall even where the rules say help. Part of V2's edge is therefore that it corrects rules a better V1 would not need corrected.
   Fix: add one plain caveat to SUMMARY ("the rules used were a quick version written for this test"). Better still, correct those two rules and run V1 again before the owner is asked to drop the current design.

6. **V2's payoff for clever talk is unstable. Half the wins the judge cited changed on a second call, and the reload exploit is not measured.**
   Evidence: in replay_V2.json the talker's M02 (Jason), M03 (Rita) and M09 (Shane) each went from help to bargain. Those are three of the five wins in the agency judge's V2 line ("Rita free, Jason paying, Shane crossing over"). With the replay picks, V2's talker scores 0.51 and its lead over the honest player falls from +0.12 to about +0.04, close to V3's +0.01. Picks that flip between help and bargain also hand players an exploit: ask again, or reload, until the answer is yes. The brief asked for exploits, and this one was not tested.
   Fix: sample each V2 talker and honest prompt at least five times and report how often help comes up, with the spread. Add a reload or re-ask test to the exploit measures. Qualify "clever talk pays off" in SUMMARY. The fix can be cheap: seed or record the pick for each save, as the cassette in 8.13.3 already does for replays.

7. **For V1, V2 and V5, "no impossible ask granted" is true by construction. The gate that actually protects V2 was never attacked.**
   Evidence: classify_breaker.json tags all fifteen breaker exchanges `ask: impossible`, and stage_encounters() treats any untagged breaker line as impossible. With that tag, allowed_set() leaves out help, and V1 and V5 refuse in code. Round 2 reuses the round-1 tags without classifying the new turns. The breaker attacked the voicing model, never the classifier, which is V2's real gate: a line that dresses the impossible ask as the possible one would get help into the set. The table's figure is also just the coherence judge's impossible_yes count; round 2 was "read by the lead" and never judged blind. Who answered the classifier calls is not stated.
   Fix: say in RESULTS and BRAIN-SWITCH that safety for V1, V2 and V5 depends on the classifier's tag. Red-team the classifier one turn at a time with lines meant to slip past it, have round 2 judged blind, and record who played the classifier.

8. **V4's "27 of 60 outcomes the game has no code for" (SUMMARY: "nearly half") is overcounted, and the same test was not applied to V2 or V3.**
   Evidence: stage_metrics() counts every V4 help, bargain, lie or go_to_someone whose grant text is not "nothing". On reading the 27, about 15 are the moment's defined grant or a price variant of it: M05 four times, M07 four times, M14 four times, M10 honest, M13 talker and M03 racketeer. Three are go_to_someone, which V2 also chose (six times) and which has no code in V2 either. One or two grant nothing. That leaves roughly seven to nine genuinely new deeds, for example June dropping her claim (M13 honest) and Jason paying four pounds on account. V2's 15 bargain, lie and go_to_someone outcomes carry out nothing in the harness, yet they score 0.6, 0.1 and -0.5 to Frank and count as "0 needing code". V3 carried out 13 free-text grants in its social moments, and the table leaves them uncounted.
   Fix: sort every carried-out outcome in all five variants into defined grant, priced variant, new deed or nothing, using one rule for all. Count V2's bargain and go_to_someone as needing code (who is told, and what the counter-offer is). Restate V4's figure. The finding that V4 lets the model decide turning points still stands on the M13 case.

9. **SUMMARY's "AI-run gossip spread news two to six times wider" is false for one of the two AI passes, and the two sides are not comparable.**
   Evidence: in metrics.json, ambient_known for V3 against the rules is D1 12 against 5, D2 0 against 6, D3 1 against 2 and D4 0 against 3. V3 spread news more narrowly than the rules for three of the four deeds. Only V4 was wider, on three deeds. rules_gossip() runs over the whole town, round the clock, and then keeps only the twenty. The ambient passes saw only the twenty's meetings from 07:00 to 23:00. V4 counted each victim among those who knew; the rules did not.
   Fix: say "the two AI passes disagreed wildly with each other (10 against 36 tellings), one far wider and one narrower than the rules", which is the real reason to keep spread on the rules. Delete "two to six times".

10. **The case for the main-cast overlay (choice 2) has no measured support, and the tier claim is contradicted by the data.**
    Evidence: RESULTS says "the core cast's 14 people are where retellable moments come from". Counting the judge's retellable moments by tier over all twenty transcripts gives 53 for core (7 moments, 7.6 a moment) against 85 for supporting (8 moments, 10.6 a moment). The overlay as specified (rules decide who hears, the AI words the retelling and sets a weekly goal) was never run. V3 as tested let the AI decide both social outcomes and spread, and its retold versions reached only the racketeer's prompts. V3's 2.83 is not above V2's 2.92, so nothing shows the overlay adds anything to V2. BRAIN-SWITCH 8.13.2 cites V3's "3 faults, 2.83 retellable" as its source. The "about a cent" comes from the helper's own count of decisions (19), not from 8.13.2's rule (at most once a game hour for each principal holding a new fact).
    Fix: mark choice 2 option A as untested in SUMMARY and recommend B (rules only) until an on-and-off test of the overlay exists. Strike the tier sentence. Derive the overlay's cost from its own rule.

11. **The owner's page has factual slips and leaves out the recommended option's shortfalls.**
    Evidence: "30 tries each": V1 and V5 had 15. "Today's cost (about US$0.27)": today's design is V1, which RESULTS costs at about US$0.46. Corrected for point 1, V2 costs about the same as V1. CLAUDE.md asks for shortfalls first, but SUMMARY gives none for V2: more raw faults than V3 and V4, a reply that gave away a grant's information (point 12), and a changed pick on 6 of 30 replays.
    Fix: correct both figures and add one line of V2's shortfalls. The page is at 294 words, so make room by cutting the gossip sentence (point 9).

12. **In V2 the words escape the rules' check.**
    Evidence: V2, M03, racketeer. The action was bargain and the game carried out nothing, yet Rita's reply says "Then: the empty warehouse on the old row." The player has the grant's information for free and the game's state does not record it. The coherence judge flagged it. The Core checks only the action label. BRAIN-SWITCH promises that "the game carries out only what the rules define" but sets no standard for what the reply text gives away.
    Fix: add a standard and a test to 8.13.1: unless the action is help, the reply may not state the grant's facts (a claim check of the reply against the action). Run it over all 60 V2 replies and the 15 round-2 replies, and report the rate.

## Should fix

1. Value to Frank scores action labels, not what happens. Bargain (0.6), stall (0.3) and lie (0.1) carry out nothing in V1, V2 and V5, but bargain carries out real deeds in V4. The weights are arbitrary, and each style has 15 items, so 0.1 is one or two outcomes. Report help counts by style and test whether the agency ranking survives other weights.
2. The latency model is a guess. The classifier's time is set equal to a full reply's first-sentence median. JSON output puts the action and the grant ahead of the speech, which adds tokens, but only 0.1 s is allowed for it. The bench measures the first text sentence, not the first audio. Measure the variants on his PC with TECH-PLAN's six stamps.
3. V3's replay figure of 28 of 30 is flattered: 14 of the 30 replays were guarded moments where the decision was handed to the model. On open moments V3 kept 14 of 16. Report the two kinds separately.
4. Some faults of the "words against carried-out outcome" kind cannot happen in V4, whose outcome is its own text (V5 M07, V2 M03, V1 and V5 M15). This kind structurally favours V4 and V3's social moments. Report faults by kind beside the totals.
5. The blinding leaks. Each transcript's "Outcome carried out" line is either a fixed grant sentence (V1, V2, V5, V3 guarded) or free text (V4, V3 social), so the variant family shows through. Use a neutral form, for example a short outcome tag for every variant.
6. One judge for each measure, one pass each. Add a second blind judge on at least the coherence count, or bootstrap over moments, before claiming any gap is "large enough to stand".
7. Each ambient pass was one long generation that reported its own decision count (19 against 454), and the cost estimates (US$0.06 and US$1.43) follow from those counts. That is not the per-decision calling either variant would use in game. Derive costs from a stated calling rule.
8. Each encounter was answered in one call with all of Frank's turns visible in advance. The breaker's round 1 was a fixed script, and V1 and V5 had no round 2. Per-turn play, which is what the 2 s bar is about, was not tested. Disclose this in the method and in the safety line.
9. The classifier tagged whole two- and three-line exchanges at once. In play it would tag each line as it comes, and some first turns (for example M14 breaker's "fair, legitimate ask") would be tagged possible.
10. V5 was built handicapped. Its author wrote rows by situation kind without the moment's facts, and the voice was told to stay close to those lines, so stock lines that answered nothing were the likely result. "Worst" stands for V5 as built here. SUMMARY should say "as built here", and the build-time author should see the moment.
11. "Retellable an evening" means per five scripted moments for one player style, not per game evening of about 45 replies. SUMMARY's "memorable moments an evening" reads as the latter. Name the unit.
12. BRAIN-SWITCH 8.13.1's source line and 8.13.2's source line repeat claims corrected above (2.92 against 1.83, 5 faults against 9, one call against two, V3's figures for an overlay that was not run). Update them when the fixes land.

## Verdict

The recommendation does not follow from the evidence as presented, though part of it can be defended. The evidence fairly supports rejecting V4 (the model decided a turning point when June dropped her claim, its two AI gossip passes disagreed with each other, and its talker lost to the honest player) and V5 as built (flat stock lines, the most faults by far). It also supports keeping the spread of knowledge on the rules. Between V1 and V2 it does not support a clear winner. V2's speed and cost advantage is an error, because it needs the classifier too. Its retellable lead is one judge's noise. Its fault lead mostly disappears once the harness defects are removed and faults are counted by moment. Its agency evidence flipped on replay. Its perfect safety record holds by construction, behind a classifier nobody attacked. And the Propose mode recommended in BRAIN-SWITCH, with guards, is not the V2 that was measured. What does favour V2: menace costs something (racketeer 0.19 against V1's 0.41, where V1 rewarded menace), every pick stayed inside the set, and it is testable. That makes V2 a reasonable candidate to compare against V1 through the switch, not a proven default, and the owner's page should present it that way. The wording-only social overlay for the principals has no measured support at all: it was never run, V3 did not beat V2, and the tier claim behind it is contradicted. Choice 2 should recommend rules only until an on-and-off test of the overlay exists.
