# How much should the AI be the brain? Summary

24 Nov 2026. Five ways of letting the AI decide for the Hook's people, run over the same three evenings (fifteen meetings, four kinds of player), judged blind. AI players and AI judges measure stand-ins for fun; fun itself shows only in real play.

Shortfalls first:
- AI writes behaviour ahead, game runs it: worst. People repeated their lines whatever Frank said (24 faults, 1.2 memorable moments an evening).
- Rules decide, AI talks (today's design): fourth. 9 faults, 1.8 moments; the rules sometimes made people act against character, and it needs two AI calls before a word, about 2.8 s, over the 2 s bar.
- AI decides everything: well spoken, but nearly half its outcomes were deeds the game cannot carry out or test, and clever talk did worse than honesty.
- Nobody was talked into the impossible in any version, 30 tries each (a floor, not proof).
- AI-run gossip spread news two to six times wider than the researched temper model.

Recommendation: AI proposes, rules check. The AI picks from what the rules allow that person; the game carries out only what the rules define. Most memorable moments (2.9 an evening), 5 faults, clever talk pays off within reason, one call (about 1.5 s), today's cost (about US$0.27 an evening).

Choices:
1. The default. A) AI proposes, rules check (recommended). B) Rules decide, as designed. C) AI runs social life.
2. The main cast's gossip. A) Recommended: rules decide who hears what; the AI words how each retells it and gives each a weekly goal, about a cent an evening. B) Rules only. C) AI decides gossip too.

The best two are specified as a switch, to compare in play.
