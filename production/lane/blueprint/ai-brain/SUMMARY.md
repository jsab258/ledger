# How much should the AI be the brain? Summary

24 Nov 2026. Five ways of letting the AI decide for the Hook's people, run over the same three evenings (fifteen meetings, four kinds of player), judged blind, then checked by a fresh reviewer. AI players and AI judges measure stand-ins for fun; fun itself shows only in real play.

Shortfalls first:
- AI writes behaviour ahead, game runs it: worst. People repeated their lines whatever Frank said (23 faults, 1.2 memorable moments an evening).
- AI decides everything: well spoken, but it decided story turning points itself, clever talk did worse than honesty, and its own two gossip runs disagreed.
- Rules decide, AI talks (today's design): fewer memorable moments (1.8 an evening against about 2.8) and more faults (5 against 2 or 3), partly because this test's rules were quick ones.
- AI proposes, rules check: as tested, two AI calls before a word (about 2.8 s, over the 2 s bar), the same as today's design; its clever-talk reward weakened when asked again.
- Nobody was talked into the impossible, 15 to 30 tries each (a floor, not proof).

Recommendation: build two brains behind a switch, AI proposes (the AI picks from what the rules allow) as the default in the loops, today's design beside it, and let play decide. Cost is about the same, US$0.46 an evening.

Choices:
1. The brains to compare. A) AI proposes and rules decide, behind a switch (recommended). B) Rules decide only. C) AI runs social life too.
2. The main cast's gossip. A) Rules only until an AI wording layer is tested on and off (recommended). B) The AI words how each retells news now.
