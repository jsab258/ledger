# Combat end state: guns defined (options)

Review applied, 17 Nov 2026: all 17 fixes from production/lane/reviews/REVIEW-COMBAT-END-STATE-OPTIONS-2026-11-17.md, and the gun list and routes reconciled with the approved July weapons spec.

Research dated 17 Nov 2026. Written for Jafar. Plain words. Numbers come from the sources at the end. Anything marked "judgement" or "proposal" was not found in a source or a design.

## Summary in five lines

1. Canon, your rulings and the July weapons spec already settle most of it: fists every day; guns rare, built last, AAA when used; never a shooter; Tom never dies; guns come from people, never from random loot.
2. In 1990 Britain criminals mostly had pistols, shotguns (often sawn off) and fakes. In England and Wales 38 to 58 people a year were shot dead from 1988 to 1991 (58 in 1990). Police on patrol carried no guns.
3. The games that matter: Sleeping Dogs and The Last of Us Part II for close fighting; Mafia: The Old Country and Red Dead Redemption 2 for guns that feel heavy and scarce; GTA as the thing to avoid.
4. Four options below. RECOMMENDED is option A: fists and improvised weapons every day, guns rare and heavily consequential, but AAA in feel when used. It needs no canon change.
5. Two decisions for you at the end: take option A (about 6 to 10 agent weeks after melee, estimate); and whether to keep the silenced pistol you asked for in July.

## What canon, your rulings and the designs already settle

- Canon (content rule): violence with blood and light gore; no torture or cruelty as spectacle; killing possible, rare, permanent, remembered forever.
- Canon (D24): LEDGER is not a shooter. Fighting must look and feel AAA (your words, 12 Oct).
- Canon (brands): every weapon is fictional. No real gun makes in the game.
- Canon (era): landlines and phone boxes, no mobiles, CCTV rare. So a shot is reported by someone getting to a phone.
- Rulings, 12 Oct and 28 Oct: fists, improvised and carried weapons, rare guns; guns only after melee works; Tom cannot die.
- Ruling, 30 Oct: fists knock out and never kill on their own; a fall against a kerb, wall or step after a blow can kill, rarely, and the town treats it as a killing.
- Ruling, 28 Oct: the camera goes wider for fights (the other man's feet, the onlookers).
- The July weapons spec (game-design/weapons-spec.md, approved in full 29 Jul 2026) already has:
  - a firearms family for its last phase: a .22 target pistol, a .38 snub revolver, a .45 automatic, a silenced .22 and a sawn-off shotgun; no rifles, no automatic weapons, "this game tops out at a sawn-off" (section 5.2, 5.5);
  - the threat as the main use of a weapon: drawing it is a verb with its own outcomes (comply, freeze, flee, call the bluff, escalate), and it cannot be undone (5.1);
  - four ways to get a weapon, all through people: bought from a named supplier, stolen (the owner notices), taken off someone, and found exactly once, at Mickey's, as a story beat; no random loot (7.3);
  - carrying under a coat, about two things; a sawn-off is "concealable in nothing"; frisks follow suspicion, never chance (7.1, 15.5);
  - arrest, no chase, approved; resisting allowed and catastrophic (15.2);
  - how far a shot carries: a .38 is heard about 177 m on a daytime street, capped at 250 m, less in rain or noise (16.2);
  - forensics: a revolver leaves no casing, an automatic leaves brass (5.2); firearms put no blood on the shooter (15.4).
- The 12 Oct combat design is the later word on the roster: its gun tier names a sawn-off shotgun and an old revolver, "hard to get, noticed, searched for", and "one shot is a town-wide event; armed police come only when a gun is involved". A gun drawn is rung 6, "news across town". A killing sends the police from procedure to investigation to manhunt (the killing code already built).
- Still open, and proposed here: the exact roster for 1990 Britain, what police come and how fast, what a prototype must prove.

## The references, short

- Sleeping Dogs (2012): close fighting built on counters, grabs and several dozen finishers against the street itself (phone boxes, market stalls, cars). Guns are treated as power-ups; the hero keeps using up his guns and is pushed back to hands and disarms. Lesson: the street is the weapon; a gun is a brief event, not a mode. [S1]
- The Last of Us Part II (2020): Naughty Dog wanted melee to "bear equal weight and complexity to gun combat"; longer, readable enemy tells and a dodge; every attack a paired animation with clear start conditions. [S2] Every human enemy has a name, and the others call out that name when he dies. [S3] Lesson: a death lands because the people around it react to who he was. LEDGER's permanent memory does this for the whole town.
- Mafia: The Old Country (2025): set in 1905 Sicily; ammunition is scarce, so the knife carries the fighting (the article's words); the studio frames combat as surviving and scavenging, the knife as tradition. [S4] A reviewer: "bullets are precious", guns jam and need clearing, firefights mostly come when stealth fails. [S5] Lesson: scarcity and failure make a gun feel serious. Its cover shooting is the part LEDGER does not copy (already ruled out in the coverage audit).
- Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018): physics driven hit reactions, "radically overhauled" for lifelike responses [S6]; witnesses run to report a crime and can be chased and intimidated before they reach the law; being recognised decides between a search and a lasting bounty. [S7] Lesson: weight in every shot, and the witness as a person you can reach. The July spec already took this as the "delivery window".
- GTA V, the counterpoint: firing any gun within police hearing gives two stars at once; you lose them by staying out of search cones, and putting on or taking off a mask drops one star. [S8] Nothing persists once the stars go. That is the opposite of LEDGER, where nothing is forgotten.
- From LEDGER's own studies: KCD2's slow, fragile early gun is the model for a rare gun, Mafia's gun-led game is not (COMBAT.md, R04). A KCD2 witness flees once any weapon is out (Study 14).

## 1990 Britain: the facts

- What criminals used. In 1986 England and Wales recorded 3,477 crimes where a firearm other than an air gun was reported used: pistols 1,314; long-barrelled shotguns 600; sawn-off shotguns 560; "supposed" (a gun claimed but not seen) 668; imitations 252; rifles 53; other 30. [S9] So pistols and shotguns were the real guns, and about a quarter of "gun" crimes (920 of 3,477) involved a fake or an unseen gun.
- Trend. Crimes with firearms reported used rose from 8,524 in 1988 to 10,373 in 1990 and 12,129 in 1991 (air guns apparently included: the 1986 total with air guns was 9,363 [S9]; S10 does not say). Homicides by shooting: 42 in 1988, 38 in 1989, 58 in 1990, 52 in 1991, for all of England and Wales. [S10] For a town the size of Meridian a shooting death is perhaps one in several years (judgement from those national numbers).
- Fakes and conversions. Criminologists describe a late 1980s shift from costly shotguns to cheap, concealable, throwaway imitation or converted guns; imitations could be bought without checks. [S11] Converted blank-firers became common later; for 1990 they are plausible but rare (judgement).
- The law in 1990. After Hungerford (1987) the Firearms (Amendment) Act 1988 banned semi-automatic and pump-action rifles and restricted certain smooth-bore guns; made shortening a shotgun barrel under 24 inches an offence; limited shotgun cartridges to people entitled to own a shotgun (which parts were in force in 1990 is not confirmed; the whole Act was in force from 2 April 1991). [S12] Handguns were still legal with a firearm certificate until the 1997 Act, after which over 160,000 were handed in. [S11] So in 1990 a stolen legal pistol, a target shooter's gun, or a shotgun cut down is the realistic source.
- Armed police. Most British officers outside Northern Ireland did not carry guns on patrol. [S15] On 31 Dec 1989 England and Wales had 7,311 authorised firearms officers in all. [S13] The Met introduced armed response vehicles in 1991: three officers, revolvers locked in a safe between the seats, two carbines in the boot, a gun taken out only for an immediate threat to life, carbines only on the control room's say-so. [S14] Earlier exceptions existed: West Yorkshire's instant response cars from 1976, and an unpublicised Met armed car in Brixton in the 1980s. [S15] Other forces followed in the early to mid 1990s [S15]; 34 forces had them by the end of 1993. [S16] So a provincial town in 1990 could go either way; most forces had no armed car until 1991 to 1993.
- How long armed police took to arrive: no source found. Judgement: the first unarmed beat officer in minutes; armed officers in tens of minutes without an armed car.
- How the street reacts to a shot: no study found for 1990 Britain. The design's "one shot is a town-wide event" and the rarity numbers above support it.

## The options

Each: what it means for play; canon; rough cost (judgement, agent weeks after melee is done); feel study order.

- A. RECOMMENDED. Fists and improvised weapons every day; guns rare and heavily consequential, but AAA in feel when used.
  - Play: most fights never see a gun. A gun is a handful of moments in 15 to 20 hours: mostly drawn to frighten (the July spec's threat verb), fired perhaps a few times, each shot changing the town for good. When it fires it is loud and heavy, with real recoil, ringing ears and physics hit reactions.
  - Canon: no change. Fits D24 "not a shooter" because there is no gunfight loop, no cover system, no waves of enemies. Fits every ruling above and the July spec.
  - Cost: about 6 to 10 agent weeks after melee passes (estimate): gun handling and animation, five guns, the shot's sound, hit reactions, hooking the shot into the existing perception, gossip and police code. The July spec costed its firearms phase at about 1.5 days, but for a rough prototype on box figures, not AAA feel in Unreal.
  - Feel studies: melee first (strike, reaction, shove into a wall; outnumbered; improvised weapons), then guns G1 to G5 below.
- B. Guns as menace only: drawn and pointed, fired only in written story moments.
  - Play: a gun is a threat, never a free choice to shoot. Cheaper and safer, but a gun in the player's hand may feel fake.
  - Canon: no canon change, but it falls short of the July spec, which has the player fire guns. Arguably short of "rare guns" too.
  - Cost: about 3 to 5 weeks (estimate).
  - Feel studies: melee first, then G1 and the story scenes.
- C. Mafia style: rare guns plus a few written gunfight set pieces with cover.
  - Play: two or three climaxes become gunfights. Exciting, but those stretches play like a shooter.
  - Canon: D24 "not a shooter" and your 12 Oct "still not a shooter" would need your ruling to allow set-piece gunfights. Cover shooting was ruled out in the coverage audit.
  - Cost: about 3 to 5 months (estimate): cover, enemy gun behaviour, building the encounters.
  - Feel studies: melee, then guns, then cover and enemy fire.
- D. GTA style: guns common, a wanted level that clears.
  - Play: easy action, but the town would forget and guns would stop meaning anything.
  - Canon: breaks D24, the permanent memory moat, the rare-guns ruling and the 1990 facts. Listed only as the counterpoint. Not recommended.
  - Cost: large, and against the game.

## Under option A: which guns exist

All makes fictional (canon brands rule). The July roster, checked against 1990 Britain and the 12 Oct design:

- Sawn-off double-barrelled shotgun (July and 12 Oct). The criminal's long gun of the period (560 crimes in 1986). Two shots, then a slow reload; shells scarce (by 1991 only certificate holders could buy them). Concealable in nothing. Deafening; devastating at a few metres, poor beyond.
- Old .38 revolver (July's ".38 snub", the 12 Oct "old revolver"). The everyday criminal handgun. Leaves no casing. Old ammunition can misfire (proposal, from the Mafia and KCD2 lesson).
- .22 target pistol (July). Fits 1990 Britain well: a legal club shooter's gun until 1997, so a stolen one is realistic. Self-loading; leaves brass.
- Heavy self-loading pistol (July's ".45 automatic", war surplus). Kept, but rarer than the rest (judgement: plausible in 1990, less common than a revolver). Loud, leaves brass for the police to find.
- Silenced .22 (July, asked for in your own words). A silencer in 1990 British criminal hands is very rare (judgement). See decision 2: keep it as one rare story item, or drop it.
- Replica (proposal, new: not in the July spec). Cannot fire. Works as a threat; a witness cannot tell the difference, and the police treat it as real. About a quarter of 1986 "gun" crimes were fakes or unseen guns, so it belongs in the town even if Tom never holds one.
- Police guns, never Tom's: a revolver and a carbine, carried only by armed officers.
- Not in the game: rifles, pump-actions, machine guns or burst-fire guns (prohibited well before 1990, and wrong for the tone; the July spec also excludes them). The pistols above are self-loading, not automatic.

## Under option A: how Tom gets one

The July spec's four routes (section 7.3), unchanged, with the canon cast filled in as proposals:

- Bought from a person. The supplier is a character with a schedule, a price, an opinion of Tom and a memory, who can be leaned on later by Tom or by the police. Proposal: Keith Garbutt, the broker between the three rivals, or the Widow's dockside people, who smuggle; expensive, slow, and a favour owed.
- Stolen, from a person or a place; the owner notices it is gone. Proposal: a target shooter's pistol, a farmer's shotgun.
- Taken off a man in a fight or off a body: free, and the worst provenance.
- Found exactly once, at Mickey's: something of his in the office, a story beat (July spec; the office is now the minicab office, not a bar).
- Carrying follows the July spec: about two things under a coat; a sawn-off shows; frisks follow suspicion. Every gun keeps its history for good: who sold it, who it was stolen from, what it did. Getting rid of it is a verb that can itself be seen.

## Under option A: what happens after a shot

- In the moment: everyone in hearing gets a "shot heard" event, with the July spec's numbers (about 177 m on a daytime street, up to 250 m, less in rain or a crowd). Those who see fill in whichever of the seven slots their eyes caught. The street empties: doors shut, people run or freeze, a shopkeeper drops the shutter. Nobody stays to watch once a gun is out (as KCD2).
- The report: someone gets to a phone box or a home phone. A threat can stop that one report, never the talk (ruling of 3 Oct).
- Police: an unarmed beat officer first, who keeps back; then armed officers from the station, tens of minutes later in 1990 (judgement). A constable who sees Tom do it arrests him, with no chase (July spec, 15.2). A shot with nobody hurt still opens an investigation and a search for the gun (judgement). A hit goes to hospital, which reports a gunshot wound (judgement; no 1990 source found). A death is a killing: procedure, investigation, manhunt, as the built code does.
- DS Carol Ellis takes a shot personally: it lifts her interest a full step (proposal, not in any design).
- The town remembers: the shot becomes a named event in gossip ("the night someone fired on Quay Street"). Everyone who heard it keeps it forever. Tom's names in the town shift with it. The rivals take note; a gun answered with a gun is possible but stays rare (judgement).
- Tom shot: he cannot die; he wakes in hospital with the wound, the days lost and a doctor who is now a witness. (Note for the town session: combat-design.md says "the bill" in the same place; NHS hospital care was free in 1990.)

## Feel studies, in order (option A)

Melee first, as already planned: one strike, a reaction and a shove into a wall; then outnumbered; then improvised weapons. Guns second, each judged in the game camera:

- G1. Draw and point, no shot: does the street react right (scatter, shutters, someone heading for a phone)?
- G2. One shotgun blast at a wall: sound, flash, recoil, ringing, the street emptying.
- G3. A shot at a man: physics hit reaction, blood within the content rule, the people around him reacting.
- G4. Tom shot: the fall, then waking in hospital.
- G5. The next morning: the police, the gossip, a changed name in someone's mouth.

## For the builder: automatic checks a prototype could run

- Rarity (the long run, Soak): over many simulated weeks guns fired per week stays under a set ceiling; in the AI tester's walk the share of time with a gun drawn stays tiny.
- Every shot heard (who sees and hears, PerceptionGolden): each person in range gets the hearing event with all seven slots, at the July spec's radius.
- Never forgotten (save and load, SaveChaos; and Soak): every witness to a shot still holds it after save, load and long time skips.
- Same result twice (CoreTests): a shot replayed with the same seed gives the same police steps and the same gossip.
- Police order: unarmed officer before armed; armed only when a gun is involved; arrival inside the set window; arrest, no chase.
- Rules still hold: fists never kill on their own; Tom never dies, shot or not; no gun in any shop or as random loot; no blood on a shooter from a firearm alone.
- Content and canon gates: gun names fictional (canon gate); no clip or slot name for executions, slow-motion gore or cruelty (the animation library gate).
- Feel numbers: time from trigger to sound and flash; camera kick present and settling; count of different hit reactions; frame time stays in budget during a shot.
