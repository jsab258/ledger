# Playtest, the friends version

Setting it up. Tell them you've made something and you want them to have a go for half an hour, and that you're going to shut up and watch. Don't explain the game. If they ask, "crime game, British town, 1990" and leave it there.

Ask them to think out loud. That's the only unnatural thing you're asking for, and it's the one that matters. "Just say whatever's in your head, including when you're bored."

Then actually shut up. No laughing at the right moment, no "try talking to him," no answering questions. If they ask something, "I'm not telling you, keep going" is fine. Write the question down, because a question is a finding.

Watch three things. Where they go on their own. When they stop talking, which means bored or lost. And whether they ever mention a person rather than a thing.

Stop at thirty minutes, then say "tell me what happened" and let them talk. Don't fill the silences. The useful stuff comes after the second pause. Then: what were you trying to do, where did you get stuck, tell me about anyone you met, would you play it again.

The one rule. Never ask whether the town felt alive. If they say it themselves, that's the test passing. If you ask, you've handed them the answer and learned nothing. Same for "did you notice people remembering you" and "how did the graphics look."

When they're nice about it, which they will be, don't argue. Just ask: "what would you have done differently if you'd made it?" People who won't criticise your thing will happily describe theirs.

Afterwards, write down four things: did they mention the graphics badly in the first couple of minutes, did the town ever react to something they'd done, did they call it alive without you asking, and would you rather play this than KCD2 on a free evening. That last one's yours alone.

One session tells you nothing. Three friends getting stuck in the same place tells you everything.
