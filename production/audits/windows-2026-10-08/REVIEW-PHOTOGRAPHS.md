# Mickey's row upper windows against the photographs: independent review, 8 Oct 2026

Measured by pixel count. The flat renders give 0.63 px per mm. Photograph 6 is scaled from its 75 mm brick courses, so it is approximate.

**1. Does it read as an 1880-1900 sash?** Yes. Two equal sashes meet mid-height, with horns under the meeting rail, a deep bottom rail (about 100 mm) and a stone sill whose ends go into the brick. The frame sits back in a half-brick reveal. All of that matches photographs 1, 6, 7 and 9. The three biggest differences, worst first:
- **The glazing bar is too thin.** It is about 16 mm, square-cut and has no putty lines. In photograph 6 the bars show about 25-30 mm, nearly as wide as the sash sides. From down the street (hook-day, the third and fourth windows) the bars almost vanish and the window reads as modern one-over-one.
- **The head has no arch or lintel in the game.** The flat render has a lintel block. In the street there is only a faint curve drawn through ordinary bricks over each window, and the underside of the opening is flat dark brown with no joints. Photographs 1 and 6 show distinct red arches and photograph 9 shows painted lintels.
- **Every edge is square.** The sash edges have no moulding and there are no putty lines round the glass, so close up the wood looks flat and stamped out. The horns' shape is right (compare photographs 7 and 8), but they come out thin.

**2. Frame width and height.** The frame width is right. Glass to brick measures 78-80 mm at the sides and head in the flat render, and roughly 74-91 mm in the game. That is the ruling's 3 inches. Photograph 6 looks heavier only because its reveals are painted white. The height suits the storey: the sill is about 0.6 m above the shop cornice and the head is a course or two under the brick eaves. The 1:2 shape matches the upper windows in photographs 3 and 9.

**3. In the game pictures.** No gaps, floating parts or parts through the brick: the frames sit tight in their reveals and the sills go into the brick at both ends. Faults and shortfalls:
- The glass is one even grey, much like the sky, in every window, with nothing behind it: no nets, blinds or dark room. Every photograph shows something behind the glass. At an angle (hook-day, third and fourth windows) the glass goes white and the windows read as blank white panels. This is the most visible shortfall.
- A dotted dark line runs under every meeting rail (mickeys-front), and the underside of the meeting rail looks combed (hook-day, nearest window). A dotted line also runs under the sills. These look like grainy shadows and may shimmer when the camera moves, so they need checking in motion.
- A tiny dark speck sits at the foot of most horns (mickeys-front).
- The reflection of the wall lamp in the second window looks believable.

**4. Verdict: pass on narrow points.** The window's shape, frame width, height and placement could stand among the photographed windows. The narrow points:
1. Widen the glazing bar to about 22-25 mm and add putty lines round the glass.
2. Clear the dotted shadow lines under the meeting rails and sills, checked in motion.
3. Remove the specks at the horn feet.

Known shortfalls, outside the window itself but failing nothing alone: the blank, uniform glass and the missing arch or lintel over the heads. Until both are dressed, the row will look unfinished beside the photographs.
