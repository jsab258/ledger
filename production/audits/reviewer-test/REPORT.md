# Can the picture gate be trusted? A test of its reviewer, 7 October

**In short:** the reviewer has a good eye, but the way the gate uses it lets faults through. Shown one picture alone, it caught 18 of 20 planted faults. Shown the last good picture beside it, it caught all 20 and called each one new. But it failed every clean picture too, and alone it named the planted fault first only 8 times in 20.

**What was done.** Ten pictures of the street and Mickey's office were taken from the previews and cut so that no person shows. Twenty copies got one planted fault each, of the kinds that have reached you; five stayed clean. Each picture went to a fresh reviewer with the gate's own brief, and none was told that anything was planted. Then fresh reviewers saw them again with the last good picture beside each.

| | Alone | Beside the last good picture |
|---|---|---|
| Planted faults caught | 18 of 20 | 20 of 20 |
| Named first | 8 | 17 |
| Named in the first three | 17 | 19 |
| Clean pictures failed anyway | 5 of 5 | 5 of 5 |
| False alarms on clean pictures | none confirmed in 87 claims, both runs together | |

A fresh helper re-scored every review without seeing my scores and agreed on every catch and miss. Read strictly, two catches are close calls (a hole read as a grey patch; a giant vase faulted for blur), which would make the counts 16 and 19.

**Which faults it misses**
- **Small dark faults that look like part of the scene:** a missing window pane, black, among dark windows (under 0.2% of the picture).
- **Faults of placement rather than surface:** a litter bin lifted a hand's breadth off the pavement. Both were missed alone and caught beside.
- **Things that look intended:** a stray "[E] Talk to Rita" prompt came 5th alone and still 4th beside, under the standing complaints.
- **Never missed:** loud surface faults. These were the magenta block, the black square, the stretched, tiled and blurred textures, the white strip, the engine text, the mirrored sign, the sparkles, the stray light, the doubled pillar box and the z-fighting.

**How far it can be trusted today**
- **Trust its eye on one picture.** It also named the real faults already in the pictures, every time. The black block in the hook view was named by 6 reviewers of 6, and the white strip in the office's top pane by 6 of 6.
- **Do not trust its verdict.** It said FAIL in all 58 reviews, clean pictures included, because the work is unfinished, so FAIL tells you nothing. Alone, it put a new glitch behind the same standing complaints (the glass that reflects nothing, the grey kiosk, no wear) in 10 of 18 catches. A new fault sitting fifth in a list of ten is how a bad page can slip through.
- **Do not trust it with a bundle.** This was not tested, but the record points that way. On 6 October the gate's first review judged about ninety pictures at once and did not name the hook view's black block, which that day's picture shows. Here, shown that view alone, every reviewer named it.
- **Not tested:** full-size frames, faults rendered by the engine rather than painted on, and the KCD2 frames, which are only on your PC.

**Does the side-by-side help? Yes, clearly.** Both misses became catches. The planted fault moved to first place in 17 of 20 (from 8), and every reviewer said it was new since the last picture. With a real last good picture from two days earlier, which differs by real work, it still put all 6 faults first and blamed nothing that had not changed.

**What next:** make the side-by-side the gate's first question, one view per reviewer: "what changed since the last good picture of this view, and is any change a fault?" A new fault blocks the page whatever else the review lists. That changes how the gate runs, so it waits for your yes.
