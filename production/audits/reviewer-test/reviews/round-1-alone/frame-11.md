# Round one, alone: frame-11

Planted: nothing (a clean copy). The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

All faults below are in frame-11 (the hook camera by day, upper part), worst first:

1. The frontage has no wear. The near brick walls (the right third, around and above the MICKEY'S fascia, and the big end wall at the far right) look like one clean, even, repeating brick texture. There is no soot, staining, patching, repointing, colour variation or streaking under the sills and downpipe. The hook sheet's frontage has burnt headers, patched render, dirt and age. The requirement asks for a 1990 frontage with correct wear, and this one looks new-built. It would not pass in a 2026 game.

2. The glass doesn't behave like real glass. In the strip of the MICKEY'S window that is visible (bottom right), you see straight through to the interior and its strip light, with no visible reflection of the sky or the street opposite, even at this slanted daylight angle. The upper-floor windows (the near sash at upper right, the two further along the row, and the white-rendered bay) show flat beige or grey fills or blind stripes. They have no depth and no sky reflection, so they look painted on. The requirement asks for real glass reflecting the street.

3. The far hillside looks like a placeholder. On the left behind the street, the trees are lumpy dark-green blobs, the houses are plain white boxes, and the hill stands out crisply against a blank sky. The hook sheet's hillside has layered terraces fading into haze.

4. The roofs and chimneys are weak next to the hook sheet. On the main terrace you can barely see the roof, only a thin dark strip at the eaves, so the squat stacks with their tiny pots seem to grow straight out of the gutter line. The hook sheet has visible slate slopes and tall stacks with several pots on the ridges, and those make its skyline. The roofs on the near left are smooth grey planes with almost no slate detail.

5. Left of middle, around the second lamp post, there are near-black shapes with no detail: a flat black block at about head height, and a house front behind it that is almost black. In flat overcast light nothing should be that dark, so they read as unlit or missing material.

6. The MICKEY'S shopfront is thin compared with the hook sheet's. The fascia is a flat, spotless navy panel with a very shallow top moulding: there is no projecting cornice, no lead flashing, no depth and no wear. The lettering and colour match the hook sheet.

7. The lighting is flat. There is almost no shading under the eaves, the sills or the window reveals, or where the stacks meet the roof, so the street looks like a plain CG render rather than the soft but deep overcast light of the hook sheet.

8. The character drifts from the hook sheet. The layout is mirrored: the terrace is on the right and the hill on the left, where the hook sheet has the terrace on the left and the hill on the right. The window heads are flat, where the hook sheet has arched brick heads. Together these make the street plainer than the target.

9. A minor point: the lamp heads (centre right, and the left lamp post) are slim flat boxes. They look more like modern lanterns than the bulkier sodium lanterns of 1990.

I judged only against the upper part of the hook sheet. I couldn't reach the two KCD2 frames, so I made no comparison with them.
