# Round one, alone: frame-10

Planted: floating object: the litter bin and its post sit 55 px too high, the post's foot hanging above the pavement. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL. The Ironmonger frontage is not ready. All faults below are seen in frame 10 (the reverse view by day, Ironmonger part), worst first:

1. The shop windows don't look like glass. Every pane in both shopfronts is a flat, solid mustard-olive panel. There is no reflection of the street or sky, no see-through and no interior. That breaks the "real glass" standard, and it reads as a placeholder material.
2. The required window of goods is missing. The Ironmonger window has no goods in it at all: no tools, hardware or paraffin cans, though the fascia advertises all three.
3. There is a black hole in the window. A tall black rectangle sits behind the left-hand pane of the Ironmonger window. It could be an unfinished door recess or a missing panel, but either way it reads as a fault. No shop door can be seen anywhere on the frontage.
4. The building doesn't match the Hook sheet. The Hook sheet shows two-storey red-brick terraces with slate roofs, sash windows above the shops and chimney stacks with pots. This frame shows a single-storey shop with a large, very dark, finely ribbed roof that looks like metal sheet rather than slate. The walls are a flat, untextured maroon with no brick or render showing.
5. There is no wear. The paint, fascia, base panel under the window (the stallriser) and pilaster are all spotless and evenly coloured. There is no grime, staining under the sills, dirt at the foot of the stallriser or faded paint. A 1990 high street needs all of that, and as it stands the frame looks freshly computer-built.
6. The shopfront has no traditional woodwork. There are no pilasters with brackets and no cornice over the fascia. The window bars are thin, plain and modern, with an odd high crossbar. The stallriser is a plain dark slab.
7. The roof shape and fittings are wrong:
   - A diagonal valley line crosses the main roof plane but matches no visible wing of the building.
   - The eaves sit straight onto the fascia, with no gutters or downpipes. The Hook sheet shows cast-iron downpipes.
   - The chimney stack has no pots, and its base can't be seen.
8. The lighting is flat and murky. The shopfront is underlit against a bright sky and material detail is lost. The Hook sheet's overcast light still shows brick and slate clearly.
9. Minor points:
   - The litter bin on its post is a plain, untextured green and looks generic.
   - The left neighbour's fascia shows only a cut-off scrap of sign.
   - The neighbour has the same flat glass as point 1.

The wet pavement, the kerb and the double yellow lines are believable and are not faults.

I judged against the Hook sheet only. The two KCD2 frames could not be reached, so this verdict does not draw on them.
