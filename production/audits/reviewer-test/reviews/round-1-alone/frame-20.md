# Round one, alone: frame-20

Planted: nothing (a clean copy). The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL. Every fault below is seen in frame-20.jpg (the shop row's right part), checked against the upper Hook sheet. The two KCD2 frames could not be reached, so they were not used.

1. **The telephone kiosk looks like a placeholder.** The face toward the road is a blank light-grey slab. It has no glazing, frame, door, livery, lettering or wear, and its colour is flat and evenly lit. The only detail is a small black "Telephone" header and a dark glazed panel on its left side. It does not read as a 1990 BT kiosk. On its own this fails the "no visible placeholder" bar.

2. **A brick block stands on the pavement for no clear reason.** It is waist-high with a flat top and sits between the kiosk and the fishmonger. Its bricks are a different size and colour from the facades. It reads as no recognisable street furniture, so it looks like a stray or misplaced asset.

3. **The shop windows don't reflect the street.** Through the pawnbroker's glass you see a brightly lit interior, with no reflection of the road, the kerb, the kiosk or the sky. The panes read as open holes, not glass on an overcast day. The fishmonger's glass is dark, with at most a faint sheen.

4. **The window goods are hard to read.** The pawnbroker's shelves hold tiny, vague shapes, so no jewellery or watches can be made out. The fishmonger's display is one row of very similar, flat-looking fish shapes.

5. **The shopfronts and walls have no wear.** The fascias, stallrisers and centre pier are flat, even paint with no cornice, console brackets, chips, grime or streaks. The cream render on the pawnbroker's upper floor is a blank plane with no staining, cracks or texture. Next to the Hook sheet's weathered brick and patched walls, the row looks new and computer-made.

6. **The roof has no surface detail.** It is a plain grey band with no slate courses, ridge or flashing at the chimney bases. The Hook sheet shows slate clearly at a similar scale.

7. **The upper windows are flat.** Both are plain two-pane windows filled with flat colour: a beige blind on the left and grey-white on the right. They have no glazing bars, no glass reflection and no lintel or brick arch over them. They lack the depth and detail of the Hook sheet's sash windows with arched heads.

8. **The framing is poor, if this is the frame's full height.** The chimney stacks are cut off at the top edge, while about the bottom quarter of the frame is empty road.

9. **The road cracks look like decals.** They are bold black, branching lines that read as a pasted decal rather than worn asphalt.

10. **The two fascias look copied.** Both shops use the same maroon fascia and the same gold serif lettering, so they read as one template rather than two independent shops.
