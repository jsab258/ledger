# Round one, alone: frame-15

Planted: a dark seam stripe down the roof's slates from ridge to eaves. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL. Every fault below is seen in frame-15, the Ironmonger part of the reverse view by day. Worst first:

1. The shop windows are placeholders. Both shopfronts have glass that is one flat, opaque ochre colour. There are no goods in the windows, nothing of the interior shows, and the glass reflects neither the street nor the sky. This breaks "real glass reflecting the street" and "window of verified CC0 goods", and it is a visible placeholder in its own right. It is clearest in the Ironmonger's large window, centre right.

2. There is a stray glitch line. A thin dark slanted rod runs from high on the roof, near the chimney, down across the eaves. It ends on the fascia just above the "I" of IRONMONGER. It is not a downpipe, an aerial or anything else that would be built there. It reads as a broken or misplaced object.

3. The roof geometry is wrong. A hard diagonal seam crosses the roof slope from the eaves above the maroon pilaster up to the chimney, and the surface changes shade on either side of it. The eaves run straight on past it, so it cannot be a hip or a valley. It reads as two roof meshes cutting through each other.

4. The building is out of keeping with the Hook sheet. The Hook sheet shows a two-storey brick terrace with slate roofs, sash windows above the shops and chimneys with pots. This frontage is a single-storey shed: a low, near-black roof that reads as ribbed sheet metal with glossy streaks rather than slate, painted panels, and no brick anywhere on its face. The chimney has no pots.

5. There is no wear anywhere. The paintwork, fascia, lettering, glazing bars, stallrisers, pavement edge and yellow lines are all clean and even, with no grime, streaks, chips or weathering. The requirement asks for correct wear, and the Hook sheet shows patched, weathered brick.

6. The shopfront detail is thin and partly unreadable.
   - There is no cornice above the fascia and there are no console brackets.
   - The stallrisers are plain slabs, and the glazing bars are thin and grey, which looks modern.
   - The lettering looks printed flat on the board.
   - At the left end of the Ironmonger's window is a dark, featureless block where a door would be, with no door, step or handle.
   - The neighbouring shop's fascia on the left is blank in the visible part.

7. The lighting is murky. The whole frame is dim and low in contrast, and the frontage sits in flat gloom. The Hook sheet's overcast is bright, with lively brick colour.

8. The litter bin is minor: a plain green cylinder on a post, with no texture, markings or wear.

Narrower points that do work: the overcast sky, the wet pavement and road with believable sheen, the kerb, the double yellow lines (right for 1990), the brick of the chimney stack, and the period wording on the sign ("Tools · Hardware · Paraffin").

I judged against the upper part of the Hook sheet only. The two KCD2 frames could not be reached, so I made no comparison against them.
