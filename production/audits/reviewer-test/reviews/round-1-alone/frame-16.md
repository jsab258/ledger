# Round one, alone: frame-16

Planted: a floating 'Talk to Rita' prompt over the pavement with nobody there. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

Every fault is in frame-16 (Rita's frontage at night, from the road), listed worst first:

1. The telephone box covers the right third of the frame and its side facing the road is a flat, dark, untextured slab. A real kiosk has glass on that side. The slab has no glazing bars, no handle, no wear and no detail. The box is unlit at night, and no handset can be seen inside it. It also hides the right end of the shop window and display. The dark, wood-like body and the orange "Telephone" lettering do not look like any 1990 British kiosk I can confirm, such as the red K6 or BT's steel KX100. This should be checked against period photographs.

2. Above the shopfront there is only black: no brick, no upper floor, no sash windows. In the Hook sheet the shopfronts sit in a red-brick terrace with sash windows above and a fascia you can read. Here the shopfront reads as a lit box floating in a void. The "PAWNBROKER / JEWELLERY · WATCHES · LOANS" sign is dark red on black and unlit, and can hardly be read. The fascia board itself cannot be made out.

3. The shop window shows no sign of glass. There is no reflection of street lamps or of the street, no glint and no dirt or smears, so it reads as an open hole. That falls short of "correct material", and of the real glass the requirement asks for.

4. There is almost no wear. The cream joinery is evenly painted and clean, with no chips, grime or weathering at the sill and stallriser base. The stallriser is plain and spotless, and the interior walls and floor are clean. The requirement asks for correct wear, and the Hook sheet shows weathered, patched surfaces.

5. A "[E] Talk to Rita" prompt sits over the stallriser, but Rita is nowhere in the frame. The prompt is attached to nothing you can see, and it covers part of the frontage.

6. A black square on the pavement at the lower left, just in front of the door, reads as a missing texture, a stray decal or a hole. It should be checked at full size.

7. The row of small panes above the main window (and the one above the door) looks like flat cream boards, not glass.

8. The light does not read as a 1990 street at night. The pavement is lit flat, even and white from no visible source. No street lamp lights the fascia, the kiosk or the pavement, and there is no orange sodium cast.

9. Narrow period points: a pawnbroker at night has a full window of jewellery and goods with no grille or shutter, and there is no three-balls sign. These may be canon or scope choices rather than faults.

The goods in the window and the lit interior behind them are the strongest part of the frame, but they do not carry it. The two KCD2 frames could not be reached, so the judgement is against the Hook sheet only.
