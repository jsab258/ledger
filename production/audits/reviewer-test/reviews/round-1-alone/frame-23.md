# Round one, alone: frame-23

Planted: nothing (a clean copy). The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL. There is only one picture, frame-23.jpg (Mickey's office through its window, by day), so every fault below is in it. I judged against the upper part of the Hook sheet only, because the two KCD2 frames could not be reached.

1. The glass does not reflect the street. On a daylit shop window the street opposite and the sky should show strongly in the glass. Here the room is seen almost perfectly clearly through every pane. The glass also has no dirt, smears or haze, so the window reads as an empty opening. The requirement names "real glass reflecting the street", and this misses it outright.

2. There is a visible glitch in the top row of small panes above the main window (top of the frame, about y 85 to 150, the whole width from x 580 to 1600). A pale band with a ragged, torn-looking top edge runs across them. It does not read as a reflection or as part of the room. It looks like a broken texture or a broken reflection.

3. The blind has two faults.
   - Shadows: dark shapes on it have no visible cause. There is a round dark blot at about x 880, y 455, and a dark diagonal smear from about x 940 to 1000, y 230 to 470, which runs across the central upright of the window.
   - Fit: the blind runs past that upright and stops in the middle of the right-hand pane (bottom rail ends at about x 990). It is not fitted to any opening, and no fixings show.

4. The frame does not read as daytime. Inside, the lamps make the office brighter than the street. The brickwork and painted frontage outside look dim, as if it were dusk. Daylight should be the stronger light, with the interior partly hidden by reflections.

5. There is too little wear and clutter for a 1990 cab office.
   - The counter is clean, unscuffed and nearly bare: a telephone, a "Bookings" sign, a desk lamp and one small box.
   - No ashtray is clearly visible. The small white object at about x 1005, y 622 might be one, but it cannot be told apart.
   - The radio set cannot be made out. The only candidate is a small box with knobs at about x 900, y 595, with no microphone or base-station look to it.
   - Walls, floor and carpet are clean. There are no papers, mugs, log books or tobacco staining.
   - The kettle (on the filing cabinet) and the telephone are there.

6. Some items look like placeholders.
   - The two flat yellow boxes on the window sill (bottom right) have unreadable text and no surface detail.
   - The plain grey cylinder behind them looks like a primitive shape.
   - The notes on the cork board are blank.
   - The flat black door at the far left has hardly any panel detail and no handle or letterbox.

7. The frontage's material and wear fall short of the Hook sheet.
   - The black pilaster (the upright column) at about x 470 to 570 has a coarse, speckled, bumpy surface. It reads as rough asphalt or a noise texture, not as painted timber or polished stone.
   - The painted uprights (red, blue-grey, dark grey) are flat slabs with no mouldings, no chipped paint and no grime.
   - The Hook sheet shows weathered, patched brick and detailed painted timber. Here the frontage is clean and boxy.

8. Overall finish is below 2026 grade. The lighting is even and flat, with weak contact shadows. The room reads as a small, tidy diorama, not a lived-in office.

What passes: the gold window lettering ("0632 960418", "MINICABS · 24 HOURS") reads correctly from outside. The blue-grey and gold scheme matches Mickey's frontage on the Hook sheet. The clock, back door, fares card, waiting bench and plants are plausible. Nothing in the picture breaks the content rules.
