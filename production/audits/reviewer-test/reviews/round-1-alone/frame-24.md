# Round one, alone: frame-24

Planted: light where none belongs: a warm glow on the blank upper wall of the corner house, no lamp. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL. Every fault below is seen in frame-24 (the street at night, upper part), worst first.

1. Most of the frame is crushed to black. The roofs, the upper storeys of all but the nearest building, the far end of the terrace and the whole left half show no detail you can read. So the frontage's geometry, material and wear cannot be seen. The Hook sheet shows readable brick, slate roofs, chimney pots, arched brick window heads and drainpipes; none of these read here. A 2026 night street would still have sky, moon or ambient fill that lets the shapes and materials read.
2. At the upper right, a small orange glowing ball with a bloom halo floats in the black. There is no post, bracket or fixture, and it lights nothing around it. If it is meant to be the moon, it is the wrong colour, too small and too low, and it sits where the building's end should be. Either way it is a visible fault.
3. The lit brick on the nearest building, above Mickey's, is a saturated blood red, not brick under amber lamp light. It reads as a colour or lighting error and is unlike the Hook sheet's brick.
4. The lit upstairs windows (the tall one above Mickey's and the ones further down the row) are flat, even cream panels. They have no curtains, interior, depth or reflection, and the glazing bars are faint. They read as placeholder glowing cards.
5. The hillside behind, on the left half, is a flat black mass of lumpy tree-like silhouettes with a few pinpoint lights, plus one dim row of houses that shows only small bright rectangles. The Hook sheet's town climbing the hill, with its roofs and gables, is not there.
6. The sky is a flat, even brown-black, with no gradient, cloud or town glow.
7. The shop signs barely read. "MICKEY'S" is dim red lettering on a dark fascia, unlike the Hook sheet's gold lettering on blue-grey. The neighbouring shopfronts' signs cannot be read at all.
8. The lamp posts, nearest and far left, show as evenly glowing orange lines, as if self-lit, rather than as posts lit from their own lamp heads.
9. A narrow point: the visible upper part of Mickey's window shows the lit interior with no reflection of the street or lamps. "Real glass reflecting the street" is not shown here, though the lower part of the window is cropped out.
