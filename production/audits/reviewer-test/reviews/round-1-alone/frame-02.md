# Round one, alone: frame-02

Planted: mirrored text: the kiosk's 'Telephone' sign reads backwards. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

Every fault below is seen in frame-02 (Rita's frontage by day). Worst first:

1. The telephone box looks like a placeholder. It fills the right third of the frame and its near side is a flat, plain grey slab. That slab has no glazing, no frame members, no panel lines, no base and no wear, and it hides the right-hand end of the shop window and the stallriser.
2. The box's "Telephone" sign reads backwards. The yellow lettering on the black fascia at the top of the box is mirrored. It is not a reflection, so this is a plain glitch.
3. Nothing shows wear. The maroon fascia, stallriser panels, right-hand console, white window frames, door and sill are all evenly clean paint. There are no chips, stains, dirt in the corners, streaks under the sill or faded paint. The paving is almost clean too. Next to the weathered brick, stained stone and leadwork in the Hook sheet, this looks like freshly made CG, and the requirement asks for correct wear.
4. The shopfront's shape is too simple. The fascia is a flat board with no projecting cornice or moulding along its top. The console at the top right, where the fascia ends, is a plain block with no carved bracket, and the window has no proper pilasters with capitals on either side. The Hook sheet's shopfronts have a projecting cornice and brackets.
5. The glass barely reflects the street. Looking from the road in daylight, the shop's inside shows clearly, and the only reflection is some faint white marks at the top left of the main window. That glass should carry a strong reflection of the far side of the street and the sky. The glass of the telephone box reflects nothing either.
6. It does not read as day. The whole frame is dim, murky and low in contrast, with no daylight cue at all. It is much darker and flatter than the bright overcast in the Hook sheet and could pass for dusk.
7. The window display is thin and toy-like. A few small, blurry ornaments sit spaced out on a bare red bed. The blue trays across the front look empty. Two thin white upright sticks stand at the left of the display and near the centre mullion, and nothing explains them. A 1990 pawnbroker's window would be packed with rings, watches, cameras and electricals, all with tickets.
8. The white jagged shape at the top left of the main window, just inside the left frame, looks like a stray smear. It does not read as a reflection of anything.
9. These are narrow points:
   - The brass kick plate at the bottom of the door is a speckled, flat gold texture.
   - The gilt fascia lettering is flat, with none of the relief or shading that painted gilding has.
   - The paving is large, uniform slabs with no kerb and little grime.
   - There is no hanging sign of three golden balls, a pawnbroker's usual mark.

The comparison was made against the upper part of the Hook sheet only. The two KCD2 frames could not be reached.
