# Round one, alone: frame-25

Planted: floating object: a copy of the yellow box files hangs in mid-air in front of the blind, nothing under it. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL. I compared it with the upper part of the Hook sheet only, because the two KCD2 frames could not be reached. Every fault below is in frame 25 (Mickey's office through its window, by day), worst first.

1. The glass does not reflect the street. The main panes are almost perfectly clear in daylight, and the room shows through as if there were no glass. The brief's central demand, "real glass reflecting the street", is not met. The only reflections are a ragged band in the top panes (point 4) and a dark curved smudge in the upper right-centre pane, just left of "0632", which looks like a stain rather than anything you could recognise from the street.

2. The goods in the window look like placeholders. There are two plain cream slabs: one high on the left, one on the sill bottom right. They have no visible detail and their labels can't be read. The upper one seems to float in mid-air in front of the blind. If it stands on a glass shelf, the shelf shows only as a thin grey line, with no brackets or edge. Nothing in the window reads as real goods.

3. The room reads as a tidy stage set, not a worn 1990 cab office, and the whole frame falls well short of the Hook sheet and of 2026 quality. The counter is smooth and clean, with no scuffs. The walls are evenly painted, with a little mottling and almost nothing pinned up. The floor is bare, and there is no grime, stains or nicotine yellowing. The clutter is thin. The kettle and telephone can be seen, but no ashtray is clearly visible; at most there is a small white speck on the counter. The radio set can't be told apart from the small grey box beside the lamp.

4. The three top panes show a ragged white-grey band with a torn-looking lower edge, across the full width of the window. It reads as a broken reflection or texture. It also contradicts the clear panes below it, which reflect almost nothing.

5. The frontage materials don't look finished, and they don't match the Hook sheet. The wide black pillar left of the window is uniform speckled noise, not believable stone or painted timber; the Hook sheet shows a painted blue-grey shopfront there. The door at far left is flat black with almost no detail and looks unlit.

6. The frontage shows no wear. The blue-grey window frames are clean, even paint, with no chips, dirt or weathering, so "correct wear" on the frontage is not shown.

7. The venetian blind hangs above the counter with no frame or window behind it, and stops short in mid-air on the right. Its bottom rail runs on past the slats.

To check against canon, not counted as a fault: the number on the glass uses 0632. As far as I recall, that was Newcastle's dialling code, replaced by 091 before 1990; it is also the code British TV used for made-up numbers. Whether it is right depends on where canon puts the town.
