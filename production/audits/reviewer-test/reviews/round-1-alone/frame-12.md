# Round one, alone: frame-12

Planted: duplicated object: a second pillar box stands into the first, half overlapping it. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL. All faults below are in frame-12 (the launderette and the empty unit to let), worst first.

1. The shop glass reflects nothing. In both the launderette window and the empty unit's window you see straight through to the machines or the grey backing, with no street, sky or opposite buildings in the glass. Meanwhile the road beneath is close to a mirror. The requirement asks for real glass reflecting the street, so this misses it outright, and the lighting doesn't agree with itself.

2. The road reflection is wrong. There are two pillar boxes but only one red shape in the wet road, lower left, about one box wide. The second box has no reflection. The rest of the reflection is smeared into vertical streaks and is far too strong for asphalt. There is also a dark horizontal streak in the road just below the kerb at far left.

3. The pillar boxes look like placeholders. They are plain red cylinders with black bases: no posting slot, no collection plate, no royal cipher, no lettering. They are the same model twice, standing touching, which isn't a real street arrangement, and they are slightly tall against the doors.

4. The empty unit's shopfront is a plain black box. It has no pilasters, console brackets or cornice, unlike the Mickey's shopfront on the Hook sheet. For a unit that is to let, the paint is spotless and the glass is clean. The "TO LET" sign has no agent's name or phone number, so it reads as a placeholder. The grey backing behind the glass is the same texture repeated in each pane.

5. There is a geometry fault on the empty unit's window frame. Its white top rail carries on past the end of the glass, across bare brickwork above the white door, and stops in mid-air at about the line between the two doors.

6. The doors are wrong. Both sit flush with the wall, with no step, threshold or recess. The black door is a blank slab with no handle, letter plate or number, and no door in the frame has a number. The two galvanised dustbins are identical copies standing across both doorways.

7. The upper storey reads as a flat brick wall with small, shallow windows. The middle window's panes are flat tan with no glass, which looks like an unlit material. Compared with the Hook sheet's tall arched sash windows on deep stone sills, the openings are too small and too flush.

8. At the right edge, the next building's upper storey is a blank, spotless cream panel with no window, texture or staining, beside heavily stained brick.

9. The street lamp is a bare column with no base, access door or number plate. Its slim flat head looks like a modern LED lantern, not a 1990 sodium lamp.

10. Nothing shows dirt where things meet the ground. The launderette's cream surround and riser, the shopfront bases, the bottoms of the doors and the bases of the pillar boxes are all clean against wet paving. Only the brick is weathered.

11. The road at the bottom of the frame has a fine, even crazing of cracks that looks like a tiled texture, not British asphalt with patches and repairs.

12. The frame's top edge cuts off the chimney stacks, so their caps and pots, a strong feature of the Hook sheet, can't be seen. This is a narrow framing point.

The poll tax and jumble sale posters fit 1990, and the brick staining is close to the Hook sheet. The KCD2 frames could not be reached, so nothing was judged against them.
