# Round one, alone: frame-09

Planted: hole in a wall: a ragged hole through the corner wall with sky behind it. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL.

Every fault, worst first. All are in frame-09 (the right-hand part of Mickey's frontage from the hook camera, with the door and the corner):

1. There is a flat, light-grey octagon on the brick side wall, right of centre at about chest height. It has no texture or shading and reads as a placeholder or missing asset. This fault fails the frame by itself.
2. The shop window glass doesn't reflect the street. The interior (shelves of binders, a beige monitor, a desk, the tube light) shows through as if there were no glass. Nothing of the sky, the street or the buildings opposite appears on it. Under a grey sky like the Hook sheet's, a shop window would carry a strong reflection, and the requirement asks for "real glass reflecting the street".
3. The maroon side door's frame is wrong. The white surround runs up its left side and across the top, but on the right the door runs straight into the blue pilaster, so the pilaster appears to cut into the door frame. Above the door, the gap between the door head and the fascia is bare brick with no fanlight or lintel, so that part of the shopfront looks unfinished.
4. The upper window has no head. It is a plain hole in the brick with no lintel or brick arch, while every upper window on the Hook sheet has a segmental brick arch.
5. The side wall and the corner are too clean and too bare. The brick is evenly patterned across a wide blank wall, and the corner edge is perfectly straight. There is no downpipe or other rainwater goods. The Hook sheet's end wall has patches, downpipes and fittings, and this wall has none.
6. The shopfront woodwork, the blue panelled shop door, the stall-riser tiles and the maroon door are spotless, with no chipped paint, fading or grime at the bottom. The requirement asks for wear, and the weathered brickwork above makes them look pasted on.

What the frame gets right: the gilt "CKEY'S" lettering on the dark fascia, the console bracket on the pilaster, the dentil course under the eaves, the overall brick colour and the wet flagstones with their reflections all sit well against the Hook sheet. The two KCD2 frames could not be reached, so the frame was judged against the Hook sheet only.
