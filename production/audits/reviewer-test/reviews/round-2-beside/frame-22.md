# Round two, beside the clean original: frame-22

Planted: z-fighting: jagged pale stripes flicker through one panel of the counter's front. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL.

Worst first:

1. There is a new glitch on the counter in frame-22. The counter's second front panel, between the FARES card and the window's middle post, is covered in cream zigzag stripes. They stop exactly at the panel's edges, so they look like a broken material or two surfaces fighting, not a real finish or a reflection. In frame-22-before the same panel is plain brown. This is the only difference between the two pictures, so the change makes the view worse and adds nothing better. The requirement says "no glitches".

2. The glass shows no reflection of the street (both pictures). The window looks like an open hole. There are no street lights or buildings mirrored in it, and no dirt or sheen on the pane. The requirement asks for real glass reflecting the street.

3. The office does not have the wear and clutter of a 1990 cab office (both pictures). There are no ashtrays anywhere. A radio set can't be made out; the small grey box on the counter is too unclear to read as one. The counter top and front are clean and unscuffed. The walls have only faint stains. The room reads as a tidy computer model, not a room people use every day. The kettle and telephone are there.

4. The left third of the frame looks unfinished (both pictures). It is made of large flat red and orange panels with no material, joints or detail. The brown post next to the window has a noisy, rock-like texture that reads as neither painted timber nor brick. Next to the Hook sheet, where Mickey's front is dark blue-grey painted joinery set in red brick, this does not match and looks like placeholder work.

5. The window display looks like placeholders (both pictures). Below the window, bottom right, there are two plain yellow boxes and a grey cylinder. They read as stand-in blocks, not period goods. The potted plant on the left is acceptable.

6. The window frame and glazing bars are flat dark brown with no paint, chips, wear or edge detail (both pictures). Their colour also doesn't match the blue-grey of the Hook sheet's shopfront.

7. Narrow: the notes on the noticeboard and the posters on the left wall are blank paper (both pictures).

8. Narrow: the venetian blind ends in a hard bright line that runs past the slats. The slats look partly see-through against the back wall (both pictures).

I judged against the upper Hook sheet only, because the two KCD2 frames could not be reached.
