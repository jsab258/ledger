# Round two, beside the clean original: frame-16

Planted: a floating 'Talk to Rita' prompt over the pavement with nobody there. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

Every fault below is in frame-16 (Rita's frontage at night). All of them except number 4 are also in frame-16-before.

1. There is no wear anywhere. The shopfront woodwork, the sills, the stall riser under the window, the door and its kick plate, and the telephone box are all smooth, evenly coloured surfaces. There are no chips, dirt, streaks, grain or patched paint. The requirement asks for correct material and wear. Next to the weathered brick and aged paint in the Hook sheet, this reads as a clean computer model, not a 1990 shop.

2. The glass doesn't read as glass. The shop window, the door's upper glazing and the telephone box glass show no reflection at all: no street lamp, no pavement, no glint on the glass edges, no dirt on the panes. The big window looks like an open hole into the shop.

3. The lighting doesn't hang together. The pavement in the bottom strip is the brightest thing in the frame. The stall riser just above it is nearly black, and so are the front of the telephone box and the whole upper third. The lettering ("PAWNBROKER / JEWELLERY · WATCHES · LOANS") floats in a black void, barely legible. There is no visible fascia board, brickwork or first-floor window above it. Whatever lights the pavement should also light the box, the fascia and the building front.

4. A game prompt reading "[E] Talk to Rita" sits in a plain black box over the stall riser, low in the centre. Rita isn't visible anywhere in the frame. A plain prompt like this has no place in a fixed street view. It is also the only difference from frame-16-before.

5. There is a pure black square on the pavement at lower left, under the shop door's edge. It reads as a missing texture or a hole, not as litter or a drain. There is also a small black dot on the pavement at lower centre.

6. The telephone box covers about a third of the frame, including the right part of the window. Its right side is a flat, dark slab with no detail. Looking through its glass you see straight into the shop: there is no handset, back panel or fittings, so the box looks empty. It also casts no shadow on the bright pavement.

7. The pavement flags are pale cream with almost no texture, like polished indoor tiles. They should look like grey, stained British concrete paving slabs.

8. Smaller point, to check against a reference photograph: the shop is fully lit at night, with jewellery trays still in the window and no grille or shutter. A 1990 pawnbroker would normally clear the window and secure the front overnight. The window display is also sparse for a pawnbroker, mostly single ornaments spaced along red cloth.

Note: apart from the prompt, frame-16 is identical to frame-16-before, so the frontage itself has no visible improvement. I judged against the upper Hook sheet only, because the two KCD2 frames couldn't be reached.
