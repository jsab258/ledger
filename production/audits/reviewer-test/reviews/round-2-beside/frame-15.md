# Round two, beside the clean original: frame-15

Planted: a dark seam stripe down the roof's slates from ridge to eaves. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL. I judged against the Hook sheet's upper part only, because the two KCD2 frames could not be reached.

1. **The shop window glass is a placeholder.** This is in the new frame and the before frame. Every pane of the Ironmonger's window is a flat, opaque olive-mustard fill. There are no goods behind it, no depth and no reflection of the street. The neighbouring shop's panes on the left are the same, and so is the black slab in the left-hand pane. The requirement asks for real glass reflecting the street and a window of verified CC0 goods; neither is there.

2. **The change added a new glitch, and it is the only visible difference.** This is in the new frame only. A thin dark line now runs from high on the roof, left of the chimney, down across the roof slope. It ends on the fascia just above and left of the first "I" of IRONMONGER, with nothing at either end to hang from. It reads as a stray wire or a rendering artifact. Apart from this line, the new frame matches the before frame, so the only visible change since the last look is a new fault.

3. **The roof has a geometry fault.** This is in both frames. A hard diagonal crease runs across what should be a single plain roof slope, from near the chimney down to the left eaves. It reads as two badly joined planes, not a real hip or valley.

4. **The roof material and the building's shape are wrong against the Hook sheet.** This is in both frames. The roof reads as ribbed or corrugated sheet with an even plastic wet sheen, not worn slate. The building is a single-storey shed with no upper floor and no sash windows. It has no gutter or downpipe along the frontage. The Hook sheet shows two-storey red-brick terraces with slate roofs, gutters and downpipes.

5. **There is no wear anywhere on the frontage.** This is in both frames. The maroon fascia, the pilaster and the stall riser are flat, clean colour with no grime, staining, chipped paint or texture. The dark blue-grey riser under the window is the same. The white window frames are thick, clean, square bars with no timber detail or weathering. This is far below "a frontage of correct material and wear" and below the weathered brick and paint in the Hook sheet.

6. **The chimney has no pots.** This is in both frames. It is a flat-topped brick stack, where every stack in the Hook sheet carries clay pots.

7. **The shopfront has no joinery.** This is in both frames. The lettering is painted straight onto a plain board, with no cornice, console brackets or moulding. The pilaster between the two shops is a plain box. The Hook sheet's shopfronts have a proper cornice and fascia.

8. **The frontage is underexposed and flat.** This is in both frames. The whole front sits in murky, low-contrast shadow under a bright sky, with no modelling, and it looks unfinished rather than overcast.

The pavement, the double yellow lines and the green litter bin are acceptable for a 1990 British street. The faults above are not narrow points, so this would not pass in a 2026 game.
