# Round two, beside the clean original: frame-20

Planted: nothing (a clean copy). The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

Faults, worst first. All are seen in frame-20.jpg (the new frame) unless another picture is named.

1. The telephone kiosk in front of the pawnbroker is a placeholder. Its large face is a blank, flat, light grey slab with no glazing, door, panel lines, material or wear. Its open side is a dark empty box with one ledge and no handset. Apart from a small "Telephone" label at the top left corner, it reads as a grey box. It stands in the middle of the view, and nothing like it would pass in a 2026 game.

2. A free-standing brick block sits on the pavement between the kiosk and the fishmonger. It is a plain cube of brick with a dark cap and no clear purpose: not a bin, a planter or a pier that reads as such. It looks like a stand-in, and it covers the bottom left of the fishmonger's window and stallriser.

3. The shop glass does not reflect anything. Both shop windows show their interiors with no reflection of the street opposite or the grey sky, so they read as open holes rather than glass. On an overcast day plate glass should mirror the street strongly. The upper window on the fishmonger's side is a flat, even grey-white pane.

4. There is too little wear for a 1990 street. The cream render above the pawnbroker is clean and even, with no staining under the sill, no streaks and no patching. Both stallrisers and the fascias are spotless, and the pavement is clean and evenly flat, with no grime at the building line or the kerb. The brick on the fishmonger's side has a little darkening near the eaves, and that is all.

5. The upper storeys are sparse next to the Hook sheet (hook-sheet-upper.png). Each building has one small, plain window in a mostly blank wall, with no brick arch or lintel, little sill detail and no sash character. The Hook sheet's frontages carry tall white sash windows under arched brick heads, and these fall well short of them.

6. The wet road has hard-edged pale patches: a light horizontal band below the pawnbroker and a lighter rectangle below the brick block. They look like stamped decals rather than puddles or reflections, and they do not line up with anything above them.

7. The camera cuts both chimney stacks off at the top edge, so no pots show. The chimneys are one of the Hook sheet's strongest features, and here they are lost.

8. A narrow point: the pawnbroker has no hanging three-ball sign or other mark of the trade apart from the fascia lettering.

Compared with frame-20-before.jpg, I see no difference by eye between the before and after pictures. I could not run a pixel comparison because I was told to run nothing. If this change was meant to show in this view, it does not. If it was not meant to touch this view, nothing here has gone backwards, but every fault above was already there before the change.

The KCD2 frames could not be reached, so this verdict rests on the Hook sheet's upper part alone.
