# Round two, beside the clean original: frame-13

Planted: untextured placeholder: the fish shop's upper window is a flat grey panel. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

Faults, worst first:

1. The fishmonger's upstairs window is now a placeholder (new picture, upper right, brick frontage). It is a flat, even grey rectangle with no frame, glazing bars, glass, reflection or depth. Only the stone sill is left. In the before picture the same spot had a white sash window with a curtain behind it. So the one visible change is a step backwards: a missing material or untextured stand-in replaced a finished window.

2. The telephone kiosk reads as a blockout (both pictures). Its side is a blank, flat grey slab with no glass, panel lines, frame, texture or wear. Only the black "Telephone" header gives it any detail. A 1990 street kiosk had glazed sides and a framed metal body. This fails the "no visible placeholder" bar.

3. The shop glass does not reflect the street (both pictures). The pawnbroker's and fishmonger's windows look like open holes onto their displays. Under the overcast daylight shown, you would expect clear reflections of the road and the buildings opposite. The requirement asks for real glass reflecting the street, and both shopfronts are below that.

4. The roof and chimneys are thin next to the Hook sheet (both pictures). The roof is a smooth grey slab with no slate courses, ridge or visible gutter. Both chimney stacks are bare brick with no clay pots, while every stack on the Hook sheet carries pots.

5. There is an unexplained brick block on the pavement beside the kiosk, in front of the fishmonger's stall riser (both pictures). It has no clear purpose and looks like a stray stand-in object.

6. The fronts look templated (both pictures). Both shops have the same maroon fascia and the same gold lettering, so the row looks copy-pasted. The Hook sheet's shopfronts each differ in colour and lettering. The upstairs render and brick are also fairly clean for 1990 wear, with little staining under the sills or at the parapet. These are narrow points.

The work was judged against the upper part of the Hook sheet only. The two KCD2 frames could not be reached, so nothing was checked against them.
