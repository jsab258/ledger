# Round two, beside the clean original: frame-02

Planted: mirrored text: the kiosk's 'Telephone' sign reads backwards. The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

Faults, worst first. All are seen in frame-02.jpg, the frame after the change.

1. The "Telephone" sign on the kiosk is mirrored and reads "enohqeleT". In frame-02-before.jpg it read correctly. Everything else in the two pictures is the same, so the only visible change in this step is a new fault.

2. The telephone kiosk looks like a placeholder. Its side facing the camera is a flat, plain grey slab that fills about a third of the frame. It has no panel seams, fixings, stickers, dirt or wear. No telephone, shelf or directory can be seen inside it. Nothing in the Hook sheet is this bare.

3. The shop window glass does not reflect the street. It reads as almost clear glass straight into the shop, and the door glass and the transom lights are the same. The only mark on it is a pale, shapeless patch in the top left corner of the main window, just right of the door frame. It reads as an artefact, not a reflection.

4. The frontage has no wear at all. The white joinery and door, the maroon fascia and the panelled base below the window are perfectly clean flat paint. There are no chips, dirt streaks, grime where the base meets the pavement, or worn brass. The Hook sheet's street is weathered and grimy; this front looks new-built.

5. The window goods are thin for a 1990 pawnbroker. A few small items sit evenly spaced on the red baize, with no tickets or price tags and no crowding. The text on the signs inside the shop cannot be read.

6. The light is dim and flat. The picture reads more like dusk than the day it is meant to show, and the inside of the shop is murky. Next to the Hook sheet's overcast daylight it looks dull and computer-made.

7. The kiosk sits close to the camera and hides about a third of the shop window, so the view does not show the frontage it is meant to prove.

Note: the two KCD2 frames could not be reached. These pictures were judged against the upper part of the Hook sheet only.
