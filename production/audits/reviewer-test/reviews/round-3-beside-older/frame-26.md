# Realism check, beside the same view two days earlier: frame-26

Planted: nothing (a clean copy). The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

Faults, worst first:

1. The door does not sit in the shopfront properly (frame-26). It is a hole in the brick next to the shopfront, with plain brick filling the space between the fascia and the top of the door. There is no fanlight or transom above it. The white door frame runs only up the left side and across the top, and the right edge of the door runs straight into the blue pilaster. The door is a flat maroon slab with no panels. It still reads as a placeholder opening. The before picture has the same fault, so this change did not fix it.

2. The glass does not reflect the street (frame-26). Both panes are perfectly clear and show only the room inside: shelves, a monitor, the desk and the strip light. The pavement below is wet and shiny, yet the windows show no sign of the street opposite or the sky. The requirement asks for "real glass reflecting the street", and this picture does not show it.

3. The frontage has no wear (frame-26). The blue joinery, pilaster, fascia and stallriser panel are spotless. There are no chips, no dirt along the base and no weathering on the sill. The new brass kick plate at the foot of the door looks brand new, and its texture is speckled and blotchy rather than worn metal. A 1990 cab office front should look used.

4. The upper window does not match the Hook sheet (frame-26 against the Hook sheet). The Hook has timber sash windows with glazing bars under curved brick arches. Here the window is a flat white modern frame with blinds, set in a plain rectangular opening with no arch or lintel visible above it.

5. There are no gutters or downpipes (frame-26 against the Hook sheet). The Hook sheet has black downpipes and gutters running down the front. Here neither the front nor the corner has any. The side wall, half the frame, is one unbroken sheet of brick with an even band of dark staining across its lower half. It looks unfinished rather than weathered.

6. The shopfront trim is too plain (frame-26 against the Hook sheet). The cornice over the fascia is a thin flat slab, and the bracket at the top of the pilaster is a plain box. The Hook's shopfront has a projecting moulded cornice.

7. The brick is too even (frame-26 against the Hook sheet). It is a darker, more uniform red-brown than the Hook's varied orange brick with its darker bricks and patched repairs, and the wall shows no repairs or patching.

What did change since the before picture: the blank blue panel that blocked the upper part of the right-hand window is gone. That pane is now glazed and shows the room inside. The door gained a knob and the brass kick plate. These are real improvements, but faults 1 to 3 go to the core of this item's requirement (correct geometry, real glass, 1990 wear). They are not narrow points for later steps.

Limits: the picture is a reduced 508 x 900 crop, so fine brick and paint texture could not be judged at full detail. The two KCD2 frames could not be reached, so nothing was judged against them.
