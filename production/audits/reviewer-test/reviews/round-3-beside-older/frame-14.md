# Realism check, beside the same view two days earlier: frame-14

Planted: a ragged white strip across the top pane over the blind (the night picture had none). The reviewer was not told. Its reply, word for word:

---

FAIL

Faults, worst first. "Frame-14" is the new picture and "before" is frame-14-before.

1. A new glitch, seen in frame-14 only. The upper-left transom pane, above the blind and left of the centre mullion (roughly x 590–920, y 90–160), has a flat white patch with a jagged lower edge like a skyline. Its top and sides are cut off straight at the pane. It is daylight-white in a night scene and sits in one pane only, so it reads as a broken reflection or texture, not glass. In the before picture this pane is plain beige.

2. The glass does not reflect the street, in either picture. Apart from the glitch above, every pane is perfectly clear. There are no street lamps, no opposite frontages and no hint of the pavement side on the glass. "Real glass reflecting the street" is not met.

3. It is not 2026-grade when set against the Hook sheet, in frame-14 and before. The sheet is photographic: weathered brick, slate, painted timber. This room reads as a clean, evenly lit, low-detail render. The walls are plain, the carpet is one flat grey and the props are simple shapes.

4. There is no 1990 cab-office wear or clutter, in frame-14 and before.
   - The counter top is nearly bare, and its new panelled front is smooth with no scuffs.
   - The carpet and walls show no marks or dirt.
   - No ashtray can be made out anywhere. The white object on the bench and the small white object on the counter cannot be identified.
   - The radio set does not read as a dispatch radio. The only candidate is the small grey box on the back shelf right of the lamp.
   - The notice-board slips and the wall sheets on the left are blank pale rectangles, which look like placeholders.
   - The kettle and telephone are now present and readable, which is progress.

5. The frontage materials are wrong, in frame-14 and before. The red and orange pilaster strips on the left are flat, untextured, unworn colour. The stone pier beside the glass has an over-strong noise texture that looks like tree bark. Neither matches Mickey's dark blue-grey painted shopfront on the Hook sheet.

6. The frontage geometry is wrong, in frame-14 and before. The right-hand window bar leans visibly, from about x 1360 at the transom to about x 1470 at the bottom. Every other upright stands vertical: the centre mullion, the door frame, the wall corners and the pier.

7. The blind has got worse, seen in frame-14 against before. Its slats are now soft and smeared with low contrast, ghosted shapes show through them, and a bright line runs along the bottom rail. In the before picture the slats were crisp. It now reads as a rendering fault rather than a blind.

8. The back doorway is empty, in frame-14 and before. The open door shows only a flat light-grey plane with no room behind it, which reads as a void.

9. A narrow point, in frame-14 and before. The window lettering ("0632 960418", "MINICABS · 24 HOURS") looks like thick glossy black letters floating in space, not signwriting or vinyl on a pane of glass.

I judged against the Hook sheet only, because the two KCD2 frames could not be reached.
