# Lab test 3: one jacket from a pre-1929 tailoring draft

Started 7 October 2026. The method on trial: take a published draft as the exact target, reproduce its pattern pieces in code with every rule re-measured automatically, then sew and drape it on Ron's body in Blender and compare its front and side outlines with period photographs; report honestly whether the lapel holds.

Context: the builder's Marvelous Designer jacket failed its fresh review on 2 October on the cut and the lapel (production/art/clothing/md-suit-jacket/review-1-ingame-ron.md): the right lapel collapsed, the front hung as a sack, the shoulders boxy, the sleeves full. Its pattern came from FreeSewing's Jaeger, corrected by measurement. This test asks whether a period draft, followed exactly and checked, does better.

Seam checks (pattern_check.py): side seams, shoulder ease (1/4 in, once X was applied to the curved seam's length as the plate shows), fish, forearm and hind-arm seams, sleeve head ease, collar to neck: all pass for the example and for Ron.

Thornton himself says the lapels ("turns") "set all rules at defiance" and depend on taste (p. 14).
- Ron's measures (pattern.py, RON): breast 24 3/4, waist 21 3/4, seat 23 1/4 (half, over waistcoat and trousers), natural waist length 18 1/4, length 31 1/2, across back 8 1/4, elbow 21 1/2, sleeve 33 1/2; no shoulder measures (the loop round his shoulder could not be taken reliably off the posed mesh: two tries cut the hanging arm lengthwise), so the scale is the half breast, as the Normal Model (p. 22) does.
- 7 Oct, 22:10, drape v1 (garment.py, tools/blender_sew.py; Blender 5.2 cloth, headless, CPU, 2 min): FAILED. The body pieces were pulled through Ron: every piece was wrapped on a cylinder, so the front and back shoulder seams started on opposite sides of the shoulder and were sewn straight through it; the sides began inside the hanging arms. Only the sleeves stayed on.
- 7 Oct, 22:15, drape v2 (6 min): FAILED. Sewn in MetaHuman's A-pose on the full body (head on: the body-only mesh has an open neck the collar could fall into), the band under each top edge laid over the shoulder ridge and round the neck, the arms lowered from frame 80 to 140. The frames show the sleeves riding up onto the shoulders by frame 20 and, from frame 30, a point flying off; by frame 140 the jacket hung off the right hand. The cause, found in the mesh: two outline points at the fish's mouth in each front belonged to no triangle (the mesher's slit was 0.12 in wide), so only seam springs held them; and the sleeve heads started 12 to 21 cm from their armholes.
- 7 Oct, 22:25, drape v3 set up: no lone points (the mesher now ties any to its neighbours; the fish's mouth 0.3 in), each sleeve wrapped by its place between its own forearm and hind-arm edges (the two pieces' seams now start together), the band under each sleeve head laid along its armhole. Every seam now starts within 8 cm of its partner (median 0.1 to 6 cm by seam).
- 7 Oct, about 22:30-22:40, drapes v4 and v5: FAILED (v4 slid off the shoulders as the arms came down, the buttons parting; v5, arms left up and stronger sewing, fell apart on the right). The frames show why both failed: the cloth never relaxed toward its flat lengths (some edges stayed 6 times longer than cut for the whole run). **Blender 5.2's "Rest Shape Key" is ignored when run headless**: a 2 m strip given a 1 m rest key stays 2 m, in three orderings tried (tests in F:/LedgerTools/lab/tmp/rest_test*.py). So v3 to v5 all took their distorted starting shape as the cloth's natural shape, which is what pulled the sleeves up. The documented alternative works: "Dynamic Mesh" with a shape key eased from the placed pieces to the flat pattern (the strip shrinks to 1 m in four frames). tools/blender_sew.py now does that (v6 on).
- Two tries, then research: the two-tries rule was broken here (five drapes before stopping to test the tool's own rest-shape setting in isolation). The isolation test took ten minutes and found the cause; it should have come after v2.
- 7 Oct, about 22:45, drape v6 (Dynamic Mesh rest): the cloth reached its cut lengths (median stretch 1.000) and the seams closed (median 2 mm), but the whole jacket was thrown 40 cm sideways through Ron between frames 10 and 20 and ended in a heap: Blender blends the rest shape linearly from the placed pieces to the flat layout, which lay elsewhere and turned over, so mid-way the rest lengths collapsed. Fixed by turning and moving each flat piece rigidly onto its placed position first (lengths unchanged; the blend then never shortens an edge below 0.73 of its cut length).
- 7 Oct, about 22:52, drape v7: stays on Ron with the right lengths (stretch p95 1.20) but ends at his waist instead of covering the seat, the sleeves bunched and some seams 24 cm open. Probable root cause, present in every run so far: I set Blender's cloth mass as a physical value (0.3 kg/m2 over 8,430 points, 0.06 g each); Blender's mass is per point on its own scale (presets 0.15 to 1), so gravity was negligible against the springs and nothing ever hung. v8 uses Blender's scale (0.3 a point, wool-like stiffness).
- 7 Oct, about 23:00, drape v8 (Blender-scale mass 0.3 a point, wool-like stiffness, sewn in the A-pose): **the first real jacket**: on Ron, sleeves to the wrists, seams closed (median 1 mm, 95th percentile 2.2 cm), every edge within 10% of its cut length (95th percentile 1.10), rounded front, buttoned. The lapels do not hold: each front ends in a thin crumpled roll down a deep V; no lapel, no notch; the collar a crumpled band (33-38% of the turned-over points still lie turned on the chest, checks/drape_v8.json).
- v9 (from v8's drape, the arms lowered over 90 frames): stays on, hangs to the seat; reads as a loose cardigan rather than a tailored lounge; lapels as v8. Against the photographs' bands (photo-landmarks.json): shoulder width 0.88 of the jacket's length (photos 0.56-0.71: Ron is broad, the sleeves full), top button 0.15 (photos 0.25-0.35), notch at the neck (the lapel collapsed): all three out of band.
- Lapel, try 2, v10: the canvas in the lapel, roll line and collar ten times stiffer (bending 300): 41% held, still a thin roll. Try 3, v11 (researched direction: the tailor's pad-stitching and pressing, as tacks from each turned point to the front beneath): the lapel lies flat and invisible on the chest; the front closes to the neck; no lapel, no notch. **Three failures: the lapel set aside, as the rules require.** It does not hold in Blender's cloth by any of the three routes tried.

## Result

- **The pattern: done, exact.** Thornton's Standard Lounge Coat (c. 1911) reproduced by code for the book's example and for Ron: all sixteen of the book's worked values, every rule re-measured, the plate within 0.15 in on average, every sewn edge within its allowed difference (checks/pattern_example.json, pattern_ron.json; previews jacket-pattern-*.jpg). The checks caught four errors on the way: one in the helper's transcription, three of mine.
- **The drape: a jacket that hangs, after eleven runs.** v8 (arms out) and v9 (arms down) stay on Ron with every edge within 10% of its cut length and the seams closed (previews jacket-v8-*, jacket-v9-*). Getting there took two faults of the tool found only by testing it in isolation (the rest-shape setting ignored headless; Blender's mass scale), plus five faults of mine in placing the pieces.
- **The lapel: does not hold.** By three routes (as cut and canvassed; ten times stiffer canvas; tacked down like pad-stitching) the turned lapel either collapses into a thin roll down a deep V or lies flat and invisible; no route gave a lapel with a notch (checks/drape_v8 to v11.json; previews jacket-v8-lapels, jacket-v11-lapels-tacked).
- **Against the photographs**: in the arms-down drape all three ratios fall outside the photographs' bands (shoulders 0.88 of the length against 0.56-0.71, top button 0.15 against 0.25-0.35, the notch at the neck); with the arms out they come close (0.72-0.75, 0.26-0.28, 0.07-0.10). The side view could not be compared: no true profile photograph survived the licence and age checks.
- **Fresh review (REVIEW.md, v9 against the photographs): FAIL, broad.** It reads as a loose wrap or smock, not a lounge jacket: sleeves about twice the arm's width and gathered at the wrist, no shoulder line, the fronts open in a deep V and crossing like a wrap, too short (the back stops at the top of the seat), the collar a puffy ring on the shoulders, the lapels collapsed into a ridge on both sides; ragged edges and what look like open seams and gaping back armholes (the reviewer says the seams were read from the pictures, not measured; the measured seam gaps are 1 mm median, 2 cm at the 95th percentile).

## Verdict on the method

**It worked for the pattern and did not reach the garment.**
- **The pattern** is where an exact target fits: the book prints its rules and a worked value for each, and the plate draws them. Checking every point against all three caught four errors in about an hour and a half (one in a helper's transcription, three of mine) and produced Ron's pattern by the book. The lapel's shape is not in the target at all: Thornton leaves it to the diagram and says it depends on taste.
- **The drape** is not checked by the target. Eleven simulation runs went to tool faults (two found only by testing Blender's cloth in isolation), placement faults and the lapel; the best jacket fails its review broadly. The lapel did not hold by any of three routes, the same failure the builder's Marvelous Designer jacket met on 2 October. The bottleneck is turning cloth into a tailored garment (pressing, canvas, padding: what holds a real lapel), and an exact pattern does not supply it.

**Cost:** about an hour of the lab's time by the log (54 minutes logged to the jacket; its drape runs ran on the processor while the window was logged, so the true share is somewhat more), plus 88 minutes of two helpers (manual, photographs), eleven drape runs of 1.5 to 6 minutes each, and one fresh review (3 minutes).

**Adopt:** the drafting, yes, wherever a garment is cut from a pattern (it is exact, checkable and cheap; the pattern files can feed any garment tool). Draping tailored jackets in Blender's cloth, no: stop spending on it. The direction the window suggests, untested here: build the jacket as a modelled, skinned mesh whose outline and seams are checked against the drafted pattern the way the window was checked against Ellis, with only the hem simulated (as the project already prefers for game clothes).
