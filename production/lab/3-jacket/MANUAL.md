# Pattern source for the lounge jacket: Thornton, International System (2nd ed.)

Sourced 7 October 2026 for the garment test. This file is a transcription only. No coordinates were computed and no values invented. The machine-readable version is `draft-source.json` in this folder, using the same step ids.

## 1. The book

- **Title:** *The International System of Garment Cutting, including Coats, Trousers, Breeches, and Vests, with Diagrams and Full Instructions for Dealing with all Forms of Disproportion*
- **Author:** J. P. Thornton. His other books include *Sectional System of Gentlemen's Garment Cutting*. The title page lists him as twelve years editor of *Minister's Gazette* and "Twenty-four years Teacher of Cutting at the London Polytechnic, Regent Street".
- **Publisher and place:** The Thornton Institute, 24-25 Castle Street East, Oxford Street, London W. The imprint reads "Published by J. P. Thornton".
- **Edition:** "SECOND EDITION" (title page, leaf 3). "All rights reserved", "Price twenty-five shillings net".
- **Date:** none is printed.
  - The latest date in the text is 1910. Page 5 says: "The closing meeting of the Metropolitan Society's yearly (1910) lecture session ... Mr. J. P. Thornton ... took as his subject the new 'International Coat System'." So this second edition came out after 1910.
  - Library and dealer listings give c. 1911-1912 for the work. I saw these only as search-engine summaries: HathiTrust record 102876853 and a WorthPoint listing, and both pages refused direct access (HTTP 403). They are not evidence, only a pointer.
  - A 1935 "Tenth edition, Jubilee edition" exists (Open Library), which notes "First edition was 1893", counting from the *Sectional System*.
  - **Working date: c. 1911-1912, not proven by a printed date.**
- **Scan used:** archive.org item `Thornton_International_System_of_Garment_Cutting`, https://archive.org/details/Thornton_International_System_of_Garment_Cutting
  - Photographed in 2019 from the library of the Swedish National Armoury (Livrustkammaren).
  - Pages are 4959 x 7017 px with no watermark.
  - **Scan licence: Public Domain Mark 1.0**, set by the uploader (archive.org `licenseurl` http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/).
  - archive.org's metadata date "1885" is wrong; the book itself reports 1910.
- **Every transcription below comes from this scan's page images**, read directly. The djvu OCR was used only to find pages. Where OCR and image differ, the image wins; Section 6 lists the corrections.

### Copyright status

- **The rules are facts from a public-domain text.** The draft's rules (points, distances, proportions) are a method and facts taken from a public-domain book. Encoding them in code copies no protected expression. The scan carries the Public Domain Mark.
- **United States:** In 2026, works published in 1930 or earlier are in the US public domain (95 years from publication). This edition was published after 1910, and the evidence points to c. 1911-1912, so it is public domain. The residual risk is a publication after 1930, which nothing suggests. That would also need the edition to fall between the c. 1911 work and the 1935 tenth edition.
- **United Kingdom (and Switzerland):** protection lasts the author's life plus 70 years.
  - I could not find Thornton's death year.
  - The preface (c. 1911) speaks of "practically fifty years' diverse experiments", so he was born about 1845-1860.
  - A "James Patrick Thornton" appears in the Polytechnic Tailoring School staff register for 1921-25 (University of Westminster archive, RSP/2/3/3), possibly the same man. A bookseller listing names him "Joseph Patrick".
  - UK copyright would survive only if he died after 1955, aged roughly 95-110. That is implausible, so the book is treated as UK public domain. **This is an inference, not a documented death date.**
  - The publisher's right in the typographical arrangement (25 years) expired long ago.
- **The scan itself:** it is a faithful photograph of flat public-domain pages, marked PD by the uploader. No watermark or licence condition appears on the images.

**The International was chosen for these reasons:**
- Its *Standard Lounge Coat* is single-breasted with notched (step) lapels and a separate collar (Plate 16 and the figure on p. 53).
- Almost every construction point has a written rule and a printed worked value in brackets.
- The collar draft is explicitly for "Lounge and Morning Coats", and the standard sleeve is on its own page.
- Every rule is printed three times, in English, French and German. The French and German columns give an independent check on damaged English print, and were used for that (Section 6).
- It is the cleanest scan with an explicit PD mark.
- Its weakness is the unprinted date (Section 1).

## 4. How the system works

### Measures (pp. 17-18, Plate 1; lounge header p. 54)

The lounge header prints: "Measures: 16½, 29, 7½, 20½, 31. Breast, 18 / Waist, 16 / Seat, 19 | Width shoulder measure, 27 ⅔ = 18 inch scale. Depth shoulder measure, 28 = 1 inch excess." The lounge "Measurements" block repeats them: waist length 16½, skirt length 29, half breast 18, half waist 16, width shoulder 27, depth 28.

| Name in JSON | Book's measure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| natural_waist_length | 1st, A to B fig. 1: seventh vertebra (top of back seam) to the back seam level with the side hollow of the waist, over the coat | 16½ |
| fashion_length | lounge "Skirt length" / "fashion length" | 29 |
| across_back | 4th, back width (or stretch), E to F fig. 2, "decided by fashion" | 7½ |
| elbow_length | 5th, E to G fig. 2 | 20½ |
| sleeve_length | 6th, E through G to H fig. 2 | 31 |
| width_shoulder | 7th, A, B, C to D fig. 3: from the back seam level with the centre of the scye, over the front-shoulder hollow, round the front of the scye, under the arm and back; close, over the coat | 27 |
| depth_shoulder | 8th, E, B, C, F to E fig. 3: from a mark one inch below the desired collar crease at the centre back, over the front shoulder, round the front scye and diagonally back; normally width + 1 | 28 |
| half_breast | 9th, I to J fig. 4, over the waistcoat, horizontal; **used as a half measure** | 18 (a 36 breast) |
| half_waist | 10th, K to L fig. 4, on the vest; **half** | 16 |
| half_seat | 11th, M to N fig. 5, over the trousers; **half** | 19 |

"Standard measurements for the average figure" (p. 18) are the same set, plus coat waist length 18 and skirt length 38 for body coats.

### Scale

"Two-thirds of width shoulder (27) gives the 'scale' (18). Most of the points are found by divisions of the 'scale.'"

- The scale is a plain number in real inches. "1/6 scale" means 3 inches here; there is **no graduated proportional tape**.
- For a normally proportioned man the scale equals the half breast.
- The normal model (p. 22) takes every division from the half breast. The lounge text covers missing shoulder measures only at I ("In the absence of shoulder measures make I from H a 1/2 inch").

### Position and directions

- p. 22: "All patterns should be drafted in the position shown on the diagram page."
- On Plate 16, A is the top-left corner. Line A-B runs down (centre-back construction line, toward the hem) and line A-C runs right (top line). The back is at the left, the front at the right, the neck at the top.
- "Squared down/up" means perpendicular to the breast line, toward hem or neck.
- Back and forepart are drafted on one sheet. Their side seams overlap below the waist.

### Seams

- The lounge text adds no seam allowance anywhere. Shoulder measures are taken over the coat. Seams and ease sit inside the "+" quantities ("the waist measure plus 2½ inches", "the real measure, plus 3 inches").
- p. 378: the allowance taken up in making is "in an ordinary way, two quarter-inch seams".
- p. 197 (small-waisted figure, read from OCR only, image not downloaded) speaks of "waist measure plus [x] inches for seam allowances".
- So **¼-inch seams appear to be included in the drafted lines. This is inferred, not stated for the lounge.**
- Inlays are mentioned only in the alteration chapters, with no amounts for the lounge.

### Plate scale

The diagram is drawn to a reduced scale. Its inches are real inches reduced, not a special scale. A to B (½ + 29 = 29½ in) spans 5053 px of the 4959 px-wide scan, about 171 px per inch. Local checks agree within about 0.1 in: C to NN reads 2.9 for 3, J to JJ reads 1.0 for 1. Longer spans are off by up to about 0.4 in (V to Z reads 3.9 where the rules give 3½). Distances quoted below as "measured" come from this scan, are approximate (±0.3 in) and are **not printed in the book**.

### 5.2 What only Plate 16 fixes

Diagram only; distances measured on the scan and approximate.

- **Fish and P.** P is on the waist line about 1.0 in behind T's vertical and about 3.3 in forward of M's. The shaded fish starts on the bottom of the scye about 1.4 in behind T and ends about 4.3 in below the waist, at the pocket mouth.
- **Pocket and point 7.** Point 7 has no rule. It sits on the line squared down from S, about 4½ in below the waist. A flap pocket about 7 in wide and 2 in deep hangs from a mouth through 7. No breast pocket is drawn.
- **Buttons.** Four buttonhole marks run on the front line. The first is at the breast line (J); the rest are about 4 in apart, one just below the waist and the last about 12 in below the breast line. Each is a short bar from the front line toward the edge. (The p. 53 figure shows a high-buttoning single-breasted lounge.)
- **Lapel.** There is no rule for lapel width, point or step.
  - On the flat pattern the edge rises straight from JJ to a lapel point on the gorge line produced, about 1.1 in forward of XX.
  - The gorge runs from C through the second NN and XX to that point.
  - The crease (roll) line is drawn from JJ up past the neck, passing about 0.8 in in front of C at C's level. That matches the collar rule "C from CC, 3/4 in. always" (5.3).
  - The lapel and collar end are also drawn turned back over the crease, showing the notch (step), with dashed stitching lines. No dimensions are written.
- **Front edge and bottom.** From JJ the edge runs through 12, roughly parallel to the front line, to about the hip line. It then rounds away to meet the bottom about 5½ in behind SSS. The forepart bottom slopes down from the side seams to the SSS level. The back bottom is square at B's level.
- **Back neck curve D-GG**, **back scye LL-N-W** continued to the side seam above M, and **both side seams**: shapes by diagram. "=" balance marks sit at the side seams near M.
- **Notches.** N is notched on the back scye. A notch on the front scye, about 0.6 in forward of T and 0.4 in above the breast line, is the forearm pitch (RR on Plate 46).
- **Making-up marks** (explained p. 53):
  - hatching on the front shoulder and the front scye means "strained out by the iron";
  - wavy lines on the outer back shoulder (about 3½ in "slightly fulled on"), along the back scye LL-W ("slightly worked in") and along the crease row (steadied by a linen "bridle").
  - p. 53 also says the back seam is sewn "from point K ... to about three inches below point O, with a 'tight hand'".
