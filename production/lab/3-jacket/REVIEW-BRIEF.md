# Fresh review brief: one lounge jacket (lab test 3)

For a reviewer who has seen nothing of the making. Judge only what the pictures and the photographs show.

## What it is meant to be

A single-breasted lounge jacket with notched lapels, cut from J. P. Thornton's *International System of Garment Cutting* (London, c. 1911), "The Standard Lounge Coat", drafted for a heavy man 1.94 m tall (chest 123 cm, waist 108 cm), and draped by cloth simulation on his body (no head on the body; it is a grey figure). The cloth is a plain charcoal, no texture: judge shape, not material.

## What you get

- Pictures of the draped jacket: front, side, three-quarter, back, and a close view of the lapels (F:\LedgerTools\lab\jacket\renders\<version>_*.jpg).
- Period photographs of British and Irish men in lounge jackets, 1905 to 1924 (production/previews/lab/jacket-photos/: front-01 to front-08, side-03 and side-04). Use side-04 for the clothes only.

## What to answer

1. Does it read as a lounge jacket of about 1905 to 1925 beside the photographs? Name the three biggest differences in outline (shoulders, chest, waist, length, hem, sleeves), most serious first.
2. The lapels: do they lie turned back on the chest, with a clear roll line and a notch between collar and lapel, as in the photographs? Or do they collapse, stand off, flip up, or tear? Say for each side.
3. The collar: does it sit round the neck and turn down over its stand?
4. Faults of the simulation itself (cloth through the body, spikes, holes, crumpled seams), separately from the cut.
5. Verdict: pass, pass on narrow points (list them), or fail. A pass means it could stand beside the photographs without a tailor wincing at the shape.
