# Fresh review brief: one sash window (lab test 2)

For a reviewer who has seen nothing of the making. Judge only what the pictures and the photographs show.

## What it is meant to be

One box (cased-frame) sliding sash window, two sashes, as on a two-storey brick terraced house built in Britain between about 1880 and 1900, set in a plain brick opening with a stone sill. Modelled from measured numbers (SOURCES.md, target.json). The renders use a flat light (Blender's Workbench): white paint, dark glass, plain brick colour, no textures. Judge shape and proportion, not materials.

## What you get

- Renders: front, oblique, close view at the meeting rail and horns, and a low view (F:\LedgerTools\lab\sash\build\render_<version>\*.jpg).
- Photographs of real windows of the period, openly licensed (production/previews/lab/sash-refs/, listed in SOURCES.md).

## What to answer

1. Does it read as an 1880-1900 British terrace sash window beside the photographs? Name the three biggest differences (proportions of the sashes, the meeting rail, the horns, the glazing bars, the frame and its reveal, the sill), most serious first.
2. Is anything missing that every photograph shows (a part of the frame, the beads, the horns, the sill's shape, the reveal's depth)?
3. Anything wrong in construction a joiner would see (sashes in the wrong plane, parts that could not be made, parts floating)?
4. Verdict: pass, pass on narrow points (list them), or fail. A pass means it could stand in a street of the photographs' windows without looking modelled from a guess.

Write plain English, at most 400 words, to production/lab/2-sash-window/REVIEW.md.
