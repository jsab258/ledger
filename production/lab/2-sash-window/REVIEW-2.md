# Fresh review 2: sash window, render v4

Sizes are fractions of the opening's width.

## 1. Does it read as an 1880-1900 terrace sash?

As a type, yes: box sash behind a half-brick reveal, upper sash outside, ogee horns under the meeting rail, opening about twice as high as wide. It does not yet look like the originals:

1. **The timber border is about half the photographs' width.** Brick edge to glass, the model shows about 7% at each jamb (lining 2.5%, stile 3.5%) and 6% at the head. Photo-06 shows 15-17%, the lining alone 9-10%; photo-01 shows 12-15%. At street distance (street.jpg beside photo-01 and 09) the model reads as a thin line round a dark hole, like a slim replacement; the originals read as a bold painted frame.
2. **Pane pattern.** Two-over-two; the originals are six-over-six (photo-01), two-over-four (photo-06) and multi-pane (photo-08). See source note.
3. **Sash members read flat and slight.** The meeting rail is one plain band about 4% of the width; photo-06's is about 7%, stepped, the lower sash's rail showing beneath. Photo-06 and 08 show moulded edges casting shadow lines; the model's are flat strips.

## 2. Missing parts

None: lining, pulley stile, parting bead, beads, horns, oak sill, stone cill and half-brick reveal (as photo-05, 06, 09) are there.

## 3. Construction

Sash planes are right: in oblique.jpg the lower sash's bar sits deeper, and the horns hang in front of the lower stile as in photo-07. Nothing floats. Two faults:

- Small square stubs at the upper sash's top-left corner and the meeting rail's ends (front.jpg; dark box in meeting_rail.jpg) read as rail ends left proud, or a gap in the joint.
- Both cill ends sit in an oversized brick slot, leaving a dark void beside and above each (front.jpg lower corners, oblique.jpg lower left). Context, but visible.

## Source note (separate)

The photographs suggest Ellis's 3/4 in of lining is the slimmest case, not the typical one: even the London original (photo-06, Tottenham) shows roughly 2 1/2 to 3 in past a half-brick reveal. None of the four originals is two-over-two; plain or two-pane sashes appear only on replacement or unconfirmed windows (photo-02 to 04). Four undated originals neither disprove Ellis nor show him typical.

## Verdict

**Pass on narrow points:**

1. Widen the visible border to 12-15% each side, mainly by showing about 2 to 2 1/2 in of lining; recheck the meeting rail against photo-06. Narrow in scope, not in effect: until done it looks thinner-framed than every original here.
2. Remove the rail-end stubs.
3. Close the void round the cill ends.

The pane pattern is a source decision, not a model fault.
