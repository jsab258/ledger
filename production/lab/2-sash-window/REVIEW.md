# Review: sash window, render v2

Renders judged against photographs 1-9, weighting the originals (1, 6, 7, 8). Widths are fractions of the brick opening, measured off the front render.

## 1. Does it read as 1880-1900?

Not yet. The layout is right (two-over-two, upper sash outside, horns, stone sill, deep bottom rail, opening about 2:1), but it reads as a slim modern unit. Worst first:

1. **Far too little timber.** Glass fills about 87% of the width; in photos 1 and 6, nearer 65-70%. The box frame hides behind the brick, leaving a 2.5% sliver of lining; photos 1, 6 and 8 show a broad painted lining (a tenth each side in photo 6). Stiles and top rail are 2.5%; photo 6's stiles 4-6%.
2. **Bars and meeting rail too thin.** The bar is 0.6% (about 5 mm on a 3 ft opening), a fifth of the stile; photo 6's is 2%, half the stile. At street distance it is a hairline. Meeting rail 3.5% against 6.8% in photo 6.
3. **Horns timid.** Narrower than the stile, with a faint S-curve; photos 7 and 8 show a bold ogee as wide as the stile. From the front they read as stubs; photo 6's read as curved brackets.

## 2. Missing

- **The head.** Every elevation photograph has an arch or lintel; the render's opening is a square hole.
- **The visible outside lining** (1.1).
- **The sill's shape.** A square block 14% of the width deep; photos 1, 5, 6 and 9 show slimmer cills (7-10%) with a sloped top and a nose.

Beads, pulley stile, oak sill and half-brick reveal look plausible.

## 3. Construction

Sashes sit in the right planes; nothing floats. The dark strip at each pane's right and bottom is shadow, not a gap. But:

- A horn is the stile left long, so starts at its full width; these look stuck on.
- A bar that thin could not be rebated for glass both sides.
- A striped flicker down the right jamb (close-up, oblique) shows two surfaces in one place, probably sash stile and parting bead.
- Brick cannot span the opening without an arch or lintel.

## 4. Verdict

**Fail.** Beside photos 1 and 6 the timber proportions give it away. Lining, stiles, rails, bars and meeting rail must reach the photographed widths; then horns, sill and head would be narrow points.
