## 3. Conflicts and gaps

Conflicts and the most authoritative reading for 1880 to 1900 terraces:

1. Frame set-back: 4 in (1709 Act, secondary), 4 1/2 in (Ellis 1902 drawing, Allen 1897, Rivington 1875, exam questions), 80 mm (a modern maker). Use 4 1/2 in (114.3 mm). Ellis 1902 is the latest period joinery text read and has a printed dimension.
2. Bottom rail: Ellis text says twice the top (4 in); his Fig. 399 labels read 2 1/2 in twice (probably 2 1/2 in of rail showing above the ventilating piece plus 2 1/2 in behind it, which would make 5 in). Hasluck's elevation (p.405, Fig. 1311) draws a bottom rail that looks at least twice the top rail (not measured). Use 4 in (101.6 mm), range 4 to 5 in.
3. Meeting rail depth: 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 in (text), 1 1/2 in (Fig. 399), 1 1/8 in (Fig. 415 section). Use 1 1/2 in (38.1 mm).
4. Glass rebate: 1/4 in (Ellis), 3/8 in or more (Allen), a little over a third of the sash thickness, so about 1/2 in (Riley). The three disagree by 6 mm; use 1/4 to 3/8 in for 1 3/8 in sashes.
5. Pane pattern: the two worked examples show two over two, but the Tring houses and the Chandos Road and Cemetery Road photographs show six over six or two over four. Both patterns were built in the period. Ellis names no date.
6. Glazing bar width: 5/8 in (Ellis), 5/8 to 1 1/4 in (Riley), 15 mm (Hull, Victorian lamb's tongue, uncertain assignment), 18 mm limit (Bassetlaw, Georgian). Use 15.9 mm (5/8 in); the other sources bracket it.

Gaps (needed, not found):

- No source read gives an opening of 850 by 1500. Height 1500 is unsupported.
