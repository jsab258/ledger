The limits, enforced every night at 04:30A fixed limit for every kind of output, a check before every build, and a line on the overview each morning

Tool caches, capped in their own settings

  • Windows' Task Scheduler runs the cleanup at 04:30 every night ("LEDGER retention"). It deletes only inside the project's own folders, never anything of yours, nothing git keeps, nothing the backup covers, and logs every deletion (F:\LedgerTools\retention\deleted.log). Its first run, this morning, removed 1,408 old scratch files and logs.
  • Every session writes only into known folders: scratch and voice tests in F:\LedgerTools\tmp, renders in F:\LedgerTools\renders, models registered with the tool that uses them. Anything that must outlive its limit gets a written hold, at most 14 days.
  • Before any build, render or large job, every session checks: C: keeps 40 GB, F: 20 GB. If cleaning cannot make room, the job stops and goes on your list; it is not retried. The build machine checks the same before it builds.
  • The overview's first line each morning: free space on both drives, and the three folders that grew most since the day before.
  • The build machine now puts its pictures into git at most once a day (it was five to ten times, about 130 MB each); every run's pictures go to its own folder for three days.

What caused itOne upload of mine, and history that only ever grew

At 22:07 last night I uploaded a merge whose history reached far back. The build machine was in the middle of a check, and the step that saves its result downloaded the project's whole history to do it: 30 GB, until C: had nothing left and the download stopped half-finished. That half-finished file stayed behind. Its pull now fetches only recent history, so this cannot repeat.

The rest grew slowly because nothing removed it: models of voice engines we stopped using, an old test copy of the game, the old player's voice graphs, scratch from every test, and the build machine's pictures, which went into git on every run and stay there for good (about 26 GB of the 33 GB history on C:). Making the history itself smaller would rewrite it on GitHub for all three sessions; I do not recommend it while C: has room.
