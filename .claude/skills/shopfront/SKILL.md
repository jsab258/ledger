# Shopfront

His rulings: Rita's window is the model and the other eleven shopfronts are made like it (RULINGS 2 Oct); Rita's front is the shops' sample, no new shops (RULINGS 22 and 28 Oct). This skill is the method as proven, not as planned; where a part passed only in the editor or not at all, it says so. It does not repeat three other skills: wear values, decals, the wet ground and lamp pools are .claude/skills/street-weathering/SKILL.md; sources, licences, sizes on import, staging and catalogue lines are .claude/skills/asset-import/SKILL.md; the review method is .claude/skills/gate/SKILL.md and the walk .claude/skills/packaged-walk/SKILL.md. **Always** (none of these bends):
- Dated photographs govern what a front looked like and win over books; the Hook sheet governs mood and palette; no design copied (RULINGS 21 Sep, 1 Oct, 8 Oct). A kit piece drawn from period books gets its target written by a fresh helper, never the builder, an automatic check and a fresh reviewer against photographs (RULINGS 8 Oct).
- No real brand, maker, shop, product or public name on any sign, card, poster or good (canon.md, "Brands and law"); before any kit import, a design check that it carries no real name (RULINGS 25 Oct); names minted within canon, placeholders never on his page (RULINGS 3 Oct). No alcohol or gambling goods; no children anywhere, so nothing in a window implies them (CLAUDE.md, project facts; canon.md's content rule).
- A front is a medium piece: the gate, at most two review rounds, then set aside or listed; a sign, decal or one texture fix is a small piece, one Sonnet reviewer, one batch (RULINGS 3 Nov). Two tries per fault; a third needs his yes (CLAUDE.md). It passes only in the packaged walk.
- Fix a fault at the source that made it (the builder script) and rebuild, never patch an export (NOTEBOOK 21 Oct, item 5). Never copy a front along the street before he approves one complete sample in the assembled game (CLAUDE.md; CORNER-PLAN §5).

**Requests this covers**, for example: "bring the grocer's front in like Rita's"; "her window burns white at night"; "Mickey's window reflects black"; "the fascia letters should be gold"; "the joinery looks like render"; "wear the front's foot"; "a sliver of sky between two pilasters"; "the cornice flickers"; "the sign reads two balls from along the street"; "is this name a real brand?".

## 1. References and the target

- Read FRONTAGE first: a parade front is pilasters (plinth, shaft, capital), consoles ending the fascia, a cornice, a stallriser under a projecting sill, a transom with toplights, a glazed shop door often in a recessed lobby, a solid panelled door to the flat (FRONTAGE §1). Colours sombre (maroon, dark green, black, dark blue, brown) with pale contrast (§2). Where FRONTAGE has no number, say "none found" and keep the spec's: pilaster width and projection ("In brief", §1), glazing bar width and sill section (§1).
- Write the target as numbered rows with negative controls before building, and have it reviewed (NOTEBOOK 13 Oct, unit 7 stage 1: Rita's check v3, 60 of 61 rows). Lay the window's goods out on paper and test overlap and floating without rendering: a failed layout costs an hour of render, the check seconds (NOTEBOOK 13 Oct, unit 7 stage 2).
- **Check:** the target reviewed; every row has a wrong copy that must fail (NOTEBOOK 13 Oct, unit 9).

## 2. Kit pieces and their real sizes

- One datum for every piece: the wall face, the pavement, the middle of the bay; sized to the parade's 6 m bay; pivots at the ground; one mesh per piece, mid-poly with bevels and imported weighted normals (FRONTAGE §5).
- Sizes that held on Rita's front: stallriser 0.60 m (guides 0.40 to 0.70), fascia 0.55 (at most 0.60 and a fifth of the front), mullions 48 mm proud of the glass (guides 40 to 70); door frames 0.944 and 1.006 m, leaving the window 3.35 m; glass 0.03 m in front of the wall face, 0.12 behind the sill's nose, behind pilasters and fascia (FRONTAGE §1, §4; DECISIONS 6 Oct, Rita's frontage from the kit). Across the road her doors read about 1.95 m and the front about 3.4 m to the cornice top (GATE.md, round 1, across). House windows: 2.5 to 3 inch frames, 1.68 m (RULINGS 8 Oct).
- The front goes into the street's own recipe (tools/art-recipes/terrace-front.py), never on top of it in the engine; the street's builder takes its own joinery out of that bay (DECISIONS 6 Oct; SHOPFRONTS-INTEGRATION "The game today"). Settle the datum first: a delivered front's wall face and the street's frontage differed by 0.17 m (SHOPFRONTS-INTEGRATION "The frame").
- **Traps that cost tries:** delivered pilasters stopped 30 mm short of the bay's edge, so sky showed in the joint: a brick pier behind each end pilaster, reaching 60 mm past the edge behind the neighbour's (try 2; GATE.md, reverse view). That pier's face sat on the frontage plane and flickered: never put two faces on one plane; filler 5 mm back (terrace-front.py CLOUD_PIER_FRONT_M 0.005; GATE.md, cornice; NOTEBOOK 22 Oct). A placement rule copied from a flush door put an hours plate in mid-air before a recessed one: write rules relative to what they stand on (NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, fifth shop). A front designed taller than its host: cut it at the eave in the wear step (clip_top_m; NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, eaves). Where a front stands behind the old line, pave the strip to its wall (NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, sixth shop). A glTF material's specular arrived at twice the engine's default and darkened the paint 45%: check it on import (NOTEBOOK 30 Oct, painted joinery).
- **Check:** `python tools/ue/asset_checks.py` as the asset-import skill runs it; terrace-front.py's self-test; three captures of one build agree at every joint (GATE.md, cornice).

## 3. Windows, glass and what is seen behind it

- The glass is the street's own: terrace-front.py builds a pane where each delivered pane was, so the crime's broken window, reflections and scripted breakages keep working; the rest of the front is joined into one mesh keeping every material (DECISIONS 21 Oct 16:45; front_for_game.py). A display is joined whole before import: 330 loose parts imported as 173 meshes and the game stood only the first, an alarm clock; the import now refuses loose parts (same).
- Behind the glass: the near metre real meshes, the room one projected picture per state (day, night lit, night dark; 4096 wide, read from F:/LedgerTools/game-inputs and staged by name) (NOTEBOOK 13 Oct, unit 7; DECISIONS 21 Oct 16:45). Goods at 0.9 to 1.1 of real size, raised or leaned, since the sill hides the lowest 40 mm from across the road (NOTEBOOK 13 Oct, unit 7 stage 2, try 2). A glazed door shows what stands behind it at every slant: walk each camera's line through each door (NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, sixth shop).
- The street in the glass: one cube capture per window, lit by Lumen, 256 by default and 1024 for the windows a camera stands at (look file glass_cube_size, glass_cube_hero_size, glass_cube_heroes; Mickey's door glass 512, GATE-OFFICE), added as emissive times Fresnel at 2.5 a true reflection by day, the live reflection cut to a tenth; at night 1.0 (DECISIONS 6 Oct, cube per window; 7 Oct, the caught glass; GATE-OFFICE, office night). Glass facing open sky: lower that pane's stored reflection alone (NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, sixth shop).
- **Traps:** the first captures ran unlit (a capture has no Lumen) and magnified 256 texels into blocks at two metres (DECISIONS 7 Oct). Mickey's window reflected black for twelve days: its cube was caught on frame 0 after a 40 s load stall that used up a wall-clock wait; the capture now waits 90 drawn frames (30 after a light change) (DECISIONS 4 Nov 15:36). Measure every picture of a bake by one number before guessing (NOTEBOOK 4 Nov, glass bake). Delivered cloth sheen became a pale wash in Unreal: tint it to the cloth's colour (DECISIONS 21 Oct 16:45). JPEG-compressed normal maps showed as dents on glossy goods: make maps lossless (GATE.md, square night; set aside).
- Upper sash windows read as white boards at low angles; the researched fix, cheapest first, is glass over a dark room, a shaded box with dark lines, then a street reflection in the upper panes (production/cloud-week/research/1a-sash-windows-low-angles.md; RULINGS 3 Oct bars: white frames, sash bars, sky in the glass). Whether that fix is in the game: unverified (NOTEBOOK "OPEN": not built).

## 4. Signs and lettering

- Lettering margin wider than the measured box; fonts run wider on another machine (NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, fifth shop). Glass lettering not cut by the window's bars (NOTEBOOK 13 Oct, unit 7 stage 1). A hanging sign is modelled in its bracket's plane, or from along the street three balls read as two (GATE.md, round 1, hook day).
- No real name: run the canon gate (brand words fail ci, "shell", "BT" among them: memory canon-gate-flags-shell); a second reader of another kind (rapidocr over every placed map, failing on any legible word not on a reviewed list) and a person on a contact sheet: the one real brand was found by the person; invented makers one letter from real ones are caught only when the blocklist holds the real names (NOTEBOOK 13 Oct, unit 7 stage 2, try 2).
- Gilt is metal: a sign called gilt in 1990 was gold leaf (DECISIONS 4 Nov 14:10). Mickey's letters are the street_gilt row on the kit's opaque master, which already had a metallic input, so no shared shader changed; gold F0 (1.0, 0.75, 0.34) in the look file's gilt_metal_rgb; night letters 45:1 on their board; passed one small review in the editor (DECISIONS 5 Nov 13:45). Trap: an earlier note took them for a decal because a name was searched; read the export's table of rows first (NOTEBOOK 5 Nov, metal detail).
- **Check:** the cloud's PC-06, the night fascia name's luma ratio at least 1.3 (SHOPFRONTS-INTEGRATION, owed list); the across-the-road view reads every word that should read.

## 5. Paint, trims and scan rows (the corner's 9a, 9b, 9d; 9c in step 6)

- How far each part got: 9a passed packaged on weak evidence (DECISIONS 5 Nov 05:45); the gilt, 9b and 9d passed one small review each in the editor ("editor only; packaged tonight": DECISIONS 5 Nov 13:45, 13:55, 14:52, 15:30, 15:53) and reached the packaged game only inside the corner's whole-frame views of 6 Nov (DECISIONS 6 Nov 6:01), with no packaged record of their own; 9c was set aside (step 6).
- The street's UV 0 is metric, 1.00 a metre, on frames, joinery, stone and lead, so a tile laid at 1/covers_m gives its density with no remap; joinery meshes are shared across the street, one mesh per row, so a row change reaches every bay (STEP9-PLAN, measurement). Use the scan-row home, production/specs/ps5-corner.json (base, asset, covers_m, match_rgb), and stage it (step 8).
- 9a, painted timber: a square cut of the wood sheet's board strip, 1,536 px over 3.15 m (487.6 px a metre, band 410 to 614), contrast held low in the maps (0.079) since rows have no contrast field, roughness 0.341, both seams cross-blended, mean matched to the palette colour (DECISIONS 4 Nov 17:59; tools/art-recipes/paint_wood_tile.py). Packaged it was barely visible: flat paint, no wear (DECISIONS 5 Nov 05:45).
- 9b, sills and rainwater goods: give a part family its own row first (street_stone_dressing, street_rainwater split in the export, the same boxes) so walls and lead roofs stay; cut trim bands out of a scan before tiling; grade a two-colour scan on its paint's brightest quarter, never its mean (mean grading turned the pipe blue) (DECISIONS 5 Nov 13:55; NOTEBOOK 5 Nov, photographed trims). Black-painted cast iron: a project choice, period unverified.
- 9d: an even overlay with no source is lightened first (the wash a third lighter); a scan whose pale patches are also glossy flashes at grazing angles: compress tone above the median and roughness below it (tools/art-recipes/dressing_tiles.py); a flashing strip on a shared near-black row read as a black bar, so its own row in weathered lead grey, linear 0.19 to 0.215 (DECISIONS 5 Nov 14:52, 15:30, 15:53).
- **Check:** 512 px a metre within 20%; one small reviewer at about 2 m and 6 m, day and night, beside the last good; no lit pier at night (CORNER-PLAN step 9; STEP9-PLAN 9a).

## 6. Wear on the front

- Values, decal counts and the brick are the street-weathering skill's. What a front adds:
- Bake the wear into the front's own paints, as a texturing tool wears a unique piece: masks from the model (height, occlusion near and far, faces turned up, convex edges, distance from the hand's place), one texture set per paint with its unused ground filled with the clean colour, dirt drawn toward a warm hue at 55% of the paint's luminance, grime roughness 0.55 (chips 0.6), a soft tide line 0.15 over 30 mm; a second copy of the front ("-worn") that the shop file names (tools/art-recipes/front_wear.py, production/specs/shop-fronts/ritas-front-wear.json; GATE.md, try 2; NOTEBOOK 22 Oct). Target: foot grime to 150 mm about 20% darker, the lever's band, chipped rails and sill nose; the clean paint within 1.5% of BEFORE.
- **Traps:** projected marks never showed on paints the 3 Oct rule exempts (DECISIONS 7 Oct, two tries). Swapping in the street's material drew the same oxblood 2 to 17% (later measured 21 to 39%) darker and redder, and its pale dust read as frost on cream (GATE.md, try 1; NOTEBOOK 22 Oct). One shared atlas on black bled hairlines into narrow faces through the mips. Matt grime (0.85) caught the sky as a chalky lilac wash from the side (GATE.md, try 2). Leave metal out of a colour-only bake (NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, fifth shop). Each worn front states whether it keeps the street's grime (tools/homes_check.py, fronts; NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, eaves).
- Edge chips on the street's shared meshes (9c) are not solved: they have no edge mask (constant vertex colour); a rebuilt front mesh lit the chandler's pier at night, cause not found in two tries (DECISIONS 30 Oct 17:33, 18:29); geometry flakes over the arrises read as confetti and rust, set aside after two tries (DECISIONS 7 Nov 15:37). A known shortfall; a third try is his.

## 7. Night light of the window

- Take the designer's own light plan first (NOTEBOOK 22 Oct, night light). Rita's: two strip lights 8 mm under each tier's front edge, 3.20 m, tipped 30° back, 3000 K, no shadow; the head lamp in the window head just behind the glass, over the window alone; set by sweep to 1.5 lm strips and 60 lm head (game lumens) against the bar "any channel at 250 on at most 1% of the window" (GATE.md, 22 Oct; VignetteShot.cpp SpawnDisplayStrips; production/specs/shop-interiors.json).
- Keep shop fronts off the window light's channel (tag LedgerShopFront), or the front's white bars burn (GATE.md, round 1 cause). A lamp's ends well off the side linings (NOTEBOOK 23 Oct, sixth shop). A light that leaked through a party wall had lit the neighbour's goods: test by filming with the lamp off (-ShopSpillLumens=0) (GATE.md, round 2). Shadows from the shop's own pieces and near only: the window lamps' shadows were 4.3 ms of 4.98 (NOTEBOOK 23 to 24 Oct, frame headroom).
- **Traps:** the night's light needs 30 s to settle after the clock's jump, or before and after differ everywhere (GATE.md, 22 Oct; CrimeProbe.cpp PageShotSettle). A model's glow invisible by day burns at night: look at the darkest state brightened (NOTEBOOK 13 Oct, unit 7 stage 2). A door card turns CLOSED after hours and is solid (GATE.md, door card).
- Open on Mickey's front: near-black window and night front; fascia about 2 and upper front 0.3 to 4 levels at night, both tries spent (DECISIONS 5 Nov 05:40; 6 Nov 6:01).
