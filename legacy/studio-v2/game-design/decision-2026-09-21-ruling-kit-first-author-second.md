## His message, verbatim

Budget: total 13, Fable 17, taken now.

A change of approach for the visual slice, and it amends this week's order rather than adding to it.

Why. Four hours after the reset the meters read thirteen and seventeen percent, and the street inches forward. The Astra demonstrations that looked fast were fast for a reason their own author wrote down: they assembled worlds from existing Unreal assets, MetaHumans included, and placed rather than authored. This studio does the opposite. It builds a British street from primitives and generated textures and then spends most of its effort proving every number. Rigour is not a street.

Ruling, numbered from the register's next free number: kit first, author second. Unreal's own free libraries, Megascans, Fab and City Sample, are scanned from real surfaces and are what AAA studios use. They are higher quality than anything our generators make, and higher than the reference sheet's own textures. The risk they carry is not quality but character: they are generic, not British, not 1990, and a street built only from them looks like every other Unreal demo. So the base is assembled and the identity is authored.

The method, in order:

1. A shopping list. Compare the in-house Hook sheet and the town atlas against what the free libraries hold: brick, stone, setts, kerbs, terrace fronts, windows, doors, shopfronts, drainpipes, lamps, bins, street clutter. Mark each item found, close enough, or missing, and check each against the licence allowlist before it is used, since the allowlist is law. Add any licence that is not yet on it as a card rather than assuming.

2. Assemble. Replace the blank walls with real kit facades and scanned materials, laid out to the Hook sheet's viewpoint and the atlas. This alone should close most of the gap in one pass.

3. Make it Meridian. Dirty it down under the grime rule, then author what no kit has: British shop signs and fascias from 1990, period details, and Mickey's as a cab office under D19. Check every kit piece against D17 and D18 before it goes in, since shopfront kits routinely carry pub signage and drink advertising.

4. The assembly lines fill only the gaps the shopping list marks missing. Nothing is generated or authored that a kit already provides.

What changes in this week's order. The measured batch becomes one facade assembled from kit and dressed as Meridian, rather than one authored from scratch: both meters read before and after, the batch as the ledger's unit, rejected attempts counted. That is the real unit of content now, so it is the number worth having. The exposure fix and the figure continue as ordered. The MESH station measurement still runs, but its purpose is now filling gaps rather than building the street. Assembly is placement rather than taste, so it runs on the lower tiers under the routing table, with the top tier kept for judging the result against the reference. D41 applies: this is visual work, ungated, and you judge it against the Hook sheet yourself.
