## His message, verbatim

The crime and combat coverage audit has landed, on research/crime-and-combat-coverage. I have read it and I am ruling on it. Record these as you go, numbered from the register's next free number. None of it is built this week: it belongs to stage 3 and waits behind the visual slice and the measurements. Two small things below are checkpoint work now, and they are marked.

The premise was wrong in our favour. The boss half is not thin: operation planning, the weapons model, lingering injuries that identify a man, blood that washes off with water and privacy, weapons with a provenance and a disposal risk, feuds, the four-stage inquiry, rivals who summon, crew loyalty with a ceiling set by nerve, and the rule that a man past it talks to the police are all built. What is missing is one act, and it is the one I asked about. There is no verb between deciding a man should be hurt and the book recording that he was, and a job's target is a building, never a person. Replace the crime layer's entry in the owed record from this week's prompt with the rulings below; the owed record for combat is replaced by the combat ruling.

The eight verbs, in, in this order, because the first two are the missing act and the rest build on them:
1. Order a named man hurt, through a named crew member. The man you send becomes a second person who knows, with his nerve, his loyalty ceiling and the rule that he talks when it breaks, all of which exist. The accomplice is the risk, not the alibi.
2. Point a plan at a person, not a building: a target field for a man, and a resolver. Everything downstream already runs.
3. Frame someone, by steering who the police point at, entirely through what the town believes.
4. Lean on a witness to change what they say. The payment machinery exists. A bought retraction that the street then learns about is the gossip mill doing what it is for.
5. Move or hide a body. The record already stores where a killing happened, and the gap between the act and the finding is where the tension lives.
6. Sanction your own crew: dock, expel or beat. A man punished is a man who knows everything and now hates you, carried by the feud system and the talks-to-police rule.
7. Send men at a rival's property rather than his person, so the player can escalate without spending a life.
8. Have someone vouch for you. Contradictions are already detected, so an alibi another person must sustain is a lie with a dependency rather than a dice roll.

The press, in, but only through a person. Feeding or killing a story happens by dealing with a reporter or an editor who is a resident, who then remembers you, and who can talk. There is no control that sets how loud the paper is. The research was right that a slider would turn a living town into a meter the player manages.

Grassing on someone, in. Informing already exists as a thing done to the player and the machinery is symmetrical. The grass becomes someone the police know and, in time, someone the street knows. Turning yourself in waits until the endings are traced, since it is an ending path rather than a verb.

Combat, three gaps, all in, in this order. Being outnumbered first, because a fight currently resolves one against one and being outnumbered is what makes a crew worth having. Then running away as a way out of a fight rather than a move inside one. Then the police arriving while it is still happening rather than only afterwards through the body.

The ending. There are five, written and wired, and the red team's finding is narrower than it sounded: a route back exists, and none costs less than half of what you have. That is the game, not a fault. It is what permanent memory means in a noir town, and no redemption path is added. It holds on two conditions.
First, checkpoint work now: confirm that the two endings left to a hunted player, keeping everything with nobody who knew you before and giving up the business to keep the people, are actually reachable from a hunted state. The audit read only the first gate of the eligibility rule and says it is one method away from certain. If either is unreachable, that is the fault, and it comes to me as a card.
Second, the player must be able to see the cost coming as it approaches, through the surface D33 already ruled, where Tom's own reading is shown with its reasons. A cost the player could not have seen is a trap; a cost he watched arrive is the game.

The content rule holds throughout. Ordering a beating is in scope and depicting one at length is not. Nothing makes drugs a player verb, and nothing leverages a child.
