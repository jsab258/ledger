## The list, in order (Jafar, 28 September)

- [ ] 1. The town shows what it has heard. Today someone who has heard a little about Tom behaves exactly like someone who has heard nothing, so the town never visibly knows him. Make faint knowledge show: someone who knows even a little looks at Tom longer, may remark on it, treats him slightly differently. Build the simulation side with tests; hand the looks and remarks to the builder to show in the game.
- [ ] 2. Friends who never meet. 55 of 80 friendships between named people never cross in their daily routines, so gossip cannot travel between them. A fault in the routines, not a dial. Fix the routines so friends meet as friends in a small town would, then measure again how far news travels. Change no constant.
- [ ] 3. Characters inventing facts. The live check catches only about half of what characters invent unprompted; one invented a van story. A character may only say what the simulation knows. Make the check catch it, measured on a set of test conversations, with the failure rate before and after.
- [ ] 4. Casting sheets for the eleven principals who have none, from CASTING.md, the casting research and the renamed cast: age, origin, occupation, build, hair, what they wear (from the clothing research), how they move, and which of his approved voices fits by age, sex and accent, flagging anyone who needs an older voice. Text only; faces and voice samples are the builder's. One approval page for the sheets.
- [ ] 5. The story. From canon, the decisions, the endings already written, the crime layer and the research on authored stories in simulations and British crime fiction, a short outline for his approval: Tom's arrival, the first days, the three acts, the rivals, and how the endings are reached. Under two pages, not a script.
- [ ] 6. Then the non-visual items of ROADMAP.md, in order.
