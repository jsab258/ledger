# RECIPE: the condensed recipe

The method that makes the game, in setting-free words, rewritten from NOTEBOOK.md at each phase's end (RULINGS 9 Nov, records). Read the chapter and its skill before any first try. Setting, period, places and people stay in data. "NB" names a notebook entry by date and subject; the entry holds its evidence file. Phase 1's rewrite 9 Nov 2026; the second rewrite 23 Nov 2026 put the whole-game order first and folded the 10 to 23 Nov entries into their chapters (their full text stays in NOTEBOOK.md).

## 0. In every chapter

- **The whole-game order: research first, then measure the design, then build it in one go.** Nothing is built at scale from a design that is not measured. Research answers written questions (chapter 1); the design is measured part by part (chapter 1, "A design counts as measured"); only a complete, measured design starts the one-go build (chapter 0b). Sources read (one article, three small-studio postmortems, one talk abstract; thin) say a playable slice is the gate out of pre-production and that pre-production should yield validated prototypes, defined pipelines and current documents, timeboxed; failures named there: a technology proof giving false security, a milestone too big, an engine not stress-tested. No source gave a measured-design stage; that step is this project's own (production/research/recipe-rewrite/WEB-NOTES-2026-11-23.md; no first-hand account from either reference studio was found).
- Reference first: how do the reference games do it (studies, then files)? Copy the method; take numbers only once they fit. Else the plan says "none found".
- Research the method end to end before a first try; on failure, question the method.
- Name the visible fault in a measure that moves with it; the frame the eye objects to must read worst (NB 30 Oct, holds on the neck; replacement clip). Prove a count measures the fault before tuning anything to it (NB 19 Nov, layered garment: 124 of 296 "through" points were the inner garment's own folded cuffs).
- Two failed tries (per fault, not per method) set a fault aside; then one genuinely different researched method; up to four tries while each closes the gap to the bar by a third with no new fault; after the fourth, a removing or simplifying fix under an hour, bigger ones listed (RULINGS 7 and 9 Nov).
- After any setting, measure that its thing moved; read the result line the run just wrote (NB 5 Nov, pale sky; 26 Oct, second wear pass).
- A pass counts only in the packaged game, launched as a player launches it (NB 7 Nov, rehearsing a gate; 8 Nov, a pass must be the player's game).
- Bars include the goal itself: a fix that passes every local bar can still miss what it was for (NB 13 Nov, motion clean-up: the man never stood up).

## 0b. The one-go build and what it waits for

**Works.** The build from one complete prompt starts only when: every part a player feels is measured, decided (and listed for the owner to overturn) or ruled out; every review theme of the reference games maps to a part with a test; two red-team rounds in a row (fresh helpers given the description with ten parts silently removed) find no material gap and each helper catches at least 9 of the 10; the owner has approved the measured story; and the preparation items are done (checkers proven on planted faults, a technical plan, the design's numbers run through the simulation, a full inventory against the build's scope, a sound plan, live talk settled from its bench). "Material" is fixed before a round: a part missing or undefined that is met in the first game week, can end a session stuck, breaks a ruling or canon, or blocks a playthrough check. Red-team rounds are capped; what stays open goes to the owner once as scope, each with a recommendation. The owner plays only after the one-go build.
**Checks.** Engine work refused while the prototype ruling stands (tools/research_rules_check.py, in ci); a page item asking the owner to play before the build's report is refused (tools/needs_you_page.py); the parts tree checked each loop and Monday (tools/lane/parts-tree/check.py, on the lane branch). No build check yet tests the five-sample rule or the start gate itself (the advisor's launch check and Monday's audit only): a gap, listed.
**Failed.** A one-go build from one giant prompt began before its design was measured (20 Nov; its own spend not in the logs): it reached 6 of 41 play stories in about 7.5 hours, its two checks found most parts thin, and its report was never written; earlier that day a 200-part checklist had produced rules with passing tests the game never called (2 of 12 parts real in play). The same evening the design was found unmeasured and everything waiting on it paused. The loop prototype's gate before it found eight real faults (crashes untold, posts and parked cars not solid on foot, keyboard steering snapping, the pad unusable, the camera hiding the player indoors), and two builds disagreed on the clock until a ruling.
**Links.** production/lane/design-proof/DESIGN-COMPLETE.md (lane branch); production/lane/PROTOTYPE-GATE-FINDINGS.md; the recipe map (https://claude.ai/artifact/PDhLE6yEQKYuHWbzy7Y3pH, the one-go run's figures); production/research/recipe-rewrite/OWN-FILES-NOTES-2026-11-23.md.

## 1. Research, studies and targets

**Works.** Written questions, then our own research on them, first (NB 21 Nov, reconciled). Footage in frame sheets, blinks stepped under half their period (NB 21 Nov); counts lower bounds. One progress table per block, by aspect and source, repriced when a source drops; log every step (NB 21 Nov). Tag, date and source every number; each finding becomes a target with a check or a reason to ignore, and an order line. Primary sources in full; unreached ones listed, never used; a terms page failing over one transport is fetched raw by another before it is marked unverified, never trusted from a quote elsewhere (NB 12 Nov). Give reviewers our own files (most faults were claims about them). Licence first when buying. Exact targets before building: parts in millimetres with material, colour, wear and source tag; photographs measured as the check measures renders (tight box on the object's face); every check row has a wrong copy that must fail it by name; placements tested against the place's own pieces, doors and walking strip.
**A design counts as measured** when each part a player feels has a standard: a reference game's measured number, a dated real-world figure or published standard, or a pass-or-fail rule of the owner's or of canon; a standard the design chose is a decision, listed, never counted as measured. A number is solid only on at least five samples from uncut, dated footage, read at a step finer than the thing measured; anything less is a lead. Each open question is priced to finish or marked not needed, naming the part it would serve. Reports give three counts side by side: measured, resting on outside numbers, decided.
**Reading footage and game data.** Durations: spells at 30 s, edges at 1 s, endings in full frames (menus and cameras fake edges); five per level; a spell still lit at a clip's end never counts (NB 22 Nov). HUD icons by code: fixed slots plus one template per slot (47 of 49); models misread them and blob search takes scenery and digits (NB 23 Nov). Timing a running game without a recorder: grab its window in a tight loop on the high-resolution clock, frames kept in memory and saved after, input stamped on the same clock; count only changed pictures (about 27 new a second of 48 grabs, each placed within about 30 ms: speeds and timings over 0.1 s, never a wind-up; FILM-RULER-2026-11-23.md, 0.2 h). A grab-then-save loop with a pause gave 5 a second. Shipped budgets from a game's own settings files: own presets first, base files decode the levels, encrypted packs give sizes only; check what a key means before using it (a "frame time" key was a hitch threshold) (NB 23 Nov).
**Day-one values** (from the studies, to test): stage what the player passes, simulate what the loop reads; kit streets (about 3 floor types, about 15 uses a mesh); variety from colourways (4 to 8) and decals; small lights shadowless, at most 8 casters a frame; costly effects on heroes; budgets first (about 3 ms animation, 3.5 ms people); something new every 3 min; streets thin, half standing at inviting places, incidents drawing onlookers every few minutes; failure as kept state; tuning as data.
**Cost.** A study 0.6 to 1.3 helper hours, its review 0.2 to 0.3; a target 1 to 2 h plus review and fix; a footage duration sheet about 1.7 h; a HUD reader about 1.2 h; shipped budgets read about 1 h; a terms fetch 0.1 h.
**Failed.** A 26 GB uncapped extract filled the disk; extracts deleted before review; wide photo boxes measured brick; a check comparing two constants never fails; guessed read times (21 Nov); a model reading 1,710 icon cells (US$3.83, unusable).
**Links.** NB "Step zero", "Universal AAA starting values"; 25 and 28 Oct studies; 13 Oct units 8, 9, 13; 8 Nov cost estimate; 12 Nov (terms by another transport: production/research/ai-video-motion/CMU-TERMS-AND-CLIPS-2026-11-12.md); 22 to 23 Nov footage, budgets and HUD entries (production/research/lane/measurements/STATUS.md, g5-heat-samples-m_2RP-2026-11-22.md; production/research/games-numbers/BUDGET-TABLE-2026-11-23.md).

## 2. Street surfaces, wear and texturing tools

**Works.** Confirm exposure from the run log, read a chart through the player's camera, validate the base-colour view, then set surfaces inside researched reflectance bands, one kind at a time. Wet ground: rough saturated stone plus water as a clear coat (lobe about 0.15) on its own material copy by a switch. Tiling scans: re-tone each unit of each copy, simulated in numpy first, mean held within 0.5%. Wear by studied counts (about one decal a wall, at most six in view, leaks two in five), measured on and off in buffers with anti-aliasing and streaming off. Stains placed from their sources and baked to the piece's unwrap; unique pieces get model-derived masks baked into their own textures; chips baked to UV on outer arrises only. Mark pictures fade at every edge; a decal tint is multiplied by the picture's grey. Texturing tools: map who does what (scan library, procedural tiles, per-asset painting, own bakes, engine masters, decals, chart) with card memory per source, reviewed before any run; install portable with checksums; prove each command line on a stock mesh twice, same pixels; read an export preset's channel order from the tool itself; per-asset painting enters the engine as one small mask over a shared tile; bake masks from the high drape, not the game mesh; a tool lacking patterns or noise gets them made outside (patterns as true-size SVG) (NB 10 Nov; the map's review took 13 fixes).
**Tools, target, cost.** Chart and region readers, a repeatable photograph runner, Blender bakes, the wear generator, the texture and material command lines. Chart grey 110 to 135; reference regions within 12 levels; edge wear at the street's 3 to 10% worn share (generator level 0.7 to 0.8). 1.5 to 4 h a surface or piece; a tool proof about 45 min.
**Failed.** One water film read as milky tile; world-aligned run maps read as grain; fixed-colour decals became cut-outs; decals print on loose objects and climb uprights; nothing a decal does reaches a clear coat; geometric chips read as confetti; masks from the game mesh came out blocky; a build run on a stop-level disk reading (check first, alone).
**Links.** [street-weathering](.claude/skills/street-weathering/SKILL.md); NB 21 to 22 Oct (lamp stains; front bake), 29 Oct (calibration; wet ground; smoke staining), 30 Oct (shop front weathering; chipped joinery), 3 Nov (tiling texture; litter), 5 Nov (ground wear), 7 Nov (worn paint as geometry), 10 Nov (texturing tools: production/research/texture-pipeline/).

## 3. Roofs and far scenery

**Works.** Read the target before choosing a detail; each part family its own material row. Scan flecks: measure tone and gloss together, compress only what is both pale and glossy. Separate roofs by a thin flashing just proud of the slope. Far trees: test gaps with haze off; leaf triangles 5 to 9 px round bough masses in three tiers; houses at range need gutters, sills and a damp plinth.
**Cost.** 10 to 40 min a fix, one small review.
**Failed.** Scans as photographed read as paint splashes; a shared dark row drew flashing as a bar; opaque leaves trade flecks for lumps (cards or impostors next).
**Links.** [slate-roofs](.claude/skills/slate-roofs/SKILL.md); NB 4 Nov (distant trees), 5 Nov (roof scan; breaks; far trees).

## 4. Shopfronts, windows, doors and props

**Works.** One command per family reading every number from the target. Lay out displays in numbers; run intersection, floating, real-size and printed-word rows before any long render; goods at 0.9 to 1.1 real size or left out and named. Fittings as parts at mount points; no two parts share a plane. Rooms behind glass as projected pictures by the engine's own function, highlights rolled off. Delivered pieces: fix faults in the source script, split glass so the game's glass works, join parts keeping slots, read each piece's own zones. Stateful parts (clocks, hour signs) driven by the game. Look by day and night in the game camera before reviewers.
**Tools, target, cost.** Blender as a module, CC0 models checked at full size, an OCR reader plus a person's contact sheet. A shop 2.5 to 4.5 h; a prop 1 to 2 h.
**Failed.** See-through cloth and lettering; lamp ends burning linings; shadowless lamps leaking through party walls; flat-shaded goods striping at night; extruded lettering at default resolution; broken glass as hidden meshes (set aside).
**Links.** [shopfront](.claude/skills/shopfront/SKILL.md), [asset-import](.claude/skills/asset-import/SKILL.md); NB 13 to 14 Oct units 7 to 9, 21 to 24 Oct delivered shops and kit lamp, 3 Nov (street props), 6 Nov (broken window).

## 5. Lighting, exposure and night

**Works.** Find the cause in code before tuning. A lit room's exposure from an unbound volume weighted by the camera's place in its box. Window lights from the designer's plan, swept against a bar (no channel over 1% clipped), after the light settles by frames drawn. Night: one soft desaturated high fill and tinted haze; measure near-black and clipped together. A pale sky: lower it as seen, raise its lighting share alike. Find a shadow's light by switching kinds off; raise a quality setting only inside the room needing it, found by halving level groups.
**Cost.** 0.3 to 2.5 h a fault.
**Failed.** A volume shaped at run time never entered; fixes for features switched off; saturated fills; wrong "before" pictures; a shadow no bounce reaches is set design, not a setting.
**Links.** NB 22 Oct (window night light), 26 Oct (room exposure; quality inside a room), 27 Oct (highlight burn), 4 Nov (night; baked reflection), 5 Nov (pale sky).

## 6. Garments

**Works.** Reference garment read from a shipped game; pattern at the wearer's measures; drape with straight elbows, bend back with the body's weights; bands built from the edge rings (cuff to wrist plus floor, welt 0.90, neckband 0.92); own retopology within budget; bake per piece; skin from a body with every part it touches. Hide skin with a hidden-face map painted from the garments on a copied item. Shoes before hems: drape trousers over a smoothed stand-in of the footwear (fixed-height slices, convex outlines, lofted, set out a few millimetres) given to the solver as a separate collider, so the body stays one piece (none inside, nearest 2.6 mm, against 575 points up to 22 mm deep bare-foot). The head mesh may reach the chest: clear on a 1 mm grid from the seen side, unsigned distance first. Options from the garment's record; the maps' UV layer first. An outside tool's skinned garment is first imported onto the wearer's skeleton in a scratch folder and every bone's rest position and both meshes' bounds compared (342 of 342 bones, worst 0.001 cm). When a fix lives on another branch, join the branches, then run once as a measurement read by part. A changed input re-runs the whole recorded chain by one cheaper helper with old-beside-new numbers as acceptance; record every command. Refits across bodies: untangle seam-side crossings before the start check, sew with bands frozen, settle, the line set before any run (one band ended 0.3 points over a self-set line, less than the source itself moves; the cure is a source at rest). Seated checks: the game's test switches jump to the sitting hour and film from the first clear side; seated shape bars come from a passed garment in the same pose (reference x 1.10 + 1 mm); film indoors under even light too. Each version seen packaged at the closest distance the player meets it, beside the last good film; on failure, back to last good.
**Tools, target, cost.** Cloth tool by script bridge, Blender headless, per-pose and posed-shape checks, packaged films at four angles, engine Python for the import check. A chain about 3 h; a fix 0.2 to 1.5 h; a re-run chain about 0.1 h of helper time; an import check 0.4 h; a refit about 7 h of lane time; a seated film 0.5 h; sitting bars 0.1 h; the second-solver test about 0.4 h helper; the failed layered fix 8 h lane, 37 min machine and 5.5 h waiting on a shared lock.
**Failed.** Bent-elbow drapes; simulator elastic cuffs (three tries); a skinned collar cannot follow trunk and neck; hems cut on bare feet; offline marks passed while the game drew holes; a second cloth solver, given the first one's job on the same body, or its pieces and seams lifted from a finished drape with default fabric: repeatable, but the garment slid or hung high with four to five times the faults (NB 12 Nov); a layered fix where seams lacked the last word after the strain limit (two tries, set aside); a coat's collar parts 45 to 69 mm inside the neck, stretched 6.5 to 6.8 times (set aside; the last try belongs to the stronger model); a re-run pose check measured the body for want of a flag, and the seated counts missed their bar on the seat, not the changed hem; the hem measure reads zero on skinned garments (needs the footwear or the film); bare ankle between hem and boot when seated.

## 8. Motion

**Works.** Measure the source clip first (planted foot travel, pelvis height after batch retargets). A planted foot is found by its height, and worst steps are judged by eye on a fixed step (-benchmark -fps=60) with a still side camera. Stops chosen by foot, stance quarter and remaining distance, played whole. Seats as interaction spots: entries derived from clips, tested in the room for limb contact, kept by tool, chosen by heading. Walk-offs at the clip's whole-clip speed. Idle breaks additive on free limbs, every 6 to 14 s. A retarget is judged against its own source frame by frame: slide and foot height over the source's planted spans by what the retarget adds, knees relative to the source's, any limb turning over 90 degrees in a frame flagged; run source against source first, it must pass (NB 17 Nov). A clean-up tool is judged on real footage and on whether the move reaches its end pose, not only its local bars (NB 13 Nov).
**Tools, target, cost.** Clip frame readers, per-frame logs on both skeletons, walk-in tests, the engine's markerless body solve (37 min for 8 s on the processor). Slide at or under 3 cm; 1 cm motion in any 4 s. 1 to 3.5 h a try; a retarget check about 0.25 h.
**Failed.** Motion-matched vendor locomotion on another skeleton; feet pinned to a sliding source; in-place turns; speed read past a clip's end; neck holds the eye did not need; absolute 2 cm foot bars, which raw capture fails on itself; a clean-up passing every bar on a clip whose man never stands.
**Links.** NB 22 to 26 Oct (walk, sit), 28 Oct (seat spots), 30 Oct (holds), 2 Nov (idle breaks; walk-offs), 5 Nov (frozen check), 8 Nov (side-on film), 13 Nov (clean-up on real footage), 17 Nov (retarget against its source: tools/meshgen/blender/cmu_measure.py).

## 9. Talk and speech

**Works.** Measure delay inside the packaged game on the real path under a spend cap; check a harness does what the product does. Alternate arms in one sitting, 40 lines or more an arm; judge the share under the bar and the tail. Cut inventions at the source: facts listed beside the rule, citations per sentence, markup stripped everywhere; a code rule before the model check; pre-filters judged on the whole chain. Escalate a failed draft to the better model. Caching saves cost, not time. Voices: corpus speakers by byte range, takes gated by accent windows, transcript and likeness, prepared lines remade, every shipped model registered. A second model provider goes into the bench's own tools, never the shipped code, behind the interfaces callers already hold; pin the old request body byte for byte first; price by the provider's own ratios; with no usage sent, estimate and mark it, never zero; where the host bills after a cancel, price a stopped reply at its whole length; ten acceptance cases; a reply is finished only with a finish reason and the closing marker (NB 16 Nov).

## 12. Testing and the packaged walk

**Works.** Every changed piece walked by the tester in that night's packaged build. The tester reads the game's movement line; stages reach their target time. Read every picture beyond the stages. The night report names the launch and stuck path legs. Asset checks sweep every shipping folder, rules fitted by kind, real faults frozen as a known list. ## 13. Review gates and tool loops

**Works.** One view per fresh reviewer beside its last good picture, briefed from the picture with what changed and known shortfalls named; new faults block. Reviewers recompute from raw files and get the requirement word for word, never the maker's verdict. Every view, day and night, both slants, reviewed once; any fix re-shoots every view it reaches. Photograph at the shipped level through the player's camera. Rehearse an exit days early. A tool improved in a loop is judged on held-back jobs from the real need list, never shown to the fixer, hash-locked with the judge and run unchanged at every milestone; no fix may add a path, setting or number serving one job; held-back results far worse than practised ones mean the method is fixed next (NB 14 Nov).
**Cost.** Minutes a reviewer, two at a time; 20 of 20 planted faults caught beside the last good.
**Failed.** Bundles of ninety pictures hid a black block; changed-views-only reviews missed faults for rounds; wrong baselines.
**Links.** [gate](.claude/skills/gate/SKILL.md); NB 22 Oct (phase exit), 24 Oct (seventh shop), 26 Oct (picture level), 7 Nov (rehearsal), 14 Nov (held-back jobs).

## 14. Session running and records

**Works.** Order items in a ledger with statuses, checked on push. One permanent owner page from one data file, taps read before asking; a check fails when a work list or next-steps note asks the owner for anything without naming an open page item (forward-looking ask phrases only; an item's id on the line clears it; the owner's own kept items exempt; run on the real files first, it found two real asks and one stale line) (NB 18 Nov). Checks key on rulings' wording. Cap what is read most; move, never drop. An hourly gate takes over after a stop; stop work ends 10 minutes before a start. The cheap go/wait check runs in the prompt-submit hook, so a wait costs no model start; only the scheduled prompt is gated, and a go, a person's message or any error in the check lets it through; a settings change only the owner's session may make goes to the owner as one line to paste (NB 15 to 16 Nov; open: whether the hook fires on the first prompt of a scheduler-opened session). A stop hook holds only the scheduled worker, known by how its log opens and the gate's go inside it, never by a name; a turn already sent back goes, so no loop; on 80 real logs 41 go runs held, 32 wait runs and the owner's chats let go (NB 11 Nov). Price cache writes by cause from logs; log a time window per piece from the start (a reconstruction from commits, gaps capped at 90 minutes, got totals within 10% but single pieces off up to fourfold: a floor, NB 12 Nov). Compare models blind on recent items. Distil skills by writer and reviewer, the writer resumed to fix. Helper-written content gets a fresh reviewer for what each person could know. Desktop tools by script APIs, their user-scripts setting found before touching an install folder (unconfirmed until tested); one permitted menu item by accessibility invoke, logged.
**Cost.** 0.2 to 1 h a tool.
**Failed.** Chained scheduled runs; a gate counting its own runs; word caps hiding entry counts; rulings cited from memory; a stop hook matching the task's name held any chat that had read the task file (synthetic tests passed, real logs caught it); settings changes refused twice in unattended runs.
