# RECIPE: the condensed recipe

The method that makes the game, in setting-free words, rewritten from NOTEBOOK.md at each phase's end (RULINGS 9 Nov, records). Read the chapter and its skill before any first try. Setting, period, places and people stay in data. "NB" names a notebook entry by date and subject; the entry holds its evidence file. Phase 1's rewrite, 9 Nov 2026.

## 0. In every chapter

- Reference first: how do the two reference games do it (studies, then files)? Copy the method; take numbers only once they fit. Else the plan says "none found".
- Research the method end to end before a first try; on failure, question the method.
- Name the visible fault in a measure that moves with it; the frame the eye objects to must read worst (NB 30 Oct, holds on the neck; replacement clip).
- Two tries per fault, not per method; then research and the owner's yes (NB 30 Oct, trousers over boots).
- After any setting, measure that its thing moved; read the result line the run just wrote (NB 5 Nov, pale sky; 26 Oct, second wear pass).
- A pass counts only in the packaged game, launched as a player launches it (NB 7 Nov, rehearsing a gate; 8 Nov, a pass must be the player's game).

## 1. Research, studies and targets

**Works.** Written questions first. Index archives before unpacking; extract metadata only, capped, one extractor at a time, outside the repository, kept until the fresh review has re-measured. Tag every number (measured, read, inferred, name only) and date it; each finding becomes a target with a check or a reason to ignore, and a tracked order line. Primary sources in full; unreached sources listed, never used. Give reviewers the project's own files: most faults were in claims about the project. Licence first when buying. Exact targets before building: parts in millimetres with material, colour, wear and source tag; photographs measured as the check measures renders (tight box on the object's face); every check row has a wrong copy that must fail it by name; placements tested against the place's own pieces, doors and walking strip.
**Day-one values** (from the studies, to test): stage what the player passes, simulate what the loop reads; kit streets (about 3 floor types, about 15 uses a mesh); variety from colourways (4 to 8) and decals; small lights shadowless, at most 8 casters a frame; costly effects on heroes; budgets first (about 3 ms animation, 3.5 ms people); something new every 3 min; failure as kept state; tuning as data.
**Cost.** A study 0.6 to 1.3 helper hours, its review 0.2 to 0.3; a target 1 to 2 h plus review and fix.
**Failed.** A 26 GB uncapped extract filled the disk; extracts deleted before review; a game key left on the system drive; wide photo boxes measured brick; a check comparing two constants never fails.
**Links.** NB "Step zero", "Universal AAA starting values"; 25 and 28 Oct studies; 13 Oct units 8, 9, 13; 8 Nov cost estimate.

## 2. Street surfaces and wear

**Works.** Confirm exposure from the run log, read a chart through the player's camera, validate the base-colour view, then set surfaces inside researched reflectance bands, one kind at a time. Wet ground: rough saturated stone plus water as a clear coat (lobe about 0.15) on its own material copy by a switch. Tiling scans: re-tone each unit of each copy, simulated in numpy first, mean held within 0.5%. Wear by studied counts (about one decal a wall, at most six in view, leaks two in five), measured on and off in buffers with anti-aliasing and streaming off. Stains placed from their sources and baked to the piece's unwrap; unique pieces get model-derived masks baked into their own textures; chips baked to UV on outer arrises only. Mark pictures fade at every edge; a decal tint is multiplied by the picture's grey.
**Tools, target, cost.** Chart and region readers, a repeatable photograph runner, Blender bakes, the wear generator. Chart grey 110 to 135; reference regions within 12 levels. 1.5 to 4 h a surface or piece.
**Failed.** One water film read as milky tile; world-aligned run maps read as grain; fixed-colour decals became cut-outs; decals print on loose objects and climb uprights; nothing a decal does reaches a clear coat; geometric chips read as confetti.
**Links.** [street-weathering](.claude/skills/street-weathering/SKILL.md); NB 21 to 22 Oct (lamp stains; front bake), 29 Oct (calibration; wet ground; smoke staining), 30 Oct (shop front weathering; chipped joinery), 3 Nov (tiling texture; litter), 5 Nov (ground wear), 7 Nov (worn paint as geometry).

## 3. Roofs and far scenery

**Works.** Read the target before choosing a detail; each part family its own material row. Scan flecks: measure tone and gloss together, compress only what is both pale and glossy. Separate roofs by a thin flashing just proud of the slope. Far trees: test gaps with haze off; leaf triangles 5 to 9 px round bough masses in three tiers; houses at range need gutters, sills and a damp plinth.
**Cost.** 10 to 40 min a fix, one small review.
**Failed.** Scans as photographed read as paint splashes; a shared dark row drew flashing as a bar; opaque leaves trade flecks for lumps (cards or impostors next).
**Links.** [slate-roofs](.claude/skills/slate-roofs/SKILL.md); NB 4 Nov (distant trees), 5 Nov (roof scan; breaks; far trees).

## 4. Shopfronts, windows, doors and props

**Works.** One command per family reading every number from the target. Lay out displays in numbers; run intersection, floating, real-size and printed-word rows before any long render; goods at 0.9 to 1.1 real size or left out and named. Fittings as parts at mount points; no two parts share a plane. Rooms behind glass as projected pictures by the engine's own function, highlights rolled off. Delivered pieces: fix faults in the source script, split glass so the game's glass works, join parts keeping slots, read each piece's own zones. Stateful parts (clocks, hour signs) driven by the game. Look by day and night in the game camera before reviewers.
**Tools, target, cost.** Blender as a module, CC0 models checked at full size, an OCR reader plus a person's contact sheet. A shop 2.5 to 4.5 h; a prop 1 to 2 h.
**Failed.** See-through cloth and lettering; lamp ends burning linings; shadowless lamps leaking through party walls; flat-shaded goods striping at night; extruded lettering at default resolution; broken glass as hidden meshes (set aside).
**Links.** [shopfront](.claude/skills/shopfront/SKILL.md), [asset-import](.claude/skills/asset-import/SKILL.md); NB 13 to 14 Oct units 7 to 9, 21 to 24 Oct delivered shops and kit lamp, 3 Nov (street props), 6 Nov (broken window).

## 5. Lighting, exposure and night

**Works.** Find the cause in code before tuning. A lit room's exposure from an unbound volume weighted by the camera's place in its box. Window lights from the designer's plan, swept against a bar (no channel over 1% clipped), after the light settles by frames drawn. Night: one soft desaturated high fill and tinted haze; measure near-black and clipped together. A pale sky: lower it as seen, raise its lighting share alike. Find a shadow's light by switching kinds off; raise a quality setting only inside the room needing it, found by halving level groups.
**Cost.** 0.3 to 2.5 h a fault.
**Failed.** A volume shaped at run time never entered; fixes for features switched off; saturated fills; wrong "before" pictures; a shadow no bounce reaches is set design, not a setting.
**Links.** NB 22 Oct (window night light), 26 Oct (room exposure; quality inside a room), 27 Oct (highlight burn), 4 Nov (night; baked reflection), 5 Nov (pale sky).

## 6. Garments

**Works.** Reference garment read from a shipped game; pattern at the wearer's measures; drape with straight elbows, bend back with the body's weights; bands built from the edge rings (cuff to wrist plus floor, welt 0.90, neckband 0.92); own retopology within budget; bake per piece; skin from a body with every part it touches. Hide skin with a hidden-face map painted from the garments on a copied item. Shoes before hems. The head mesh may reach the chest: clear on a 1 mm grid from the seen side, unsigned distance first. Options from the garment's record; the maps' UV layer first. Each version seen packaged at the closest distance the player meets it, beside the last good film; on failure, back to last good.
**Tools, target, cost.** Cloth tool by script bridge, Blender headless, per-pose check, packaged films at four angles. A chain about 3 h; a fix 0.2 to 1.5 h.
**Failed.** Bent-elbow drapes; simulator elastic cuffs (three tries); a skinned collar cannot follow trunk and neck; hems cut on bare feet; offline marks passed while the game drew holes; a second cloth solver judged by running the first one's exact job on the same body and the same check (about 0.6 h): call-compatible and repeatable, but the garment slid down the body in both tries, so the job layer is not the drape.
**Links.** [game-garment](.claude/skills/game-garment/SKILL.md); NB "Clothing: one game garment"; 26 to 30 Oct garment entries; 2 Nov (shoe; body importer; pose-driven fault); 7 to 9 Nov (head mesh; knitwear; stray part).

## 7. Faces and hair

**Works.** Faces from the character system's presets, landmark moves and measured colours, judged blind, then by the owner. Undressed bodies hidden in review pictures, rechecked on every later show. Hair chosen by a side-by-side in the shipped build: one level-bias switch, same camera, a patch's grey normalised by skin, a decision table written first.
**Cost.** 0.4 to 1.5 h a task.
**Failed.** One face's blend route (six blind reviews); strand sky light read blond at middle distance; cards blotched, the mesh level read as helmets; iris colours rendered green (test a grid). A pale strand crown set aside after a cause test: turning the suspected light off in the shipped build at the played level, measured by the same patch box re-checked against its old reading, left the patch pale, so the light was not the cause (0.3 h, four runs; note: one run's two pictures differed by up to 36 levels, so read several frames).
**Links.** NB "What came before" (People), 4 Nov (unfinished people), 6 Nov (recoloured garment), 7 Nov (hair sky light), 8 Nov (hair method), 9 Nov (crown cause test: production/research/hair/CROWN-STEP0-2026-11-09.md).

## 8. Motion

**Works.** Measure the source clip first (planted foot travel, pelvis height after batch retargets). A planted foot is found by its height, and worst steps are judged by eye on a fixed step (-benchmark -fps=60) with a still side camera. Stops chosen by foot, stance quarter and remaining distance, played whole. Seats as interaction spots: entries derived from clips, tested in the room for limb contact, kept by tool, chosen by heading. Walk-offs at the clip's whole-clip speed. Idle breaks additive on free limbs, every 6 to 14 s.
**Tools, target, cost.** Clip frame readers, per-frame logs on both skeletons, walk-in tests. Slide at or under 3 cm; 1 cm motion in any 4 s. 1 to 3.5 h a try.
**Failed.** Motion-matched vendor locomotion on another skeleton; feet pinned to a sliding source; in-place turns; speed read past a clip's end; neck holds the eye did not need.
**Links.** NB 22 to 26 Oct (walk, sit), 28 Oct (seat spots), 30 Oct (holds), 2 Nov (idle breaks; walk-offs), 5 Nov (frozen check), 8 Nov (side-on film).

## 9. Talk and speech

**Works.** Measure delay inside the packaged game on the real path under a spend cap; check a harness does what the product does. Alternate arms in one sitting, 40 lines or more an arm; judge the share under the bar and the tail. Cut inventions at the source: facts listed beside the rule, citations per sentence, markup stripped everywhere; a code rule before the model check; pre-filters judged on the whole chain. Escalate a failed draft to the better model. Caching saves cost, not time. Voices: corpus speakers by byte range, takes gated by accent windows, transcript and likeness, prepared lines remade, every shipped model registered.
**Tools, target, cost.** The speech bench through the game's engine, accent classifier, speech-to-text. Median first sound 2.0 s (reached 1.07). 0.3 to 2.5 h, under US$5 a try.
**Failed.** Four local checkers never cleared enough safely; first-word racing biased verdicts; partial-check benches; a cleanup deleted an unregistered voice.
**Links.** [speech-bench](.claude/skills/speech-bench/SKILL.md); NB 21 to 27 Oct voice and checker entries, 1 to 2 Nov (latency; lost voice; delay halved), 4 to 7 Nov (third try; cost lever; voice swap), 8 Nov (bulk batch).

## 10. Simulation and its port

**Works.** Each rule has its home in the reference simulation; the port answers golden rows through real code. Plant one-line breaks to find port-only rules, give each a home (test first), emit rows across every edge from real data, replant to prove rows fail. Pin text contracts byte for byte; give verdicts denominators. Wire a ported behaviour only when every reachable rung can show.
**Cost.** 0.25 to 1 h a rule; 0 mismatches.
**Failed.** Text rows compared numbers as text; standalone builds hid engine errors; mixed line endings; a check counted onlookers measured, not informed.
**Links.** NB 26 to 28 Oct port entries, 3 Nov (save refusal), 5 Nov (town deaf to its crime), 6 Nov (unwired port).

## 11. Performance and builds

**Works.** Measure the shipped build on a scripted walk with the real background load; report worst frame, count over 33.3 ms and peak memory. Trace each slow frame (the row above covers it); remove one load at a time; read per-process card memory. Lights cast near and from their own objects; reflections baked once, hash-stamped. Count a rare fault before claiming a fix. Content loaded by name needs always-cook and a staging entry; the game logs its path.
**Cost.** A headroom round 4 to 12.5 h.
**Failed.** Drifting conditions; a cache for two shader platforms stopped the cook; idle programs held card memory; the shipped copy lacked its scans for a month.
**Links.** NB 21 Oct (worst frame), 23 to 25 Oct (headroom; shared card), 27 Oct (editor-only feature; idle memory), 4 Nov (memory peak), 6 Nov (stutter; faster builds), 8 Nov (peak reading).

## 12. Testing and the packaged walk

**Works.** Every changed piece walked by the tester in that night's packaged build. The tester reads the game's movement line; stages reach their target time. Read every picture beyond the stages. The night report names the launch and stuck path legs. Asset checks sweep every shipping folder, rules fitted by kind, real faults frozen as a known list. Detectors run read-only on real files before a fix counts.
**Cost.** 0.1 to 0.6 h a fix.
**Failed.** Nights measured nothing (wrong picture type); a stale pace; empty paths counted as nights; a self-tested fix failed real files.
**Links.** [packaged-walk](.claude/skills/packaged-walk/SKILL.md); NB 27 Oct (tester steering), 5 Nov (late tester), 8 Nov (stuck path; launch line; asset checks).

## 13. Review gates

**Works.** One view per fresh reviewer beside its last good picture, briefed from the picture with what changed and known shortfalls named; new faults block. Reviewers recompute from raw files and get the requirement word for word, never the maker's verdict. Every view, day and night, both slants, reviewed once; any fix re-shoots every view it reaches. Photograph at the shipped level through the player's camera. Rehearse an exit days early.
**Cost.** Minutes a reviewer, two at a time; 20 of 20 planted faults caught beside the last good.
**Failed.** Bundles of ninety pictures hid a black block; changed-views-only reviews missed faults for rounds; wrong baselines.
**Links.** [gate](.claude/skills/gate/SKILL.md); NB 22 Oct (phase exit), 24 Oct (seventh shop), 26 Oct (picture level), 7 Nov (rehearsal).

## 14. Session running and records

**Works.** Order items in a ledger with statuses, checked on push. One permanent owner page from one data file, taps read before asking. Cap what is read most; move, never drop. An hourly gate takes over after a stop; stop work ends 10 minutes before a start. Price cache writes by cause from logs. Compare models blind on recent items. Distil skills by writer and reviewer, the writer resumed to fix. Helper-written content gets a fresh reviewer for what each person could know. Desktop tools by script APIs; one permitted menu item by accessibility invoke, logged.
**Cost.** 0.2 to 1 h a tool.
**Failed.** Chained scheduled runs; a gate counting its own runs; word caps hiding entry counts; rulings cited from memory.
**Links.** [his-page](.claude/skills/his-page/SKILL.md); NB 25 Oct (order ledger), 30 Oct (accessibility invoke), 2 Nov (standing page), 4 to 9 Nov (lean start; take-over; caps; cache money; skills distilled; blind comparison; GUI tool unattended).

### Texturing tools mapped and proved before any chain (10 Nov)
- Made: a map of who does what in materials (scan library, procedural tile tool, per-asset painting tool, own bakes, engine masters, decals, the chart), with card memory per source, checked by one fresh reviewer (13 fixes) before any run; then both tools proved headless on a built-in mesh.
- Method: read the makers' docs in full; install portable with checksums on the data drive; prove the command line on a stock mesh (bake, mask, engine preset export), run twice and compare pixels; read the export preset's channel order from the tool itself, not the docs. Hand per-asset painting into the engine as one small mask over a shared tile, never as a unique full set.
- Tools: TextureCraft 0.5.0 and MaterialCraft 0.3.0 CLIs; Blender to export the high drape; Python for packing masks and seamless noise.
- Target and check: same pixels twice (passed); the edge-wear generator's level against the street's 3 to 10% worn share (0.7 to 0.8; the default 0.5 shows nothing).
- Cost: about 45 min. Failed: masks from the game mesh alone came out blocky; baking from the high drape fixed it. The procedural tool's 0.3 has no ready patterns or noise: patterns drawn as true-size SVG, noise made outside. A build was run on a STOP disk reading (chained after the check): check first, alone.
- Evidence: production/research/texture-pipeline/ (MAP, MAP-REVIEW, PROOF).

### A turn-end check that holds only the scheduled worker (11 Nov)
- Made: a stop hook that sends the scheduled production session back once when it ends a turn without handing over; tightened the same day so it holds nothing else. Never-used skills hidden for this project only (local settings).
- Method: tell who the session is from how its log opens and from the start gate's verdict inside it (the opening message is the scheduled task's tag AND the gate said GO), never from a name appearing anywhere in the log; a guard lets a turn already sent back go, so it cannot loop.
- Tools: Claude Code Stop hook (command, JSON on stdin, decision block); Python reading the session's JSONL log.
- Target and check: hold every GO run and nothing else. Own tests 14 of 14; on the last 80 real logs, 41 GO runs held, 32 WAIT runs and the owner's own chats let go (the first version held all of them).
- Cost: about 0.5 h. Failed: the first test (the task's name in the first 400 KB of the log) held any chat that had read the task file, and would have sent every WAIT run of a 15-minute schedule back to work beside the live session; its own synthetic tests passed, running it on real logs found it.
- Evidence: tools/stop_hook.py (--selftest).
