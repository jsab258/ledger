# A threat to a witness

For Jafar's page, 30 September (town list 6cd; the eleventh sweep,
production/research/checklist-sweep-2026-09-28/NOTE-11.md).

In the first hour Sheila or Ada may see Tom at Rita's window and ask him about
it. The most natural thing a player types in a crime game is a threat: "Say a
word and you'll regret it." Today the town never learns he threatened anybody:
it never makes them warier, never becomes a story, and next day they treat him
as if he had said nothing. The old code for threats cannot simply be switched
on: every one of the forty has the same middling nerve (their own is the
traits question already on your page), so every threat would silence
everybody.

## What 1990 says (production/research/threats-1990/NOTE-2026-09-29.md)

There was no offence of threatening a witness until 1994; a threat meant to
stop one was perverting the course of justice, rarely charged (816
convictions in all of England and Wales in 1992), and a vague "you'll regret
it" was at most a public-order fine. Home Office work of the time found
threats silenced witnesses mostly over serious or organised crime, and that
people who were threatened usually told a neighbour rather than the police.
Over a broken window, a threat buys little and gets talked about.
