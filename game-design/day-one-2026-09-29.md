# Day one: Sheila's walk-round, and the street's first talk of him

For Jafar's page, 30 September (town list 6cg; the first hour you approved,
minutes 0 to 7). None of it was written: the builder had nothing to stage,
and the street said nothing about him until night one's story came back.

## Sheila's walk-round, in her words

From canon and the street's plain facts as recommended on your 29 September
page (Mickey's own office locked since he died, and she keeps the key; the
flat over the office). She calls him new management, as you ruled.

1. At the door: "New management. Sheila Dunn, I keep the books. Come on, I'll
   show you round before you start looking lost."
2. The office: "Phone, radio, and the fare book. Every fare goes in the book.
   That was Mickey's rule, and it's mine now."
3. The drivers: "One driver by day, one by night, and the radio in between.
   The takings have been thin since the docks laid men off."
4. The door she does not open: "That's Mickey's office. It's been locked since
   he died, and I've the key. Not today."
5. The flat: "Your flat's upstairs, over the office. His things are still in
   it. I didn't have the heart."
6. And, already approved, sending him out: "That's Ron on the rank. Go and say
   hello. Everyone out there wants a look at you."

Played or skipped, it ends the same way: she has met him, and the hint to talk
comes.

## The street's first talk of him

Whoever is about Mickey's when he comes, and Ada at her window, hold it
first-hand, and the town passes it on: of the 41, six see him come (Ron,
Sheila, the dispatcher, a day driver, Ada, and the sign painter doing the
street's boards), 19 hold it by
Monday noon, 30 by the evening, 33 by Tuesday noon. It is nobody's secret,
changes nothing in how they treat him, and gives way to any other story of
him. Said to his face once, by somebody who can tell it is him (they saw
him come, or know him well enough; never in the coat), who has nothing else
of him in mind and has not yet talked with him; never by Sheila, who showed
him round, nor by June. On sight of a stranger, that is the five who saw him
come:

- By someone who saw him come: "You'll be Mickey's nephew, then." / "So
  you're the new owner." / "Saw you come in. Mickey's nephew, is it?" / "You've the look of Mickey about you." / "Settling in, are you?" /
  "New in the office, then."
- By someone who heard: "Heard Mickey's nephew had come. That'll be you." /
  "You'll be the new owner they're all on about." / "Word is Mickey's nephew's
  taken the office. That you?" / "So you're the one taking on Mickey's." /
  "They say you've come to run the cabs." / "Heard there's a new face at
  Mickey's."

## The reviewers' remaining notes

Three fresh reviews; their faults (the arrival entering people's manner,
Sheila remarking on it, naming him to people who only heard, speaking over a
deed) are fixed. Narrow points left: the game must call the arrival once, when
he comes; a first-hand copy lingers about nine days, so "Saw you come in" can
still come in week two; and a reply that draws on it shows in the session
record as knowing something from before the session.
