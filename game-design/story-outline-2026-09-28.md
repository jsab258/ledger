# LEDGER: the story, in outline

For Jafar's approval, 28 September (town list, item 5). Under two pages, not a
script. From canon, DECISIONS (D30, D33, D58), the July act drafts he approved
(legacy/v1-era/act-drafts), the endings as built (ActThree.cs), the crime layer,
and the research on authored stories in simulations and on British crime
fiction. Where the July drafts assumed a pub, drink or dollars, this follows
canon since: Mickey's is a minicab office.

## The spine

A stranger inherits a position, not a business. Tom Nowak arrives with one
suitcase and a letter; his uncle Mickey, dead three weeks, has left him Mickey's,
a cab office on Quay Street in the Hook, a half-dead outfit and a book of debts
nobody can collect. The town decides who he is by what it knows, and what it
calls him says how far he has got: the new owner, Nowak, Tom, Tommy. Three
questions in turn: what did you inherit; how big can it get before your two
lives touch; which life was real.

Underneath, never explained: the dock labour scheme ended in July 1989 and more
than half the registered dockers' jobs went. That is why the debts will not pay,
why the outfit is half dead, and why a loud new crew can rise. The register is
British noir, which runs on class, not greed: who may speak to whom, and how.

## Arrival

He comes after the funeral, which he missed; June, Mickey's daughter, came back
for it and is still in town, wanting nothing from the office. Sheila Dunn shows
him round and stops at the one door she does not open; she promised Mickey to
size him up. Ron Kirby, kept on since the docks let him go, calls him boss and
watches him. Darren Milner is already on the pavement, friendly and owing. Ada
watches from her window. By nightfall half the street has seen him and none of
it knows him, and the first thing the town does is talk.

## Act I: The Inheritance (the first week)

The most scripted act, as the research advises: the world is most predictable
before the player changes it. Five threads, as approved in July: the outfit's
claim (Mickey's arrangements outlive him; the first ask comes on night one, and
refusing breaks his deal, not a quest); the warehouse fire, the founding rumour
Alison Sedman will not drop and Ellis will not close; Mickey's real book, which
Sheila shows when she trusts him or when Ellis's pressure forces her hand, with a page in Mickey's hand tying the
fire to the outfit; the street deciding who he is, every act seen, retold and
now shown; and someone worth being honest for, Alison (built) or Philip Danby
(still to write). Ada's tea is the week's first test of which life he keeps. The
week ends with Sheila, over the real book: "So which is it going to be?" Wind it
down, take it over, or refuse to say; the street learns the answer.

## Act II: The Squeeze

Opens when the business has grown. Each rival attacks a different ledger, and
notices him only through what its own people see. Maureen Jensen, the Widow,
and the dockside syndicate go after his people: poaching Ron, taxing his
drivers. Geoffrey Agar and the old-money machine go after his clean money with
paper: the office's private-hire licence, a solicitor's letter, an inspection.
Danny Cammack's new crew go after his cover: noise on Quay Street that brings
heat he did not earn. The Fixer sells true reads, a meeting, one truce. DS Carol
Ellis joins the street's crimes to the warehouse fire: one case. One collision
is guaranteed: an evening with his day life is interrupted by his night life at
the door, and both worlds see each other. It ends at a sit-down with one head:
accept, defy, or counter with what he knows.

## Act III: The Ledger Comes Due (ruled in July: an audit)

A courteous letter naming a date: a VAT inspection by Customs and Excise, which
ran VAT in 1990. The fare book has been lying since day one: too little
laundered and the night money has no source, too much and a cab office on this
street earned more than it could. The inspector brings one item each morning;
Tom produces it or asks for it in writing. Sheila, if she trusts him, tells him
where the lie holds. Ellis offers a trade: give her a rival, at the price that
the street learns who talks; refusing her can still reach the best ending, but
only through what the town believes. Someone in his crew may ask to take it
over. On the last day he can reach only a few people, and whoever picks up is
the campaign he played. Then the books open.

## How the endings are reached (as built)

Never picked from a list: each is a state the world is in when the inspection
closes, and when several fit, the stronger wins: Quiet, then Both, then the Straight Life, then the Kingdom. **Quiet**: he hands it to
someone who can hold it (competence, loyalty, standing, no feud), never while
he is hunted for a killing; three watch-only days
show whether it holds. **Both**: the business survives, one friendship holds,
the street does not hold his rackets as fact, Ellis's case is answerable, and
the books both hold and make sense; rare, and by the July ruling not meant to
be reached on a first playthrough (aimed at by the balance, not enforced). **The Kingdom**: the business survives and the books
hold; he keeps what he built, and finds the people in it civil and no more
unless one friend still counts him.
**The Straight Life**: no business at the close, never built or sold up, and
one friendship holds. **Burn Both**: none of these; what doing nothing produces.
By D58 there is no redemption path, and every cost must be visible as it
approaches, through Tom's own reading with its reasons (D33): a cost he could
not see is a trap.

## Rules the writing keeps

Beats fire on states, not on sequence; the only dates are those people in the
story name (the week's end, the letter). No single beat can fail the run. The
simulation decides who is alive, who knows what, who is where and how the law
stands; the models only voice it. No drink, gambling or children; the town
remembers everything, and now shows it.

## Owed, already ordered

Act III's text in the Core still reads as a pub (the letter, the cellar, duty
on stock, a cap on laundering set by bar takings): D58 made rewording it
checkpoint work, and the cap must be re-derived for a cab office (fares against
fuel, cars and drivers; VAT powers under the 1983 VAT Act, not the excise act).
