# Jafar's ruling, 2026-09-21: the concept sheet and the research govern every authored asset, and they outrank his own reply

STATUS: LOG, 2026-09-21. NOT CURRENT once the agent definitions, the queue item
and the consolidation it names have landed: from then those are the reading

## His words, verbatim

"Correction to the lamp brief: its form comes from the approved in-house Hook
sheet and the period research, not from my reply, which was general knowledge
rather than our own research and could contradict it. Read the Hook sheet's
lamps and whatever the research says about street furniture, and if they
disagree with what I said, they win.

And a rule from now on: every authored asset's brief names the concept sheet
and the research that govern it, and follows them. This is the second time a
design question was debated that the research and the concept art had already
answered, and the facades must not be the third. Pull the research
consolidation forward far enough that the studio can actually read what governs
the next asset."

## What it decides

1. HE SUBORDINATED HIS OWN WORDS TO THE PROJECT'S OWN MATERIAL, explicitly, and
   that is the unusual half of this ruling. "If they disagree with what I said,
   they win." The approved in-house sheet and the commissioned research outrank
   a general-knowledge answer from him, including one given an hour earlier
   with a budget reading attached.

2. THE STANDING RULE: every authored asset's brief NAMES the concept sheet and
   the research that govern it, and FOLLOWS them. Naming is not enough on its
   own and neither is following without naming: the brief has to say which
   sheet and which sections, so that a reader can check the asset against them
   without reconstructing the reasoning.

3. THE DIAGNOSIS IS HIS AND IT IS A COUNT: "the second time a design question
   was debated that the research and the concept art had already answered, and
   the facades must not be the third." So this rule exists because the studio
   twice spent effort deciding something already decided. The cost is not a
   wrong asset, it is a debate that need not have happened.

4. THE CONSOLIDATION IS PULLED FORWARD, scoped by the words "FAR ENOUGH that
   the studio can actually read what governs the next asset". That is not all
   of queue 394. It is the part that makes the next brief writable.

## What the sources actually say, read tonight rather than assumed

The correction was applied to the running lamp job within the same turn it
arrived. What the two governing sources say, checked rather than recalled:

- RESEARCH, `game-design/research/art-direction.md:346`, in a list of British
  tells against American street reads: "sodium lanterns on swan-neck STEEL
  columns", beside drainpipes, chimney pots, TV aerials and double yellow
  lines. The street-furniture section from about line 309 specifies colours by
  name for other furniture, and R-B4 at line 319 sets the accent budget: a
  low-chroma British field punctuated by a very small number of mandated
  high-chroma objects, of which "period sodium amber" is one of three.
- THE HOOK SHEET, `production/art/atlas-01/concepts/hook.png` on
  `origin/art/atlas-01`, cropped at 3x: the street column is SLENDER and DARK,
  reading near-black against the sky, with a SHALLOW LONG swan-neck arc and a
  SMALL FLAT CANOPY head aimed down the street, lit warm amber. No fluting, no
  ladder bar, no scroll, no finial.

THEY DO NOT CONTRADICT HIM, THEY SHARPEN HIM, and the sharpening is exactly
what his rule is for. He said "concrete or steel"; the research names STEEL and
the sheet shows a slender dark painted column, so concrete is out for the hero
column. He said "swan-neck"; the sheet shows a shallower, longer arc than the
quarter circle the blockout approximates, which the resident's brief had
carried forward as if it were settled. THE ENDPOINTS STAY: `lantern0..3` at
x = 8, 18, 28, 38 m, y = 4.965 m, and a 4.7 m shaft at 0.114 m round. Only the
arc between them changes.

ALSO IN THE SHEET AND NOT THE SAME ASSET, recorded so it is not lost twice: the
Harbour Office carries a dark conical BRACKET lamp with a wire cage guard over
a warm bulb, under the eaves. A wall fixture the street will need, already
specified by the approved sheet, and authored by nobody yet.
