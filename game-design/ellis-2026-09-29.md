# When DS Ellis turns up, and what she has

For Jafar's approval, 29 September (town list 6ar; the checklist's K7, A15.09
and A15.11). The first hour he approved says that on days 4 and 5, if the
street has got loud about him, DS Carol Ellis turns up on Quay Street. Nothing
decided when she comes or what she has. This follows the research on 1990
policing (production/research/police-response-1990/): most offences are never
reported, a victim reports far more than a witness, a detective walks a street
only after serious crime, and a charge needs a witness who names him.
The street should always know more than she does.

## Who goes to the police

- **A shopkeeper whose window is put in** reports it, for the insurance. That
  brings a uniformed constable and a crime report, not a detective.
- **Somebody hurt or robbed** reports it unless they know him well enough to
  settle it themselves, or are too frightened: a wounding or a robbery more
  readily than a push. The dead report nothing; whoever finds the body does.
- **A witness** goes only for a crime a detective takes: a wounding or a
  robbery only unafraid and not on his side; a killing whenever not on his
  side, the brave because they can and the nervous because they crack. An
  ordinary witness, neither brave nor against him, says nothing about a
  wounding, as most witnesses said nothing.
- **A sighting that is no crime** (a man with an envelope at the landing) is
  never reported. It is talk.
- **Anybody on a hook** says nothing; **anybody bought or frightened quiet**
  says nothing, except about a body, which no money keeps.

Each person's own nerve and loyalty decide, so the same deed goes differently
with different people there. The cast file does not yet give anybody their
own; until it does (the town list's next item), everybody is ordinary and no
witness goes to the police.

## When she comes

Ellis is already on the warehouse fire, the outline's Act I thread, so her
enquiries bring her through the Hook. She comes to Quay Street, each reason
once: for a body; for a crime a detective takes, once somebody reports it; and,
from day 4, for the street's talk once three people of his day world are
passing talk of his nights round (heard, not seen).

Measured with the town's routines (TownReach --loud): on night one only four
of the cast are out to see him, and whether the talk gets loud depends on
which of them does. Plainly seen by Ron, who meets half the street, five to
eight people are passing it round on days 3 to 5, so she comes on day 4; by
Dusan, two; by the other two, one or none. A second night seen changes that
only when Ron is one of the two who saw him; any pair without Ron stays at two
or below. In the coat, one night seen by Ron reaches three only on day 3,
before she can come for talk, and a second night with Ron holds it at three on
day 4 or 5. So at three she comes in the first hour only if Ron saw him, on
either night; at two, Dusan's sighting would bring her too.

## What she can put to him

| She holds | She can |
|---|---|
| the street's talk | ask him where he was; nothing more |
| statements from people who saw it but could not say who, however many | put the description to him |
| a signed statement from someone who saw it was him | arrest: for a window, a constable's arrest (she does not come for damage); for a wounding, a robbery or a killing, hers; for a common assault, only a summons |

That follows the law of 1990 (production/research/police-response-1990/ARREST-2026-09-29.md):
criminal damage was an arrestable offence even for a window, a common assault
was not. The law also let an officer arrest on reasonable suspicion, talk
included; she waits for a witness who names him, because that is what a charge
needs, so that the street's talk alone never takes him. That is the game's
choice, and yours to change.

A face somebody could pick out, short of knowing him, counts only as a
description: no identification parade is modelled, which is a choice for you.
In 1990 his silence could not be held against him; the caution's exact words
are not yet found, so no line of hers quotes it.
