# LEDGER: the first hour, on paper

For Jafar's approval (town list 6j; ROADMAP stage 4, "the first hour").
Under two pages, not a script. It follows the story outline on the 28
September page (arrival and Act I), canon, and three pieces of research:
teaching a social system in thirty minutes, what players do when a world
remembers them, and how stories travel between the named cast (measured 28
September).

## What the first hour has to do

The research is plain about it. Remembering is quiet and forgetting is loud: a
town that tracks you only tints its greetings unless something comes back and
is said to your face. So the first hour is built on four steps, and the last
is the one no hint can fake:

1. **He moves.** Walking, talking, the office, the street.
2. **He is seen** doing something that matters, or is known to have refused.
3. **It is written down.** The town holds it; his Ledger holds what he
   believes it holds.
4. **It comes back**, said to his face by somebody who was not there.

The Meridian Test asks that the world visibly know him at least once in the
first thirty minutes. Among the forty named people as they live now, a plainly
seen night reaches a second person in about six real minutes (the slowest
tenth, nineteen); in the coat that makes him harder to recognise, it reaches
anyone at all only about six times in ten within a week. Those figures are a
best case: they assume he has met everyone and average sightings over every
hour of the week. Only somebody who can tell it is him shows what they have
heard, so the one who brings it back on day 2 has to be somebody he met on day
1, and whether that happens by minute thirty is not yet measured.

## The clock

The old game ran two game minutes to a real second, so a day was twelve real
minutes; the new game has not set its clock yet. The times below assume the old
rate and a nine o'clock start. At a slower rate the hour covers fewer days, and
day 2 falls later: if it falls past minute thirty, "it comes back" misses the
Meridian bar, so the rate is chosen with this hour in mind. The days are the
likely order when the story is played straight; as the outline says, beats
fire on states, not on the clock.

## The hour

**Minutes 0 to 7: day 1.** Tom arrives with one suitcase and the letter, after
the funeral he missed. Sheila Dunn is at Mickey's to meet him, shows him round,
and stops at the one door she does not open. The
first time he can speak to anyone, the game tells him, once, that the town
answers in words written by an AI model and that a line can be reported. On the
street: Ron Kirby at the rank calls him boss and watches him; Darren Milner is
on the pavement, friendly and owing; Ada is at her window; June, Mickey's
daughter, back for the funeral, wants nothing from the office and says so.
Strangers call him the new owner; the people he has met use his name, because
they know him now (Sheila's card has her keep him at "new management" until he
earns it: see the question below).

**Minutes 7 to 11: night one.** The outfit's first ask comes, as Mickey's
arrangements outlive him: a small job for the night, and a coat that makes him
harder to recognise. He can refuse; refusing ends Mickey's arrangement, and does
not fail the game.

**Minutes 12 to 24: day 2, it comes back.** Whatever he did with the ask, the
outfit's people talk about it, so the night is a story either way. If he went
and was seen, it reaches a second person within minutes;
somebody who heard looks at him longer, and the first time Darren, or anyone he
met yesterday who has heard, sees him, they say it to his face ("Funny hours
you keep"). If he went unseen, or refused, it is the outfit's talk that comes
back the same way ("Heard you told them no"). His Ledger shows what he
believes the street now knows.

**Minutes 24 to 36: day 3.** Ada asks him in for tea on the evening the
outfit's second ask falls: the week's first test of which life he keeps.
Alison Sedman asks him about the warehouse fire, the story she will not drop.

**Minutes 36 to 60: days 4 and 5.** If the street has got loud about him, DS
Carol Ellis turns up on Quay Street. What he did on those two nights keeps
coming back, from more of the people he has met.

The week ends on day 7, a little under half an hour later: Sheila, over the
real book, "So which is it going to be?"

## How it teaches

Every hint fires the first time it can mean something, never on the clock: the
first time he can talk, the first night job, the first time he is seen, the
first remark he overhears about himself, the first time he opens the Ledger.
The research found the old game's four hints all firing inside ninety seconds
because they ran on the clock, two of them overlapping. Hints are the world's
own words where they can be (Sheila on the rank, Ron on the coat), and a line
of plain text only where the world has nobody to say it.

## What is not in the first hour

The three rivals, beyond a name overheard. Act II. Any ending. Money beyond the
takings and the first job's envelope. Nothing that needs a number to be
understood. And not carried over from the old game: its day-5 scene with Ron,
which breaks the content rule.

## What exists and what does not

- **In the new game:** walking, talking with its check on what a character may
  know, being seen, the story spreading.
- **In the simulation, not yet in the game:** the longer look and the word said
  about him or to his face; the AI notice and the report button; the name
  ladder.
- **Not built:** the outfit's asks, the outfit's people talking about what he
  did with them, Ada's tea and
  Alison's question as scenes, Ellis turning up, the Ledger's screen, the
  hints on first occurrence, and the new game's clock.
