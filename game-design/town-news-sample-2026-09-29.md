# The town's own news: one sample

Until now every story the street passed on was about Tom, so before he did
anything the street had nothing to say, and over two hours the only stories
anyone told were his. This is the first story of the town's own, to hear
before any more are written.

## The story

**Hal and Rita had words in the pawn, and nobody knows what about.**

It happens at Rita's pawn at eleven on Monday morning, when Hal calls there as
he does every Monday. Everybody in the pawn then saw it: Rita and Hal
themselves, Darren Milner, the pawn's back-room hand, its appraiser, its clerk,
and the locksmith who works its counter in the mornings. From them it travels
the way any story does, friend to friend, as their days cross; Hal and Rita
keep it to themselves.

It names only people the street knows by name, says only what was seen, and
leaves the why open: nobody in the town can say what the row was about, so no
character can invent it.

## How Tom hears it

Two neighbours, as he passes:

- "Here, Hal and Rita had words in the pawn, and nobody knows what about." / "Never."
- "Talk of the street, this. Hal and Rita had words in the pawn, and nobody knows what about." / "I'd not have thought it."
- "Seems Hal and Rita had words in the pawn, and nobody knows what about." / "Well, it's none of my business."
- "Hal and Rita had words in the pawn, and nobody knows what about, apparently." / "Who told you that?"

He makes out four tellings at most; after that it is the street's murmur.

## What it does

Measured over two hours of play on the named cast's street (TownReach): he
overhears it in every run, the first time about a minute in, four times in
all, never the same words twice. It raises nobody's suspicion of him.

## If you approve

About ten more of the same shape, each drawn from the routines (who is where,
when), each checked the same way, then the builder puts them in the game and
records the crowd voices for the telling lines.
