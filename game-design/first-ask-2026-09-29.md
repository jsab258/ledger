# The outfit's first ask

For Jafar's approval, 29 September (town list 6z; the checklist's A33.01). The
first hour he approved says: on night one the outfit's first ask comes, as
Mickey's arrangements outlive him: a small job for the night, and a coat that
makes him harder to recognise. He can refuse; refusing ends Mickey's
arrangement and does not fail the game. The second ask falls on day 3, the
evening of Ada's tea. Whatever he does, the night is a story, and it comes
back to him on day 2 ("Heard you told them no"). The old game's rule, three
missed nights and "the outfit stopped calling, then they sent someone", ended
the game; this replaces it.

## One thing for you first: whose outfit

Canon says Tom inherits "a half-dead criminal outfit". This reads it as
Mickey's place in an outfit, not a crew Tom now commands: the July drafts and
the outline you approved have the outfit asking and Tom able to break "his
deal", which only works if the outfit is somebody else's. It is not one of the
three rivals (the outline has Mickey's real book tie the warehouse fire to it).
Your page asks you to confirm or correct this.

## The ask

- **Who brings it:** Ron, after dark, at the office, with a plain dark coat
  over his arm and an envelope. Not Mickey's own coat: one people know would
  help them place whoever wears it. He is Mickey's own man and calls Tom boss;
  he does not dress it up: "Mickey took this down the ferry landing, regular.
  They'll be expecting it." Then the coat line from the hints: "If you go
  tonight, boss, wear that. In the dark nobody looks twice at a coat."
- **When:** the first night, then every other night: the second ask falls on
  day 3, as the first hour has it.
- **The job:** carry the envelope to the ferry landing after ten and hand it to
  the man who asks for Mickey's. What is in it is never said.
- **The outfit's man at the landing:** never named; to the street, a man who
  hangs about the ferry landing at night. He has his breakfast at the cafe on
  weekdays, where Darren starts his day, and the two know each other a little.

## What can happen, and what it leaves

| He | The arrangement | What the outfit's man tells, having seen it himself |
|---|---|---|
| hands the envelope over | stands; the next ask two nights later | "Mickey's nephew brought the envelope down the landing" (the only answer that is a secret of Tom's night life, so a friend of his days who hears it grows warier) |
| tells Ron no | ends at once: no more asks; Ron carries the answer down to the landing | "Ron came down the landing to say Mickey's nephew told them no" |
| says nothing and does not go | stands; the outfit's patience falls, and recovers a little with each night done: three nights away in a row end it | "Mickey's nephew never turned up at the landing" |

It is the outfit's talk, as the first hour has it: the outfit's man tells it
first, and Ron, who keeps things quiet for the owner, tells nobody. A night
nobody answered, however it happened, counts as a night he stayed away, so the
asks never simply stop. Never a game over: an ended arrangement is a fact the
town talks about and the story can use later (Act I's outfit thread), not a
loss.

Measured with the town's routines (TownReach --teller outfit_man, each
answer), told at ten on night one, each answer reaches Darren at his breakfast,
minute 13 of play, and he says it to Tom's face. A Friday's or Saturday's night
waits for Monday's breakfast. With the outfit's man in the town, the first
hour's own figures rise: a plainly seen night reaches Tom's face 16 times in 16
(was 13 in 13), in the coat 8 in 16 (was 6 in 13).

## What they say to his face

Each answer has its own lines, so the night comes back as what it was: one
said as he passes by somebody who heard it (not the man who was there), who can
be stopped and asked:

- **Handed over:** "Heard you did Mickey's run." "Down the landing after dark,
  I hear. Same as Mickey." "They say you've picked up where Mickey left off."
  "Word is you kept Mickey's arrangement. I'd keep that quiet." "Late one, was
  it? Down by the ferry." "So you're doing Mickey's rounds now."
- **Told them no:** "Heard you told them no." "Heard you sent Ron back with
  it." "They say you turned Mickey's lot down." "Word is you said no to them.
  Brave or daft, I've not decided." "You told them no, then. Not like Mickey, that."
  "Not doing Mickey's errands, I hear."
- **Stayed away:** "Heard they waited on you at the landing." "Somebody stood
  by the ferry half the night, I'm told." "They say you never turned up." "Word
  is you left them waiting. They'll not like that." "Mickey'd never have kept
  them waiting, they say." "Busy, were you? Not at the landing, anyway."
