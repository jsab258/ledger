# A way to wait, and what stops it

Town list 6ci, decided by the town on 29 September under Jafar's ruling that
detail is ours; he may overturn it. The twelfth sweep is
production/research/checklist-sweep-2026-09-28/NOTE-12.md and the research is
production/research/waiting/NOTE-2026-09-29.md. The clock runs at two game
minutes a second, with a way to wait until evening. Built plainly, a wait
simply skips the hours, and the week's beats pass while he is away:

- Ron never reaches him with the envelope, so he never gets the chance to say
  yes or no.
- Ada's tea becomes a stand-up.
- The constable, DS Ellis and Sheila's Sunday question all happen without him.

One key would do what the game never warned him of.

## What it does

A wait, or a sleep, ends early for what the town has for him, with one line:

| When | The line |
|---|---|
| Ron comes after dark (eight) on an ask night | "Ron's at the door with something for you." |
| The landing, from ten, once he has the envelope | "They'll be expecting the envelope at the landing after ten." |
| Ada's pot at nine, once she has asked him | "Ada's pot goes on at nine." |
| A constable calls at ten | "There's a constable asking for you." |
| DS Ellis on the street at nine | "DS Ellis is on Quay Street, asking after you." |
| Sunday at ten, Sheila in the office | "Sheila's in the office on her day off, waiting for you." |
| Once she has asked: in time to be with her by half past five, as she goes home at six | "Sheila wants your answer before the day's out." |
| In the cells, at his release | "They're letting you go." |

- **Each line stops a wait once.** The game remembers which lines he has seen.
  Once told, he may wait on past any of them; it does not stop him twice.
- **The tea, the landing and the office:** the wait ends early enough for his
  walk there. Each has its own walk time. If that time has already come when
  he starts a wait, the line shows at once. (The independent check found the
  first version skipped such a line, so a sleep ran straight through the tea.)
- **Ron:** if Ron has not found him yet on an ask night, Ron knocks, even on a
  sleep, and after midnight it is still that night's envelope.
- **Refused:** the only wait refused is during Sheila's walk-round.
- **"Wait until":** the game can offer to wait until each known appointment,
  such as "wait until Ada's tea". The research found this is what makes a wait
  something players use to reach appointments rather than to miss them.

## Measured

The whole week on paper, with him waiting every evening from six till ten the
next morning, and on the Saturday night till Sunday noon. The command is
`TownReach --week-waits` on the forty-one.

- **Skipping the hours plainly:** none of the 16 rows comes out as it would
  with no waiting.
  - Ron never reaches him, so he never gets the chance to tell Ron no.
  - Ada is stood up, and where she saw the window he is charged on day 5.
  - Sheila's Sunday question never happens.
- **The wait that stops:** in a wait's hours he does a thing only once its
  line has stopped the wait. With walks of 0, 30 to 120, and 60 to 180
  minutes, nothing he does is missed for want of a stop, so the week comes out
  exactly as with no waiting. That is one measure, not two.
- **What the paper week does not cover:** the constable, who comes at ten when
  every wait here has ended, and Sheila's last hour; the Core's tests cover
  both.

## One thing to know

The ask's design let a man sleep through the evening, with the envelope
passing unasked. With a wait key, that is the silent skip the sweep found, so
Ron knocks.
