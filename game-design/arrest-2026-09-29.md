# What an arrest does

For Jafar's page, 29 September (town list 6bp; ROADMAP stage 3, an arrest
reachable from live play). From the research
(production/research/police-response-1990/CUSTODY-2026-09-29.md): in 1990 an
arrest cost hours, not the game.

## One question for you first

Today nothing can lead to an arrest. The friends' build has one crime, Rita's
window; Rita never goes to the police (her trade), and a witness went to the
police only for a crime a detective takes. So:

- **(A) A witness to the window can go to the police** (recommended): only
  somebody who saw him plainly, is not afraid and has cooled on him (Ada, say,
  after he stood her up for her tea); at the town's middle tempers nobody
  does, so an arrest is rare and earned. Built this way meanwhile.
- **(B) Rita reports her own window**, for the insurance, as most shopkeepers
  did; a constable asks round, and a witness who cooled on him names him.
- **(C) No arrest in the first build**: only for violence, which it does not
  have yet.

## What happens

- **Who comes:** for a window, a uniformed constable, the morning after
  somebody gave a statement naming him; for a wounding, a robbery or a
  killing, DS Ellis on her visit, once she holds a statement.
- **The station:** the custody sergeant books him in and keeps anything that
  may be evidence: the coat, if he wore it to the deed.
- **How long:** as the Home Office's own study at the end of the 1980s
  found: a window about six hours (two if he owns up), a wounding eight, a
  robbery twelve.
- **How it ends:** a window he owns up to, a caution and nothing more (and his
  coat back); otherwise charged, on the named statement, and bailed to the
  magistrates' next weekday sitting, so a Friday's charge waits for Monday
  (that date is my inference). Never taken twice for the same deed. Never a
  game end.
- **A killing is my choice, not the record's:** held the twenty-two hours the
  most serious offences averaged, then bailed to come back in four weeks while
  they make their enquiries. In 1990 he would more likely have been kept for
  court; the game has no prison, so say if a killing should end differently.
- **The street:** whoever is about sees the police put him in the car and
  passes it on; once he is out, they say so to his face, as those who saw it
  ("Saw the police put you in the car.") or heard it ("Heard the police had
  you in."). Time in the cells on an ask night is time Ron cannot find him
  with the outfit's ask.

## Measured

On the forty (TownReach --arrest): Rita's window on a Tuesday night, seen
plainly by Ada after she had cooled on him; she goes to the police on the
Wednesday, a constable takes him from the office at ten on the Thursday, six
at Mickey's see it, he is out at four, charged, to answer at the magistrates on
the Friday; twenty of the forty-one hold it by that evening and twenty-five by
the next noon.

## The lines

Whoever saw it: "Saw the police put you in the car." "They had you in the
back of a police car, didn't they." "Saw you go off with the police. You're
out, then." "I watched them take you. Didn't look like a social call."
"You're back. I saw them take you off." "Didn't expect to see you out so
soon."

Whoever heard it: "Heard the police had you in." "They say you were taken
in." "Heard you'd been down the station." "Word is the police lifted you."
"Out already? Heard they'd taken you in." "They say the police came for you."
