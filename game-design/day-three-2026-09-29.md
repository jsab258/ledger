# Day 3: Ada's tea, and Alison's question

For Jafar's approval, 29 September (town list 6bg). The first hour he approved
puts two scenes on day 3: "Ada asks him in for tea on the evening the outfit's
second ask falls: the week's first test of which life he keeps. Alison Sedman
asks him about the warehouse fire, the story she will not drop."

## Ada's tea

- **When:** the evening of the outfit's second ask (the third day), for a man
  who has met her by then; she does not ask a stranger in. She asks him the
  first time she sees him that day, before nine: "There'll be a pot on at nine
  tonight, if you want it. I don't ask twice, mind." If she does not see him
  that day, she does not ask.
- **Coming means staying:** to count as having come, he is there by half past
  nine and sits with her through half past ten, never away more than a few
  minutes. Dropping in and going is leaving early.
- **The test:** the landing is open till one, but at the first hour's clock the
  walk from her step to the landing takes about two game hours, so a man who
  stays for his tea reaches the landing near one, if he hurries. And Ada keeps a
  window on Quay Street: if he goes down to the landing for the outfit that
  night, before his tea, after it or instead of it, she sees him go ("Mickey's
  nephew went off down towards the ferry, late, the night I'd asked him in for
  his tea"), and that is hers to tell like any sighting of his nights.
- **What it leaves:** her regard for him. Sat with her, she thinks better of
  him, remembers it ("Mickey's nephew came for his tea and sat with me till gone
  half ten. There's more to him than they're saying."), and would no longer go
  to the police about him over something she saw. Left early, a little warmer,
  not enough for that. Stood up, cooler, and she remembers it ("I asked Mickey's
  nephew in for his tea. He never came. I'll not ask again."). A woman at a
  window who has not warmed to him is one who might go to DS Ellis. Never the
  game.
- If he no longer has an arrangement (he told them no), the tea is still that
  evening, and is only tea: a walk that night is only a walk.

## Alison's question

Waits on you. She asks him about the warehouse fire, and the new game has no
fire yet: not when it burned, whose warehouse it was, whether anybody was hurt,
or what the street says about it. The outline has Mickey's real book tie the
fire to the outfit, so what the street believes and what is true differ. Your
page asks how the fire should be written.

Approve Ada's tea, or say what to change.
