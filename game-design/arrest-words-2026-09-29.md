# What he is told at the arrest and on release

For Jafar's page, 29 September (town list 6bu; the checklist's A15.17, the
consequences made clear, and A46.01, plain words). From the research
(production/research/police-response-1990/WORDS-2026-09-29.md): the caution is
the Code of Practice's own words of 1990, safe to voice.

## Taking him in

The constable, or DS Ellis: "I'm arresting you on suspicion of criminal
damage. You do not have to say anything unless you wish to do so, but what
you say may be given in evidence." (wounding, robbery or murder in place of
criminal damage.) The caution is the Code's; the first sentence is plain
words.

## At the station

The custody sergeant, the gist of the written notice of his rights: "You can
have someone told you're here. You can see a solicitor, and the duty solicitor
is free. You can read the codes of practice. You don't have to do any of that
now." A London force's notice of the time was found, not Humberside's, so
these are plain words, not a form's.

## Letting him go

- **Cautioned** (a window he owned up to): "You've been cautioned for the
  criminal damage: you admitted it, so there's no charge, but the caution
  stays on your record and can be mentioned in court if you're ever back.
  You're free to go."
- **Charged**, the notice beginning with the Code's own words: "You are
  charged with the offence(s) shown below. You do not have to say anything
  unless you wish to do so, but what you say may be given in evidence. The
  offence: criminal damage. You're bailed to appear at the magistrates' court
  on Thursday at ten o'clock. Not turning up is an offence in itself."
- **Bailed to come back** (a killing, the game's choice): "You're released on
  bail without charge, while enquiries continue. You're to come back to this
  station in four weeks, on a Thursday, at ten o'clock. Not turning up is an
  offence in itself."
- In either of the last two, if they kept it: "We're keeping the coat you had
  on, as evidence."

Ten o'clock and the four weeks are the game's (inferred); a charge's day is
the next weekday sitting. All checked against the content rules.
