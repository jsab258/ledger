# The hints, each the first time it matters

For Jafar's approval, 29 September (town list 6y; the checklist's A04.07,
"instructions shown when their actions become relevant"). The first hour he
approved says every hint fires the first time it can mean something, never on
the clock, in the world's own words where somebody can say it (Sheila, Ron on
the coat), and a line of plain text only where nobody can.

## Why

The old game's four hints ran on the clock: at two game minutes a real second
they came at 1, 5, 30 and 90 seconds, two of them overlapping, and a player
who had not yet found the walking keys was told about the Ledger and a night
job inside the first minute and a half
(production/research/teaching-in-thirty-minutes/SUMMARY.md). The first
session's own job is four steps: he moves, he is seen, it was written down, it
came back. The hints follow those steps.

## The six moments

Keys are shown as the player has them bound, in braces here. A spoken line is
said in that person's approved voice when they are with him; the plain line
always shows.

1. **A new game, and he has not moved after four seconds.** Plain: "{Move}
   walks, {Run} runs. People on this street notice who's about."
2. **He is free to talk to somebody**: in the first hour, when Sheila's
   walk-round ends at the office door, after the notice that the town answers
   in words written by an AI. Sheila: "That's Ron on the rank. Go and say
   hello. Everyone out there wants a look at you." Plain: "Walk up to anyone
   and press {Talk}. What you tell them, they remember." (Sheila's approved
   voice still drifts American in stretches; until a take of this line passes
   the accent gate, the plain line shows alone.)
3. **Ron hands him the coat and the outfit's envelope**, the first ask. Ron,
   as he hands it over: "If you go tonight, boss, wear that. In the dark nobody
   looks twice at a coat." Plain, with it: "{Coat} puts the coat on or takes it
   off: in the dark it makes you harder to recognise."
4. **Somebody saw him do something the town will talk about.** Plain: "Somebody
   saw that. What they make of it can go round."
5. **He overheard a remark about himself.** Plain: "That was about you.
   {Ledger} shows what you believe the street knows."
6. **He opens the Ledger.** Plain, at the top of its screen: "What you believe
   the street now knows about you."

## Rules

- Each shows once in a game, and the save keeps which have.
- Walking first: in a new game nothing else shows until he has walked or been
  told how. After a load he has walked already.
- Never two within twelve real seconds; a hint that falls due inside that
  waits its turn. Ron's handover and the Ledger's line show when they happen,
  since they are part of what is happening.
- A hint about what just happened (4 and 5) is dropped if it has waited fifteen
  seconds, and the next time it happens it can teach; the talk hint waits up to
  forty-five.
- The simulation decides the moments (the Core); the game shows the text and
  plays Sheila's and Ron's lines (the builder's, in their approved voices).
