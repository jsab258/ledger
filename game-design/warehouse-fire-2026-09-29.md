# The warehouse fire

For Jafar (town list cl): backstory, so it is yours, one of your calls on the
1 October page.

What we already have:
- **The outline (approved 28 September):** it is Act I's founding rumour.
  Alison Sedman will not drop it, DS Ellis will not close it, and Mickey's real
  book holds a page in his hand tying it to the outfit.
- **July's Act I (approved), and the Core as built:** the page, dated the week
  of the fire, reads "The warehouse is settled. The fire settled what the rent
  couldn't."
- **The town:** a night watchman walks the warehouse row. His secret is that he
  slipped off home on his break the night of the fire and lied in his report,
  and Darren knows it. A scrap dealer bought fittings stripped from the burned
  building. One man lost a shed to it "last year".
- **One conflict:** Father Walsh's July card has him hearing Mickey's
  confessions "from the fire to the end", for twenty years. That would put the
  fire around 1970; everything else puts it last year.

Nothing yet says when it burned, whose warehouse it was, or whether anybody
was hurt. When Alison asks Tom about it on day 3, the town has nothing to say.

## Recommended

- **When:** a Saturday night last November, eleven months before Tom comes in
  October.
  Father Walsh's card changes to hearing Mickey's confession for twenty years,
  and keeping the last year's.
- **Whose:** an importer's warehouse at the far end of the old warehouse row.
  The stock was his own, tinned goods and machine parts, and insured. He never
  rebuilt, and left the Hook in the spring. The watchman walks the whole row for
  the warehouses still standing.
- **Hurt:** nobody. The watchman should have been walking the row. He had
  slipped off home, and he swore in his report that he had done his rounds.
- **What the town knows:** the evening paper called it arson, since it started
  in two places, and nobody was charged. The street says the owner had it done
  for the insurance, his trade being dead since the docks went (the street's
  talk, not a fact).
- **Alison:** Mickey's outfit was the story she never landed. She has heard the
  owner stopped paying somebody, so she thinks the outfit burned him out, and
  that Mickey knew. So she asks his nephew. Her editor wants it dropped.
- **Ellis:** she was the detective sergeant on it. The file was marked
  undetected, and she keeps asking, because the watchman's times do not fit the
  brigade's, and she thinks someone paid him to be away. Nobody did, but it is
  why she will not let it go, and why she joins the street's crimes to it
  later.
- **The truth, kept out of the talk:**
  - The owner had stopped paying the outfit's rent, and the outfit burned him
    out as a lesson to the row.
  - Mickey found them the men, from outside the Hook; Ron was not one of them.
  - The page in the real book, dated that week, is Mickey's.
  - Father Walsh heard him confess it, and the seal of confession keeps him
    silent for good, so the book is the only way to the truth.

  Only the outfit, Father Walsh and the book hold it; Sheila keeps the book.

Nobody new is named: the importer and the watchman are only what the
street calls them, as its rule for people outside the named cast has it.

## Other ways

- **Long ago, about 1970, as Father Walsh's card has it:** Ellis and Alison
  were too young then to have a case or a story, the watchman's fifteen years
  on the row and his secret go, and so does the shed "last year".
- **The watchman hurt, getting out with burned hands:** it raises the stakes,
  but his secret (he was not there) goes, and he is rewritten.
- **A death:** a murder inquiry, an incident room under a senior officer and
  an inquest. The whole town would still talk of it, not only Alison and
  Ellis. Heavier, and it changes Act I.

## Left open (the reviewer's notes)

- **Whether Sheila has read the page:** her talk depends on it. My leaning is
  that she keeps the books, not Mickey's own pages, and has not read it.
- **The scrap dealer's secret:** he "stripped fittings before the investigation
  started". With a set fire, the scene would have been held from the first
  morning, so his line wants "after the police let it go".
