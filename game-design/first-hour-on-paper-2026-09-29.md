# The first hour, on paper

For Jafar, 29 September (town list 6bk). The first hour you approved, played
through the simulation's own pieces together for each of Tom's choices, before
anybody plays it: the outfit's asks every other night from night one while the
arrangement stands; Ada's tea on day 3; the town's talk hour by hour on the
cast's routines; each morning whether DS Ellis comes; and what the five he met
on day 1 (Sheila, Ron, Darren, Ada, June) show or say as he passes them. Two
game minutes a real second, from nine on day 1 to minute sixty
(`TownReach --first-hour`).

By minute thirty, in every row, the night has already been said to his face,
and at most one person is passing talk of his nights round.

What it shows: whatever he does, the night comes back to his face by minute 13,
in its own words, as the first hour wants; but the outfit's own talk never gets
past Darren, so it is Darren alone who says it. What brings DS Ellis is Ada's
window: a man who takes the envelope on the tea's night is seen going by her,
and her telling puts it round ten people by day 5, loud enough for Ellis near
the end of the hour, whether he sat with her or stood her up. Taking it only on
night one, or never, stays quiet. Ada's tea decides how she thinks of him, not
whether she talks: what she saw, him going down to the landing, is no crime, so
she tells the street, not the police.

What it leaves out, and so how to read it: he is taken to pass each of the
five once an hour, a best case; nobody else sees him on his way to the landing
(the game's own sight would add to that); Ada sees him on day 3 and asks him;
the night he stays away is counted at dawn, six; Alison's question is not in
it, since the fire is not written; the clock is the one your page still asks
about.
